# Lesson 15.5: Parent trigonometric graphs

[Unit 15: Trigonometric functions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Graphs are built by tracking unit-circle values as the input changes. The bounded sine and cosine cycles are contrasted with tangent’s separate unbounded branches.

## Learning objectives

- Construct parent trigonometric graphs from exact anchor values.
- Identify zeros, extrema, domains, ranges, and periods.
- Explain tangent asymptotes and why tangent has no amplitude.

## Learning outcome

The student can sketch and compare the three parent trigonometric graphs using their definitions and domain restrictions.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Produce smooth sine and cosine cycles with correctly placed quarter-cycle anchors and extrema.
- Relate each graph’s zeros and period to its unit-circle values.
- Separate tangent branches at every excluded input and show their correct limiting directions.
- Compare bounded sinusoidal ranges with tangent’s unbounded range without assigning tangent an amplitude.

## Concepts

### Concept U15-L05-C01: Sine and cosine graphs

**Content**

Key quarter-cycle points; domains and ranges; zeros, extrema, and fundamental periods.

**Learning objectives**

- **U15-L05-C01-O1:** Construct sine and cosine graphs from unit-circle coordinates.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7e (direct); CCSS HSF-TF.A.2 (direct).
- **U15-L05-C01-O2:** Identify and compare zeros, extrema, range, and period of sine and cosine.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7e (direct); CCSS HSF-IF.C.9 (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Construct a full sine or cosine cycle from unit-circle coordinates at successive quarter turns.
- Locate zeros, extrema, range, and period from the graph and distinguish the two functions’ phase positions.
- Extend the graph smoothly and periodically without replacing its arcs by straight segments or connecting unrelated points.

**Explanation**

As the input angle changes, plot its vertical coordinate for sine and horizontal coordinate for cosine. At each quarter turn those coordinates give five anchors for one cycle. Join them smoothly, not with straight triangular segments. Both functions have domain $\mathbb R$, range $[-1,1]$, and period $2\pi$, but their maxima occur at different inputs.

**Worked examples**

1. Sine over $[0,2\pi]$ passes through $(0,0),(\pi/2,1),(\pi,0),(3\pi/2,-1),(2\pi,0)$.
2. Cosine over that interval passes through $(0,1),(\pi/2,0),(\pi,-1),(3\pi/2,0),(2\pi,1)$. Its first maximum is at 0 rather than $\pi/2$.

**Exercises**

1. List sine zeros on $[-\pi,\pi]$. **Answer:** $-\pi,0,\pi$.
2. State cosine’s minimum and inputs on $[0,2\pi]$. **Answer:** Minimum $-1$ at $\pi$.

**Assessment**

Sketch one cosine cycle on $[0,2\pi]$ and mark its zeros, maximum, minimum, and period. **Expected:** five cosine anchor points above; zeros $\pi/2,3\pi/2$, maxima 1 at endpoints, minimum $-1$ at $\pi$, period $2\pi$. **Rubric (4):** accurate smooth anchored graph (2, O1); labeled features and period (2, O2).

Objective coverage: **U15-L05-C01-O1**, **U15-L05-C01-O2**.

### Concept U15-L05-C02: Tangent graph and asymptotes

**Content**

Quotient graph; zeros; vertical asymptotes; unbounded range; period $\pi$.

**Learning objectives**

- **U15-L05-C02-O1:** Construct a tangent graph using exact values and denominator zeros.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7e (direct); CCSS HSF-TF.A.2 (direct).
- **U15-L05-C02-O2:** Explain tangent’s domain, period, and lack of amplitude.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7e (direct); CCSS HSF-TF.A.4 (+, direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Locate tangent zeros and vertical asymptotes from the sine numerator and cosine denominator.
- Sketch separate branches using exact anchor values and the correct one-sided unbounded behavior.
- Explain tangent’s domain, all-real range, fundamental period, and absence of a finite amplitude.

**Explanation**

Tangent is sine divided by cosine, so it is undefined at $\pi/2+k\pi$. On $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ it passes through $(-\pi/4,-1),(0,0),(\pi/4,1)$ and grows without bound near its boundary asymptotes. Repeat this branch every $\pi$. Tangent has no maximum or minimum, so its vertical scale factor is not an amplitude.

**Worked examples**

1. At $\pi/4$, tangent is 1. Moving toward $\pi/2$ from the left, sine approaches 1 while positive cosine approaches 0, so the quotient grows without bound.
2. At $3\pi/4$, sine is positive and cosine negative, giving tangent $-1$; this lies on the next branch, not a line connecting across the asymptote.

**Exercises**

1. Give tangent zeros in $[0,2\pi]$. **Answer:** $0,\pi,2\pi$.
2. Give asymptotes in $[0,2\pi]$. **Answer:** $\pi/2,3\pi/2$.

**Assessment**

Sketch tangent on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ with three exact points and asymptotes; state its range and explain why amplitude is undefined. **Expected:** points $(-\pi/4,-1),(0,0),(\pi/4,1)$, asymptotes at the endpoints; range $\mathbb R$, unbounded above/below. **Rubric (4):** correct branch, points, asymptotes (2, O1); range and amplitude explanation (2, O2).

Objective coverage: **U15-L05-C02-O1**, **U15-L05-C02-O2**.
