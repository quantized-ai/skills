# Lesson 15.6: Sinusoidal transformations

Sinusoidal transformations connect equations with amplitude, midline, period, frequency, and phase. Graph construction and equation recovery use these features together with an anchor that fixes position and orientation.

## Learning objectives

- Determine sinusoidal features and distinguish angular frequency from cycle frequency with units.
- Graph combined transformations with correct phase, reflection, and orientation.
- Recover a matching equation from graph features and recognize equivalent representations.

## Learning outcome

Move consistently between a transformed sinusoidal equation and its labeled graph.

## Proficiency criteria

- Extract amplitude, midline, and range, interpreting coefficient signs as reflections.
- Derive period and cycle frequency from a radian argument and attach appropriate units.
- Factor the inner argument and construct a full cycle using quarter-period spacing and the correct orientation.
- Recover an equation from extrema, cycle spacing, and a directional anchor; recognize phase and sine/cosine nonuniqueness.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amplitude, midline, period, and frequency** | For $y=A\sin(Bx)+D$ or $A\cos(Bx)+D$ with real $A,B,D$, $A\ne0$, $B\ne0$, and a radian argument, amplitude is $\lvert A\rvert$, midline is $y=D$, and range is $[D-\lvert A\rvert,D+\lvert A\rvert]$. A full cycle requires an argument change of $2\pi$, so fundamental period is $T=2\pi/\lvert B\rvert$; cycle frequency is $f=1/T$, while angular frequency is $\omega=\lvert B\rvert=2\pi f$. A negative $A$ reflects about the midline; a negative $B$ reverses the input. Amplitude and midline use output units, $T$ uses input units, $f$ uses cycles per input unit, and $\omega$ uses radians per input unit. If $A=0$ or $B=0$, the function is constant and has no least positive period. | Identify nonconstant sinusoidal equations and determine amplitude, midline, range, fundamental period, and cycle frequency with units, justifying period from a full radian cycle. Explain coefficient reflections and distinguish angular frequency from cycle frequency. | Extract vertical features without assigning negative amplitude. Derive period from a full angular cycle and state frequency with units. Distinguish angular and cycle frequency and interpret coefficient signs. Identify constant cases where the nonconstant period formulas do not apply. |
| **Phase shift and transformed graphs** | In $y=A\sin(B(x-C))+D$ or the cosine form, assume real parameters, $A\ne0$, $B\ne0$, and radians. Factoring $Bx+E=B(x-C)$ gives $C=-E/B$; $T=2\pi/\lvert B\rvert$. Anchors at $x=C+jT/4$, $j=0,1,2,3,4$, must respect both coefficient signs. Negative $B$ can be normalized using sine’s oddness and cosine’s evenness. Extrema determine amplitude and midline; spacing between adjacent peaks determines period; a peak, trough, or directed midline crossing fixes phase. Equivalent sine/cosine forms and shifts by whole periods describe the same graph, so phase representation is not unique. | Determine phase shift by factoring the radian argument and graph combined sinusoidal transformations with correct quarter-cycle anchors and orientation. Recover a sinusoidal equation from graph features and a positional or directional anchor, and recognize equivalent phase and sine/cosine representations. | Factor the argument to identify a valid phase shift and retain all reflection effects. Construct and label a complete cycle with correct quarter-period spacing. Recover an equation matching extrema, spacing, and orientation, and verify that equivalent representations describe the same graph. |
