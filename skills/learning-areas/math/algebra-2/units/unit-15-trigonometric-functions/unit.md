# Unit 15: Trigonometric functions

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Trigonometric functions extend angle ratios to all real inputs through the unit circle. This unit links radian measure and coordinates to exact values, identities, graphs, transformations, and periodic models. Geometric reasoning establishes the definitions and signs before graph features and fitted parameters are interpreted. Learners finish by constructing a sinusoid from measurements and judging whether its repeated timing and amplitude remain suitable for prediction.

## Prerequisites

Right-triangle ratios and similar triangles; the Pythagorean theorem; coordinate geometry; signed numbers and simplified radicals; function notation, domains, and transformations. Learners should be able to connect a ratio to triangle sides, work with exact square roots, and explain how input and output transformations move graph points. Graphing and numerical work must keep angle units explicit.

## Main learning objectives

- Connect directed rotations, arc length, and radian inputs to unit-circle positions. [Lesson 15.1](lesson-1-radian-measure/lesson.md), [Lesson 15.2](lesson-2-unit-circle-definitions/lesson.md).
- Derive exact ratios and explain their signs, symmetry, and periodicity geometrically. [Lesson 15.2](lesson-2-unit-circle-definitions/lesson.md), [Lesson 15.3](lesson-3-exact-special-angle-values/lesson.md).
- Prove the Pythagorean identity and use quadrant information to recover consistent ratios. [Lesson 15.4](lesson-4-pythagorean-identity/lesson.md).
- Construct parent and transformed trigonometric graphs and interpret their defining features. [Lesson 15.5](lesson-5-parent-trigonometric-graphs/lesson.md), [Lesson 15.6](lesson-6-sinusoidal-transformations/lesson.md).
- Build periodic models from data and evaluate their predictions, parameters, and usable domains. [Lesson 15.7](lesson-7-periodic-function-models/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Concepts include directed angles and coterminal positions; coordinate definitions and tangent exclusions; special triangles and reference angles; identity proofs and sign recovery; sinusoidal and tangent graphs; amplitude, midline, frequency, period, and phase; and parameter estimation, residuals, and model limitations. The distinction between exact periodic functions and approximately periodic observations remains explicit.

## Lessons

- [15.1: Radian measure](lesson-1-radian-measure/lesson.md): Interpret radians through arc length, convert angular units, and locate terminal positions after signed rotations.
- [15.2: Unit-circle definitions](lesson-2-unit-circle-definitions/lesson.md): Define the three ratios from coordinates and explain their quadrant signs, symmetry, periods, and exclusions.
- [15.3: Exact special-angle values](lesson-3-exact-special-angle-values/lesson.md): Derive special-angle magnitudes from triangles and extend exact values through unit-circle reflections and rotations.
- [15.4: Pythagorean identity](lesson-4-pythagorean-identity/lesson.md): Prove the circle-based identity, use it algebraically, and recover ratios with quadrant-consistent signs.
- [15.5: Parent trigonometric graphs](lesson-5-parent-trigonometric-graphs/lesson.md): Construct sine, cosine, and tangent graphs with justified anchor points, ranges, periods, and asymptotic behavior.
- [15.6: Sinusoidal transformations](lesson-6-sinusoidal-transformations/lesson.md): Analyze and graph sinusoidal transformations, then recover equations from graph features and orientation anchors.
- [15.7: Periodic function models](lesson-7-periodic-function-models/lesson.md): Estimate a periodic model from measurements and use prediction discrepancies and observed coverage to assess it.

## Standards and scope

Common Core Appendix A identifies radian measure, unit-circle extension, periodic modeling, and the Pythagorean identity as Algebra II content. The Common Core mathematics standards also support graphing and transformations. Selected (+) special-angle and symmetry content is included and assessed here. Inverse trigonometric functions, general trigonometric equation solving, and addition formulas remain outside required scope. The Texas Algebra II standards have no direct trigonometry-content counterpart.
