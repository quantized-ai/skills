# Lesson 15.2: Unit-circle definitions

Unit-circle coordinates define sine, cosine, and tangent for angles beyond acute triangles. Reflections and rotations determine their signs, symmetry, periods, and tangent exclusions.

## Learning objectives

- Read sine and cosine from coordinates and connect them to acute-angle triangle ratios.
- Compute tangent and identify its complete set of excluded inputs.
- Explain quadrant signs, parity, and fundamental periods using unit-circle geometry.

## Learning outcome

Derive trigonometric values and structural properties from unit-circle position.

## Proficiency criteria

- Connect coordinate definitions to acute-angle triangle ratios and determine axis-angle values.
- Determine signs from coordinates and form tangent only when its denominator is nonzero.
- Explain odd and even symmetry through reflection.
- Explain full-turn sine and cosine repetition and half-turn tangent repetition, retaining all tangent exclusions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sine, cosine, and tangent as coordinates** | For a real radian angle $\theta$ in standard position, its unit-circle point is $(x,y)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. Tangent is $y/x$ when $x\ne0$; its excluded inputs are $\pi/2+k\pi$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. In quadrant I, these definitions agree with adjacent/hypotenuse, opposite/hypotenuse, and opposite/adjacent ratios. Coordinate definitions also apply on axes and in the other quadrants. | Determine sine and cosine from unit-circle points, including axis positions, and connect them to acute-angle triangle ratios. Compute tangent where defined and express its complete family of excluded inputs. | Read cosine as the horizontal coordinate and sine as the vertical coordinate. Connect those coordinates to acute-angle triangle ratios. Determine axis-angle values. Divide coordinates only when the horizontal coordinate is nonzero and identify all excluded tangent inputs. |
| **Quadrant signs, periodicity, and symmetry** | Coordinate signs determine ratio signs. Reflection across the horizontal axis gives $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$, $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$, and $\tan(-\theta)=-\tan\theta$ where tangent is defined. A full turn restores the point; a half-turn sends $(x,y)$ to $(-x,-y)$ and preserves $y/x$. Fundamental period means the least positive period: $2\pi$ for sine and cosine, $\pi$ for tangent. | Use quadrant position and unit-circle reflections to determine ratio signs and justify sine/tangent odd symmetry and cosine even symmetry. Explain the fundamental periods of sine, cosine, and tangent using unit-circle rotations and preserve tangent exclusions. | Determine signs from terminal coordinates, including for negative angles. Justify each parity relation by reflection. Explain why full turns restore all values and half-turns preserve tangent but generally negate sine and cosine. State the fundamental periods with tangent relations restricted to defined inputs. |
