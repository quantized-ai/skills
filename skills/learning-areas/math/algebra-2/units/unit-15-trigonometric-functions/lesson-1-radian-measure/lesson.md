# Lesson 15.1: Radian measure

[Unit 15: Trigonometric functions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Radian measure links rotation to arc length rather than to an arbitrary angular scale. Signed rotations, unit conversion, and coterminal angles prepare every real input for unit-circle interpretation.

## Learning objectives

- Relate radius, arc length, and signed radian measure.
- Convert between degree and radian units.
- Locate terminal positions after positive or negative full rotations.

## Learning outcome

The student can express a directed rotation in compatible units and identify its unit-circle position.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Use the arc-to-radius ratio to justify a radian measure and preserve it under circle scaling.
- Distinguish nonnegative traveled arc length from signed angular displacement.
- Convert angular units exactly when possible using a consistent full-turn relationship.
- Find a coterminal representative and identify its quadrant or axis without treating it as the same total rotation.

## Concepts

### Concept U15-L01-C01: Directed angles and arc length

**Content**

Counterclockwise positive direction; clockwise negative direction; $\theta=s/r$; unit-circle arc length.

**Learning objectives**

- **U15-L01-C01-O1:** Interpret radians using arc length divided by radius.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-TF.A.1 (direct).
- **U15-L01-C01-O2:** Represent signed rotations and relate arc length to radius and radian measure.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-TF.A.1 (direct); CCSS HSF-TF.A.2 (supporting knowledge).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Define radian measure as the ratio of directed arc displacement to radius and explain why the angle is unchanged by scaling the circle.
- Use rotation direction to assign the angle’s sign while keeping physical traversed arc length nonnegative.
- Translate between radius, radian measure, and arc length, identifying the simplification on a unit circle.

**Explanation**

A radian measures angle by the ratio of an arc length to its radius. On a unit circle, the numerical radian measure equals the directed arc length. A full turn has measure $2\pi$ because circumference is $2\pi r$. For a physical nonnegative arc length use $s=r|\theta|$; a directed arc displacement can carry the sign of $\theta$.

**Worked examples**

1. An arc of 6 cm on a circle of radius 3 cm subtends $6/3=2$ radians; doubling both measurements preserves the angle.
2. A clockwise quarter turn is $-\pi/2$. On a radius-4 circle its traversed arc length is $4(\pi/2)=2\pi$, while the directed arc displacement is $-2\pi$.

**Exercises**

1. An arc is 15 m on a radius-5 m circle. Find the angle. **Answer:** 3 radians.
2. Find arc length for a positive $3\pi/4$ rotation on radius 8. **Answer:** $8(3\pi/4)=6\pi$.

**Assessment**

A point rotates clockwise through 9 cm of arc on a radius-6 cm circle. Give its signed angle and the corresponding arc length on a unit circle. **Expected:** $\theta=-9/6=-1.5$ radians; unit-circle traversed length 1.5 (directed displacement $-1.5$). **Rubric (4):** ratio and radian measure (2, O1); direction and unit-circle interpretation (2, O2).

Objective coverage: **U15-L01-C01-O1**, **U15-L01-C01-O2**.

### Concept U15-L01-C02: Degree conversion and coterminal angles

**Content**

$180^\circ=\pi$ radians; multiples of $2\pi$; representative angles and quadrants.

**Learning objectives**

- **U15-L01-C02-O1:** Convert exactly between degree and radian measure.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-TF.A.1 (supporting knowledge).
- **U15-L01-C02-O2:** Locate a real radian input by reducing full rotations without changing its terminal point.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-TF.A.2 (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Convert degrees and radians using the full-turn relationship while retaining exact multiples of pi where appropriate.
- Find a representative angle in a specified full-turn interval by adding or subtracting complete rotations.
- Distinguish coterminal position from total rotation and locate the terminal point’s quadrant or axis.

**Explanation**

Multiply degrees by $\pi/180$ to convert to radians and radians by $180/\pi$ for degrees. Coterminal angles differ by $2\pi k$ for integer $k$. They end at the same unit-circle point but need not describe the same amount of rotation. Keep exact multiples of $\pi$ when possible.

**Worked examples**

1. $150^\circ(\pi/180^\circ)=5\pi/6$, which is in quadrant II.
2. $-7\pi/3+4\pi=5\pi/3$, so the input ends in quadrant IV. The original measure is $-420^\circ$, while its representative is $300^\circ$.

**Exercises**

1. Convert $225^\circ$. **Answer:** $5\pi/4$.
2. Find the representative of $17\pi/6$ in $[0,2\pi)$. **Answer:** $5\pi/6$, quadrant II.

**Assessment**

Convert $-300^\circ$ to radians, then give a coterminal angle in $[0,2\pi)$ and its quadrant. **Expected:** $-5\pi/3$; adding $2\pi$ gives $\pi/3$, quadrant I. **Rubric (4):** conversion method and result (2, O1); representative and quadrant (2, O2).

Objective coverage: **U15-L01-C02-O1**, **U15-L01-C02-O2**.
