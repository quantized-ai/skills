# Lesson 15.3: Exact special-angle values

Special triangles determine exact trigonometric magnitudes. Reference angles, reflections, and rotations extend these values to other quadrants and to negative or multi-turn inputs.

## Learning objectives

- Derive special-angle values from triangle geometry and unit-radius normalization.
- Use reference angles and coordinate transformations to find exact ratios.
- Evaluate negative or multi-turn special angles with correct signs and tangent restrictions.

## Learning outcome

Reconstruct exact trigonometric values from triangle geometry and unit-circle transformations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Derive special-triangle side ratios and normalize them to unit hypotenuse.
- Locate the actual terminal position while using a reference angle for magnitude.
- Justify signs using reflected or opposite coordinates.
- Return exact trigonometric values and identify undefined tangent at axis positions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Special triangles** | An isosceles right triangle has side ratio $1:1:\sqrt2$; bisecting an equilateral triangle gives $1:\sqrt3:2$ for a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle. Dividing by the hypotenuse produces unit-circle coordinates for $\pi/4$, $\pi/6$, and $\pi/3$. Sine and cosine are the corresponding opposite and adjacent ratios; tangent is their quotient. Exact radical forms retain the geometric values. | Derive exact values at $\pi/4$, $\pi/6$, and $\pi/3$ using the Pythagorean theorem, special triangles, and normalization to unit hypotenuse. Calculate exact sine, cosine, and tangent values from special-triangle ratios without decimal approximation. | Derive both special-triangle side ratios geometrically. Normalize the lengths to unit hypotenuse and assign the correct coordinates. Calculate all three ratios exactly and recognize equivalent radical forms. |
| **Reference angles and reflected coordinates** | For any real radian angle $x$ with unit-circle point $(c,s)$, angles $\pi-x$, $\pi+x$, and $2\pi-x$ have points $(-c,s)$, $(-c,-s)$, and $(c,-s)$. For a terminal point off the axes, its reference angle is the acute angle with the horizontal axis: it supplies ratio magnitudes, while the actual quadrant supplies signs. Reduce whole turns first when useful; axis values follow directly from coordinates, with tangent undefined where cosine is zero. | Use unit-circle reflections and half-turn rotations to express trigonometric ratios at related angles and justify their signs. Find exact values at special-angle terminal positions, including negative, multi-turn, and axis inputs, and identify undefined tangent. | Locate the terminal point after reducing whole turns. Identify the reference angle for a point off the axes and apply coordinate transformations to determine signs. Combine exact triangle magnitudes with those signs; handle axis values directly and retain tangent exclusions. |
