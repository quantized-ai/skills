# Lesson 4.3: Multiplication and conjugates

Multiply complex numbers through distribution and study the cancellation in conjugate products. Extend polynomial identities to factor sums of squares over the complex numbers.

## Learning objectives

- Multiply complex numbers and explain the real and imaginary contributions.
- Identify conjugates and justify their real products.
- Factor real sums of squares over the complex numbers and distinguish complex zeros from real intercepts.

## Learning outcome

Connect complex multiplication, conjugate cancellation, and complex polynomial factorization.

## Proficiency criteria

- Account for every partial product and replace $i^2$ by $-1$.
- Explain why conjugate cross terms cancel and produce a nonnegative real product.
- Use complex factors for sums of squares and state when the resulting zeros are nonreal.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Multiplying complex numbers** | For real $a,b,c,d$, distribution and $i^2=-1$ give $(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i$. The product of the imaginary terms contributes the real term $-bd$. A product is in standard form after all powers of $i$ are reduced and corresponding components are collected. | Multiply complex numbers using distribution and $i^2=-1$, expressing the result in standard form. Explain the sign change and real contribution from the product of imaginary terms. | Include all partial products. Replace $i^2$ by $-1$ and collect real and imaginary components. Trace the signs of the resulting components. |
| **Conjugate products and complex factorization** | The conjugate of $a+bi$ is $a-bi$ for real $a,b$; $(a+bi)(a-bi)=a^2+b^2$, which is positive unless both components are zero. The identity $A^2+B^2=(A-Bi)(A+Bi)$ extends sum-of-squares factorization to complex factors. For real $k\ne0$, $x^2+k^2$ has nonreal zeros $\pm ki$ and no real horizontal intercepts; when $k=0$, zero is a repeated real root. | Identify a conjugate, compute its product with the original number, and explain the cancellation of imaginary terms. Use a difference-of-squares identity to factor a real sum of squares over the complex numbers and distinguish nonreal zeros from real graph intercepts, including the zero-constant case. | Change only the imaginary coefficient sign when forming a conjugate. Justify the real sum-of-squares product. Construct and verify complex factors; distinguish nonreal zeros from the degenerate repeated-real-root case. |
