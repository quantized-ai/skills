# Lesson 4.6: Completing the square

Create perfect-square equations while preserving equivalence. Extend the half-coefficient method to nonmonic equations and explain the scaling of every added term.

## Learning objectives

- Complete the square in monic and nonmonic real-coefficient quadratic equations.
- Justify equality-preserving adjustments, including scaling by an outside coefficient.
- Solve completed-square equations for all exact real or complex roots.

## Learning outcome

Complete the square with valid normalization and recover all exact roots.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the linear coefficient of a monic expression when constructing the missing square term.
- Balance additions on both sides and account for any outside multiplier.
- Retain exact arithmetic and recover every distinct root, including repeated and nonreal cases.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Completing the square when the leading coefficient is one** | For real $b,c$, $x^2+bx=c$ is equivalent to $(x+b/2)^2=c+(b/2)^2$. Half the linear coefficient determines the binomial; its square is the missing constant. Adding the same quantity to both sides preserves solutions. The sign of the completed square’s right-hand side determines two real roots, one repeated real root, or two nonreal roots. | Transform $x^2+bx=c$ into an equivalent perfect-square equation and justify the equal additions. Solve the completed-square equation for all exact real or complex roots, identifying any repeated root. | Choose $(b/2)^2$ and retain the sign of $b/2$ in the binomial. Add the same quantity to both sides. Apply the square-root property and report every distinct exact root. |
| **Completing the square with a nonunit leading coefficient** | For real $a,b,c$ with $a\ne0$, dividing $ax^2+bx+c=0$ by $a$ gives linear coefficient $b/a$; completing the square uses $(b/(2a))^2$. Alternatively, factor $a$ from the quadratic and linear terms before completing the square inside the bracket. Adding $t$ inside that bracket changes the whole expression by $at$, which must be balanced. Fractions and roots remain exact. | Complete the square in a nonmonic quadratic by dividing by the nonzero leading coefficient or factoring it from the quadratic and linear terms, preserving the solution set. Solve the resulting equation exactly and explain the outside scaling of an adjustment made inside a factored bracket. | Apply the half-coefficient rule only to a monic expression. Retain exact fractional arithmetic and balance the full scaled adjustment. Explain the scaling and recover all roots from the completed square. |
