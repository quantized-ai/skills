# Lesson 4.8: Complex quadratic roots and method choice

Solve quadratics whose roots are nonreal and compare valid solution methods. Reconcile exact answers and verify complex roots through substitution.

## Learning objectives

- Solve real-coefficient quadratics with negative discriminant in standard complex form.
- Explain conjugate pairing, verify complex roots, and distinguish them from real graph intercepts.
- Choose and compare exact quadratic-solving methods while preserving the complete solution set.

## Learning outcome

Obtain, verify, and reconcile complete quadratic solution sets across real and complex cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose and justify a method from the equation structure; preserve solutions at every step.
- Simplify both nonreal roots to standard complex form and explain their conjugate relationship.
- Verify a complex root by substitution and distinguish complex zeros from real intercepts.
- Use two valid methods and simplify their answers to establish equivalent solution sets.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Solving quadratics with complex roots** | For real $a,b,c$ with $a\ne0$ and $D=b^2-4ac<0$, $\sqrt D=i\sqrt{-D}$ and the roots are $\frac{-b}{2a}\pm\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2a}i$. After simplification they have equal real parts and opposite nonzero imaginary parts, forming a conjugate pair. They are complex zeros, not horizontal intercepts of the real graph. Substitution checks use $i^2=-1$. | Solve real-coefficient quadratics with negative discriminant in simplified standard complex form, distinguishing their zeros from real graph intercepts. Check a complex root by substitution and explain why its conjugate also appears for real coefficients. | Rewrite the negative discriminant as an imaginary radical and simplify both root components. Justify conjugate pairing from real coefficients. Verify a root using complex arithmetic and identify why neither root is a real intercept. |
| **Choosing and comparing quadratic methods** | Exact methods include inspection, the square-root property, factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula. An isolated square favors square roots; visible factors favor zero product; the formula applies to any quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient. Completing the square reveals the roots’ shared center. Method choice is justified by structure; every valid method preserves and yields the same complete solution set, even when root expressions initially differ. | Select and use an efficient exact method from the equation structure, justify the choice, and preserve all solutions. Solve a quadratic by two valid methods and reconcile their equivalent root expressions. | Justify the selected method from the equation structure. Preserve the complete solution set at each step. Carry out two methods and simplify their root expressions to demonstrate agreement. |
