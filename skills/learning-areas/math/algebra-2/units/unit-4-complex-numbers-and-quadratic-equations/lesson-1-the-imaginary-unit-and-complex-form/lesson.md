# Lesson 4.1: The imaginary unit and complex form

[Unit 4: Complex numbers and quadratic equations](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Extend the real number system with the imaginary unit and standard complex form. Separate a radical’s principal value from the solutions of an equation and identify the components of complex numbers.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify negative real square roots using the imaginary unit.
- Distinguish principal roots from equation solutions.
- Classify complex numbers and identify their real and imaginary parts.

## Learning outcome

The student can express and classify complex numbers while explaining the conventions governing negative square roots.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Use the defining imaginary-unit relation consistently.
- Distinguish the principal radical value from a complete square-equation solution set.
- Identify real and imaginary coefficients, including the real and purely imaginary special cases.
- Explain why unrestricted square-root product rules do not transfer automatically to complex inputs.

## Concepts

### Concept U04-L01-C01: The imaginary unit and negative square roots

**Content**

$i^2=-1$; principal square roots of negative reals; equation roots versus the square-root symbol.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L01-C01-O1:** Use $i^2=-1$ to express principal square roots of negative real numbers.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(A) (supporting knowledge).
- **U04-L01-C01-O2:** Distinguish a principal square root from the two solutions to $x^2=-a$.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.1 (direct); CCSS HSN-CN.C.7 (direct for quadratic solving); TEKS 2A.4(F) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Use the defining square of the imaginary unit when interpreting a negative real radicand.
- Distinguish the principal radical value from the two opposite roots of a square equation.
- Reject an unrestricted real square-root product rule when negative or complex inputs make it invalid.

**Explanation**

No real number squares to a negative number. Define $i$ so that $i^2=-1$; for $a>0$, the principal square root of $-a$ is $i\sqrt a$. The equation $x^2=-a$ has two roots, $\pm i\sqrt a$. The symbol $\sqrt{-a}$ itself denotes the principal one, not both. The real identity $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b$ cannot be applied without restriction to negative or complex inputs: $\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{-1}=-1$, while $\sqrt{1}=1$.

**Worked examples**

$\sqrt{-72}=i\sqrt{72}=6i\sqrt2$. To solve $x^2=-72$, include both signs: $x=\pm6i\sqrt2$. Squaring either yields $36(-1)(2)=-72$.

**Exercises**

1. Simplify $\sqrt{-20}$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $2i\sqrt5$.
2. Solve $x^2=-49$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x=\pm7i$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Simplify $\sqrt{-32}$, solve $z^2=-32$, and explain why the answer formats differ.

Expected answer: The principal square root is $4i\sqrt2$; the equation has roots $\pm4i\sqrt2$. The radical selects one principal value.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for simplified values; 2 points for the principal-root versus equation distinction. Assesses **U04-L01-C01-O1** and **U04-L01-C01-O2**.

### Concept U04-L01-C02: Real and imaginary parts of a complex number

**Content**

Standard form $a+bi$; real part $a$; imaginary part $b$; real numbers and pure imaginary numbers; equality.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L01-C02-O1:** Write a complex number in standard form and identify its real and imaginary parts.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.1 (direct).
- **U04-L01-C02-O2:** Classify complex numbers and equate their real and imaginary components.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(A) (supporting knowledge).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Express each number with real coefficients in standard complex form.
- Identify the imaginary part as its real coefficient rather than the entire imaginary term.
- Classify real and purely imaginary cases consistently and equate corresponding components when two complex numbers are equal.

**Explanation**

Every complex number has a unique form $a+bi$ with real $a,b$. Its real part is $a$, and its imaginary part is the real coefficient $b$, not $bi$. Real numbers have $b=0$ and are included in the complex system; nonzero purely imaginary numbers have $a=0,b\ne0$. Two complex numbers are equal exactly when their real parts and imaginary parts agree. Zero is both real and complex.

**Worked examples**

$5-3i$ has real part $5$ and imaginary part $-3$. Write $-7i=0-7i$ and $4=4+0i$. If $a+bi=2-6i$, uniqueness gives $a=2,b=-6$.

**Exercises**

1. Give real and imaginary parts of $-2+i\sqrt3$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $-2$ and $\sqrt3$.
2. Find real $r,s$ if $(r+1)+(2s)i=5-8i$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $r=4,s=-4$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Classify $6$, $-4i$, and $2+3i$ within the complex system and state the imaginary part of each.

Expected answer: $6$ is real complex with imaginary part $0$; $-4i$ is pure imaginary with part $-4$; $2+3i$ is nonreal complex with part $3$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for classifications; 2 points for correctly identifying coefficients as imaginary parts. Assesses **U04-L01-C02-O1** and **U04-L01-C02-O2**.
