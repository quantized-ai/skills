# Lesson 4.5: Factoring and square-root methods

[Unit 4: Complex numbers and quadratic equations](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Select factoring or the square-root property from the structure of a quadratic equation. Preserve repeated roots, both square-root branches, and real or complex solutions.

## Learning objectives

- Solve quadratics by factoring in zero form.
- Solve isolated-square equations with all branches.
- Justify the method choice and verify solutions.

## Learning outcome

The student can choose an efficient structural method and produce the complete exact quadratic solution set.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Rearrange equations equivalently before applying zero product or isolating a square.
- Recover all roots from the appropriate factor equations or square-root branches.
- Distinguish repeated roots from distinct roots and real solutions from complex solutions.
- Verify a solution in the original equation and justify the selected method from its structure.

## Concepts

### Concept U04-L05-C01: Selecting and using a factoring method

**Content**

Quadratic standard equation form; zero product; repeated roots; choosing efficient structure.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L05-C01-O1:** Solve a quadratic equation by factoring after moving all terms to one side.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-REI.B.4b (direct); TEKS 2A.4(F) (direct).
- **U04-L05-C01-O2:** Identify when factoring is efficient and verify a repeated or distinct solution.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-REI.A.1 (direct); CCSS HSA-REI.B.4b (direct); TEKS 2A.4(F) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Rearrange a quadratic equation into equivalent zero form before applying factorization.
- Use every factor to obtain all distinct solutions and identify repeated-root cases.
- Justify factoring as an efficient method for the presented structure and verify a root in the original equation.

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has the form $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with $a\ne0$. Factoring is efficient when integer or rational factors are visible. Move every term to one side before applying zero product. A repeated factor such as $(x-r)^2$ gives one distinct solution $r$ with multiplicity two. Verify solutions in the original equation, especially when the initial form differs from standard form.

**Worked examples**

Solve $2x^2=5x-2$. Rearrange to $2x^2-5x+2=(2x-1)(x-2)=0$, so $x=1/2$ or $2$. Checking $1/2$ gives $2(1/4)=5(1/2)-2=1/2$. For $x^2-6x+9=0$, $(x-3)^2=0$ gives only $x=3$.

**Exercises**

1. Solve $x^2+x=12$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x=3,-4$.
2. Solve $4x^2-12x+9=0$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x=3/2$, a repeated root.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Solve $3x^2+x=2$ by factoring and verify one solution in the original equation.

Expected answer: $(3x-2)(x+1)=0$, so $x=2/3,-1$; at $-1$, $3-1=2$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for factoring/solutions; 2 points for method rationale and original-equation check. Assesses **U04-L05-C01-O1** and **U04-L05-C01-O2**.

### Concept U04-L05-C02: The square-root property

**Content**

Isolating a squared expression; both signs; real versus complex roots; square-root simplification.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L05-C02-O1:** Solve $(x-h)^2=k$ using both square-root branches.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-REI.B.4b (direct); TEKS 2A.4(F) (direct).
- **U04-L05-C02-O2:** Express roots exactly when $k$ is negative or a nonsquare positive number.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-REI.B.4b (direct); CCSS HSN-CN.C.7 (direct); TEKS 2A.4(F) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Isolate the complete squared expression before taking square roots.
- Include both square-root branches unless they coincide and continue solving each resulting equation.
- Distinguish real and complex outcomes from the sign of the isolated square value and retain exact simplified roots.

**Explanation**

If $u^2=k$, then $u=\pm\sqrt k$ when $k>0$, $u=0$ when $k=0$, and $u=\pm i\sqrt{-k}$ when $k<0$. Isolate the square before taking roots, then solve both resulting linear equations. A square-root symbol gives the principal root, so the $\pm$ is needed in solving. Avoid expanding a square already in a useful form.

**Worked examples**

Solve $2(x-3)^2+8=0$. Subtract $8$ and divide by $2$: $(x-3)^2=-4$. Thus $x-3=\pm2i$, giving $x=3\pm2i$. For $(2x+1)^2=12$, obtain $2x+1=\pm2\sqrt3$, hence $x=-1/2\pm\sqrt3$.

**Exercises**

1. Solve $(x+4)^2=18$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x=-4\pm3\sqrt2$.
2. Solve $3(x-1)^2=-27$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x=1\pm3i$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Solve $4(x+2)^2+20=0$, retaining exact roots and explaining the two signs.

Expected answer: $(x+2)^2=-5$, so $x=-2\pm i\sqrt5$; both opposite square roots square to $-5$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for isolating and solving; 2 points for exact complex form and sign explanation. Assesses **U04-L05-C02-O1** and **U04-L05-C02-O2**.
