# Lesson 4.5: Factoring and square-root methods

Select factoring or the square-root property from the structure of a quadratic equation. Preserve repeated roots, both square-root branches, and real or complex solutions.

## Learning objectives

- Solve real-coefficient quadratic equations by factoring in zero form.
- Solve equations with an isolated squared linear expression, retaining all real or complex roots.
- Justify a structural method choice, distinguish repeated roots, and verify solutions.

## Learning outcome

Choose an efficient structural method and produce the complete exact quadratic solution set.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve equivalence when rearranging to zero form or isolating a square.
- Recover every distinct root from factor equations or square-root branches; identify multiplicity when branches coincide.
- Give exact simplified real or nonreal roots, justify the method choice, and verify a root in the original equation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Selecting and using a factoring method** | A real-coefficient quadratic equation has zero form $ax^2+bx+c=0$, with $a\ne0$. Once a product equals zero, at least one factor must be zero. Factoring is efficient when integer or rational factors are readily identified. A repeated linear factor gives one distinct root of multiplicity two. Rearrangement must preserve equivalence; roots are checked in the original equation. | Solve a quadratic equation by factoring after moving all terms to one side, identifying every distinct root and any repeated root. Justify when factoring is efficient and verify a root in the original equation. | Obtain equivalent zero form before using zero product. Solve every factor equation and identify repeated roots. Justify factoring from the structure and verify a root by substitution. |
| **The square-root property** | For real $k$, $u^2=k$ gives $u=\pm\sqrt k$ if $k>0$, $u=0$ if $k=0$, and $u=\pm i\sqrt{-k}$ if $k<0$. In an isolated-square equation $(mx+n)^2=k$, with real $m,n,k$ and $m\ne0$, both branches must be solved for $x$; they coincide only when $k=0$. The radical denotes a principal value, while the equation requires the complete solution set. Roots remain exact. | Isolate a squared linear expression and solve $(mx+n)^2=k$, with real $m,n,k$ and $m\ne0$, using every square-root branch. Classify roots from the sign of $k$ and express them exactly, including negative, zero, and nonsquare positive cases. | Isolate the entire square. Solve both branches unless they coincide. Identify repeated, distinct real, or nonreal roots from $k$ and retain simplified exact values. |
