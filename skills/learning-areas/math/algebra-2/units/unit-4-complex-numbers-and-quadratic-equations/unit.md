# Unit 4: Complex numbers and quadratic equations

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

This unit extends quadratic solving from the real numbers to the complex numbers. Students define the imaginary unit, calculate in standard complex form, and use conjugates to connect arithmetic with polynomial factorization. The equation sequence revisits factoring and isolated squares, develops completing the square, and derives the quadratic formula. Discriminant analysis organizes the possible root types without confusing complex roots with real intercepts. Complex division and rectangular-plane representations are optional enrichment; their completion is not required for mastery of the core quadratic and arithmetic work.

## Prerequisites

Use Units 2 and 3 for polynomial multiplication, special products, factorization, and zero-product reasoning. Students need signed arithmetic, linear-equation solving, and simplification of numerical square roots. They should be able to distinguish a formula’s principal radical value from the task of finding every equation solution; the opening lesson establishes this distinction explicitly where negative radicands are involved.

## Main learning objectives

- Represent and classify complex numbers using the imaginary unit and real and imaginary components. See [Lesson 4.1](lesson-1-the-imaginary-unit-and-complex-form/lesson.md).
- Compute complex sums, differences, products, and conjugate products and factor real sums of squares. See [Lesson 4.3](lesson-3-multiplication-and-conjugates/lesson.md).
- Choose between factoring and the square-root property while preserving every quadratic root. See [Lesson 4.5](lesson-5-factoring-and-square-root-methods/lesson.md).
- Complete the square, derive the quadratic formula, and classify roots with the discriminant. See [Lesson 4.7](lesson-7-the-quadratic-formula-and-discriminant/lesson.md).
- Solve and verify quadratics with nonreal roots and reconcile equivalent answers from different methods. See [Lesson 4.8](lesson-8-complex-quadratic-roots-and-method-choice/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Core concepts include the imaginary-unit power cycle, componentwise addition, distribution with the imaginary-unit relation, conjugate cancellation, and complex identities. Quadratic methods address zero form, repeated roots, both square-root branches, normalization, fractional square completion, formula derivation, and discriminant conditions. The optional extension studies nonzero complex quotients, modulus, rectangular coordinates, addition as displacement, and conjugation as reflection; it does not claim polar-form coverage.

## Lessons

- [4.1: The imaginary unit and complex form](lesson-1-the-imaginary-unit-and-complex-form/lesson.md) — Define the imaginary unit, simplify negative square roots, distinguish principal values from equation roots, and identify standard complex components.
- [4.2: Powers and additive arithmetic](lesson-2-powers-and-additive-arithmetic/lesson.md) — Use the repeating power cycle and combine complex components through addition, subtraction, and complete additive inverses.
- [4.3: Multiplication and conjugates](lesson-3-multiplication-and-conjugates/lesson.md) — Multiply complex numbers, explain conjugate cancellation, and extend polynomial factorization to sums of squares over the complex numbers.
- [4.4: Optional complex-number extensions](lesson-4-optional-complex-number-extensions/lesson.md) — Optionally divide using conjugates and modulus and represent selected complex operations on the rectangular plane without adding core completion requirements.
- [4.5: Factoring and square-root methods](lesson-5-factoring-and-square-root-methods/lesson.md) — Choose factoring or the square-root property from equation structure, include all branches, and verify repeated or distinct roots.
- [4.6: Completing the square](lesson-6-completing-the-square/lesson.md) — Complete the square in monic and nonmonic equations while balancing every adjustment and retaining exact real or complex solutions.
- [4.7: The quadratic formula and discriminant](lesson-7-the-quadratic-formula-and-discriminant/lesson.md) — Derive and apply the quadratic formula and distinguish root count, multiplicity, and conditional rationality conclusions from the discriminant.
- [4.8: Complex quadratic roots and method choice](lesson-8-complex-quadratic-roots-and-method-choice/lesson.md) — Solve negative-discriminant quadratics, verify conjugate roots, and compare exact methods until their apparently different answers are reconciled.
