# Lesson 4.7: The quadratic formula and discriminant

Derive the quadratic formula from completing the square and interpret its discriminant. Distinguish root count, multiplicity, and rationality without extending a classification beyond its assumptions.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and apply the quadratic formula for real-coefficient quadratic equations.
- Classify roots by discriminant sign, distinguishing distinct-root count from multiplicity.
- State and apply the rational-coefficient conditions for rationality conclusions.

## Learning outcome

Justify the quadratic formula and use its discriminant to describe the resulting roots accurately.

## Proficiency criteria

- Derive the formula through equivalent square-completion steps for any nonzero real leading coefficient.
- Substitute signed coefficients and divide the complete numerator by $2a$ to obtain exact roots.
- Relate discriminant sign to the number and type of roots, with multiplicity.
- Use rational-square discriminant conclusions only when all coefficients are rational.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deriving and applying the quadratic formula** | For real $a,b,c$ with $a\ne0$, completing the square in $ax^2+bx+c=0$ gives $(2ax+b)^2=b^2-4ac$ and $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. This derivation does not assume $a>0$. The whole numerator is divided by $2a$; signed coefficients are substituted as complete quantities. The branches coincide when the radicand is zero; negative radicands give complex roots. | Derive the quadratic formula by completing the square for any nonzero real leading coefficient. Apply the formula with signed coefficients to obtain all exact roots, including repeated and nonreal cases. | Preserve equivalence throughout the derivation and state $a\ne0$. Avoid assuming $a$ is positive. Substitute signed coefficients accurately, divide the full numerator, and simplify both branches. |
| **The discriminant and root classification** | For real coefficients in $ax^2+bx+c=0$, $a\ne0$, let $D=b^2-4ac$. If $D>0$, there are two distinct real roots; if $D=0$, one distinct real root of multiplicity two; if $D<0$, two nonreal conjugate roots. There are always two complex roots counting multiplicity. With rational coefficients, roots are rational exactly when $D$ is the square of a rational number; positive nonsquare rational $D$ gives irrational real roots. | Compute the discriminant and predict the number and type of roots of a real-coefficient quadratic, including multiplicity. Distinguish root-count conclusions from rationality conclusions and justify the rational-coefficient and rational-square conditions. | Use coefficients from an equivalent zero-form quadratic. Classify roots by discriminant sign and distinguish multiplicity from distinct-root count. Apply rationality conclusions only under the stated rational-coefficient conditions. |
