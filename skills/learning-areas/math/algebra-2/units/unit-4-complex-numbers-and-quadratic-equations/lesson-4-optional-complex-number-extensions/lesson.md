# Lesson 4.4: Optional complex-number extensions

This optional lesson extends complex arithmetic to division and rectangular-plane representations. Completion of these extensions is not required for mastery of the core unit.

## Learning objectives

- Divide by a nonzero complex number using its conjugate and justify preservation of the quotient.
- Compute modulus and relate its square to a conjugate product.
- Represent complex numbers in rectangular coordinates and interpret addition and conjugation geometrically.

## Learning outcome

For this optional extension, the student can justify complex division and represent selected complex operations in rectangular coordinates.

## Proficiency criteria

- For this optional extension, require a nonzero divisor and justify the conjugate ratio as one.
- Compute modulus, explain the positive real denominator, and simplify the quotient to standard form.
- Translate between complex form and rectangular coordinates; interpret addition as displacement and conjugation as reflection.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dividing complex numbers — optional extension** | For real $a,b$, $z=a+bi$ has $\lvert z\rvert=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $z\overline z=\lvert z\rvert^2$. A divisor $c+di$, with real $c,d$, must be nonzero, equivalently $c^2+d^2>0$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $c-di$ preserves the quotient and gives the positive real denominator $c^2+d^2$. Division by zero is undefined. This concept is optional. | Divide by a nonzero complex number using its conjugate and justify the equivalent quotient in standard form. Compute modulus and use $z\overline z=\lvert z\rvert^2$ to explain the positive denominator for a nonzero divisor. | Check that the divisor is nonzero. Use a conjugate ratio equal to one. Simplify both quotient components and justify the denominator as a positive modulus square. |
| **The rectangular complex plane — optional extension** | The point $(a,b)$ represents $a+bi$ on a horizontal real axis and vertical imaginary axis. Addition combines coordinate displacements: $(a,b)+(c,d)=(a+c,b+d)$. Conjugation maps $(a,b)$ to $(a,-b)$, a reflection across the real axis. This optional concept uses rectangular coordinates; polar form and multiplication as rotation are outside its scope. | Represent $a+bi$ by rectangular coordinates $(a,b)$ and recover a complex number from a plotted point. Interpret complex addition as coordinate displacement and conjugation as reflection across the real axis. | Assign real and imaginary coefficients to the correct axes and translate in both directions. Combine displacements for addition. Reflect only the imaginary coordinate for conjugation, using the stated rectangular representation. |
