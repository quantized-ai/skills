# Lesson 4.4: Optional complex-number extensions

[Unit 4: Complex numbers and quadratic equations](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

This optional lesson extends complex arithmetic to division and rectangular-plane representations. Completion of these extensions is not required for mastery of the core unit.

## Learning objectives

- Divide complex numbers using conjugates.
- Relate conjugate products to modulus.
- Interpret rectangular coordinates, addition, and conjugation geometrically.

## Learning outcome

For this optional extension, the student can justify complex division and represent selected complex operations in rectangular coordinates.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- For optional extension proficiency, require a nonzero denominator and justify multiplication by the conjugate ratio.
- Explain the real denominator as a modulus square and reduce the quotient to standard form.
- Represent complex components on the appropriate axes and interpret addition as displacement.
- Identify conjugation as reflection and distinguish the covered rectangular representation from polar form.

## Concepts

### Concept U04-L04-C01: Dividing complex numbers — optional extension

**Content**

Nonzero complex denominator; multiplying by its conjugate; real denominator; modulus.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L04-C01-O1:** Divide complex numbers using the denominator conjugate.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.3 (+) (direct, optional extension beyond Appendix A Algebra II allocation).
- **U04-L04-C01-O2:** Compute the modulus and use $z\overline z=|z|^2$ to explain why the denominator is nonzero.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.A.3 (+) (direct, optional extension).

**Proficiency criteria**

This optional concept is assessed separately from required core proficiency.

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Require a nonzero divisor before forming a complex quotient.
- Multiply by a conjugate ratio equal to unity and explain why its denominator is a positive real modulus square.
- Simplify both quotient components and relate the conjugate product to the modulus definition.

**Explanation**

For $z=a+bi$, its modulus is $|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. A nonzero denominator $c+di$ has $c^2+d^2>0$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $c-di$ therefore replaces the complex denominator with the positive real $c^2+d^2$. This multiplies the fraction by one and preserves its value. Division by $0+0i$ remains undefined.

**Worked examples**

$\frac{3+2i}{1-i}=\frac{(3+2i)(1+i)}{(1-i)(1+i)}=\frac{1+5i}{2}=\frac12+\frac52i$. The denominator is $|1-i|^2=1^2+(-1)^2=2$.

**Exercises**

1. Compute $(2+i)/(1+i)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $(3-i)/2$.
2. Find $|-5+12i|$ and the product with its conjugate.
   - Answer and reasoning: Modulus $13$; conjugate product $169$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Divide $(4-i)/(2+i)$ and explain the value of the denominator after conjugation.

Expected answer: Numerator $(4-i)(2-i)=7-6i$; denominator $5$, so $7/5-(6/5)i$. The denominator is $|2+i|^2=5>0$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for quotient; 2 points for modulus/nonzero explanation. Assesses **U04-L04-C01-O1** and **U04-L04-C01-O2**.

### Concept U04-L04-C02: The rectangular complex plane — optional extension

**Content**

Horizontal real axis; vertical imaginary axis; point representation; addition and conjugation geometrically.

**Learning objectives**

- **U04-L04-C02-O1:** Represent $a+bi$ by $(a,b)$ and recover a complex number from a plotted point.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.B.4 (+) (partial direct: rectangular form only; optional extension).
- **U04-L04-C02-O2:** Interpret complex addition and conjugation geometrically.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-CN.B.5 (+) (partial direct: addition and conjugation; optional extension).

**Proficiency criteria**

This optional concept is assessed separately from required core proficiency.

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Assign real and imaginary coefficients to the horizontal and vertical coordinates consistently.
- Interpret addition as the combination of coordinate displacements.
- Identify conjugation as reflection across the real axis and distinguish this rectangular treatment from unaddressed polar-form requirements.

**Explanation**

The complex plane records a complex number as an ordered pair of real coordinates: $(a,b)$ represents $a+bi$. Addition adds coordinates, so a complex number can be viewed as a displacement. Conjugation reflects the point across the real axis, since only the imaginary coordinate changes sign. This lesson covers rectangular representation; polar form and general multiplication as rotation are beyond the required core.

**Worked examples**

Plot $z=-2+3i$ at $(-2,3)$ and $w=4-i$ at $(4,-1)$. Their sum is $2+2i$, located at $(2,2)$ after adding displacements. The conjugate $-2-3i$ lies at $(-2,-3)$, the reflection of $z$ across the horizontal real axis.

**Exercises**

1. Give the complex number at $(5,-2)$ and its conjugate point.
   - Answer and reasoning: $5-2i$; conjugate point $(5,2)$.
2. Add the displacements for $1+4i$ and $-3+2i$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Endpoint $(-2,6)$, representing $-2+6i$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Plot or specify coordinates for $z=3-2i$, $\overline z$, and $z+(-1+5i)$; describe the geometric operations.

Expected answer: $(3,-2)$, $(3,2)$, and $(2,3)$; conjugation is reflection across the real axis, and addition combines coordinate displacements.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for coordinates; 2 points for geometric interpretation. Assesses **U04-L04-C02-O1** and **U04-L04-C02-O2**.
