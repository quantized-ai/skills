# Lesson 12.6: Inverse function families

Exponential and logarithmic functions, square-root and restricted quadratic functions, and cubic and cube-root functions form inverse pairs. Their formulas, domains, ranges, and graph features must agree under reversal.

## Learning objectives

- Derive inverses of simple transformed exponential, logarithmic, square-root, and cubic functions.
- Verify both compositions, preserve family-specific restrictions, and describe graph features exchanged by reflection.

## Learning outcome

Connect inverse function families algebraically and graphically while retaining complete domain and range conditions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Isolate the defining exponential, logarithmic, root, or cubic operation and solve for the original input.
- Determine inverse domains from original ranges, including positive logarithm arguments and square-root sign restrictions.
- Verify both compositions on their stated sets.
- Relate reflected domains, ranges, and asymptotes; distinguish restricted quadratic inverses from all-real cube-root inverses.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exponential and logarithmic inverses** | For $f(x)=A b^{c(x-h)}+k$ with $A,c\ne0$, $b>0$, and $b\ne1$, the inverse is $h+\frac1c\log_b((x-k)/A)$ on $(x-k)/A>0$. Reversal exchanges exponential and logarithmic formulas, their domains and ranges, and horizontal and vertical asymptotes. The identities $\log_b(b^u)=u$ for real $u$ and $b^{\log_b v}=v$ for $v>0$ support verification; a transformed logarithm is inverted by isolating the logarithm and exponentiating. | Derive inverses of transformed exponential and logarithmic functions with valid bases, nonzero scales, and explicit domains. Verify both compositions and explain the reflected domains, ranges, asymptotes, and graph points. | Isolate the defining operation and apply its inverse correctly. Require a valid base and positive logarithm argument; exchange complete domains and ranges. Verify both identities on their sets and identify the reflected asymptotes and corresponding graph points. |
| **Inverses of square-root and cubic functions** | For $f(x)=A\sqrt{x-h}+k$ with $A\ne0$, the inverse is $h+((x-k)/A)^2$ restricted by $(x-k)/A\ge0$; its range is $[h,\infty)$. Squaring does not remove that inherited inverse-domain restriction. For $g(x)=A(x-h)^3+k$ with $A\ne0$, the inverse is $h+\sqrt[3]{(x-k)/A}$, with domain and range $\mathbb R$. Root sign conditions and real cube-root identities determine whether both compositions return their inputs. | Derive inverse formulas for simple transformed square-root and cubic functions and verify both compositions. Determine and preserve inverse domains and ranges, distinguishing a restricted quadratic inverse from an all-real cube-root inverse. | Record the original range before squaring and retain its sign condition as the inverse domain. Give the corresponding restricted quadratic or cube-root formula with complete sets. Verify both compositions using the required branch conditions and explain the different domain restrictions. |
