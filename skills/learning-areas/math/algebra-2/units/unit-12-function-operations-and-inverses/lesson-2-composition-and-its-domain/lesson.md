# Lesson 12.2: Composition and its domain

Composition feeds an inner function’s output into an outer function. Its order and domain follow the complete process, including restrictions hidden by a simplified formula.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and evaluate compositions from formulas or tables, comparing order and checking contextual units.
- Determine composition domains from inner existence and outer admissibility, including after simplification.

## Learning outcome

Represent composition as an ordered process and justify its formula, contextual compatibility, and allowed inputs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Evaluate the inner function first; distinguish composition from multiplication and from reversed composition.
- Check that inner output units match outer input units in a stated context.
- Substitute the complete inner expression into every occurrence of the outer input.
- Require the inner function to exist and its output to lie in the outer domain; retain both conditions after simplification.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Composition order and evaluation** | $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$: $g$ acts first and $f$ acts on its output. Composition generally depends on order and differs from pointwise multiplication. Evaluation from a table requires both stages to be specified; missing entries do not authorize interpolation. In context, inner output units must match outer input units. | Evaluate compositions from formulas or tables in the correct order, checking that both stages are defined. Distinguish composition from multiplication, compare reversed compositions, and check compatible units in context. | Identify inner and outer roles and evaluate both stages in order. Use only values supported by the supplied formulas or tables. Distinguish reversed order and multiplication, and verify contextual unit compatibility. |
| **Algebraic composition with restrictions** | An algebraic composition substitutes the full inner expression into each occurrence of the outer input. Its domain is $\{x\in D_g:g(x)\in D_f\}$. Both inner existence and outer admissibility are required, even when cancellation or another simplification gives a formula with a larger natural domain. | Construct and simplify an algebraic composition by substituting the entire inner expression. Determine the composition domain using the inner domain and outer input conditions, retaining inherited restrictions after simplification. | Substitute the complete inner expression with correct grouping. Determine every input satisfying both stages. State the composition domain explicitly and preserve restrictions hidden by simplification. |
