# Unit 12: Function operations, composition, and inverses

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

This unit combines previously studied functions and reverses their input-output relationships. Arithmetic operations use a common input; composition passes an output from one function into another; an inverse reverses an entire mapping. Their differences are developed through formulas, tables, graphs, and domains. Students use those distinctions to construct valid combinations and verify inverse formulas across the rational, root, exponential, and logarithmic families.

## Prerequisites

Function notation, graph transformations, interval and set notation, equation solving, rational expressions, square and cube roots, exponentials, and logarithms. Students should be able to state the natural domain of each family and preserve restrictions during algebraic simplification.

## Main learning objectives

- Construct arithmetic combinations and determine their original-domain restrictions. See [Lesson 12.1](lesson-1-arithmetic-combinations-of-functions/lesson.md).
- Evaluate and express compositions with correct order and admissible inputs. See [Lesson 12.2](lesson-2-composition-and-its-domain/lesson.md).
- Identify one-to-one functions and read inverse mappings from pairs and graphs. See [Lesson 12.3](lesson-3-inverse-relations-and-one-to-one-functions/lesson.md).
- Derive linear and rational inverse formulas and exchange domains with ranges. See [Lesson 12.4](lesson-4-solving-for-inverse-formulas/lesson.md).
- Restrict noninvertible domains and verify inverse relationships across function families. See [Lesson 12.5](lesson-5-restrictions-and-inverse-verification/lesson.md), [Lesson 12.6](lesson-6-inverse-function-families/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Pointwise sums, differences, products, and quotients establish domain intersections and divisor conditions. Composition adds a two-stage admissibility test. One-to-one behavior then explains inverse existence, graphical reflection, and exchanged input-output sets. Algebraic inverse construction develops from linear and rational equations to restricted quadratics, exponential-logarithmic pairs, and square-root or cubic relationships. Throughout, a simplified expression is interpreted together with the domain inherited from the original operation.

## Lessons

- [12.1: Arithmetic combinations of functions](lesson-1-arithmetic-combinations-of-functions/lesson.md) — Combine outputs at the same input and determine the additional restriction required for division.
- [12.2: Composition and its domain](lesson-2-composition-and-its-domain/lesson.md) — Track inner-to-outer order and derive domains before and after simplifying compositions.

- [12.6: Inverse function families](lesson-6-inverse-function-families/lesson.md) — Connect exponential, logarithmic, square-root, and cubic inverse families, including hidden range restrictions.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards retain (+) labels for composition, inverse verification, inverse values from tables or graphs, domain restriction, and the exponential-logarithmic inverse relationship. Texas standards directly include several of these inverse-function requirements, and Common Core Appendix A extends inverse construction to simple rational, radical, and exponential functions. Those (+) labels describe the standards and do not make this unit’s included lessons optional. Original concept-level objectives, mappings, examples, exercises, and scored assessments remain in the lesson files.
