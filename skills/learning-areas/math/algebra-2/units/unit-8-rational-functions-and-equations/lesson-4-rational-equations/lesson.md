# Lesson 8.4: Rational equations

Rational equations are solved on their original domains by clearing denominators. Algebraic verification and graph or table comparisons distinguish valid solutions from excluded candidates.

## Learning objectives

- Solve rational equations that reduce to linear or quadratic equations, including cases with no solutions or an identity.
- Verify candidates in the original equation and relate valid solutions to equal outputs in graphs or tables.

## Learning outcome

Find the complete real solution set of a rational equation that reduces to a linear or quadratic equation, and justify every exclusion.

## Proficiency criteria

- State original denominator exclusions and multiply every term by an LCD.
- Explain that denominator clearing preserves equivalence on the original domain.
- Find every real candidate, verify it in the original equation, and distinguish contradictions from identities.
- Confirm valid solutions with graph intersections or a table while rejecting apparent intersections at excluded inputs.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clearing denominators and checking candidates** | The original denominator zeros are excluded before solving. Multiplying every term by the least common denominator (LCD) gives an equivalent equation on that restricted domain, where the LCD is nonzero. Solutions of the cleared equation are candidates until checked in the original equation; excluded inputs never qualify. A contradiction gives no solutions; an identity gives every input in the original domain. | Solve rational equations by multiplying every term by an LCD, explain equivalence on the restricted domain, and recognize contradictions or identities. Reject excluded candidates and verify remaining solutions in the original equation. | List all original exclusions and apply an LCD to the complete equation. Explain why the multiplication is reversible only on the permitted domain. Solve the resulting equation, distinguish contradiction and identity cases, and verify every retained candidate in the original equation. |
| **Multiple solutions and graphical confirmation** | Clearing rational denominators can produce a quadratic with zero, one, or two distinct real candidates. Each candidate must satisfy the original domain restrictions and equation. Valid solutions are input coordinates where the original left- and right-side functions have equal outputs. Graphs and tables can confirm candidates, but a finite plotting window or sample table does not prove completeness; plotting artifacts across vertical asymptotes or holes are not intersections. | Solve rational equations producing quadratics, obtain all real candidates, and classify their validity in the original equation. Use graph intersections or a table to verify rational-equation solutions and distinguish valid equal outputs from discontinuity artifacts. | Obtain all real roots of the cleared quadratic and test each in the original equation. Connect valid solutions to equal outputs at allowed inputs in a graph or table. Reject apparent intersections at poles or missing points and support completeness algebraically. |
