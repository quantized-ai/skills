# Lesson 8.5: Rational equations from relationships

Inverse variation and rate relationships generate rational equations. Variables, units, and contextual restrictions establish what a solution means and whether it is viable.

## Learning objectives

- Identify and formulate inverse-variation relationships using a constant product.
- Formulate rational equations from constant-rate relationships with defined quantities and compatible units.
- Solve and verify the equations against original relationships, domain restrictions, and contextual expectations.

## Learning outcome

Translate an inverse-variation or rate relationship into a rational equation and justify its solution mathematically and in context.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish constant-product inverse variation from direct variation or merely decreasing behavior.
- Construct an inverse-variation or rate equation with defined quantities, compatible units, and justified restrictions.
- Solve within the original domain and any stricter contextual conditions.
- Verify the result in the original relationship and against expected scaling or combined-rate behavior.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Inverse variation** | Inverse variation has $y=k/x$, equivalently $xy=k$, for a fixed nonzero constant $k$; both variables are nonzero. Scaling one variable by a nonzero factor scales the other by its reciprocal. Direct variation instead has constant ratio $y/x$. A decreasing graph alone does not establish inverse variation, and one pair determines $k$ only when the model is assumed. Consistent products support the model for supplied data. Units give $k$ the product of the variables’ units; context can further restrict the domain. | Identify inverse variation using a constant product, distinguish it from direct variation, and formulate an assumed inverse-variation model from a pair of values. Solve and interpret inverse-variation equations, including reciprocal scaling, units, and relevant restrictions. | Use products to assess supplied data and determine the constant in an assumed model. Distinguish constant-product behavior from constant ratio or merely decreasing behavior. Solve for either variable, explain reciprocal scaling, and retain nonzero and contextual restrictions with consistent units. |
| **Formulating rational equations and testing reasonableness** | For a positive constant rate, amount equals rate times time. Completing one common job in time $t>0$ gives rate $1/t$ jobs per time unit. Simultaneous processes with constant, additive contributions to the same job have combined rate equal to the sum of their rates; completion times are not added. The combined completion time is less than each individual time when all contributions are positive. Variables, compatible units, nonzero denominators, and contextual conditions constrain every model and its solution. | Translate constant-rate relationships into rational equations using defined quantities, compatible units, and justified additive-rate assumptions. Solve rational rate equations and verify algebraic validity, contextual restrictions, and reasonableness, including combined versus individual completion times. | Convert completion times to rates before combining contributions to a common job. Define variables and units and state positivity or other contextual restrictions. Verify the solved value in the original equation and check expected combined-rate behavior when its assumptions apply. |
