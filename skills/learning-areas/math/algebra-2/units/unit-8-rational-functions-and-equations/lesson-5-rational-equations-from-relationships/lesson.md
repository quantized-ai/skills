# Lesson 8.5: Rational equations from relationships

[Unit 8: Rational functions and equations](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Inverse variation and rate relationships generate rational equations from quantities. Formulation includes units and contextual restrictions, so a numerical answer can be checked for both algebraic validity and practical meaning.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate inverse-variation and rate equations from stated relationships.
- Solve the equations and judge solutions against units and context.

## Learning outcome

The student can translate a quantitative relationship into a rational equation and defend its solution as a viable value.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Define each quantity and distinguish a constant-product relationship from mere decreasing behavior.
- Construct the appropriate reciprocal or combined-rate equation with consistent units.
- Solve within the nonzero and contextual restrictions established during formulation.
- Verify the answer in the original relationship and compare it with expected qualitative behavior.

## Concepts

### Concept U08-L05-C01: Inverse variation

**Content**

- Constant product $xy=k$
- Distinguishing inverse from direct variation

**Learning objectives**

- **U08-L05-C01-O1:** Formulate an inverse-variation equation from a pair of values.
  - Standards: TEKS 2A.6(L) (direct); CCSS HSA-CED.A.2 (direct).
- **U08-L05-C01-O2:** Solve and interpret inverse-variation equations with relevant restrictions.
  - Standards: TEKS 2A.6(L) (direct); CCSS HSA-CED.A.1 (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Use a constant product to identify and construct an inverse-variation relationship.
- Explain how changing one variable changes the other under a fixed nonzero constant.
- Solve for either variable and retain nonzero or stricter contextual domain conditions.

**Explanation**

Inverse variation means $y=k/x$ for a fixed nonzero $k$, so $xy=k$. Doubling $x$ halves $y$ when the model applies. A decreasing graph alone does not prove inverse variation: test the constant product. Positive measured quantities often restrict the mathematical domain $x\ne0$ further to $x>0$.

**Worked examples**

If $y=12$ when $x=3$, then $k=36$ and $y=36/x$. At $x=8$, $y=4.5$. To find the input when $y=9$, solve $9x=36$, giving $x=4$. The pairs $(3,12),(8,4.5),(4,9)$ all have product 36.

**Exercises**

1. Find a model through $(5,8)$ and predict at $x=10$. **Answer:** $y=40/x$; $y=4$.
2. Do $(2,6),(3,4),(6,2)$ show inverse variation? **Answer:** yes, every product is 12; $y=12/x$.

**Assessment**

For positive quantities in inverse variation, $y=15$ when $x=4$. Write the equation and find $x$ when $y=10$.

- **U08-L05-C01-O1 — 2 points:** $k=4\cdot15=60$, so $y=60/x$, with context domain $x>0$.
- **U08-L05-C01-O2 — 2 points:** $10=60/x$ gives $x=6$; $6\cdot10=60$ verifies the constant product.

### Concept U08-L05-C02: Formulating rational equations and testing reasonableness

**Content**

- Rates and reciprocals
- Context constraints and units

**Learning objectives**

- **U08-L05-C02-O1:** Translate a rate relationship into a rational equation.
  - Standards: TEKS 2A.6(H) (direct); CCSS HSA-CED.A.1 (direct).
- **U08-L05-C02-O2:** Solve the equation and evaluate whether its solution is reasonable in context.
  - Standards: TEKS 2A.6(I) (direct); TEKS 2A.6(J) (direct); CCSS HSA-CED.A.3 (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Translate completion time into a rate before combining independent contributions.
- Keep variable definitions, units, and positivity restrictions consistent throughout the equation.
- Check the solved value against both the original rates and the expected relationship between combined and individual times.

**Explanation**

If a process completes one job in $t$ hours, its rate is $1/t$ jobs per hour. Add rates when independent processes work together; do not add completion times. Define the unknown and its units, state positivity restrictions, then check the result against the original relationship and the situation.

**Worked examples**

One process takes 6 hours alone; together with a second it takes 2 hours. Let $t>0$ be the second process’s time. Then $1/6+1/t=1/2$, so $1/t=1/3$ and $t=3$ hours. The combined time 2 is shorter than both individual times, as positive rates require.

**Exercises**

1. Two processes take 4 and 12 hours alone. Find combined time. **Answer:** $1/t=1/4+1/12=1/3$, so 3 hours.
2. A 120 km trip takes $120/v$ hours. Write an equation for a 3-hour trip and solve. **Answer:** $120/v=3$, $v=40$ km/h, $v>0$.

**Assessment**

One machine takes 8 hours alone; together with another it finishes in 3 hours. Formulate an equation for the other machine’s time, solve, and interpret.

- **U08-L05-C02-O1 — 2 points:** With $t>0$ in hours, $1/8+1/t=1/3$ in jobs per hour.
- **U08-L05-C02-O2 — 2 points:** $1/t=5/24$, so $t=24/5=4.8$ hours; both solo times exceed 3 and the rates sum to $1/3$.
