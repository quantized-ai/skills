# Unit 8: Rational functions and equations

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Rational functions connect polynomial quotients to graphs and equations. This unit begins with the reciprocal parent, then studies how factors control missing points, vertical asymptotes, intercepts, signs, and end behavior. Domain restrictions remain central when solving equations or interpreting quantitative relationships. Students coordinate formulas, graphs, tables, and solution checks rather than treating those representations as separate procedures.

## Prerequisites

Rational-expression operations and original-domain restrictions from Unit 7; factoring; linear and quadratic equations; function notation, transformations, and interval notation. Students should be able to reduce a fraction while retaining its excluded inputs.

## Main learning objectives

- Construct reciprocal graphs and explain their transformed features. See [Lesson 8.1](lesson-1-reciprocal-functions-and-transformations/lesson.md).
- Relate factors to discontinuities, intercepts, signs, and end behavior. See [Lesson 8.2](lesson-2-discontinuities-and-intercepts/lesson.md), [Lesson 8.3](lesson-3-end-behavior-domain-and-range/lesson.md).
- Determine domain and range without confusing asymptotes with missing outputs. See [Lesson 8.3](lesson-3-end-behavior-domain-and-range/lesson.md).
- Solve rational equations and verify allowed intersections. See [Lesson 8.4](lesson-4-rational-equations/lesson.md).
- Formulate and interpret inverse-variation and rate equations. See [Lesson 8.5](lesson-5-rational-equations-from-relationships/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

The core sequence covers reciprocal transformations; holes versus surviving denominator zeros; intercepts and sign intervals; horizontal asymptotes; range reasoning; LCD-based equation solving; and quantitative rational relationships. Graph features are checked against the original domain, while equation solutions are checked against the original expressions. Slant asymptotes and rational inequalities extend the same tools in a separately optional lesson.

## Lessons

- [8.1: Reciprocal functions and transformations](lesson-1-reciprocal-functions-and-transformations/lesson.md) — Graph the reciprocal parent and map its points, asymptotes, and excluded values under transformations.
- [8.2: Discontinuities and intercepts](lesson-2-discontinuities-and-intercepts/lesson.md) — Use original and reduced factors to classify discontinuities, locate intercepts, and determine signs.
- [8.3: End behavior, domain, and range](lesson-3-end-behavior-domain-and-range/lesson.md) — Analyze horizontal end behavior and express fully justified domains and ranges in three notations.
- [8.4: Rational equations](lesson-4-rational-equations/lesson.md) — Clear denominators, solve resulting equations, and verify solutions algebraically and graphically.
- [8.5: Rational equations from relationships](lesson-5-rational-equations-from-relationships/lesson.md) — Build inverse-variation and rate equations with units, restrictions, and reasonableness checks.
- [8.6: Optional rational-function extensions](lesson-6-optional-rational-function-extensions/lesson.md) — Optional: use division for slant asymptotes and sign charts for rational inequalities.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards retain the (+) label on general rational graphing; Texas standards provide direct Algebra II coverage of reciprocal transformations, rational equations, asymptotic restrictions, and inverse variation. Common Core Appendix A supplies the rational-equation and graphical-solution connections. Lesson 8.6 is enrichment: its criteria and assessments do not determine required-unit completion. The core assessment samples Lessons 8.1–8.5 and complements the concept-level evidence.

Complete the [Unit 8 assessment](assessment.md) after the required lessons. Use its results alongside the concept assessments to identify any remaining gaps.
