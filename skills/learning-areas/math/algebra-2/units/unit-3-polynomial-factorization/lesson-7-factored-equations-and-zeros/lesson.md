# Lesson 3.7: Factored equations and zeros

Solve polynomial equations by establishing zero form and using a factorization. Preserve every solution and connect real zeros with horizontal intercepts.

## Learning objectives

- Solve polynomial equations in zero form using the zero-product property and verify solutions.
- Explain how dividing by a potentially zero expression can discard roots.
- Distinguish repeated roots, distinct solutions, zero inputs, and real intercept coordinates.

## Learning outcome

Obtain a complete solution set from a factored polynomial equation and interpret each real solution as a zero and horizontal intercept.

## Proficiency criteria

- Establish an equivalent equation with zero on one side before applying zero product.
- Solve each factor equation in the stated number system and combine the solutions without duplicating repeated roots.
- Handle the zero case separately before dividing by a potentially zero expression.
- Verify solutions in the original equation and distinguish real zero inputs from intercept coordinates.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The zero-product property** | A product of real or complex numbers equals zero exactly when at least one factor equals zero. The solution set is the union of the factor-equation solution sets in the specified number system; a repeated factor does not create a new distinct solution. The rule does not apply to a nonzero product. Division by an expression assumes it is nonzero, so its zero case must be checked separately to avoid losing roots. | Solve factored polynomial equations using the zero-product property in the specified number system, distinguishing repeated roots from distinct solutions. Explain why the product must equal zero and preserve any solutions excluded by division through a potentially zero expression. | Apply zero product only when the entire product equals zero. Solve every relevant factor equation and take the union without duplicate solutions. Identify the assumption introduced by division and retain or separately check the excluded zero case. |
| **Connecting zeros, factors, and polynomial equations** | The equation $p(x)=q(x)$ is equivalent to $p(x)-q(x)=0$; factor the difference and solve its factor equations. For a polynomial function $f$, a factor $(x-r)$ makes $r$ a zero because substitution gives $f(r)=0$. A real zero corresponds to the horizontal intercept $(r,0)$; a nonreal zero is not an intercept on the real coordinate plane. Check solutions in the original equation. | Rewrite a polynomial equation with zero on one side, solve by factoring in the stated number system, and verify all solutions in the original equation. Relate a factor $(x-r)$ to a zero $r$, distinguishing a real zero input from its horizontal-intercept point $(r,0)$. | Create an equivalent zero-form equation before factoring. Recover every solution from the factors and check it in the original equation. Use substitution to justify the factor-to-zero connection and report intercept coordinates only for real zeros. |
