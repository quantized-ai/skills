# Unit 3: Polynomial factorization

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Polynomial factorization reverses the arithmetic developed in Unit 2. Each lesson isolates a recognizable structure before students combine methods into complete factorizations of higher-degree expressions. The default coefficient set is the integers or rationals unless a question states otherwise; completeness is always relative to that choice. The final lesson connects a product to an equation only after zero form has been established. Cubic and quartic work ensures that factoring extends beyond the familiar quadratic patterns without abandoning their structural foundations.

## Prerequisites

Students should have completed Units 1 and 2, especially distribution, signed coefficients, monomial exponent rules, standard form, and special products. They must distinguish an expression from an equation and be able to verify a product by expansion. Real square roots may be used when contrasting rational and real coefficient sets; systematic complex factorization follows in Unit 4.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret and verify a factorization, including a greatest common monomial factor. See [Lesson 3.1](lesson-1-factoring-and-greatest-common-factors/lesson.md).
- Expose repeated polynomial factors through sign changes, regrouping, and distribution. See [Lesson 3.2](lesson-2-common-binomial-factors-and-grouping/lesson.md).
- Recognize and factor quadratic, square, and cube structures with their required coefficient conditions. See [Lesson 3.4](lesson-4-square-structures/lesson.md).
- Use substitution and a deliberate sequence of methods to achieve complete factoring. See [Lesson 3.6](lesson-6-substitution-and-a-complete-strategy/lesson.md).
- Solve zero-form polynomial equations without losing roots and connect real zeros to graph intercepts. See [Lesson 3.7](lesson-7-factored-equations-and-zeros/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Topics include numerical and variable greatest common factors, repeated binomials, opposite factors, and four-term grouping. Trinomial work distinguishes monic sum-product reasoning from nonmonic middle-term splitting. Square and cube identities provide exact patterns, while substitution exposes quadratic structure in higher powers. A complete strategy integrates these methods and tests irreducibility in the requested coefficient set. Zero-product reasoning then separates distinct solutions, repeated roots, and the intercept points of an associated function.

## Lessons

- [3.1: Factoring and greatest common factors](lesson-1-factoring-and-greatest-common-factors/lesson.md) — Reverse distribution to interpret factoring, extract a greatest common monomial, and verify equality and completeness over the specified coefficients.
- [3.2: Common binomial factors and grouping](lesson-2-common-binomial-factors-and-grouping/lesson.md) — Treat repeated polynomial expressions as single factors and create matching factors by sign changes and carefully chosen term groupings.
- [3.3: Factoring quadratic trinomials](lesson-3-factoring-quadratic-trinomials/lesson.md) — Use sum-product conditions for monic trinomials and middle-term splitting for nonmonic trinomials, checking all resulting coefficients.
- [3.4: Square structures](lesson-4-square-structures/lesson.md) — Identify differences of squares and perfect-square trinomials, continue nested factoring, and distinguish valid patterns from superficially similar expressions.
- [3.5: Cube structures](lesson-5-cube-structures/lesson.md) — Factor sums and differences of cubes with correct linear and quadratic factors and justify each sign pattern through cancellation.
- [3.6: Substitution and a complete strategy](lesson-6-substitution-and-a-complete-strategy/lesson.md) — Replace repeated objects with a temporary variable and combine factoring methods until every factor is complete in the requested coefficient set.
- [3.7: Factored equations and zeros](lesson-7-factored-equations-and-zeros/lesson.md) — Apply zero product to equivalent zero-form equations, preserve roots threatened by cancellation, and interpret real solution inputs as graph intercepts.
