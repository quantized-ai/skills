# Unit 13: Systems of equations, inequalities, and graphical solutions

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

This unit develops simultaneous equations and inequalities as shared solution sets. Algebraic, matrix, graphical, and numerical methods lead to solutions that must be checked against the original constraints and any restrictions imposed by a context.

## Prerequisites

Function notation and graphs; linear equations; quadratic solution methods; domain restrictions. Numerical intersection tasks also use the rational, exponential, and logarithmic families developed earlier.

## Main learning objectives

These objectives summarize the learning objectives in the linked lessons.

- Preserve solution sets when choosing and applying substitution or elimination. Lessons [13.1](lesson-1-solution-sets-and-equivalent-systems/lesson.md), [13.2](lesson-2-three-variable-linear-systems/lesson.md).
- Formulate and solve three-variable systems by substitution, Gaussian elimination, and matrix technology. Lessons [13.2](lesson-2-three-variable-linear-systems/lesson.md), [13.3](lesson-3-matrix-representation-and-solution-classification/lesson.md).
- Classify unique, inconsistent, and infinite linear solution sets and describe free variables. Lessons [13.3](lesson-3-matrix-representation-and-solution-classification/lesson.md).
- Solve linear–quadratic systems and numerical intersections with justified counts, domains, and precision. Lessons [13.4](lesson-4-linear-quadratic-systems/lesson.md), [13.5](lesson-5-nonlinear-systems-and-numerical-intersections/lesson.md).
- Translate simultaneous inequalities into feasible regions and interpret valid choices. Lessons [13.6](lesson-6-linear-inequalities-and-feasible-regions/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Simultaneous solutions; reversible equation operations; ordered triples; Gaussian elimination; augmented matrices; contradictions and parameters; linear–quadratic systems; numerical refinement; quadratic–quadratic intersections; half-planes and feasible regions.

## Lessons

- [13.1: Solution sets and equivalent systems](lesson-1-solution-sets-and-equivalent-systems/lesson.md) — A system requires every equation to hold simultaneously.
- [13.2: Three-variable linear systems](lesson-2-three-variable-linear-systems/lesson.md) — Three-variable systems extend simultaneous constraints to ordered triples.
- [13.3: Matrix representation and solution classification](lesson-3-matrix-representation-and-solution-classification/lesson.md) — Augmented matrices encode coefficients and constants for systematic computation.
- [13.4: Linear–quadratic systems](lesson-4-linear-quadratic-systems/lesson.md) — Linear–quadratic systems combine constraints whose simultaneous solutions are graph intersections.
- [13.5: Nonlinear systems and numerical intersections](lesson-5-nonlinear-systems-and-numerical-intersections/lesson.md) — Equations can be studied by intersecting function graphs or finding zeros of their difference.
- [13.6: Linear inequalities and feasible regions](lesson-6-linear-inequalities-and-feasible-regions/lesson.md) — Linear inequalities define half-planes, and simultaneous inequalities define their common feasible region.
