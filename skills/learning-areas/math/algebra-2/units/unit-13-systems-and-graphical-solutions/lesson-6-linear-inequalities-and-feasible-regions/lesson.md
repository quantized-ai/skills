# Lesson 13.6: Linear inequalities and feasible regions

Linear inequalities define half-planes, and simultaneous inequalities define their common solution set. Contextual restrictions determine which points in that real-valued region are feasible choices.

## Learning objectives

- Graph linear inequalities using equivalent forms, correct boundary inclusion, and half-plane tests.
- Find and describe the complete intersection of linear-inequality solution sets.
- Formulate contextual constraints and verify feasible points, including integer restrictions.

## Learning outcome

Formulate, graph, and interpret systems of linear inequalities as complete sets of feasible choices.

## Proficiency criteria

- Translate constraints into compatible inequalities and state contextual sign, bound, and integer restrictions.
- Graph each boundary with correct strictness, preserve equivalence when rearranging, and test a point off the boundary to select its half-plane.
- Intersect all regions and classify boundary membership, unboundedness, lower-dimensional cases, or an empty solution set.
- Verify candidate points in every original inequality and apply contextual restrictions before declaring feasibility.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Boundary lines and half-planes** | For a nonconstant linear inequality in two variables, equality defines a boundary line. Strict inequalities exclude the boundary (dashed); inclusive inequalities retain it (solid). A test point off the boundary selects the correct half-plane, including for vertical lines. Multiplication or division by a negative number reverses the comparison. Solutions are all permitted ordered pairs, not only boundary points. | Graph the solution half-plane of a linear inequality in two variables using equivalent forms and correct boundary inclusion. Test boundary and nonboundary points in the original inequality. | Identify and draw the boundary, including vertical lines. Preserve equivalence when rearranging and reverse the comparison for negative multiplication or division. Select shading with a point off the boundary. Verify included and excluded points directly in the original inequality. |
| **Intersections of half-planes** | A system of linear inequalities requires every inequality simultaneously; its solution set is the intersection of their half-planes. A region may be bounded, unbounded, empty, or reduced to a line, ray, segment, or point. Interior points as well as permitted boundary points are solutions. Each edge or vertex is included only when it satisfies every strict or inclusive condition. | Solve systems of two or more linear inequalities graphically and describe bounded, unbounded, empty, or lower-dimensional solution sets. Identify and verify solution points, including interior, edge, and vertex cases. | Graph each component with correct strictness and retain only the common intersection. Recognize the resulting region type without assuming a two-dimensional interior. Check edge and vertex inclusion against every condition. Verify proposed solutions in all original inequalities rather than listing vertices alone. |
| **Formulating and interpreting feasible sets** | Contextual constraints compare quantities with compatible units. Maximum and minimum bounds give inclusive inequalities unless the stated condition is strict. Nonnegativity, additional bounds, and integrality may restrict the feasible set. When both variables count whole items, only integer-coordinate points in the real-valued graph region are admissible. Feasibility means satisfying all constraints; optimization is not required here. | Formulate at least two linear inequalities in two variables with defined quantities, compatible units, and contextual domain restrictions. Determine and interpret feasible choices using all original inequalities and contextual restrictions. | Translate every stated constraint with the correct comparison and units. Include all relevant nonnegativity, bounds, and integer restrictions. Use the graph and original inequalities to check every candidate. Interpret feasible coordinates in context and distinguish integer choices from the surrounding real-valued region. |
