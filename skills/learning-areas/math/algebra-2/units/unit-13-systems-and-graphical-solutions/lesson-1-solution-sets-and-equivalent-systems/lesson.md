# Lesson 13.1: Solution sets and equivalent systems

Linear systems require every equation to hold simultaneously. Equivalent systems preserve the complete solution set through reversible operations; substitution and elimination provide exact solutions that can be checked geometrically.

## Learning objectives

- Verify simultaneous solutions and classify two-variable linear systems.
- Justify reversible equation replacements and solve systems by both substitution and elimination.
- Choose efficient methods and distinguish exact solutions from graphical estimates.

## Learning outcome

Solve two-variable linear systems using both substitution and elimination, justify equivalence, and verify the complete solution set.

## Proficiency criteria

- Verify every original equation and distinguish inconsistent systems, uniquely solvable systems, and consistent systems with infinitely many solutions.
- Justify equation replacements by their inverses while retaining the other constraints.
- Demonstrate substitution and elimination across appropriate systems; explain method choice and recover both coordinates.
- Report exact solutions when available and distinguish algebraic verification from graphical estimation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simultaneous solutions and reversible elimination** | A solution satisfies every equation simultaneously. Two line equations have one solution when their lines intersect once, no solutions when parallel and distinct, and infinitely many when coincident. Replacing one equation by itself plus a scalar multiple of another preserves the solution set when the other equation is retained; subtracting the same multiple reverses the operation. Keeping only a sum can discard an independent constraint. | Verify a proposed solution and classify a linear system as uniquely solvable, inconsistent, or consistent with infinitely many solutions using equations or geometry. Justify reversible equation replacement and explain why discarded constraints can change the solution set. | Test all original equations. Distinguish intersecting, parallel distinct, and coincident lines. Recover the prior equation by the inverse replacement and retain the other equations. Identify lost constraints in a proposed nonequivalent system. |
| **Substitution and elimination in two variables** | Substitution replaces a variable with an equivalent expression from another equation. Elimination scales equations by nonzero constants and combines them to remove a variable while retaining enough information to recover both coordinates. Coefficient structure and isolated variables inform method choice. Graphical intersections estimate solutions; exact substitution into both original equations verifies them. | Solve two-variable linear systems using both substitution and elimination across appropriate systems. Choose an efficient method, verify exact solutions in both original equations, and distinguish them from graphical estimates. | Carry out each required method and retain the relations needed to recover both coordinates. Explain method choice from equation structure. Check exact coordinates in both original equations and label graphical estimates as approximate. |
