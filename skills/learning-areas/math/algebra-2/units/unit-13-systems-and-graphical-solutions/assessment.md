## Unit 13 cumulative assessment

1. **Three-variable elimination, 5 points.** Solve $x+y+z=9$, $2x-y+z=5$, $x+2y-z=4$ by Gaussian elimination. **Key:** $(2,3,4)$; two valid eliminating replacements (2), remaining elimination (1), recovery and checks (2). To obtain fresh retest evidence, replace the constants by $6,3,2$, whose solution is $(1,2,3)$.
2. **Matrix interpretation, 3 points.** Interpret rows $(1,0,1\mid3),(0,1,-2\mid4),(0,0,0\mid0)$. **Key:** $(3-t,4+2t,t)$; infinite set. Classification (1), parameterization (2).
3. **Linear–quadratic system, 4 points.** Solve $y=x+1$, $y=x^2-1$ and verify both ordered pairs. **Key:** $x^2-x-2=0$; $(-1,0),(2,3)$. Equation/factoring (2), pairs/checks (2).
4. **Graphical equation, 3 points.** Explain why intersections of $y=\log_2x$ and $y=2-x$ solve an equation; verify $x$ is restricted to $x>0$ and bracket the intersection between $1$ and $2$. **Key:** Equal outputs solve $\log_2x=2-x$; logarithm requires positive input; at $1$ difference is $-1$, at $2$ it is $1$, and continuity brackets a solution. One point each. Approximate value $1.46$ is optional.
5. **Feasible region, 5 points.** Formulate and graph nonnegative counts $x,y$ with at least four items and resource limit $2x+y\le8$. Give two feasible integer pairs and one real pair satisfying the inequalities that the context disallows. **Key:** $x+y\ge4$, $2x+y\le8$, $x,y\ge0$ integers; real region has vertices $(0,4),(0,8),(4,0)$. Examples $(0,4),(2,3)$; fractional $(1.5,3)$ is disallowed. Constraints (2), region (1), valid/invalid examples (2).

**Interpretation.** Total 20 points. Keep separate evidence that the learner can use substitution, Gaussian elimination, and matrix technology; proficiency in one method does not automatically establish the other two. Revisit Unit 1 interval and boundary conventions when inequality errors concern endpoints rather than algebra.
