# Lesson 13.4: Linear–quadratic systems

Linear–quadratic systems combine a line with a quadratic relation. Their real intersections must be found completely, verified in both equations, and interpreted within any contextual restrictions.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate simultaneous linear and quadratic constraints with meaningful units and domains.
- Solve simple linear–quadratic systems algebraically and graphically.
- Classify the actual reduced equation, verify the full real intersection set, and determine contextual feasibility.

## Learning outcome

Find and verify the real solution set of a simple linear–quadratic system and identify the solutions permitted by its context.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define variables and units, translate both relationships, and state contextual restrictions.
- Substitute using the linear equation and classify the resulting equation by its actual degree or constant condition.
- Find every real solution, recover corresponding coordinates, and reconcile the algebraic solution set with a graph.
- Verify both original equations and apply contextual sign, interval, integrality, and unit conditions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Formulating simultaneous linear and quadratic constraints** | A linear–quadratic system combines a line with a relation of total degree two; the quadratic relation need not define a function of one coordinate. Variables and equations represent simultaneous quantitative constraints with compatible units. Sign, interval, and integrality restrictions further limit contextual solutions. An algebraically valid intersection may be infeasible in context. | Formulate one linear and one quadratic equation from quantitative constraints, defining variables, units, and contextual restrictions. Distinguish a quadratic relation from a function graph and evaluate candidate solutions against both equations, contextual restrictions, and units. | Translate both relationships and specify relevant domains. Do not assume every quadratic relation is a function graph. Check each candidate against both equations and the context. Explain any rejection using the stated restrictions and interpret accepted coordinates with units. |
| **Algebraic solutions and intersection counts** | Isolate a variable in the nonconstant linear equation and substitute into the quadratic relation. Classify the actual reduced equation: a genuine quadratic has two, one, or no distinct real roots according to its discriminant; cancellation may instead leave a linear equation, contradiction, or identity. An identity means the entire line lies in the quadratic relation. For a nondegenerate conic, a repeated intersection root represents tangency. Complex roots are not real-plane intersections. | Solve a simple linear–quadratic system algebraically and graphically, recovering every corresponding coordinate. Classify the actual reduced equation, justify its real intersection set, and verify all solutions in both original equations. | Substitute with correct grouping and retain the actual resulting degree. Use the discriminant only for a genuine quadratic and handle linear, contradictory, and identity cases appropriately. Recover all real ordered pairs or describe the shared line. Verify both original equations and reconcile the result with the graph; exclude complex roots from real intersection counts. |
