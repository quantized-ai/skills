# Lesson 13.2: Three-variable linear systems

Three-variable linear systems express simultaneous constraints on ordered triples. Substitution and Gaussian elimination reduce those constraints without changing their solution set.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate three-variable systems with consistent quantities, units, and contextual restrictions.
- Solve systems by both substitution and Gaussian elimination and verify ordered triples.
- Recognize reduced equations that contradict one another or leave variables undetermined.

## Learning outcome

Formulate three-variable linear systems, demonstrate both algebraic methods, and verify solutions against every original constraint.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define variables, units, and contextual restrictions and translate every stated relationship into a compatible equation.
- Use substitution in both remaining equations and recover all three coordinates.
- Use reversible Gaussian elimination to echelon form and back-substitute when a unique solution exists.
- Recognize contradictions or free variables; verify claimed ordered triples in the original equations and context.

| **Formulation and substitution** | A three-variable linear system constrains ordered triples in a declared variable order. Equations use fixed real coefficients and compatible units; context may impose further restrictions. Isolating a variable and substituting into both remaining equations reduces a three-equation system to two unknowns. Recovering the eliminated variable and checking all original conditions completes a unique solution. | Formulate a three-variable linear system with defined variables, compatible units, and contextual restrictions. Solve a three-variable system by substitution, recover the ordered triple, and verify every original and contextual constraint. | Translate every stated relationship with consistent variable meanings and units. Substitute into both remaining equations, solve the reduced system, and recover the third coordinate. | **Gaussian elimination and back-substitution** | Gaussian elimination uses reversible operations: swapping equations, multiplying an entire equation by a nonzero scalar, or adding a multiple of another equation. Systematic elimination produces echelon form, including triangular form for a uniquely solvable three-variable system. Back-substitution recovers variables in reverse order. A contradiction means no solution; a consistent echelon system without a pivot for every variable leaves free variables. | Use reversible Gaussian elimination to transform a three-variable linear system into echelon form. Back-substitute and verify a unique solution, or identify contradictory equations and undetermined variables when uniqueness fails. | Apply operations to whole equations, including constants. Eliminate variables in a consistent order. For a unique solution, back-substitute to recover and check every coordinate in the original system. Identify contradictions or missing pivots rather than reporting an unjustified unique solution. |
