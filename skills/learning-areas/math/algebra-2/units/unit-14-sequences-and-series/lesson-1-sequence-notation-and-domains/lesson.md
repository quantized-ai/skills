# Lesson 14.1: Sequence notation and domains

Sequences are functions on integer indices. Explicit rules and recursive definitions specify term values, indexing conventions, and finite or infinite domains.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret sequence notation and specify the permitted integer indices.
- Evaluate explicit rules and represent terms as discrete points.
- Generate recursive terms and explain the role of initial conditions.

## Learning outcome

Describe a sequence as an indexed function and determine its terms from a complete explicit or recursive definition.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish an index from its term value and state the initial index and permitted domain.
- Evaluate explicit rules at allowed indices and represent sequence values as discrete points.
- Generate recursive terms in dependency order from sufficient initial values.
- Identify missing initial values or recurrence bounds and explain how changed initial values can change the sequence.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Terms and indices | A sequence assigns one value $a_n$ to each allowed integer index $n$; the subscript denotes an input, not multiplication. Specify the initial index and finite endpoints or infinite integer domain. Its graph consists of discrete points $(n,a_n)$; a continuous interpolation is a separate function. | Interpret an indexed term, evaluate an explicit sequence rule, and represent terms as discrete points. Specify a sequence’s integer domain and distinguish an index from its term value. | Distinguish indices and values in notation, tables, and graphs; state the initial index and permitted integer inputs, including finite endpoints; evaluate only allowed inputs and plot discrete points. |
| Recursive definitions and initial values | A recursive definition uses earlier term values to determine later ones. It includes sufficient initial values and the indices on which the recurrence applies. Terms are computed in dependency order; a shared update rule may yield different sequences from different initial values. | Generate terms from a recurrence with sufficient initial values and stated recurrence bounds. Identify incomplete recursive definitions and explain how initial values affect the resulting sequence. | State the necessary initial values and recurrence bounds; substitute earlier term values, not earlier indices, where required; generate terms in order; explain nonuniqueness when initial information is missing and compare the effect of different initial values. |
