# Unit 14: Sequences and series

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Sequences describe quantities indexed by integer steps, while series describe totals of those quantities. This unit develops arithmetic and geometric structure, translates between explicit and recursive rules, compares growth, and derives finite-sum formulas. The final lessons connect those formulas to precisely timed accumulations. Earlier sequence ideas are consolidated before the Algebra II finite-geometric-series requirement; learners are expected to preserve index conventions rather than rely on a formula detached from its starting point.

## Prerequisites

Function notation and discrete domains; signed arithmetic and exponent laws; solving linear equations; linear and exponential functions; percentages and unit interpretation. Learners should be able to evaluate a rule at an allowed input, distinguish an integer domain from a continuous interval, and explain repeated addition versus repeated multiplication. Indexing and exponent errors should be resolved before moving into finite sums.

## Main learning objectives

- Represent sequences with complete domains, initial conditions, and explicit or recursive rules. [Lesson 14.1](lesson-1-sequence-notation-and-domains/lesson.md), [Lesson 14.2](lesson-2-arithmetic-sequences/lesson.md), [Lesson 14.3](lesson-3-geometric-sequences/lesson.md).
- Construct and interpret arithmetic and geometric models from terms and change mechanisms. [Lesson 14.2](lesson-2-arithmetic-sequences/lesson.md), [Lesson 14.3](lesson-3-geometric-sequences/lesson.md).
- Compare discrete models using equal-step evidence and distinguish local comparisons from eventual growth. [Lesson 14.4](lesson-4-comparison-of-sequence-models/lesson.md).
- Derive finite-sum formulas and apply them with correct term counts and exceptional cases. [Lesson 14.5](lesson-5-finite-sums/lesson.md).
- Build finite accumulation models whose contributions, timing, and units match the situation. [Lesson 14.6](lesson-6-finite-geometric-models/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

The concepts progress from terms, indices, and recurrences to common differences and ratios; percent change and sign alternation; family selection and crossover behavior; sigma notation, arithmetic pairing, and geometric cancellation; and finite proportional totals and deposit timing. Connections between representations are assessed alongside numerical calculations.

## Lessons

- [14.1: Sequence notation and domains](lesson-1-sequence-notation-and-domains/lesson.md): Define indexed terms and discrete domains, then complete recursive rules with the initial conditions needed to generate them.
- [14.2: Arithmetic sequences](lesson-2-arithmetic-sequences/lesson.md): Build arithmetic formulas from constant differences and translate them between explicit and recursive forms without changing their indexing.
- [14.3: Geometric sequences](lesson-3-geometric-sequences/lesson.md): Use constant ratios to construct geometric sequences and interpret their signs, magnitudes, and percentage changes.
- [14.4: Comparison of sequence models](lesson-4-comparison-of-sequence-models/lesson.md): Select sequence families from evidence and compare their values, crossover positions, and long-term growth claims.
- [14.5: Finite sums](lesson-5-finite-sums/lesson.md): Expand finite sums and derive arithmetic and geometric total formulas with careful bounds and the unit-ratio exception.
- [14.6: Finite geometric models](lesson-6-finite-geometric-models/lesson.md): Model proportional totals and repeated deposits by assigning every contribution its correct place and growth period.

## Standards and scope

The Common Core mathematics standards supply the sequence-function and finite-geometric-sum objectives. Common Core Appendix A assigns finite geometric sums to Algebra II; Texas standards add explicit recursive exponential modeling and identify the prerequisite sequence work. Arithmetic sums support the finite-sum development. Infinite geometric series remain an optional extension and are excluded from required unit proficiency.
