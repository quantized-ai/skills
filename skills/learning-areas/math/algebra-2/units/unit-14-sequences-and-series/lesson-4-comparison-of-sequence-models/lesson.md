# Lesson 14.4: Comparison of sequence models

Sequence families are selected from change mechanisms and numerical evidence. Comparisons use shared integer inputs and distinguish a crossover in a finite interval from eventual dominance.

## Learning objectives

- Select and justify arithmetic or geometric models from contextual and numerical evidence.
- Account for index gaps, ambiguous model choices, and incomplete evidence.
- Compare sequence values and identify equality or strict exceedance within a stated interval.
- Distinguish finite observations from justified claims about eventual growth.

## Learning outcome

Justify a sequence-model choice and compare models without claiming more than the assumptions and evidence establish.

## Proficiency criteria

- Compare changes across equal index intervals or adjust for the number of steps.
- Use the stated change mechanism and all supplied terms to justify a family, acknowledging when both or neither family fits.
- Compare outputs at common inputs and identify equality separately from first strict exceedance in the stated search interval.
- Explain why finite evidence alone does not establish indefinite continuation or permanent dominance.
- State that a positive initial value and ratio greater than one ensure eventual exponential dominance over a fixed polynomial.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Differences, ratios, and model selection | Arithmetic models have constant additive change per step; geometric models have a constant multiplier. A gap of $h$ indices produces change $hd$ or multiplier $r^h$, respectively, so skipped indices can leave the sign of $r$ undetermined when $h$ is even. Quotient tests require nonzero denominators. Constant sequences can fit both families; other data may fit neither. A finite table does not uniquely determine an unrestricted continuation. | Select arithmetic or geometric models from tabular and verbal evidence, recognizing when both or neither family fits. Justify model selection using index-adjusted differences or multipliers, the change mechanism, and the limits of finite evidence. | Account for index gaps and zero denominators; distinguish a multi-step multiplier from a one-step ratio and state any unresolved sign ambiguity; check the proposed family against all supplied data and the stated mechanism; acknowledge nonunique continuation or family choice when evidence permits it. |
| Comparative growth | Tables and discrete graphs compare arithmetic, geometric, and polynomial sequence values at shared indices. Equality differs from strict exceedance; a first exceedance is relative to a specified search interval and direction of comparison. For $A>0$ and $r>1$, $Ar^n$ eventually exceeds any fixed polynomial in $n$ as integer $n$ increases without bound. Early comparisons alone do not establish permanent ordering. | Compare arithmetic, geometric, and polynomial sequences at shared integer inputs and locate equality and first strict exceedance within a stated interval. Distinguish finite crossover evidence from eventual dominance and state the conditions for exponential growth to exceed a fixed polynomial. | Produce consistent common-input comparisons; distinguish equality from strict exceedance and check earlier inputs before claiming a first exceedance in the search interval; apply the eventual-growth statement only under its stated conditions; avoid inferring a permanent ordering from a finite table. |
