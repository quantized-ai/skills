# Unit 14 assessment

[Unit overview](unit.md)

## Unit cumulative assessment

Complete independently; show index conventions and reasoning. Five tasks, 8 points each, 40 total.

1. An arithmetic sequence has $a_2=4,a_6=16$. Find $a_1$, an explicit rule, a recursive rule, and $a_{10}$. **Answer:** $d=3$, $a_1=1$, $a_n=1+3(n-1)$; $a_1=1,a_n=a_{n-1}+3$ for $n\ge2$; $a_{10}=28$. **Scoring:** 2 each for first term/difference, explicit rule, complete recurrence, final term. **Objectives:** U14-L01-C02-O1; U14-L02-C01-O1–O2; U14-L02-C02-O1.
2. A quantity starts at 64 at step 0 and loses 25% each step. Give both sequence forms, its first four terms, and why it is not arithmetic. **Answer:** $a_n=64(0.75)^n$; $a_0=64,a_n=0.75a_{n-1}$; $64,48,36,27$; differences $-16,-12,-9$ vary. **Scoring:** 2 each for forms, terms, percent interpretation, difference evidence. **Objectives:** U14-L03-C01-O1–O2; U14-L03-C02-O1–O2; U14-L04-C01-O2.
3. Derive the finite geometric-sum formula; evaluate $\sum_{k=0}^{5}3(2)^k$; explain the $r=1$ case. **Answer:** subtraction gives $(1-r)S_n=a_1(1-r^n)$; six-term sum $3(64-1)=189$; $r=1$ gives $na_1$. **Scoring:** 3 derivation, 3 evaluation and term count, 2 exceptional case. **Objectives:** U14-L05-C01-O1; U14-L05-C02-O1–O2.
4. At indices 0–5 compare $A_n=6+2n$ and $B_n=2^n$. Find first strict exceedance by $B$, and explain the limitation of a finite table. **Answer:** $A=6,8,10,12,14,16$; $B=1,2,4,8,16,32$; first exceedance at 4; a table alone does not prove all later behavior, though exponential growth eventually dominates. **Scoring:** 4 table, 2 crossover, 2 explanation. **Objectives:** U14-L04-C02-O1–O2.
5. Four end-of-period deposits of 100 earn 10% per period. Model and calculate the value just after the fourth deposit; compare with beginning-of-period deposits valued at period 4 end. **Answer:** $100(1+1.1+1.1^2+1.1^3)=464.10$; beginning-period total $510.51$. **Scoring:** 3 timeline/sum, 2 first total, 2 extra-period reasoning, 1 second total. **Objectives:** U14-L06-C01-O1–O2; U14-L06-C02-O1–O2.

**Optional extension boundary.** Infinite sums extend HSA-SSE.B.4 beyond its direct finite requirement. For $|r|<1$, the term $r^n$ approaches zero, so partial sums approach $a_1/(1-r)$; for nonzero $a_1$ and $|r|\ge1$, this finite limit does not exist. For example $6+3+1.5+\cdots$ approaches 12, whereas $1-1+1-1+\cdots$ has alternating partial sums and no ordinary sum. This extension is excluded from the required assessments above.
