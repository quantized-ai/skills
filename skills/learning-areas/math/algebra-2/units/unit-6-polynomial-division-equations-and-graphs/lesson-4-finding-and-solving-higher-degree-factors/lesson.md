# Lesson 6.4: Finding and solving higher-degree factors

Search for rational zeros systematically and use given or discovered factors to reduce cubic and quartic equations to linear and quadratic factors. Find every root of the reduced factors in the requested number system.

## Learning objectives

- Generate and test possible rational roots.
- Reduce suitable cubic and quartic equations to linear and quadratic factors through division or factorization.
- Solve all remaining linear and quadratic factors and verify the complete root set.

## Learning outcome

The student can use verified factors to solve cubic and quartic equations reducible by these methods and explain what a rational-root search does and does not establish.

## Proficiency criteria

- Apply rational-root candidate rules under their integer-coefficient assumptions and distinguish candidates from roots.
- Use a verified zero to obtain a valid linear factor and lower-degree quotient.
- Solve every remaining linear and quadratic factor, including quadratics without rational roots.
- Verify the reconstructed polynomial and the total root count with multiplicity.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Rational Root Theorem as a search method** | For a nonconstant integer-coefficient polynomial, a rational root $m/n$ in lowest terms with $n>0$ has $m$ dividing the constant coefficient and $n$ dividing the leading coefficient. Remove powers of $x$ first when the constant is zero. The signed reduced fractions are candidates to test, not guaranteed roots; exhausting them excludes rational roots only. | List all possible rational roots of an integer-coefficient polynomial in reduced form, first extracting zero roots when necessary. Test candidates and distinguish failure to find rational roots from absence of irrational or nonreal roots. | Check the integer-coefficient and lowest-terms assumptions. Extract zero roots before listing a finite set of signed candidates. Generate the complete reduced list and test candidates. Distinguish candidates from roots and state exactly what an exhausted search proves. |
| **Solving cubic and quartic equations by degree reduction** | A verified root supplies a linear factor for degree reduction by division. Continue reducing remaining factors until linear or quadratic methods apply, using quadratic structure when available. Quadratic factors may yield irrational or nonreal roots. Reconstruction verifies the factorization; a degree-$n$ polynomial has $n$ complex roots counted with multiplicity. Rational-root search alone is not a complete solver for arbitrary cubic or quartic equations. | Solve cubic and quartic equations reducible to linear and quadratic factors using known or discovered factors, division, and quadratic structure or methods. Verify the complete factorization and account for every root in the requested number system, including complex and repeated roots. | Use verified factors to reduce degree and solve every remaining linear or quadratic factor, even without rational roots. Distinguish real solutions from the full complex root set. Reconstruct the polynomial and reconcile its degree with the complex root count, including multiplicities. |
