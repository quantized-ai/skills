# Lesson 6.7: End behavior and sign intervals

Describe a polynomial’s distant tails from its leading term and its interval signs from real zeros. Combine these ideas without confusing eventual behavior with behavior near the middle of the graph.

## Learning objectives

- Determine both tails from degree and leading coefficient.
- Find positive and negative intervals from factor signs.
- Solve sign conditions with correct treatment of zeros.

## Learning outcome

The student can give a coherent account of global tails and interval signs from polynomial structure.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify degree parity and leading-coefficient sign in the available form.
- Justify eventual leading-term dominance and state both tails.
- Analyze every interval determined by the real zeros and track multiplicity-driven sign changes.
- Distinguish strict sign intervals from nonstrict solution sets, including any isolated zero solutions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **End behavior from degree and leading coefficient** | For a nonconstant real polynomial with leading term $a_nx^n$, $p(x)/(a_nx^n)\to1$ as $x\to\pm\infty$. Degree parity determines whether tails agree or oppose; the sign of $a_n$ determines the right tail. Lower-degree terms affect finite-input behavior but not these tails. Degree and leading coefficient can be read from expanded or factored form after accounting for cancellation. | Determine degree, leading coefficient, and both tails of a nonconstant real polynomial from expanded or factored form. Explain why lower-degree terms do not change polynomial end behavior. | Identify the actual degree and leading coefficient. State both tails consistently with parity and coefficient sign. Justify eventual leading-term dominance without claiming it describes all finite inputs. |
| **Positive and negative intervals of polynomials** | The real zeros of a nonzero real polynomial partition the line into intervals on which continuity and absence of zeros force a constant sign. Factor signs or test points determine each sign; odd multiplicity reverses it and even multiplicity preserves it. Strict inequalities exclude zeros. Nonstrict inequalities include every zero, including isolated solutions when neighboring intervals fail the sign condition. | Find and justify all positive and negative intervals of a factored real polynomial. Use multiplicity and test points to solve polynomial inequalities, distinguishing strict endpoints, included zeros, and isolated zero solutions. | Order all real zeros and determine each interval’s sign. Justify sign reversal or preservation through multiplicity. Express strict solution sets without zeros and nonstrict sets with every zero, including isolated qualifying zeros. |
