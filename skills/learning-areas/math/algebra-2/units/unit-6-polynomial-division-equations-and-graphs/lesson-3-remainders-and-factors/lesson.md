# Lesson 6.3: Remainders and factors

Derive the remainder and factor theorems from the division identity. Use evaluation to test a factor or impose a condition on an unknown coefficient.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and apply the Remainder Theorem.
- Justify both directions of the Factor Theorem.
- Determine coefficients that make a linear factor valid.

## Learning outcome

The student can move logically among evaluation, remainder, and factor claims.

## Proficiency criteria

- Substitute the correctly signed divisor root into the division identity.
- Explain why the resulting value is the constant remainder for a linear divisor.
- Justify both factor-to-zero and zero-to-factor implications.
- Solve and verify a coefficient condition without confusing remainder information with a complete quotient.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Remainder Theorem** | Division by $x-c$ gives $p(x)=(x-c)q(x)+r$ with constant $r$; substitution yields $r=p(c)$ for real or complex $c$. The theorem determines the remainder, not the quotient. A divisor $x+c$ uses input $-c$; a quadratic remainder is not determined by a single evaluation. | Prove the Remainder Theorem from the polynomial division identity and state its linear-divisor scope. Calculate a remainder under division by a monic linear polynomial without full division, distinguishing remainder information from quotient information. | Derive the constant remainder by substituting the divisor root into the division identity. Evaluate at the correct signed input. State what the result establishes and avoid treating it as a quotient or a complete quadratic-divisor remainder. |
| **The Factor Theorem** | The Factor Theorem states $p(c)=0$ if and only if $x-c$ is a factor. A factor implies a zero by substitution; a zero implies a factor because the division remainder is zero. An unknown coefficient is constrained by the equation $p(c)=0$. | Use and justify both directions of the Factor Theorem. Test proposed linear factors and solve and verify coefficient conditions that make them factors. | Justify factor-to-zero and zero-to-factor implications. Test a proposed factor at the correct input and interpret nonzero remainders. Form and solve the coefficient condition, then verify the factor claim. |
