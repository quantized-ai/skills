# Unit 6: Polynomial division, equations, and graphs

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Polynomial division links arithmetic to factors, zeros, and graphs. This unit develops the division identity and its domain distinction, proves the remainder and factor connections, and uses verified roots to reduce higher-degree equations. Multiplicity and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra organize complete complex root counts, while conjugate pairing supports real-coefficient construction. Graph work combines local signs with distant tail behavior. The unit finishes with binomial coefficients and expansion, returning to multiplication as the source of a general identity rather than treating the theorem as an isolated formula.

## Prerequisites

Complete Units 1–5, especially standard-form coefficient lists, polynomial arithmetic, factorization, complex arithmetic, quadratic methods, transformations, and graph attributes. Students should be able to verify a product and distinguish real zeros from nonreal complex roots. Rational-expression restrictions are introduced as needed for the division identity; fuller rational-expression and rational-function analysis follows in subsequent units.

## Main learning objectives

- Divide cubic and quartic polynomials by linear and quadratic divisors and verify quotient-remainder identities. See [Lesson 6.2](lesson-2-quadratic-divisors-and-synthetic-division/lesson.md).
- Use evaluation, factor theorems, and justified rational-root searches to solve higher-degree equations. See [Lesson 6.4](lesson-4-finding-and-solving-higher-degree-factors/lesson.md).
- Account for multiplicities and conjugate roots and construct least-degree polynomials from sufficient conditions. See [Lesson 6.6](lesson-6-conjugate-roots-and-polynomial-construction/lesson.md).
- Synthesize end behavior, signs, intercepts, multiplicities, and cubic transformations into coherent graphs. See [Lesson 6.8](lesson-8-graph-synthesis-and-cubic-transformations/lesson.md).
- Generate binomial coefficients and apply the Binomial Theorem to expansions and selected coefficients. See [Lesson 6.9](lesson-9-binomial-coefficients-and-expansion/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

The topic sequence includes long and synthetic division, remainder degree, quotient-domain restrictions, theorem proofs, rational candidates, and degree reduction. Root analysis distinguishes distinct counts from multiplicity, demonstrates the real-coefficient quadratic case of the Fundamental Theorem, and explains the real-coefficient assumption behind conjugate pairing. Graphs use leading-term dominance, interval signs, touching and crossing, and coordinate transformations. Pascal’s triangle and the general binomial term provide complementary descriptions of expansion coefficients.

## Lessons

- [6.1: The division algorithm and linear long division](lesson-1-the-division-algorithm-and-linear-long-division/lesson.md) — Establish the division identity and perform linear long division with aligned powers, complete subtraction, a valid remainder, and verification.
- [6.2: Quadratic divisors and synthetic division](lesson-2-quadratic-divisors-and-synthetic-division/lesson.md) — Extend long division to quadratic divisors and use ordinary synthetic division only when the divisor satisfies its required form.
- [6.3: Remainders and factors](lesson-3-remainders-and-factors/lesson.md) — Derive the Remainder and Factor Theorems and use evaluation to test factors or determine coefficients that make a factor valid.
- [6.4: Finding and solving higher-degree factors](lesson-4-finding-and-solving-higher-degree-factors/lesson.md) — List rational candidates under the correct assumptions, verify roots, reduce degree, and solve every remaining factor over the requested numbers.
- [6.5: Multiplicity and the Fundamental Theorem](lesson-5-multiplicity-and-the-fundamental-theorem/lesson.md) — Connect factor exponents to local crossing behavior and precise complex-root counts while demonstrating the real-coefficient quadratic theorem case.
- [6.6: Conjugate roots and polynomial construction](lesson-6-conjugate-roots-and-polynomial-construction/lesson.md) — Justify conjugate partners and construct least-degree real polynomials with specified multiplicities and enough independent information to determine scale.
- [6.7: End behavior and sign intervals](lesson-7-end-behavior-and-sign-intervals/lesson.md) — Determine both polynomial tails and every real sign interval, distinguishing strict sign sets from nonstrict inequalities and isolated zero solutions.
- [6.8: Graph synthesis and cubic transformations](lesson-8-graph-synthesis-and-cubic-transformations/lesson.md) — Draw structurally consistent polynomial sketches and map transformed cubic coordinates without inventing exact turning points or treating the central point as an extremum.
- [6.9: Binomial coefficients and expansion](lesson-9-binomial-coefficients-and-expansion/lesson.md) — Explain Pascal’s triangle through multiplication and use the general binomial term for signed expansions and targeted coefficient calculations.
