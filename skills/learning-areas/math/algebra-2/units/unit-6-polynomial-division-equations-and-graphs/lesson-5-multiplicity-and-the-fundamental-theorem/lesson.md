# Lesson 6.5: Multiplicity and the Fundamental Theorem

Interpret repeated factors as root multiplicities and connect parity to local graph behavior. State the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra and demonstrate its real-coefficient quadratic case.

## Learning objectives

- Determine root multiplicities and local crossing behavior.
- Apply the complex root count with multiplicity.
- Demonstrate the theorem for real-coefficient quadratics.

## Learning outcome

The student can reconcile factor multiplicities, graph behavior, and the polynomial’s complete complex root count.

## Proficiency criteria

- Read multiplicity only after isolating a factor whose remaining multiplier is nonzero at the root.
- Distinguish distinct, real, and multiplicity-counted root totals.
- Explain touching or crossing through the sign behavior of even or odd powers.
- State the general theorem precisely and limit the demonstrated proof to the real-coefficient quadratic case.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Multiplicity of zeros** | A zero $r$ has multiplicity $m$ when $p(x)=(x-r)^m q(x)$ and $q(r)\ne0$. For real polynomials near a real zero, $q$ keeps its sign; odd $m$ reverses the sign and crosses the axis, while even $m$ preserves the sign and touches without crossing. Distinct-root counts differ from counts with multiplicity. | Determine root multiplicities from a factorization and distinguish distinct-root counts from counts with multiplicity. Justify crossing or touching at a real zero from odd or even multiplicity and local signs. | Check that the remaining factor is nonzero at the proposed root before assigning multiplicity. Report distinct and multiplicity-counted totals correctly. Explain crossing or touching through local sign reversal or preservation. |
| **The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra** | Every nonconstant complex-coefficient polynomial has a complex root. A degree-$n$ polynomial consequently factors as $a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-r_j)$ and has exactly $n$ complex roots counting multiplicity, not necessarily distinct or real. For real quadratics with nonzero leading coefficient, the quadratic formula gives two real roots, one double real root, or two nonreal roots according as the discriminant is positive, zero, or negative. | State and apply the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, connecting complex linear factorization to the degree count with multiplicity. Demonstrate the theorem for every real-coefficient quadratic using all three discriminant cases of the quadratic formula. | State complex-root existence and the complete degree count with multiplicity. Distinguish counted, distinct, and real roots. Demonstrate the real-quadratic case for each discriminant sign without presenting it as a proof for all degrees. |
