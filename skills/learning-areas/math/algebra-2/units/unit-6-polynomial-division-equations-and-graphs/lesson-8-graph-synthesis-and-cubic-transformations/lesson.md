# Lesson 6.8: Graph synthesis and cubic transformations

Synthesize polynomial graph features from factors and analyze transformed cubic functions. Use structural evidence to draw sketches without inventing exact turning-point information.

## Learning objectives

- Sketch polynomial graphs from verified algebraic features.
- Check graph consistency with zeros, multiplicities, signs, and tails.
- Map transformations of the cubic parent.

## Learning outcome

The student can produce a structurally justified polynomial sketch and a consistent transformed cubic graph.

## Proficiency criteria

- Combine all available intercept, multiplicity, sign, and end-behavior evidence.
- Draw a smooth graph that respects the root count and turning-point bound.
- Apply inside and outside cubic transformations through coordinate mappings with correct sign effects.
- Distinguish the cubic central point from a vertex or extremum and separate known attributes from unsupported coordinates.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sketching a polynomial graph from factors** | A polynomial sketch combines real zeros and multiplicities, $p(0)$, interval signs, and both tails. The curve is smooth and continuous, with no extra zeros. A degree-$n$ nonconstant polynomial has at most $n-1$ turning points; this bound neither determines their number nor locates them. A structural sketch does not establish exact turning-point coordinates. | Construct a rough polynomial graph consistent with factors, intercepts, multiplicities, signs, tails, and the turning-point bound. Check proposed graphs for contradictions and distinguish determined structural features from unestablished turning-point coordinates. | Reconcile zeros, multiplicities, vertical intercept, signs, and tails. Draw a smooth curve with correct crossings and touches and no extra zeros. Respect the turning-point bound without treating it as an exact count or claiming unsupported coordinates. |
| **Transformations of the cubic parent** | For $f(x)=x^3$ and $g(x)=a f(b(x-h))+k$, where $a,b\ne0$, parent points $(u,v)$ map to $(h+u/b,k+av)$. The central inflection point is $(h,k)$, not a vertex or extremum. Horizontal scale is $1/\lvert b\rvert$ and vertical scale is $\lvert a\rvert$; negative parameters reflect the corresponding parent coordinates. Since $f(bx)=b^3x^3$, inside and outside scalings may yield equivalent cubic formulas. | Analyze and graph transformed cubic functions through coordinate mappings, identifying the central inflection point correctly. Compare inside and outside scaling, including negative parameters and algebraically equivalent cubic formulas. | Apply the inside and outside coordinate rules with correct shifts and signs. Distinguish horizontal and vertical scale factors and account for negative-parameter reflections. Identify the central point without calling it an extremum. Determine whether two cubic transformation formulas are equivalent. |
