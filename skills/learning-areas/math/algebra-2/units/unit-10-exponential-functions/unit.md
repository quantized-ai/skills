# Unit 10: Exponential functions

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Exponential functions describe repeated multiplicative change. This unit connects formulas, equally spaced tables, percent-change descriptions, recursive representations, and graphs. Students construct models with an explicit input scale, distinguish growth factors from additive rates, and solve equations by common bases or numerical approximation. General logarithmic solution methods follow in Unit 11, while the later sequences unit develops the recursive viewpoint more fully.

## Prerequisites

Function notation, graph transformations, positive-base rational exponents, percentages, linear equations, units, and table interpretation. Students should be able to evaluate a power at zero or a negative input and convert between a decimal fraction and a percentage.

## Main learning objectives

- Recognize exponential structure and identify equal-interval multiplicative factors. See [Lesson 10.1](lesson-1-exponential-structure/lesson.md).
- Construct and interpret growth, decay, and recursive models from descriptions or observations. See [Lesson 10.2](lesson-2-growth-decay-and-construction/lesson.md).
- Graph exponential parents and transformations with consistent key features. See [Lesson 10.3](lesson-3-exponential-graphs/lesson.md).
- Convert time scales and distinguish continuous rates from effective percentage changes. See [Lesson 10.4](lesson-4-time-units-and-the-base-e/lesson.md).
- Solve and compare exponential relationships using algebra, graphs, tables, and average rates. See [Lesson 10.5](lesson-5-exponential-equations/lesson.md), [Lesson 10.6](lesson-6-rates-of-change-and-comparisons/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Topics progress from variable-exponent structure and ratios to parameter construction, growth and decay, graph transformations, and time-scale interpretation. The natural base introduces continuous-rate notation without calculus. Common-base equations connect one-to-one functions to exact solving; numerical intersections prepare for logarithms. Final comparisons separate constant ratios from changing additive slopes and distinguish observed local behavior from eventual exponential dominance.

## Lessons

- [10.1: Exponential structure](lesson-1-exponential-structure/lesson.md) — Recognize exponential formulas, evaluate them, and interpret ratios with the correct input spacing.
- [10.2: Growth, decay, and construction](lesson-2-growth-decay-and-construction/lesson.md) — Build percent-change and two-point models and express their integer samples recursively.
- [10.3: Exponential graphs](lesson-3-exponential-graphs/lesson.md) — Sketch named parent bases and transformations using points, range, intercepts, and end behavior.
- [10.4: Time units and the base e](lesson-4-time-units-and-the-base-e/lesson.md) — Convert factors across time periods and interpret doubling, halving, and continuous-rate models.
- [10.5: Exponential equations](lesson-5-exponential-equations/lesson.md) — Use common bases for exact equations and graph or table brackets for numerical solutions.
- [10.6: Rates of change and comparisons](lesson-6-rates-of-change-and-comparisons/lesson.md) — Compute average rates and compare exponential with polynomial behavior over changing scales.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards provide requirements for exponential construction, parameter interpretation, graphing, rate comparisons, and growth comparisons. Texas standards directly include exponential models, transformations, and equations; Common Core Appendix A connects them within Algebra II function modeling. Each concept retains the applicable objective-level mappings. A model’s time unit and intended domain must accompany its interpretation, and numerical answers retain the distinction between exact and approximate evidence.
