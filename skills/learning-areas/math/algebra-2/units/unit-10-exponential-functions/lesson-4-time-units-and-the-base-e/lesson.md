# Lesson 10.4: Time units and the base e

An exponential factor depends on its input time scale. Equivalent forms connect per-period change, doubling or halving periods, and continuous-rate notation using the natural base.

## Learning objectives

- Rewrite and construct exponential models for different time units and specified factor periods.
- Interpret continuous-rate notation and convert it to an effective per-period change.

## Learning outcome

Compare exponential rates on a common time scale while distinguishing factor, period, and continuous-rate parameter.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use compatible units for time and period and preserve model values when changing units.
- Convert a period-specific factor to a per-unit factor using exponent laws.
- Construct and interpret doubling and halving models.
- Convert a continuous-rate parameter to a factor and effective percentage for the stated period.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equivalent forms and time scales** | In $A(t)=A_0b^{t/T}$, assume $A_0>0$, $b>0$, and $T>0$. The factor $b$ applies over $T$ time units, and $b^{1/T}$ is the factor per unit; $t$ and $T$ must use the same unit. Doubling every $T$ units gives $A_0 2^{t/T}$; halving every $H>0$ units gives $A_0(1/2)^{t/H}$. Changing time units changes the numerical time and factor together without changing the modeled amount. | Rewrite an exponential model to use a different time unit while preserving its values. Interpret factors over specified periods and construct doubling or halving models from those periods. | Identify the interval associated with each multiplier. Rewrite factors and exponents consistently with the new time unit. Construct doubling and halving formulas with matching time and period units, and distinguish period-specific change from per-unit change. |
| **The constant e and continuous-rate notation** | The compounding values $(1+1/n)^n$ approach $e\approx2.71828$ as positive integers $n$ increase. In $A(t)=A_0e^{kt}$ with $A_0>0$, $k$ is a continuous-rate parameter measured per time unit; $k>0$ gives growth, $k<0$ decay, and $k=0$ a constant. Over $\Delta t>0$ time units, the factor is $e^{k\Delta t}$ and the effective percent change is $100(e^{k\Delta t}-1)\%$. The parameter $k$ is not the effective per-unit percentage. | Interpret the initial amount and continuous-rate parameter, including its sign and time unit, in a model $A_0e^{kt}$. Convert a continuous-rate model to an equivalent per-period factor and effective percent change, distinguishing the two rates. | Identify the initial amount, continuous-rate parameter, time unit, and growth or decay direction. Compute the factor for the specified period. Calculate the effective percentage and distinguish it from the continuous-rate parameter. |
