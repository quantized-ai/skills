# Lesson 10.6: Rates of change and comparisons

Constant exponential ratios do not imply constant additive rates. Average rates and comparisons at different scales distinguish change over a specified interval from eventual growth behavior.

## Learning objectives

- Calculate and interpret exponential average rates of change and distinguish them from percentage change.
- Compare exponential and polynomial growth across table or graph scales without overstating finite evidence.

## Learning outcome

Distinguish multiplicative change, average additive rate, and eventual growth when comparing functions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Calculate net output change divided by input change over the specified interval.
- Interpret the rate’s sign and output-per-input units.
- Explain different additive changes arising from the same factor applied to different starting amounts.
- Compare functions across scales and separate observed comparisons from a claim about all sufficiently large inputs.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Average rates of change for exponentials** | On $[p,q]$ with $p<q$, average rate of change is $[f(q)-f(p)]/(q-p)$ in output units per input unit. This additive rate differs from the output ratio and percent change. For $f(x)=ab^x$ with $a>0$, $b>0$, and $b\ne1$, equal-length intervals have the same ratio $b^{q-p}$ but different starting amounts. If $b>1$, later equal-length intervals have larger increases; if $0<b<1$, they have negative changes of decreasing magnitude. | Calculate and interpret average rates of change over specified intervals, including units and signs. Explain why constant percentage growth or decay does not imply constant additive change. | Use net change divided by interval length rather than an output ratio. Calculate and interpret rates with correct signs and units. Relate different additive changes to different starting amounts despite a fixed multiplicative factor. |
| **Comparing exponential and polynomial growth** | An exponential $ab^x$ with $a>0$ and $b>1$ eventually exceeds every fixed polynomial as $x$ increases: for each such polynomial there is a threshold beyond which the inequality holds for all inputs. Earlier orderings and crossovers depend on the functions. Compare common inputs and multiple viewing scales. Finite tables and displayed graph windows provide evidence over the inspected inputs; they do not prove dominance for every later real input. A general proof of eventual dominance is outside this lesson. | Use tables or graphs at multiple scales to compare exponential and polynomial functions. Distinguish observed interval or sample behavior from eventual growth and state the conditions for exponential dominance. | Compare outputs at common inputs accurately. Extend the table or change graph scale to investigate hidden crossovers. State eventual dominance with its coefficient and base conditions, and distinguish the general claim from what finite observations establish. |
