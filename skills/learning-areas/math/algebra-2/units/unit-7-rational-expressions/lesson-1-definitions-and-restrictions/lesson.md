# Lesson 7.1: Definitions and restrictions

Rational expressions are quotients of polynomials with explicit input restrictions. Classification, evaluation, and cancellation depend on the original domain, even when a reduced formula has a larger natural domain.

## Learning objectives

- Classify rational expressions and evaluate them only at allowed real inputs.
- Simplify by canceling common nonzero factors; compare original and reduced formulas with their domains.

## Learning outcome

The student can classify, evaluate, and simplify rational expressions while distinguishing equivalent formulas from functions with different domains.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify polynomial numerator and denominator roles, including after rewriting, and find every original denominator zero.
- Distinguish a zero output from an undefined expression.
- Cancel common nonzero factors and retain every original exclusion.
- Determine where original and reduced formulas agree and whether they define the same function on their stated domains.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rational expressions and allowed inputs** | A rational expression is $p(x)/q(x)$ with polynomials $p,q$ and $q$ not identically zero; its real domain excludes all zeros of $q$. Polynomials are rational expressions with denominator $1$. Classification may require rewriting variable powers. A zero numerator gives zero only when the denominator is nonzero. | Classify an expression as rational, rewriting when needed, and identify its polynomial numerator and denominator. Determine all excluded real inputs and evaluate a rational expression only at permitted inputs. | Identify polynomial parts and distinguish a nonzero denominator polynomial from its zero values; find every denominator zero; evaluate on the allowed domain and distinguish zero from undefined. |
| **Original domains and equivalent formulas** | Cancellation divides numerator and denominator by a common factor only where that factor is nonzero. A reduced formula agrees with the original on the original domain; all original exclusions remain. Two formulas define the same function only when their domains match and their values agree throughout that domain. | Simplify by canceling common nonzero factors and state the domain on which original and reduced formulas agree. Distinguish equivalent formulas on a restricted domain from functions with different natural domains. | Justify each cancellation by its nonzero-factor condition; state the reduced formula with every original exclusion; compare both domains and values before asserting equality of functions. |
