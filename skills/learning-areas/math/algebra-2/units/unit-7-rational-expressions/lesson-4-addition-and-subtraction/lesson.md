# Lesson 7.4: Addition and subtraction

[Unit 7: Rational expressions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Addition and subtraction require a common denominator before numerators can be combined. Factored denominators reveal the least common denominator and prevent unnecessary repeated factors.

## Learning objectives

- Add and subtract rational expressions with common or unlike denominators.
- Construct an LCD and preserve restrictions while reducing the result.

## Learning outcome

The student can explain each equivalent-fraction step in a rational sum or difference and defend the resulting domain.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Keep a common denominator while combining entire signed numerators.
- Build an LCD from each distinct factor at its greatest required multiplicity.
- Multiply each numerator and denominator by the same missing factors.
- Factor and reduce the combined numerator while retaining exclusions from all original terms.

## Concepts

### Concept U07-L04-C01: Common denominators

**Content**

- Combining entire numerators
- Distribution of subtraction

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L04-C01-O1:** Add or subtract expressions having the same denominator.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L04-C01-O2:** Simplify the resulting numerator without losing original restrictions.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Combine numerators only after confirming the same denominator.
- Distribute subtraction over every term of the following numerator.
- Reduce the result by factors and keep the original common-denominator restrictions.

**Explanation**

Keep the common denominator and combine the numerators. A subtraction sign applies to every term in the following numerator, so use parentheses. Factor the new numerator before deciding whether reduction is possible.

**Worked examples**

$\frac{2x+3}{x-1}-\frac{x+4}{x-1}=\frac{2x+3-(x+4)}{x-1}=\frac{x-1}{x-1}=1$, $x\ne1$. The second numerator contributes $-x-4$, not $-x+4$.

**Exercises**

1. Add $3/(x+2)+x/(x+2)$. **Answer:** $(x+3)/(x+2)$, $x\ne-2$.
2. Subtract $(3x-1)/(x+1)-(x-3)/(x+1)$. **Answer:** 2, $x\ne-1$.

**Assessment**

Simplify $(4x+1)/(x-2)-(3x+3)/(x-2)$, showing the numerator calculation and restriction.

- **U07-L04-C01-O1 — 2 points:** Numerator $4x+1-(3x+3)=x-2$; denominator remains $x-2$ initially.
- **U07-L04-C01-O2 — 2 points:** The result is 1 for $x\ne2$, with the restriction retained after cancellation.

### Concept U07-L04-C02: Least common denominators

**Content**

- Complete factorization and maximum multiplicity
- Equivalent fractions with unlike denominators

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L04-C02-O1:** Construct a least common denominator from factored polynomial denominators.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L04-C02-O2:** Add or subtract unlike-denominator rational expressions with all restrictions.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Factor the denominators and justify the multiplicity of every factor included in the LCD.
- Construct equivalent fractions using the missing factors in both numerator and denominator.
- Combine, simplify, and state the domain without treating denominator addition as a valid operation.

**Explanation**

The LCD contains each distinct irreducible factor to its highest exponent appearing in a denominator; include the numerical least common multiple when using integer coefficients. Multiply each numerator and denominator by the missing factor. Never add denominators.

**Worked examples**

$1/[x(x-1)]+2/(x-1)^2$ has LCD $x(x-1)^2$, not $x(x-1)^3$. Its numerator is $(x-1)+2x=3x-1$, so the answer is $(3x-1)/[x(x-1)^2]$, $x\ne0,1$. For $1/(x-1)-1/(x+1)$, the numerator becomes $(x+1)-(x-1)=2$, giving $2/(x^2-1)$, $x\ne\pm1$.

**Exercises**

1. Add $1/x+1/(x+2)$. **Answer:** $2(x+1)/[x(x+2)]$, $x\ne0,-2$.
2. Subtract $2/(x-1)-1/(x^2-1)$. **Answer:** $(2x+1)/(x^2-1)$, $x\ne\pm1$.

**Assessment**

Find the LCD and simplify $3/[x(x+1)]+2/(x+1)^2$.

- **U07-L04-C02-O1 — 2 points:** LCD $x(x+1)^2$: use the greatest exponent of each factor.
- **U07-L04-C02-O2 — 2 points:** Result $(5x+3)/[x(x+1)^2]$, $x\ne0,-1$, from numerator $3(x+1)+2x$.
