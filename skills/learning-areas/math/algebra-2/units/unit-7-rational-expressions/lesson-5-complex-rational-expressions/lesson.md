# Lesson 7.5: Complex rational expressions

[Unit 7: Rational expressions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Complex rational expressions contain division at more than one level. Two methods—simplifying the top and bottom separately or clearing inner denominators—must preserve the same original restrictions.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify nested rational expressions by division or an inner LCD.
- Account for inner denominators and a nonzero overall denominator.

## Learning outcome

The student can choose either complex-fraction method and explain why it produces an equivalent expression only on the original domain.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Identify the main fraction bar and distinguish inner denominators from the entire denominator expression.
- Simplify top and bottom separately or distribute an inner LCD to every term at both levels.
- Require each inner denominator and the entire original divisor to be nonzero.
- Explain why simplification does not restore an excluded input or permit multiplying only one level.

## Concepts

### Concept U07-L05-C01: Complex fractions by division

**Content**

- Nested fraction bars
- Top and bottom simplified separately

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L05-C01-O1:** Simplify a complex rational expression using division.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L05-C01-O2:** Identify restrictions from inner denominators and the entire divisor.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.B.5 (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(I) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Translate the main fraction bar into division of the complete numerator by the complete denominator.
- Simplify each level before reciprocal multiplication without losing its exclusions.
- Determine where the entire denominator expression is zero in addition to inner-denominator restrictions.

**Explanation**

A complex fraction is a quotient with fractions in its numerator or denominator. The longest fraction bar determines the main division. Simplify the top and bottom first, then multiply by the reciprocal, retaining restrictions at every level.

**Worked examples**

$\frac{1/x+1}{1/x-1}=\frac{(1+x)/x}{(1-x)/x}=\frac{x+1}{1-x}$. Inner denominators require $x\ne0$; the bottom expression must not be zero, so $x\ne1$. Neither exclusion may be discarded.

**Exercises**

1. Simplify $(2/x)/(3/x^2)$. **Answer:** $2x/3$, $x\ne0$.
2. Simplify $(1+1/x)/(1-1/x)$. **Answer:** $(x+1)/(x-1)$, $x\ne0,1$.

**Assessment**

Simplify $(1/x+2)/(1/x+3)$ by writing an explicit division; state restrictions.

- **U07-L05-C01-O1 — 2 points:** $[(1+2x)/x]\div[(1+3x)/x]=(1+2x)/(1+3x)$.
- **U07-L05-C01-O2 — 2 points:** $x\ne0,-1/3$, from inner denominators and the original bottom expression.

### Concept U07-L05-C02: Complex fractions by clearing inner denominators

**Content**

- Multiplication of top and bottom by an LCD
- Method comparison

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L05-C02-O1:** Clear inner denominators by multiplying the entire numerator and denominator by the same expression.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L05-C02-O2:** Explain why a complex-fraction simplification preserves values only on its original domain.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-SSE.A.2 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (supporting knowledge).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Choose a common multiple that clears every inner denominator.
- Multiply and distribute across both the entire numerator and the entire denominator.
- Justify equivalence using a nonzero multiplier and retain the full original domain.

**Explanation**

Multiplying the top and bottom by an LCD multiplies the quotient by 1 wherever that LCD is nonzero. Distribute it to every term on both levels. This is simplification of an expression, so do not multiply only one side as if solving an equation.

**Worked examples**

For $(1/x+1/y)/(1/x-1/y)$, multiply numerator and denominator by $xy$: $(y+x)/(y-x)$. Initially $x,y\ne0$, and $1/x-1/y\ne0$ requires $y\ne x$. Clearing inner denominators does not make zero values of $x$ or $y$ permissible.

**Exercises**

1. Simplify $(1+2/x)/(3-1/x)$. **Answer:** $(x+2)/(3x-1)$, $x\ne0,1/3$.
2. Explain the error in changing $(1+1/x)/(2+1/x)$ to $(x+1)/(2+1/x)$. **Answer:** only the numerator was multiplied by $x$; both levels must be multiplied.

**Assessment**

Use the inner LCD to simplify $(2+1/x)/(1-2/x)$; explain the domain of validity.

- **U07-L05-C02-O1 — 2 points:** Multiply both levels by $x$ to obtain $(2x+1)/(x-2)$.
- **U07-L05-C02-O2 — 2 points:** Equality holds only for $x\ne0,2$: inner denominator and whole denominator restrictions respectively.
