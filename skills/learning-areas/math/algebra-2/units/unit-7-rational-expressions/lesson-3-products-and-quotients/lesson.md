# Lesson 7.3: Products and quotients

[Unit 7: Rational expressions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Multiplication and division extend numerical fraction rules to polynomial quotients. Division adds the requirement that the divisor itself must be nonzero at the chosen input.

## Learning objectives

- Multiply and divide rational expressions in factored form.
- Determine the restrictions inherited from factors and from a divisor.

## Learning outcome

The student can distinguish and apply the domain conditions for a rational product and a rational quotient.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Organize products so that all canceled quantities are visible as factors.
- Convert division into reciprocal multiplication without changing the intended operand order.
- Combine every original denominator restriction and, for division, every zero of the divisor.
- Report a simplified expression whose stated domain matches the original operation.

## Concepts

### Concept U07-L03-C01: Multiplication of rational expressions

**Content**

- Products of numerators and denominators
- Cross-cancellation of factors

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L03-C01-O1:** Multiply rational expressions and reduce the resulting factors.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L03-C01-O2:** Give the domain inherited from both factors.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.B.5 (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(I) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Multiply numerator factors and denominator factors with a justified cancellation order.
- Explain cross-cancellation as reduction of the single combined fraction.
- Restrict a product to inputs where both original factors are defined.

**Explanation**

To multiply, factor all numerators and denominators, cancel common factors across the whole product, and multiply what remains. Cross-cancellation is a shortcut for cancellation in one large fraction. The product requires both original factors to be defined.

**Worked examples**

$\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-x}\cdot\frac{x}{x+2}=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)x}{x(x-1)(x+2)}=\frac{x-2}{x-1}$. The original denominators require $x\ne0,1,-2$; all three exclusions remain.

**Exercises**

1. Multiply $(x/3)(6/x^2)$. **Answer:** $2/x$, $x\ne0$.
2. Multiply $[(x^2-9)/(x+1)][(x+1)/(x-3)]$. **Answer:** $x+3$, $x\ne-1,3$.

**Assessment**

Multiply $[(x^2-25)/(x^2+5x)][x/(x-5)]$ and state the original domain.

- **U07-L03-C01-O1 — 2 points:** The factored product cancels to 1.
- **U07-L03-C01-O2 — 2 points:** $x\ne-5,0,5$ because the two original denominators are $x(x+5)$ and $x-5$.

### Concept U07-L03-C02: Division and nonzero divisors

**Content**

- Reciprocal multiplication
- Zeros of a divisor create exclusions

**Learning objectives**

- **U07-L03-C02-O1:** Divide rational expressions by multiplying by the reciprocal.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.D.7 (+) (direct); TEKS 2A.7(F) (direct).
- **U07-L03-C02-O2:** Exclude inputs making either expression undefined or the divisor zero.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.B.5 (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(I) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Identify the entire divisor before taking its reciprocal.
- Exclude inputs where either original expression is undefined or the divisor equals zero.
- Distinguish restrictions introduced by division from denominator exclusions visible in the final formula.

**Explanation**

In $A/B$, the divisor $B$ must exist and must not equal zero. For a quotient of rational expressions, record restrictions before taking the reciprocal. Zeros in the divisor’s numerator may add exclusions that were not denominator zeros initially.

**Worked examples**

$\frac{x^2-9}{x+1}\div\frac{x-3}{x+2}=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x+1}\frac{x+2}{x-3}=\frac{(x+3)(x+2)}{x+1}$. Exclude $-1,-2$ from denominators and 3 because the divisor is zero there.

**Exercises**

1. Divide $(2/x)\div(4/x^2)$. **Answer:** $x/2$, $x\ne0$.
2. Divide $1\div[(x-2)/(x+4)]$. **Answer:** $(x+4)/(x-2)$, $x\ne-4,2$.

**Assessment**

Simplify $[x/(x-1)]\div[(x+2)/(x-1)]$ and list every restriction.

- **U07-L03-C02-O1 — 2 points:** $x/(x+2)$ after multiplication by $(x-1)/(x+2)$.
- **U07-L03-C02-O2 — 2 points:** $x\ne1,-2$; 1 makes the original fractions undefined and $-2$ makes the divisor zero.
