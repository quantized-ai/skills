# Unit 7: Rational expressions

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Rational expressions extend polynomial arithmetic by allowing division. This unit develops their structure, equivalence, simplification, and four operations while treating the original domain as part of every representation. The central connection is between numerical fraction rules and polynomial factors: the same arithmetic laws apply, but variable inputs create restrictions that must be recorded and preserved. Graphs and equations involving these expressions follow in Unit 8.

## Prerequisites

Polynomial vocabulary and degree, factoring, polynomial division, integer exponent laws, numerical fraction arithmetic, and real-number set notation. Students should distinguish factors from terms and verify a factorization by multiplication before beginning rational simplification.

## Main learning objectives

- Classify rational expressions and explain equivalence using their original domains. See [Lesson 7.1](lesson-1-definitions-and-restrictions/lesson.md).
- Simplify and multiply or divide polynomial quotients using factors and explicit restrictions. See [Lesson 7.2](lesson-2-simplification-by-factoring/lesson.md), [Lesson 7.3](lesson-3-products-and-quotients/lesson.md).
- Add and subtract rational expressions using justified common denominators. See [Lesson 7.4](lesson-4-addition-and-subtraction/lesson.md).
- Simplify nested fractions while accounting for every level of division. See [Lesson 7.5](lesson-5-complex-rational-expressions/lesson.md).
- Connect quotient-plus-remainder forms and closure to polynomial structure. See [Lesson 7.6](lesson-6-structure-and-closure/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Definitions and domain restrictions lead to factor cancellation, opposite-factor signs, products and quotients, common-denominator arithmetic, and complex fractions. The concluding structural lesson connects polynomial division to alternative representations and distinguishes formal closure from pointwise evaluation. Across these topics, the same distinction recurs: equivalent values on an allowed domain do not automatically imply identical functions on their natural domains.

## Lessons

- [7.1: Definitions and restrictions](lesson-1-definitions-and-restrictions/lesson.md) — Define rational expressions, determine permitted inputs, and compare original and reduced domains.
- [7.2: Simplification by factoring](lesson-2-simplification-by-factoring/lesson.md) — Use complete factorization and sign analysis to justify cancellation and correct common errors.
- [7.3: Products and quotients](lesson-3-products-and-quotients/lesson.md) — Apply product and quotient rules while distinguishing denominator restrictions from zero-divisor restrictions.
- [7.4: Addition and subtraction](lesson-4-addition-and-subtraction/lesson.md) — Construct least common denominators, combine signed numerators, and simplify the result.
- [7.5: Complex rational expressions](lesson-5-complex-rational-expressions/lesson.md) — Compare two methods for nested fractions and preserve all inner and overall restrictions.
- [7.6: Structure and closure](lesson-6-structure-and-closure/lesson.md) — Use polynomial division to change form and justify closure under the allowed rational operations.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards mark rational arithmetic standard HSA-APR.D.7 with (+), and Common Core Appendix A includes it in Algebra II. Texas standards 2A.7(F) directly cover degree-one and degree-two rational-expression operations; higher-degree illustrations use the broader Common Core scope. Concept assessments retain their individual standards mappings and scoring rubrics.
