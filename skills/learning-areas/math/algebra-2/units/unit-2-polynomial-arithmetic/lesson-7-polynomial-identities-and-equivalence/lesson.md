# Lesson 2.7: Polynomial identities and equivalence

Distinguish identities from equations that hold only at particular inputs and establish polynomial equivalence symbolically. Use a proven identity to generate numerical relationships and state their conditions.

## Learning objectives

- Prove polynomial identities by symbolic transformation and coefficient comparison.
- Disprove false identities and explain the limits of numerical evidence.
- Prove and apply the Pythagorean-triple identity, distinguishing valid triples from primitive triples.

## Learning outcome

Justify polynomial identities and transfer them to numerical relationships with explicit input conditions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish a claim about every permitted input from an equation satisfied only by selected inputs.
- Prove equivalence symbolically and refute false identities with a valid counterexample or unequal polynomial coefficients.
- Account for the mixed terms in the Pythagorean identity.
- Generate positive integer triples under the stated conditions, verify the relationship, and determine whether a common factor remains.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Proving and disproving polynomial identities** | An identity is an equation true for every permitted input; other equations may hold only on a smaller solution set. Polynomial expressions with real coefficients are identical on all real inputs exactly when their collected coefficients match. Valid symbolic transformations prove equivalence. An unequal coefficient or a single unequal-output counterexample refutes identity. Sample agreement alone is not proof without an argument establishing its sufficiency. | Distinguish identities from equations with restricted solution sets and prove polynomial identities by symbolic transformation or coefficient comparison. Disprove false identities with counterexamples or unequal coefficients and explain why sample agreement alone is insufficient. | State the inputs covered by the claim. Establish equivalence through valid transformations or matching collected coefficients without assuming the claim. Refute a false identity using a permitted input or coefficient mismatch and explain the limits of numerical checks. |
| **Identities and numerical relationships** | $(u^2-v^2)^2+(2uv)^2=(u^2+v^2)^2$ holds for all real $u,v$; its mixed terms combine to $2u^2v^2$ on both sides. Integers $u>v>0$ generate positive integer legs $u^2-v^2$ and $2uv$ and hypotenuse $u^2+v^2$. A triple is primitive when its three entries have no common integer factor greater than $1$; generated triples need not be primitive. | Prove the Pythagorean-triple polynomial identity by expansion and explain its mixed-term combination. Use the identity with integers $u>v>0$ to generate and verify a positive integer triple and determine whether it is primitive. | Expand both sides and account for all mixed terms. Choose integers satisfying the input conditions and identify the two legs and hypotenuse. Verify the exact numerical relationship and use the common-factor definition to distinguish primitive from nonprimitive triples. |
