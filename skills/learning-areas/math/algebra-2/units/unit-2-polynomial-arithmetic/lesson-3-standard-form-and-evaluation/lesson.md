# Lesson 2.3: Standard form and evaluation

Organize polynomial information in standard form and coefficient lists, then evaluate accurately. Distinguish numerical checks from general evidence of equivalence.

## Learning objectives

- Translate between standard form and complete coefficient lists.
- Evaluate polynomials with correct signs and operation order, preserving equivalent representations.
- Use counterexamples to disprove identities and state the limits of sample agreement.

## Learning outcome

Organize and evaluate a polynomial while explaining what numerical checks can and cannot establish.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve powers, signs, and zero placeholders when moving between polynomial representations.
- Substitute consistently and parenthesize negative inputs before applying powers.
- Explain why equivalent representations have the same value at every common valid input.
- Use an unequal-output counterexample to refute an identity; do not treat sample agreement alone as proof.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Writing a polynomial in standard form** | Standard form in one variable collects like terms and orders the remaining terms by descending exponent, preserving signs. A coefficient list records every power from a stated highest exponent through $0$, including zero placeholders for omitted powers. Reordering and collection preserve the polynomial. | Write a polynomial in descending powers after collecting like terms. Translate between standard form and a coefficient list with a specified highest exponent and zero placeholders. | Collect repeated powers and order terms without changing signs. Match each list position to its exponent, including omitted powers. Reconstruct the same polynomial and detect coefficient shifts caused by missing placeholders. |
| **Evaluating polynomial expressions** | Evaluation substitutes the same input for every occurrence of the variable. Parentheses distinguish a negative base from a minus sign outside a power; powers precede multiplication and addition. At zero, positive-power terms vanish. Equivalent expressions agree at every common valid input. One unequal output disproves an identity; matching sample outputs alone, without an argument establishing sufficiency, do not prove it. | Evaluate polynomials at positive, negative, and zero inputs and explain why equivalent representations yield equal values. Use evaluation to disprove a claimed polynomial identity and distinguish sample agreement from a sufficient proof. | Substitute consistently and apply powers and signs in the correct order. Explain agreement between equivalent representations. Produce a valid counterexample to a false identity and state why sample agreement alone does not establish a general identity. |
