# Lesson 2.6: Special products

Recognize binomial squares and conjugate products as applications of distribution. Connect their cross-term behavior to exact symbolic and numerical calculations.

## Learning objectives

- Expand binomial squares and justify doubled cross terms.
- Expand conjugate products and justify cross-term cancellation.
- Use the difference-of-squares identity to justify exact numerical products.

## Learning outcome

Distinguish the two special-product structures and apply each with correct signs, coefficients, and powers.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify complete binomial components before applying an identity.
- Derive doubled or cancelled cross terms through distribution.
- Carry coefficients, powers, and signs through every squared component.
- Express an appropriate numerical product as a conjugate product and justify its exact value.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Squares of binomials** | $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ and $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$ hold for real quantities or polynomial expressions $a,b$. Two equal cross products create the middle term. The final term is $+b^2$ in both identities; for real inputs its value is nonnegative. Each complete component, including its coefficient and powers, is squared. | Expand both binomial-square forms, including polynomial or composite components. Derive the square identities by distribution and use the cross terms to explain and correct missing or incorrectly signed middle terms. | Identify both complete components and square their coefficients and powers correctly. Account for both cross products and the $+b^2$ term. Reconstruct distribution to justify the middle term and correct an erroneous expansion. |
| **Products of conjugate binomials** | Conjugate factors have a shared first component and opposite second components: $(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2$. Their cross products cancel. The identity permits polynomial components and is unchanged by reversing factor order. Numerically, two values equally spaced from a center $a$ have product $a^2-b^2$, where $b$ is their offset. | Expand products of conjugate binomials, including composite terms, and justify cross-term cancellation. Use the difference-of-squares identity to compute and justify exact numerical products. | Recognize the shared and opposite components and square each complete component. Explain cancellation and preserve the order in the difference of squares. Identify a numerical product's center and offset and justify the resulting exact value. |
