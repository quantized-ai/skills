# Lesson 2.2: Classification and degree

[Unit 2: Polynomial arithmetic](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Classify simplified polynomials by term count and degree. Separate these classifications and identify the information carried by a leading term.

## Learning objectives

- Classify polynomials after combining like terms.
- Determine total degree and identify one-variable leading terms.
- Apply consistent conventions to constant and zero polynomials.

## Learning outcome

The student can describe a simplified polynomial by term count, degree, and leading information without confusing these properties.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Combine like terms before counting terms or determining degree.
- Distinguish term-count names from degree and explain how cancellation can change either.
- Determine total degree in several variables while using leading-term language only with an appropriate order.
- Apply the stated conventions for nonzero constants and the zero polynomial.

## Concepts

### Concept U02-L02-C01: Monomials, binomials, and trinomials

**Content**

Counting nonzero terms after simplification; one-, two-, and three-term polynomial names; zero polynomial convention.

**Learning objectives**

- **U02-L02-C01-O1:** Classify a simplified polynomial by its number of nonzero terms.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(B) (supporting knowledge).
- **U02-L02-C01-O2:** Combine like terms before determining the number of terms.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(B) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Collect like terms and remove zero terms before assigning a term-count classification.
- Distinguish the number of nonzero terms from the size of their exponents.
- Apply the stated zero-polynomial convention instead of assigning it a nonzero-term classification.

**Explanation**

A monomial has one nonzero term, a binomial two, and a trinomial three. More terms are described by their count. These labels do not specify degree: $x^{100}+1$ is a binomial. Combine like terms first, because written pieces may cancel. The zero polynomial is a polynomial with no nonzero terms; this course does not label it a monomial. Factored expressions should be expanded if classification by additive terms is requested.

**Worked examples**

Classify $3x^2+4x-2x^2-4x+7$. Combine the square terms to get $x^2$ and the linear terms to get $0$, leaving $x^2+7$. It is a binomial, despite having five written summands initially.

**Exercises**

1. Classify $8x^5$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Monomial; one term.
2. Classify $x^3+2x-5-x^3$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Binomial $2x-5$ after cancellation.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Simplify and classify $2x^3+x^2-2x^3+4x-1$; explain why counting the original pieces gives the wrong answer.

Expected answer: $x^2+4x-1$ is a trinomial; the cubic terms cancel.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for simplification; 2 points for classification and cancellation explanation. Assesses **U02-L02-C01-O1** and **U02-L02-C01-O2**.

### Concept U02-L02-C02: Degree, leading term, and leading coefficient

**Content**

Degree of a nonzero term; total degree in several variables; degree of a polynomial; leading term; constant and zero cases.

**Learning objectives**

- **U02-L02-C02-O1:** Determine the degree of terms and of a simplified polynomial, including multivariable examples.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(B) (supporting knowledge).
- **U02-L02-C02-O2:** Identify the leading term and leading coefficient of a one-variable polynomial.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (supporting knowledge); CCSS HSF-IF.C.7c (supporting knowledge); TEKS 2A.7(B) (supporting knowledge).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Use the sum of variable exponents for a monomial and the largest surviving term degree for a polynomial.
- Identify a one-variable leading term and its signed coefficient only after combining like terms.
- Distinguish nonzero constant degree from the undefined degree of the zero polynomial and avoid an unspecified multivariable leading-term order.

**Explanation**

The degree of a nonzero monomial is the sum of its variable exponents; a nonzero constant has degree zero. The degree of a nonzero polynomial is the largest degree among its nonzero terms after combining like terms. In one variable, the highest-degree term is the leading term, and its coefficient is the leading coefficient. The degree of the zero polynomial is left undefined in this course; do not assign it degree zero. Multivariable leading terms require an ordering convention, so only total degree is requested there.

**Worked examples**

For $5-2x^4+3x^2$, term degrees are $0,4,2$; the polynomial has degree $4$, leading term $-2x^4$, and leading coefficient $-2$. For $7x^2y^3-4xy$, degrees are $2+3=5$ and $1+1=2$, so total degree is $5$.

**Exercises**

1. Find degree and leading coefficient of $6x-x^5+2$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Degree $5$; leading coefficient $-1$.
2. Find degrees of $8$, $0$, and $3a^2b^4$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $0$, undefined, and $6$, respectively.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Find degree, leading term, and leading coefficient of $4x^3-4x^3-2x^2+9$; also give the degree of $x^3y^2$.

Expected answer: The simplified polynomial is $-2x^2+9$: degree $2$, leading term $-2x^2$, coefficient $-2$. The multivariable term has degree $5$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for correct degrees after cancellation; 2 points for leading term and coefficient. Assesses **U02-L02-C02-O1** and **U02-L02-C02-O2**.
