# Lesson 2.4: Addition and subtraction

Add and subtract polynomials by combining like terms and applying additive inverses. Explain closure and the possible cancellation of leading terms.

## Learning objectives

- Compute polynomial sums and differences, including degrees greater than two.
- Explain closure under addition and subtraction and degree changes caused by cancellation.
- Diagnose sign errors using complete additive inverses and the order of subtraction.

## Learning outcome

Produce and justify polynomial sums and differences while tracing signs and degree changes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Combine coefficients only for matching variable powers.
- Negate every term of a subtracted polynomial and preserve operand order.
- Explain closure by preservation of finite sums of nonnegative integer powers.
- Explain leading-term cancellation, treat a zero result separately, and use reversed differences to check signs.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Adding polynomials and closure** | Like terms have identical powers of every variable; addition combines their coefficients without changing exponents. Polynomial sums remain finite sums of nonnegative integer powers. For nonzero input polynomials with a nonzero sum, the sum's degree is at most the larger input degree; leading-term cancellation can lower it. Complete cancellation gives the zero polynomial, whose degree is undefined here. | Add polynomials, including degree greater than two, by collecting like terms. Explain closure under addition and distinguish degree reduction from complete cancellation. | Match all variable powers before combining coefficients. Justify closure from the polynomial definition. Identify leading-term cancellation and state degree conclusions only for a nonzero result. |
| **Subtracting polynomials and additive inverses** | $p-q=p+(-q)$: the additive inverse negates every term of the subtrahend. Polynomial subtraction is closed because negation and addition preserve polynomial form. Operand order matters: $p-q=-(q-p)$. As with addition, leading terms may cancel, and a zero difference has undefined degree here. | Subtract polynomials by negating every term of the subtrahend and collecting like terms. Explain subtraction closure and degree changes from cancellation; use complete additive inverses and reversed differences to diagnose sign errors. | Change every subtrahend sign, including constant signs, and collect only like terms. Preserve operand order and explain why reversing it negates the difference. Justify closure and distinguish partial from complete cancellation. |
