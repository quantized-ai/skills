# Lesson 2.5: Distributive multiplication

[Unit 2: Polynomial arithmetic](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Build polynomial multiplication from monomial products and the distributive property. Extend the same reasoning from a single term to factors with any finite number of terms.

## Learning objectives

- Multiply monomials with correct coefficient and exponent rules.
- Distribute every term across a polynomial product.
- Use degree and leading coefficient as checks on a product.

## Learning outcome

The student can expand polynomial products completely and justify each partial product through distribution.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Separate exponent rules for multiplication from the coefficient rules for combining like terms.
- Account for every term pairing, including negative and constant terms.
- Collect the expansion without dropping or duplicating a partial product.
- Explain product closure and reconcile the result with expected degree and leading coefficient.

## Concepts

### Concept U02-L05-C01: Multiplying monomials and distributing a monomial

**Content**

Product of coefficients; addition of exponents; distribution to every term.

**Learning objectives**

- **U02-L05-C01-O1:** Multiply monomials using whole-number exponent laws.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(B) (direct).
- **U02-L05-C01-O2:** Distribute a monomial across a polynomial and express the product in standard form.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(B) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Separate coefficient multiplication from same-variable exponent addition.
- Apply the monomial factor to every polynomial term, including the constant term.
- Check the resulting signs and verify the expected product degree when both factors are nonzero.

**Explanation**

For the same variable, multiplication adds exponents: $x^mx^n=x^{m+n}$. Multiply numerical coefficients separately. Then use $m(A+B+C)=mA+mB+mC$; every term receives the factor, including the constant. Exponent addition applies to multiplication, not addition. The product of nonzero polynomials has degree equal to the sum of degrees because its highest-degree coefficient is the nonzero product of the leading coefficients.

**Worked examples**

$-3x^2(2x^3-x+4)=(-3)(2)x^{2+3}+(-3)(-1)x^{2+1}+(-3)(4)x^2=-6x^5+3x^3-12x^2$. Degree $2+3=5$ provides a useful check.

**Exercises**

1. Multiply $(4a^2b)(-2ab^3)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $-8a^3b^4$.
2. Expand $2x(3x^2-5x+1)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $6x^3-10x^2+2x$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Expand $-2x^3(x^2-4x+5)$, showing the exponent calculation for each term.

Expected answer: $-2x^5+8x^4-10x^3$; exponents are $3+2$, $3+1$, and $3+0$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for coefficient and exponent products; 2 points for complete distribution. Assesses **U02-L05-C01-O1** and **U02-L05-C01-O2**.

### Concept U02-L05-C02: Multiplying binomials and general polynomials

**Content**

Every term times every term; area/grid organization; combining products; product degree.

**Learning objectives**

- **U02-L05-C02-O1:** Multiply binomials and polynomial factors with three or more terms.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(B) (direct).
- **U02-L05-C02-O2:** Justify polynomial multiplication through distribution and degree checks.
  - Standards: CCSS HSA-APR.A.1 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(B) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Generate every pairing of a term from one factor with a term from the other.
- Collect only like partial products and explain the product through distribution rather than an incomplete shortcut.
- Use leading coefficients and degrees to check a nonzero product without treating those checks as a full verification.

**Explanation**

For a product of two sums, each term in the first sum multiplies every term in the second. A grid with one factor along each edge is a bookkeeping device for distribution. FOIL is only the two-by-two instance; it is not a new law and does not cover larger products. Before combining, an $m$-term factor and an $n$-term factor produce $mn$ partial products. Nonnegative integer exponents remain nonnegative integers, explaining closure under multiplication.

**Worked examples**

$(x-2)(x^2+3x-4)=x^3+3x^2-4x-2x^2-6x+8=x^3+x^2-10x+8$. There are six partial products and degree $1+2=3$. Separately, $(2x+1)(x-3)=2x^2-6x+x-3=2x^2-5x-3$.

**Exercises**

1. Expand $(x+3)(x-5)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x^2-2x-15$.
2. Expand $(x^2+1)(x^2-x+2)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $x^4-x^3+3x^2-x+2$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Expand $(2x-1)(x^2+x+3)$ and explain why six partial products and a cubic result are expected.

Expected answer: $2x^3+x^2+5x-3$; two terms times three terms gives six products, and degrees add to $3$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for expanded result; 2 points for distribution and degree reasoning. Assesses **U02-L05-C02-O1** and **U02-L05-C02-O2**.
