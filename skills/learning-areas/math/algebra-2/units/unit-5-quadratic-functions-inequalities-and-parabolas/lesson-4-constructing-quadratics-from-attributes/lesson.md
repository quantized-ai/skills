# Lesson 5.4: Constructing quadratics from attributes

Construct quadratics from a vertex or real zeros plus an additional point. Use the point to determine scale and identify insufficient or degenerate attribute sets.

## Learning objectives

- Construct a quadratic from a vertex or specified real roots and an additional point.
- Diagnose sufficient, redundant, contradictory, or degenerate data.
- Verify every supplied attribute in the resulting rule.

## Learning outcome

The student can choose a suitable quadratic form, determine its scale, and verify all supplied attributes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Select vertex form or factored form according to the supplied attributes.
- Use a point that contributes independent information to determine a nonzero scale.
- Identify redundant, contradictory, or constant-function outcomes.
- Verify both the defining attributes and the additional point in the constructed equation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A vertex and an additional point** | A stated vertex $(h,k)$ gives $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$, $a\ne0$. A point $(x_1,y_1)$ with $x_1\ne h$ determines $a=(y_1-k)/(x_1-h)^2$; $y_1\ne k$ is necessary for a genuine quadratic. If $x_1=h$, the point is redundant when $y_1=k$ and contradictory otherwise. If $x_1\ne h$ but $y_1=k$, substitution forces a constant rule, so no quadratic satisfies the data. | Construct a quadratic function from its vertex and an additional point that determines a nonzero scale. Verify the supplied attributes and distinguish sufficient, redundant, contradictory, and constant-rule data. | Choose vertex form and determine $a$ by point substitution. Check that the data determine $a\ne0$. Verify vertex and point, or justify why the supplied data fail to determine a unique quadratic. |
| **Real zeros and an additional point** | Two specified real roots, counting multiplicity, give $f(x)=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$, $a\ne0$; a specified repeated root gives $a(x-r)^2$. A point $(x_1,y_1)$ with $x_1$ distinct from both roots determines $a=y_1/[(x_1-r_1)(x_1-r_2)]$, which must be nonzero. A supplied root point is redundant; nonzero output at a stated root is contradictory. A third distinct zero is also contradictory for a quadratic. Zeros alone do not fix scale. | Construct a quadratic from two specified real roots, counting multiplicity, and a nonroot point with nonzero output. Verify a constructed rule and distinguish sufficient, redundant, or contradictory conditions when zeros and an additional point are supplied. | Form the correct factors, including specified multiplicity. Determine a nonzero scale from independent point data. Verify all roots and the additional point. Explain why roots alone leave scale free and detect contradictory point data. |
