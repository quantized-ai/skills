# Lesson 5.6: Focus, directrix, and vertical parabolas

Define a parabola by equal distances from a focus and directrix, then derive its vertical form. Connect focal geometry to vertex-form coefficients and opening direction.

## Learning objectives

- Derive a vertical parabola from a focus and horizontal directrix.
- Recover focal attributes from a quadratic function.
- Construct equations from sufficient vertex and focal data and identify insufficient information.

## Learning outcome

The student can translate between a vertical parabola’s distance definition, equation, and focal attributes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Express equal focus and directrix distances and simplify them to the correct parabola form.
- Identify the vertex, axis, and nonzero signed focal parameter.
- Use the reciprocal relation between focal parameter and leading coefficient consistently.
- Place focus and directrix at matching distances and distinguish direction-only data from data determining width.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A parabola as an equidistance locus** | A nondegenerate parabola is the locus of points equally distant from a fixed focus and a directrix line not containing the focus. For focus $(h,k+p)$ and directrix $y=k-p$, $p\ne0$, equidistance gives $(x-h)^2+(y-k-p)^2=(y-k+p)^2$, hence $(x-h)^2=4p(y-k)$. Vertex $(h,k)$ is midway between the focus and its perpendicular projection onto the directrix. Axis $x=h$ is perpendicular to the directrix; $p>0$ opens upward and $p<0$ downward. | Derive a vertical parabola equation from a focus and horizontal directrix using equal distances. Identify the vertex, axis, nonzero signed focal parameter, and direction of opening from the derivation. | Equate point-to-focus and perpendicular point-to-line distances. Derive the vertical focal equation by equivalent algebra. Locate the vertex and axis and interpret the signed parameter consistently with the geometry. |
| **Converting between vertex form and focal attributes** | For a vertical parabola, $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ and $(x-h)^2=4p(y-k)$ satisfy $a=1/(4p)$ and $p=1/(4a)$, with $a,p\ne0$. Focus $(h,k+p)$ and directrix $y=k-p$ lie on opposite sides of the vertex at distance $\lvert p\rvert$. Larger $\lvert a\rvert$ means smaller focal distance and a narrower graph. A vertex plus a focus on its vertical axis, or a horizontal directrix, determines $p$; opening direction alone does not determine its magnitude. | Find and interpret the focus, directrix, axis, and focal distance from a quadratic in vertex form. Write a vertical parabola equation from sufficient vertex and focal data and explain why opening direction alone is insufficient. | Use the reciprocal relation between $a$ and $p$ with correct sign. Place focus and directrix at equal distances on opposite sides of the vertex. Recover the equation from compatible nondegenerate focal data and distinguish direction from width information. |
