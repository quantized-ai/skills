# Lesson 5.7: Horizontal parabolas and general equations

Extend the focus/directrix definition to horizontal parabolas and recover attributes from general equations. Distinguish these geometric relations from functions of the horizontal input.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and analyze horizontal parabola equations.
- Complete the square to recover focal form and verify equidistance.
- Explain why a full horizontal parabola is not a function of the horizontal coordinate.

## Learning outcome

The student can identify a nonrotated parabola’s orientation and focal attributes and explain whether it is a function of the horizontal coordinate.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the squared variable and the corresponding orientation.
- Complete the square with correct sign and scale to obtain the focal parameter.
- Recover vertex, focus, directrix, axis, and opening consistently with equidistance.
- Explain the two-branch obstruction to treating a full horizontal parabola as a function of the horizontal coordinate.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Horizontal focus/directrix equations** | For focus $(h+p,k)$ and directrix $x=h-p$, $p\ne0$, equidistance gives $(x-h-p)^2+(y-k)^2=(x-h+p)^2$, hence $(y-k)^2=4p(x-h)$. Vertex is $(h,k)$ and axis is $y=k$; $p>0$ opens right and $p<0$ left. Whenever $p(x-h)>0$, the relation has two outputs $y=k\pm\sqrt{4p(x-h)}$. Thus every full nondegenerate horizontal parabola fails to be a function $y=f(x)$, although $x$ is a function of $y$. | Derive a horizontal parabola equation from a focus and vertical directrix and identify its vertex, axis, focus, directrix, and opening. Explain why a full nondegenerate horizontal parabola is not a function $y=f(x)$. | Derive the focal equation from equal distances. Interpret the signed parameter and all focal attributes consistently. Show that inputs strictly inside the horizontal domain have two outputs and apply the vertical-line test. |
| **Recovering parabola attributes by square completion** | Nonrotated parabola equations have the form $Ax^2+Bx+Cy+D=0$ or $Ay^2+By+Cx+D=0$, with $A\ne0$ and $C\ne0$. The squared variable determines vertical or horizontal orientation respectively. Completing the square and factoring the remaining linear expression yields $(x-h)^2=4p(y-k)$ or $(y-k)^2=4p(x-h)$, with $p\ne0$. The assumptions exclude degenerate relations; rotated equations require a different analysis. | Convert a general nonrotated vertical or horizontal parabola equation to focal form by completing the square. Extract the vertex, axis, focus, directrix, and opening, and verify a point using equal distances. | Identify orientation and preserve equivalence through square completion, including coefficient scaling and constant compensation. Read the nonzero signed focal parameter and all geometric attributes. Verify that a point on the parabola has equal focus and directrix distances. |
