# Unit 5: Quadratic functions, inequalities, and parabolas

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Quadratic functions are studied through their algebraic forms, graphs, construction conditions, and inequalities. The unit makes the connection between an expression and its attributes explicit before moving to data that determine a quadratic. It then derives parabolas from the focus/directrix definition, including horizontal parabolas that generally are not functions of the horizontal coordinate. The final lesson applies sign reasoning to quadratic inequalities, including repeated-root and no-real-root cases. Throughout, sketches must agree with algebraically justified features rather than replace that justification.

## Prerequisites

Complete Units 1–4, with particular attention to transformations, function tests, interval notation, factoring, square completion, and real versus complex quadratic roots. Coordinate distance and basic elimination of linear equations are also needed. The small coefficient systems used for three-point construction are developed directly here; the broader study of systems is not a prerequisite for understanding their purpose.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret equivalent quadratic forms to identify intercepts, vertex, symmetry, extrema, domain, and range. See [Lesson 5.1](lesson-1-three-forms-of-a-quadratic-function/lesson.md).
- Apply transformations and construct coherent graphs with correct sign and monotonicity descriptions. See [Lesson 5.3](lesson-3-transformations-and-graph-construction/lesson.md).
- Construct quadratics from attributes or three points and diagnose insufficient or degenerate conditions. See [Lesson 5.5](lesson-5-three-point-construction/lesson.md).
- Derive and recover vertical and horizontal parabola equations from focal geometry. See [Lesson 5.7](lesson-7-horizontal-parabolas-and-general-equations/lesson.md).
- Solve quadratic inequalities with complete sign arguments and correct endpoint treatment. See [Lesson 5.8](lesson-8-quadratic-inequalities/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Topics progress through standard, factored, and vertex forms; square completion; discriminant and real intercepts; inside and outside transformations; and graph construction. Function-building uses a vertex or zeros with a scale-determining point, then exact three-point interpolation and its exceptional cases. Geometry adds distance equality, the signed focal parameter, reciprocal width relationships, horizontal orientation, and general-equation square completion. Inequalities include ordinary sign intervals as well as all-real, empty, singleton, and excluded-point solution sets.

## Lessons

- [5.1: Three forms of a quadratic function](lesson-1-three-forms-of-a-quadratic-function/lesson.md) — Read the useful attributes of standard, factored, and vertex forms while preserving the leading coefficient and distinguishing real from complex roots.
- [5.2: Converting forms and locating zeros](lesson-2-converting-forms-and-locating-zeros/lesson.md) — Complete the square to expose the vertex and reconcile its position with root symmetry, the discriminant, and real intercepts.
- [5.3: Transformations and graph construction](lesson-3-transformations-and-graph-construction/lesson.md) — Map quadratic transformations, build labeled sketches from verified points, and separate monotonic intervals from intervals of positive or negative output.
- [5.4: Constructing quadratics from attributes](lesson-4-constructing-quadratics-from-attributes/lesson.md) — Construct functions from a vertex or zeros plus an independent point and determine whether the supplied attributes actually fix a nonzero scale.
- [5.5: Three-point construction](lesson-5-three-point-construction/lesson.md) — Solve coefficient equations through three points and distinguish unique quadratics from collinear, repeated-input, redundant, or contradictory data.
- [5.6: Focus, directrix, and vertical parabolas](lesson-6-focus-directrix-and-vertical-parabolas/lesson.md) — Derive vertical parabola equations from equal distances and translate between the signed focal parameter, focus, directrix, and vertex-form coefficient.
- [5.7: Horizontal parabolas and general equations](lesson-7-horizontal-parabolas-and-general-equations/lesson.md) — Analyze horizontal parabolas and recover focal attributes from general equations while explaining why the full relation may fail the function test.
- [5.8: Quadratic inequalities](lesson-8-quadratic-inequalities/lesson.md) — Solve quadratic inequalities by interval signs or square structure and justify every included or excluded zero, including degenerate cases.
