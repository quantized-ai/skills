# Lesson 5.3: Transformations and graph construction

Transform the quadratic parent and construct sketches from verified attributes. Distinguish the meaning of input scaling, output scaling, monotonic intervals, and sign intervals.

## Learning objectives

- Describe and apply quadratic transformations and recognize equivalent graphs.
- Construct labeled graphs from coordinates and verified attributes.
- State increasing and decreasing intervals and distinguish them from output-sign intervals.

## Learning outcome

The student can connect a transformed equation to a coherent labeled sketch and describe how its output changes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Map coordinates with the appropriate inside and outside transformation rules.
- Reconcile the mapped points with vertex, symmetry, opening, and intercepts.
- Use consistent scale and a smooth graph that respects every established attribute.
- Distinguish intervals of increasing or decreasing output from intervals of positive or negative output.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transformations of a quadratic parent function** | For the parent $f(x)=x^2$, $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ with $a\ne0$ maps $(u,u^2)$ to $(u+h,au^2+k)$: horizontal translation, vertical scale and possible reflection, then vertical translation. In $y=f(bx)$ with $b\ne0$, input coordinates divide by $b$; negative $b$ includes horizontal reflection. For this parent, $f(bx)=b^2f(x)$, so inside and outside scalings can give the same graph despite having different coordinate rules. | Describe and apply coordinate transformations from $y=x^2$ to $y=a(x-h)^2+k$. Analyze inside and outside scaling, including when different transformations of the quadratic parent produce the same graph. | Map parent points using the correct signs, scale factors, and order. Distinguish division of input coordinates from multiplication of outputs. Explain equivalence for the quadratic parent without asserting it for arbitrary functions. |
| **Sketching from verified attributes** | A quadratic sketch is determined by consistent vertex, axis, opening, intercepts where real, and symmetric point pairs. Axes and scales must be labeled. With vertex input $h$, an upward parabola decreases on $(-\infty,h)$ and increases on $(h,\infty)$; a downward parabola reverses these intervals. These open intervals describe behavior on either side of the turning point. Increasing/decreasing concerns output change; positive/negative concerns output relative to zero. | Construct an accurate labeled sketch from the vertex, axis, opening, intercepts, and symmetric points. Describe increasing and decreasing intervals from the vertex and opening, and distinguish them from output-sign intervals. | Build one smooth parabola consistent with verified attributes and symmetric point pairs. Use consistent coordinate scales. Justify monotonic intervals using the opening and distinguish them from where outputs are positive or negative. |
