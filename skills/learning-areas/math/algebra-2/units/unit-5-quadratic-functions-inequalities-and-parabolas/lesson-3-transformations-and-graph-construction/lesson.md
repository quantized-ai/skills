# Lesson 5.3: Transformations and graph construction

[Unit 5: Quadratic functions, inequalities, and parabolas](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Transform the quadratic parent and construct sketches from verified attributes. Distinguish the meaning of input scaling, output scaling, monotonic intervals, and sign intervals.

## Learning objectives

- Describe and apply quadratic transformations.
- Construct graphs from coordinates and key attributes.
- State increasing and decreasing intervals accurately.

## Learning outcome

The student can connect a transformed equation to a coherent labeled sketch and describe how its output changes.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Map coordinates with the appropriate inside and outside transformation rules.
- Reconcile the mapped points with vertex, symmetry, opening, and intercepts.
- Use consistent scale and a smooth graph that respects every established attribute.
- Distinguish intervals of increasing or decreasing output from intervals of positive or negative output.

## Concepts

### Concept U05-L03-C01: Transformations of a quadratic parent function

**Content**

Vertical/horizontal translation; vertical scaling; reflection; $f(bx)$ scaling; vertex movement.

**Learning objectives**

- **U05-L03-C01-O1:** Describe transformations from $y=x^2$ to $y=a(x-h)^2+k$.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-BF.B.3 (direct); TEKS 2A.4(D) (supporting knowledge).
- **U05-L03-C01-O2:** Analyze an inside scaling and distinguish it from an outside scaling.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-BF.B.3 (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Map parent-function coordinates according to the order and location of shifts, reflections, and scales.
- Distinguish division of input coordinates by an inside factor from multiplication of outputs by an outside factor.
- Explain when different transformations of the quadratic parent produce the same final formula without treating them as identical rules for every function.

**Explanation**

For $a(x-h)^2+k$, shift the parent right by $h$, multiply vertical offsets by $a$, then shift up by $k$. Negative $a$ reflects across the horizontal line through the vertex relative to the translated square. For $f(bx)$ with $b\ne0$, original input coordinates are divided by $b$; negative $b$ also reflects horizontally. For $f(x)=x^2$, $f(bx)=b^2x^2$, so some different transformation descriptions produce the same final graph.

**Worked examples**

$y=-2(x-3)^2+1$ has vertex $(3,1)$. Parent points $(0,0),(1,1),(-1,1)$ become $(3,1),(4,-1),(2,-1)$. For $f(2x)=4x^2$, horizontal compression by factor $1/2$ is equivalent for this parent to vertical stretch by $4$.

**Exercises**

1. Describe $y=\frac12(x+2)^2-3$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Shift left $2$, scale vertical offsets by $1/2$, shift down $3$.
2. For $f(x)=x^2$, compare $f(-2x)$ and $-2f(x)$.
   - Answer and reasoning: $f(-2x)=4x^2$ opens upward; $-2f(x)=-2x^2$ opens downward.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Give the vertex and two transformed points for $y=-3(x+1)^2+2$, then simplify $f(3x)$ for $f(x)=x^2$.

Expected answer: Vertex $(-1,2)$; points $(0,-1),(-2,-1)$; $f(3x)=9x^2$ corresponds to input-coordinate division by $3$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for transformed graph data; 2 points for inside-scaling interpretation. Assesses **U05-L03-C01-O1** and **U05-L03-C01-O2**.

### Concept U05-L03-C02: Sketching from verified attributes

**Content**

Vertex, axis, opening, intercepts, symmetric points; increasing/decreasing intervals; labeled axes.

**Learning objectives**

- **U05-L03-C02-O1:** Construct an accurate sketch from vertex, intercepts, and symmetric points.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7a (direct).
- **U05-L03-C02-O2:** Describe increasing and decreasing intervals using the vertex and direction of opening.
  - Standards: CCSS HSF-IF.C.7a (supporting knowledge); CCSS HSF-IF.B.4 (supporting knowledge; contextual interpretation later).

**Proficiency criteria**

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Build a coherent sketch from a verified vertex, symmetry axis, intercepts, and symmetric point pairs.
- Use the direction of opening to justify increasing and decreasing intervals.
- Distinguish output sign intervals from monotonic intervals and label coordinate scales consistently.

**Explanation**

Find the vertex and axis, mark intercepts if real, and choose at least one symmetric point pair. Draw one smooth parabola through the points with the correct opening and label axis scale. An upward parabola decreases to its vertex then increases; a downward one increases then decreases. These intervals concern how output changes as input moves left to right. They are different from intervals where the output is positive or negative.

**Worked examples**

For $y=(x-2)^2-1$, vertex $(2,-1)$, axis $x=2$, zeros $1,3$, and vertical intercept $(0,3)$ determine a sketch; symmetry also gives $(4,3)$. The function decreases on $(-\infty,2)$ and increases on $(2,\infty)$. It is below the horizontal axis only for $1<x<3$.

**Exercises**

1. Give five graph points for $y=-(x+1)^2+4$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Vertex $(-1,4)$; $(0,3),(-2,3),(1,0),(-3,0)$.
2. Give monotonic intervals for $y=-2(x-5)^2+1$.
   - Answer and reasoning: Increasing $(-\infty,5)$; decreasing $(5,\infty)$.

**Assessment**

Independent prompt: Sketch $y=x^2-4x$ with labeled intercepts, vertex, axis, and monotonic intervals.

Expected answer: $y=(x-2)^2-4$; vertex $(2,-4)$, axis $x=2$, intercepts $(0,0),(4,0)$; decreasing before $2$, increasing after $2$.

Scoring (4 points): 2 points for correct labeled sketch/features; 2 points for justified monotonic intervals. Assesses **U05-L03-C02-O1** and **U05-L03-C02-O2**.
