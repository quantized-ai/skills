# Lesson 9.4: Division and rationalization

Simplify radical quotients and rationalize denominators while preserving the original values and domain.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify radical quotients and rationalize one-term or binomial denominators.
- Explain denominator restrictions and account for inputs lost by a rationalizing multiplier.

## Learning outcome

Produce a rationalized form and state exactly where it agrees with the original expression, retaining any otherwise lost valid inputs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish a real-radicand condition from the nonzero condition required by a denominator.
- Choose a multiplier that creates a perfect power or difference of squares.
- Multiply the complete numerator and denominator by the same nonzero expression.
- Detect inputs excluded by a zero conjugate and represent the original function there using its original form or a separate case.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Radical quotients and monomial denominators** | For integer $n\ge2$, when $n$ is even, $\sqrt[n]{a/b}=\sqrt[n]{a}/\sqrt[n]{b}$ holds when $a\ge0$ and $b>0$; for odd $n$, it holds for real $a$ and nonzero real $b$. These conditions concern the quotient of separate roots, whose domain may be smaller than that of the single root. Rationalize a one-term radical denominator by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor so the denominator radicand becomes a perfect nth power. Original exclusions remain in force. | Simplify radical quotients and rationalize one-term denominators using valid root identities. Determine and preserve the original restrictions of radical quotients and denominators. | Check that all separate roots exist and the denominator is nonzero. Choose and apply a nonzero multiplier that produces a perfect-power denominator. State the original domain and verify that the rationalized form agrees throughout it. |
| **Conjugate denominators** | The conjugates $u+v$ and $u-v$ satisfy $(u+v)(u-v)=u^2-v^2$; when their squares remove the radicals, this identity rationalizes the denominator. Multiplying by a conjugate divided by itself requires that conjugate to be nonzero. A variable conjugate can vanish at a valid original input, leaving a rationalized formula with a smaller domain. Retain the original form or supply the missing values separately to represent the full original function. | Rationalize a binomial denominator using a suitable conjugate. Identify and account for valid original inputs excluded by a variable conjugate. | Choose the conjugate and apply the difference-of-squares identity correctly. Identify where the multiplier is zero. State the common domain of equality and recover any otherwise lost original values with the original formula or separate cases. |
