# Lesson 9.2: Rational exponents and their laws

Interpret rational powers through roots and reciprocals, and apply exponent laws with explicit real-domain conditions.

## Learning objectives

- Translate and evaluate rational powers using reduced fractional exponents and the stated real-number convention.
- Simplify with valid exponent laws while preserving the original domain and sign.

## Learning outcome

Evaluate and simplify rational powers, explaining when a root interpretation or exponent-law step fails.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reduce a fractional exponent before determining the permitted bases and translating it into a root, power, or reciprocal.
- Apply even-root, odd-root, zero-base, and negative-exponent restrictions.
- Use valid product, quotient, and power laws with correct exponent arithmetic.
- Check zero or negative bases separately and reject rewrites that change a value or its original domain.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rational exponents as roots** | Write $m/n$ in lowest terms with integers $m,n$ and $n>0$. For $a>0$, $a^{m/n}=(\sqrt[n]{a})^m$. For $a<0$, this convention permits real values only when $n$ is odd. Negative exponents require nonzero bases and reciprocals. Zero raised to a positive exponent is zero; $0^0$ and negative powers of zero are undefined. Nonzero bases raised to zero give $1$. An unreduced radical rewriting may change the value or domain, even though the rational number itself is unchanged. | Convert between radical and rational-exponent notation and evaluate values using the reduced-exponent convention. Determine real-domain restrictions for positive, zero, and negative bases, and identify invalid radical rewritings. | Reduce the exponent before deciding whether a negative base is permitted. Translate root index, power, and reciprocal correctly. Apply zero-base and nonzero-denominator conditions. Reject an unreduced radical rewriting that changes the original value or domain. |
| **Exponent laws with stated hypotheses** | For $a,b>0$ and rational $r,s$, $a^ra^s=a^{r+s}$, $a^r/a^s=a^{r-s}$, $(a^r)^s=a^{rs}$, $(ab)^r=a^rb^r$, and $(a/b)^r=a^r/b^r$. Zero and negative bases require separate checks of roots, reciprocals, and signs; the power-of-a-power rule is not universal over real bases. In particular, $(x^2)^{1/2}=\lvert x\rvert$. Simplification retains the original domain. | Simplify rational-exponent expressions using exponent laws with valid base conditions. Identify and correct exponent-law steps that change a real value, sign, or original domain. | State the conditions for each identity used. Apply exponent arithmetic appropriate to multiplication, division, and powers. Check zero and negative bases separately, retaining absolute values and original exclusions when required. |
