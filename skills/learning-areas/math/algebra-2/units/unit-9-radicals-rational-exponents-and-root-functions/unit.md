# Unit 9: Radicals, rational exponents, and root functions

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

This unit connects radical notation, rational exponents, root graphs, and equations over the real numbers. The organizing principle is that algebraic transformations must preserve the intended root and its domain. Students distinguish principal values from equation solutions, apply exponent rules with stated hypotheses, and use verification to detect missing signs or extraneous candidates. Square-root and cube-root functions provide graphical and modeling connections to the same ideas.

## Prerequisites

Integer exponent laws, polynomial factoring, absolute value, numerical square-root simplification, linear and quadratic equation solving, function transformations, and domain/range notation. Students should be able to distinguish an expression’s value from an equation’s solution set.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret real roots and rational powers with correct signs and domain conditions. See [Lesson 9.1](lesson-1-root-definitions-and-principal-values/lesson.md), [Lesson 9.2](lesson-2-rational-exponents-and-their-laws/lesson.md).
- Simplify radical arithmetic and justify rationalization without changing the original domain. See [Lesson 9.3](lesson-3-simplification-and-radical-arithmetic/lesson.md), [Lesson 9.4](lesson-4-division-and-rationalization/lesson.md).
- Graph transformed square-root and cube-root functions and identify their key features. See [Lesson 9.5](lesson-5-square-root-and-cube-root-functions/lesson.md).
- Solve radical and rational-power equations with complete candidate checks. See [Lesson 9.6](lesson-6-radical-equations/lesson.md), [Lesson 9.7](lesson-7-rational-power-equations-and-root-formulas/lesson.md).
- Construct and verify square-root models from endpoint and table information. See [Lesson 9.7](lesson-7-rational-power-equations-and-root-formulas/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Root definitions lead to absolute-value extraction and rational-exponent conventions. Arithmetic then develops perfect-power simplification, like radicals, distribution, quotient rules, and conjugates. Function lessons compare even-root endpoints with odd-root symmetry. Equation lessons distinguish squaring from cubing, preserve sign branches in rational-power equations, and connect root formulas to data. Negative radicands of even index do not receive real values in this unit; complex-number treatment belongs elsewhere in the course.

## Lessons

- [9.1: Root definitions and principal values](lesson-1-root-definitions-and-principal-values/lesson.md) — Distinguish principal roots from all equation solutions and extract powers using necessary absolute values.
- [9.2: Rational exponents and their laws](lesson-2-rational-exponents-and-their-laws/lesson.md) — Translate rational powers into roots and apply exponent laws only under justified conditions.
- [9.3: Simplification and radical arithmetic](lesson-3-simplification-and-radical-arithmetic/lesson.md) — Simplify radicals, combine like terms, and distribute products without inventing a sum rule.
- [9.4: Division and rationalization](lesson-4-division-and-rationalization/lesson.md) — Rationalize denominators while detecting inherited restrictions and any artificial exclusion from a conjugate.
- [9.5: Square-root and cube-root functions](lesson-5-square-root-and-cube-root-functions/lesson.md) — Map root graphs and derive endpoints, centers, domains, ranges, and symmetry.
- [9.6: Radical equations](lesson-6-radical-equations/lesson.md) — Solve single- and multiple-radical equations and explain the reversibility of each power operation.
- [9.7: Rational-power equations and root formulas](lesson-7-rational-power-equations-and-root-formulas/lesson.md) — Preserve all rational-power solutions and build root models checked against tables and domains.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards supply rational-exponent, radical-equation, and root-graph requirements, while Texas standards explicitly include square-root transformations and equations, cube-root equations, rational-power equations, and root models from tables. Common Core Appendix A connects these topics within Algebra II. Each concept retains its objective-specific standards and assessment rubric; proficiency criteria describe the reasoning that those tasks should reveal.
