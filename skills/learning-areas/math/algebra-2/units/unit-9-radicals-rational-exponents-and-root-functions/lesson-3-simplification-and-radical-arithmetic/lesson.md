# Lesson 9.3: Simplification and radical arithmetic

[Unit 9: Radicals, rational exponents, and root functions](../unit.md) · [Algebra 2](../../../curriculum.md)

Perfect powers simplify radicals and reveal which terms can be combined. Addition uses like radicals, while multiplication uses distribution and valid product identities.

## Learning objectives

- Extract perfect powers from numerical and variable radicals.
- Add, subtract, and multiply radical expressions under appropriate domain conditions.

## Learning outcome

The student can simplify radical arithmetic by identifying the relevant structure and explaining why each identity applies.

## Proficiency criteria

To demonstrate proficiency, the student must independently satisfy all of the following criteria.

- Factor a radicand into usable perfect powers and a remaining factor.
- Preserve the real-domain and absolute-value conditions when splitting or extracting factors.
- Simplify before identifying like radicals and combine only their coefficients.
- Distribute radical products completely and distinguish product identities from invalid sum-of-roots rules.

## Concepts

### Concept U09-L03-C01: Extracting perfect powers

**Content**

- Product property with real-domain conditions
- Variable radicals and retained restrictions

**Learning objectives**

- **U09-L03-C01-O1:** Simplify numerical and variable radicals by extracting perfect powers.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-RN.A.2 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(G) (direct).
- **U09-L03-C01-O2:** State domain conditions under which a product of radicals is equivalent to a single radical.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-RN.A.2 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(G) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Identify extractable perfect powers without changing the original radicand.
- Use absolute values for even-root extraction unless a sign is already established.
- Check that each factor has a permitted real root before splitting an even-root product, and retain the original domain.

**Explanation**

For even roots, $\sqrt[n]{ab}=\sqrt[n]{a}\sqrt[n]{b}$ is valid when $a,b\ge0$; splitting a nonnegative product into two negative radicands is not valid in real arithmetic. Odd-root products allow all real factors. Factor out perfect powers and use absolute values for even-root extraction.

**Worked examples**

$\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2$. For real $x$, $\sqrt{18x^4}=3x^2\sqrt2$ because $x^2\ge0$. For $\sqrt{12x^3}$, the original domain is $x\ge0$; hence $\sqrt{4x^2\cdot3x}=2x\sqrt{3x}$ on that domain.

**Exercises**

1. Simplify $\sqrt{50x^2}$. **Answer:** $5|x|\sqrt2$ for all real $x$.
2. Simplify $\sqrt[3]{54x^4}$. **Answer:** $3x\sqrt[3]{2x}$ for all real $x$.

**Assessment**

Simplify $\sqrt{48x^2}$ for all real $x$ and explain why $\sqrt{(-2)(-8)}$ cannot be split into two real square roots.

- **U09-L03-C01-O1 — 2 points:** $4|x|\sqrt3$, from extracting $16x^2$.
- **U09-L03-C01-O2 — 2 points:** The original numerical radical equals 4, but $\sqrt{-2}$ and $\sqrt{-8}$ are not real, so the real product rule does not apply to those factors.

### Concept U09-L03-C02: Adding, subtracting, and multiplying radicals

**Content**

- Like radicals
- Distributive multiplication and simplification

**Learning objectives**

- **U09-L03-C02-O1:** Combine like radical terms after simplifying.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-RN.A.2 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(G) (direct).
- **U09-L03-C02-O2:** Multiply radical expressions by distribution and simplify products.
  - Standards: CCSS HSN-RN.A.2 (direct); TEKS 2A.7(G) (direct).

**Proficiency criteria**

- Recognize like radical terms by matching both index and simplified radicand.
- Combine coefficients only for like terms and leave unlike radicals distinct.
- Show all distributed products and cross terms, using valid radical product rules rather than splitting a sum under a root.

**Explanation**

Like radicals have the same index and the same simplified radicand; add their coefficients. Different radicands usually do not combine. Multiplication uses distribution and the product property under valid domain conditions. In particular, $\sqrt{a+b}$ does not generally equal $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$.

**Worked examples**

$2\sqrt{12}-\sqrt{27}=4\sqrt3-3\sqrt3=\sqrt3$. Also $(\sqrt3+2)(\sqrt3-5)=3-5\sqrt3+2\sqrt3-10=-7-3\sqrt3$. By contrast, $\sqrt{9+16}=5$ while $\sqrt9+\sqrt{16}=7$.

**Exercises**

1. Simplify $\sqrt{20}+3\sqrt5-\sqrt{45}$. **Answer:** $2\sqrt5+3\sqrt5-3\sqrt5=2\sqrt5$.
2. Expand $(\sqrt2+3)^2$. **Answer:** $11+6\sqrt2$, including the middle term.

**Assessment**

Simplify $\sqrt{75}-\sqrt{12}$ and expand $(\sqrt5+1)(\sqrt5-2)$.

- **U09-L03-C02-O1 — 2 points:** $5\sqrt3-2\sqrt3=3\sqrt3$, combining coefficients of like radicals.
- **U09-L03-C02-O2 — 2 points:** $5-2\sqrt5+\sqrt5-2=3-\sqrt5$ by distribution.
