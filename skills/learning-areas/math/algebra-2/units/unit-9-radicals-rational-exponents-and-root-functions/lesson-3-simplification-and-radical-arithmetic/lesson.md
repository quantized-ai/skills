# Lesson 9.3: Simplification and radical arithmetic

Simplify radicals by extracting perfect powers, combine like radical terms, and multiply radical expressions under valid real-domain conditions.

## Learning objectives

- Extract perfect powers from numerical and variable radicals while preserving signs and domains.
- Add, subtract, and multiply radical expressions using like terms, distribution, and valid root identities.

## Learning outcome

Simplify radical arithmetic and justify each operation without changing its real domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Factor radicands into useful perfect powers and remaining factors.
- Preserve the original domain and required absolute values when splitting or extracting factors.
- Simplify before combining like radicals; combine only their coefficients.
- Include every distributed product and cross term, and distinguish product identities from invalid sum-of-roots rules.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Extracting perfect powers** | For integer $n\ge2$, when $n$ is even, $\sqrt[n]{ab}=\sqrt[n]{a}\sqrt[n]{b}$ requires $a,b\ge0$; for odd $n$, real factors of either sign are permitted. Extracting a perfect nth-power factor from an even-index root produces the absolute value of the extracted factor unless its sign is established; odd-root extraction retains the signed factor. Simplify using perfect-power factors while retaining the original domain. A factorization that creates an undefined separate root cannot represent the original expression at that input, even if another factor is zero. | Simplify numerical and variable radicals by extracting perfect powers with correct absolute values. Determine when splitting or combining radical products preserves real values and the full original domain. | Identify perfect-power factors without changing the radicand. Use absolute values for even-root extraction unless a sign condition removes them. Check that all separate roots exist at each original input; reject or qualify a rewrite that loses permitted inputs. |
| **Adding, subtracting, and multiplying radicals** | After simplification, like radical terms have the same index and radicand and combine by adding or subtracting coefficients. Unlike radical parts remain distinct. Multiplication uses distribution, including all cross terms, followed by valid root product rules and simplification. A root does not distribute over addition or subtraction; product identities remain subject to their real-domain conditions. | Combine like radical terms after simplification and distinguish valid combination from invalid root-of-a-sum rules. Multiply radical expressions by distribution and simplify products under valid real-domain conditions. | Match both index and simplified radicand before combining coefficients. Leave unlike terms distinct. Include all distributed products and cross terms. Apply radical product rules only on their valid domains, and reject splitting a sum under a root. |
