# Unit 11: Logarithms

[Algebra 2](../../curriculum.md)

Logarithms reverse exponential functions and make unknown exponents accessible. This unit develops the definition before moving to graphs, expression properties, change of base, equations, and duration models. Real-domain conditions connect every stage: a logarithm’s argument must be positive even when its output is negative, and equivalent-looking transformations may agree on only part of their natural domains.

## Prerequisites

Exponential functions and their one-to-one behavior, growth and decay, exponent laws for positive bases, function transformations, linear and quadratic equations, and domain restrictions. Students should be able to interpret a multiplicative factor with its time unit and distinguish exact values from approximations.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret and evaluate logarithms through the inverse exponential definition. See [Lesson 11.1](lesson-1-definition-and-basic-evaluation/lesson.md).
- Construct parent and transformed logarithmic graphs with justified features. See [Lesson 11.2](lesson-2-logarithmic-graphs/lesson.md).
- Apply logarithm properties and change of base while preserving domains. See [Lesson 11.3](lesson-3-logarithm-properties-with-domains/lesson.md), [Lesson 11.4](lesson-4-change-of-base-and-inverse-identities/lesson.md).
- Solve exponential and logarithmic equations and validate original arguments. See [Lesson 11.5](lesson-5-exponential-and-logarithmic-equations/lesson.md).
- Interpret logarithmic duration solutions, doubling times, half-lives, and threshold observations. See [Lesson 11.6](lesson-6-duration-and-reasonableness/lesson.md).

## Concepts and topics

Base-two, common, and natural logarithms support exact and numerical evaluation. Graphs connect coordinate reversal to vertical asymptotes and positive-argument domains. Product, quotient, and power rules are developed with domain comparisons and absolute-value corrections. Change of base and inverse identities lead into equations. Duration modeling then separates continuous equality times from the first permitted discrete observation and checks whether a solution belongs to the model’s future domain.

## Lessons

- [11.1: Definition and basic evaluation](lesson-1-definition-and-basic-evaluation/lesson.md) — Define logarithms as exponents, enforce valid bases and arguments, and check exact or approximate values.

## Standards and assessment

Common Core mathematics standards mark the general exponential-logarithmic inverse relationship with (+), while logarithmic graphing and exponential-model solving also appear in non-(+) standards. Texas standards directly include logarithmic conversion, equations, reasonableness, and related graph transformations. Common Core Appendix A connects the properties of logarithms with exponent properties. Concept mappings identify supporting tools separately from directly specified content, and the original assessment rubrics remain attached to their objective evidence.
