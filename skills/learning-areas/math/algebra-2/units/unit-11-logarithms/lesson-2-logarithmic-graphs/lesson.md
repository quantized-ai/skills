# Lesson 11.2: Logarithmic graphs

Logarithmic graphs reflect exponential input-output pairs across the diagonal. Their transformed domains and asymptotes come from positive arguments rather than from memorized shifts alone.

## Learning objectives

- Construct logarithmic parent graphs by reflecting exponential graphs and describe their features and end behavior.
- Graph transformations of logarithms using mapped points and determine domains, ranges, asymptotes, intercepts, and end behavior.

## Learning outcome

The student can explain a logarithmic graph through inverse relationships, transformations, and its positive-argument condition.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reverse exponential input-output pairs to obtain logarithmic points and exchange domain and range.
- Identify parent intercepts, asymptote, monotonicity, and end behavior for bases above or below one.
- Map points under horizontal and vertical transformations; derive the domain from the full argument inequality.
- Determine permitted intercepts and the direction of unbounded behavior on the allowed side of the vertical asymptote.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parent logarithms and reflection** | For $b>0$, $b\ne1$, reflecting $y=b^x$ across $y=x$ gives $y=\log_bx$. Domain $(0,\infty)$ and range $\mathbb R$ exchange those of the exponential. The logarithm has intercept $(1,0)$, no $y$-intercept, and vertical asymptote $x=0$. For $b>1$, it increases from $-\infty$ as $x\to0^+$ to $+\infty$ as $x\to\infty$; for $0<b<1$, it decreases with the opposite end behavior. | Graph a logarithmic parent by reversing exponential coordinates and identify its domain, range, intercepts, and asymptote. Describe logarithmic monotonicity and end behavior for bases above and below one. | Reverse coordinate pairs and exchange domain and range consistently. Identify the parent intercepts and vertical asymptote. State monotonicity and both end directions for each valid base interval. |
| **Transformations of logarithmic graphs** | For $g(x)=a\log_b(s(x-h))+k$, with $a\ne0$, $s\ne0$, $b>0$, and $b\ne1$, parent points $(u,v)$ map to $(h+u/s,av+k)$. The domain satisfies $s(x-h)>0$, the range is $\mathbb R$, and the vertical asymptote is $x=h$. The sign of $s$ selects the domain side. As $x$ approaches $h$ within that domain, the parent logarithm tends to $-\infty$ for $b>1$ and $+\infty$ for $0<b<1$; a negative $a$ reverses these output directions. Intercepts must satisfy the original domain. These parameters express horizontal shifts/scales/reflections and vertical stretches/reflections/shifts. | Graph shifted, reflected, and scaled logarithmic functions using mapped parent points. Determine transformed domains, ranges, asymptotes, intercepts, and end behavior from the complete argument and transformation parameters. | Map coordinates according to every specified transformation. Solve the complete positive-argument inequality, including reflected arguments. Determine the range, asymptote, and all existing intercepts within the domain. Explain the allowed approach side and output direction at the asymptote. |
