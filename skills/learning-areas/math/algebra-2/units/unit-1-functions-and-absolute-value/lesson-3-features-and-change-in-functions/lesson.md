# Lesson 1.3: Features and change in functions

Function features describe where outputs lie and how they change. Average rate of change summarizes endpoint change and must be interpreted separately from behavior inside an interval.

## Learning objectives

- Describe zeros, intercepts, sign, monotonicity, and local or absolute extrema in context.
- Compute and interpret average rate of change, including its units and limitations.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to give a consistent account of a function’s key features and its net change over an interval.

## Proficiency criteria

- Report zeros as inputs and intercepts as points; distinguish positive output intervals from increasing intervals.
- Identify increasing and decreasing intervals and distinguish local extrema from extrema over the entire stated domain.
- Compute or estimate a secant slope using corresponding endpoint changes in a consistent order.
- Interpret the sign and output-per-input units of an average rate without claiming that it determines behavior at every interior point.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intercepts, intervals, and extrema** | Zeros are inputs satisfying $f(x)=0$; intercepts are graph points. Output sign differs from monotonic behavior. Increasing means strictly larger outputs at larger inputs, decreasing means strictly smaller outputs, and constant means equal outputs. Local extrema compare nearby allowed outputs; absolute extrema compare all outputs in the domain. Endpoint extrema are identified as one-sided. Monotonic intervals may be reported open at turning points; endpoint-inclusive descriptions are valid when the same comparison property holds. | Identify zeros, intercepts, sign intervals, and increasing, decreasing, or constant intervals from a graph or description. Interpret local and absolute extrema, including domain endpoints, and function intervals in context. | Distinguish input zeros from intercept points and output sign from direction of change. State complete intervals with valid boundary membership. Compare nearby versus all allowed outputs and identify endpoint extrema as one-sided. Give an extremum’s input location and output value with their contextual units. |
| **Average rate of change** | Average rate of change between distinct allowed inputs $a$ and $b$ is $\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$, the secant-line slope. It can be computed from a formula or table and estimated from a graph. Units are output units per input unit. Its sign describes net output change as input increases, not behavior at every interior point; zero rate means equal endpoint outputs, not a constant function. | Compute or estimate average rate of change from a formula, table, or graph. Interpret the sign, units, and limitations of average rate of change. | Use distinct endpoints and corresponding differences in a consistent order. Compute exactly from exact data or justify graph-reading precision. State output-per-input units and interpret net change. Explain why an average rate does not establish constant or monotone interior behavior. |
