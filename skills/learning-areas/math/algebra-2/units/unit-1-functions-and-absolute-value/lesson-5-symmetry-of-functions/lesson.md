# Lesson 1.5: Symmetry of functions

Even and odd functions express symmetry through identities. The symmetry of the domain is part of the test, and the available representation determines how strong the evidence can be.

## Learning objectives

- Classify functions as even, odd, both, or neither using the domain and function identities.
- Connect algebraic classifications to graph and table symmetry and judge the completeness of the evidence.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to justify an even, odd, both, or neither classification with evidence valid over the stated domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Check that the domain contains the negative of each allowed input before applying an even or odd identity.
- Compare the substituted rule with the original rule and its negative, preserving signs and grouping.
- Relate evenness to reflection across the vertical axis and oddness to symmetry about the origin, including the possibility of both.
- Distinguish a proof over the domain from a pattern observed in only a few points of a larger unspecified function.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Even and odd functions** | Evenness and oddness require a domain symmetric under $x\mapsto-x$. An even function satisfies $f(-x)=f(x)$ throughout its domain and has vertical-axis symmetry; an odd function satisfies $f(-x)=-f(x)$ and has origin symmetry. A function can be neither; the zero function on a symmetric domain is both. A complete finite table can establish symmetry on its listed domain, but sampled points cannot establish an identity on a larger domain. | Test and justify even, odd, both, or neither classifications algebraically, including domain symmetry. Connect even and odd identities to graph and table symmetry, distinguishing complete-domain evidence from samples. | Check domain symmetry before testing either identity. Substitute the full negated input with valid signs and grouping. Justify the classification and corresponding graph symmetry. Limit table-based conclusions to the represented domain unless a rule establishes the identity elsewhere. |
