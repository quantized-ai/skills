# Lesson 16.2: Equations and constraints

Equations describe quantitative relationships, while simultaneous inequalities describe feasible choices. Solving either requires the model’s units, integer conditions, and contextual limits to remain attached.

## Learning objectives

- Create, graph, and solve equations from quantitative descriptions.
- Represent simultaneous constraints with equations or inequalities.
- Evaluate whether candidate values are feasible in the original context.

## Learning outcome

The student can translate a relationship and its constraints into a model and identify its viable solutions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define unknowns, select equations matching the described relationships, and graph two-variable models with labeled axes and scales.
- Preserve original restrictions when solving and verify candidate values in the stated situation.
- Translate every constraint with its correct boundary condition and include integer or nonnegative domains where required.
- Distinguish an algebraic solution from a feasible solution that satisfies all simultaneous requirements.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equations from quantitative relationships** | Quantitative descriptions determine variables, units, domains, and linear, quadratic, rational, radical, or exponential equations. One-variable equations identify candidate values; two-variable equations describe relationships that can be graphed with labeled axes and scales. Solving must preserve original algebraic restrictions; contextual restrictions determine which candidates represent viable quantities. | Create equations in one or more defined variables from quantitative descriptions and graph two-variable relationships with labeled axes and scales. Solve a constructed model, verify candidates, and interpret solutions using consistent units and a meaningful domain. | Define quantities and translate their relationships into an appropriate equation family. Represent a two-variable relationship on axes labeled with quantities, units, and scales. Solve with original restrictions intact; check candidates and reject contextually invalid values. |
| **Feasible sets and inequality constraints** | A feasible set contains exactly the values satisfying every equation, inequality, and domain restriction simultaneously. “At least” and “at most” include equality; strict limits exclude their boundaries. Nonnegativity and integrality apply when required by the quantities. A continuous graphical region can contain points that are infeasible for integer-valued quantities. | Represent simultaneous contextual constraints by equations, inequalities, and required nonnegative or integer domains. Test candidate solutions against every constraint and distinguish algebraic solutions from contextually feasible solutions. | Translate all conditions with correct inequality directions and strict or inclusive boundaries. Include nonnegative and integer restrictions when the quantities require them. Check every condition for each candidate and justify its feasibility or identify a violated condition. |
