# Lesson 16.8: Prediction and model revision

Predictions depend on where data were observed and whether a model’s assumptions remain credible. Competing models are compared with new evidence and revised through a documented modeling process.

## Learning objectives

- Calculate predictions and distinguish interpolation from extrapolation.
- Evaluate prediction reliability and reporting precision using data coverage, residual variation, and model assumptions.
- Compare, revise, and communicate models with explicit limitations.

## Learning outcome

The student can make a qualified model-based prediction and defend a documented selection or revision.

## Proficiency criteria

- Locate each prediction relative to the observed domain and check contextual feasibility.
- Explain prediction assumptions and uncertainty; report estimates with justified precision and identify unsupported extrapolations.
- Compare candidate models using common observations, validation evidence, and mechanism rather than flexibility alone.
- Report a chosen equation together with its variables, method, usable domain, supporting evidence, and remaining limitation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Interpolation, extrapolation, and uncertainty** | Interpolation uses inputs within the observed input range; extrapolation uses inputs outside it. Both produce estimates and remain subject to mathematical and contextual domains. Reliability depends on data coverage, residual variation, plausible mechanisms, model choice, and extension beyond observed inputs. Residuals describe observed fitting errors, not guaranteed future-error bounds. Numerically detailed predictions do not justify matching reporting precision, and infeasible outputs signal a limitation of the model. | Calculate model predictions, check their contextual feasibility, and classify them as interpolation or extrapolation relative to observed inputs. Critique prediction reliability and reporting precision using data coverage, model assumptions, residual variation, and sensitivity to model choice. | Calculate predictions from the fitted equation, check permitted inputs and outputs, and locate them relative to the observed range. State the assumptions needed for extrapolation and identify limits arising from data coverage, residual variation, or competing models. Report predictions as estimates with justified precision; do not treat residual size or interpolation as a guarantee of accuracy. |
| **Model comparison and documented revision** | Model comparison uses common fitting observations and response scales, plausible mechanisms, domain constraints, and additional validation observations when available. Validation data are separated from coefficient fitting when possible. Greater flexibility or exact interpolation alone does not establish better prediction. Revision may change coefficients, the family, or the usable domain. A model report records variables, units, equation, fitting method, assumptions, evidence, domain, and remaining limitations; conclusions remain provisional. | Compare competing models using common-scale data fit, contextual mechanisms, domain constraints, and additional validation observations when available. Communicate a justified selection or revision with variables, units, equation, fitting method, assumptions, evidence, usable domain, and limitations. | Compare candidates on shared fitting data and scale and check predictions against separate observations when available. Use fit, mechanism, domain, and validation evidence to justify a provisional choice or a revision to coefficients, family, or domain. Document the selected model and its assumptions, supporting evidence, usable domain, and a specific remaining limitation. |
