# Lesson 17.1: Data distributions and summaries

Distribution shape determines which summaries are informative. The lesson reviews center and spread while distinguishing sample estimates, complete-population quantities, and the units of standard deviation.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish data types, describe quantitative distributions, and justify suitable summaries.
- Calculate and interpret mean and standard deviation using the stated sample or population convention.
- Compare center and spread and explain their response to shifting or scaling observations.

## Learning outcome

The student can summarize a dataset with justified measures and interpret center and variability separately.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose a display and summaries appropriate to the variable type; describe shape, center, and unusual values.
- Calculate mean and median and justify a typical-value summary using skewness and sensitivity to extremes.
- Calculate standard deviation using the stated population or sample denominator and report it in the original units.
- Compare spread separately from center and determine the effects of shifting or scaling every observation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shape, center, and appropriate summaries** | Quantitative distributions are described by shape, center, spread, and unusual values using dot plots or histograms. The mean uses every value and is sensitive to extremes; the median is resistant. Mean and standard deviation often suit roughly symmetric distributions without strong outliers; median and interquartile range often better describe skewed distributions. Categorical variables are summarized by category counts or proportions, not averages of category labels. | Distinguish quantitative and categorical variables, choose an appropriate display, and describe a quantitative distribution’s shape, center, and unusual values. Calculate and compare mean and median and justify a typical-value summary appropriate to the distribution. | Match displays and numerical summaries to the variable type. Describe shape, center, and unusual values from a quantitative display. Calculate mean and median and explain how skewness or extreme values affect their interpretation and selection. |
| **Mean, standard deviation, and units** | For a complete finite population, $\mu=\sum x_i/N$ and $\sigma=\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\mu)^2/N}$. For a sample estimating population spread, $\bar x=\sum x_i/n$ and $s=\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2/(n-1)}$, with $n>1$. Standard deviation is nonnegative, is zero exactly when all values agree, and has the observations’ units. Under $y=cx+a$, the mean becomes $c\bar x+a$ and standard deviation becomes $\lvert c\rvert s$; the population formulas transform likewise. | Calculate mean and standard deviation using the stated population or sample convention and explain the denominator choice. Interpret standard deviation in the original units, compare spread independently of center, and determine the effects of shifting or scaling observations. | Identify the intended population or sample convention and use its denominator. Calculate squared deviations, divide by the stated denominator, and take the square root with the correct units. Distinguish equal means from equal spreads and apply the shift and scale rules, including negative and zero scale factors. |
