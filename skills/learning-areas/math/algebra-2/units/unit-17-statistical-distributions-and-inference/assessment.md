[Unit overview](unit.md)

## Unit cumulative assessment

Six independent tasks, 10 points each, 60 total. Give enough details for another learner to reproduce every proposed simulation.

1. A bell-shaped measurement distribution has mean 60 and SD 8. Estimate the percentage between 52 and 76 using the supplied normal-table values; assess applying the same method to a strongly skewed distribution with those summaries. **Answer:** $z=-1,2$, area $0.9772-0.1587=0.8185$ or 81.85%; the skewed shape does not justify the normal fit. **Scoring:** 3 standardization, 3 area/percentage, 4 appropriateness explanation. **Objectives:** U17-L02-C01-O1; U17-L02-C02-O1–O2.
2. A voluntary website poll of 2000 respondents reports 65% agreement and claims to represent the entire country. Identify population/statistic, two design concerns, and a repair. Explain whether a larger opt-in sample fixes the problem. **Answer:** target parameter is national agreement proportion; statistic is 0.65 among respondents; voluntary response/undercoverage (and possible nonresponse), repair with an appropriate probability sampling frame and neutral questions/follow-up; size alone does not remove bias. **Scoring:** 2 parameter/statistic, 4 specific concerns, 2 repair, 2 sample-size reasoning. **Objectives:** U17-L03-C01-O1–O2; U17-L03-C03-O1–O2; U17-L08-C01-O1.
3. A random sample mean is 35 minutes. A representative empirical-population simulation with the same sample size gives a 95th percentile absolute mean error of 2.2 minutes. Explain the simulation’s construction, give the interval, and explain what it does not mean. **Answer:** resample same-size groups with replacement under a large-population approximation, compute each mean’s absolute deviation from 35, sort, take the 95th percentile; interval $[32.8,37.2]$; it concerns population mean uncertainty, not a claim that 95% of individuals fall there, and it does not remove bias. **Scoring:** 4 reproducible simulation, 2 interval, 4 interpretation/limitations. **Objectives:** U17-L05-C01-O1–O2; U17-L05-C02-O1–O2.
4. A random survey has 180 successes among 300 people. Specify a plug-in simulation for a proportion margin; suppose its 95th absolute-error percentile is $17/300\approx0.05667$. Report the interval to three decimal places and distinguish it from a simulation testing the separate claim $p=0.5$. **Answer:** estimate 0.60; simulate 300 trials with probability 0.60 for estimation, giving $[0.60-17/300,0.60+17/300]\approx[0.543,0.657]$; a test of the specified 0.5 model simulates with 0.5 and compares a preselected statistic to the observed result. **Scoring:** 3 estimation simulation, 2 interval, 3 correct null-model distinction, 2 limitations. **Objectives:** U17-L06-C01-O1; U17-L06-C02-O1–O2; U17-L04-C02-O1–O2.
5. Volunteers are randomly assigned two treatments. Their observed mean difference is 6 units; 24 of 2000 valid no-effect shuffles produce an absolute difference at least 6. Describe the shuffling, interpret the rate, and critique “this proves the treatment helps everyone in the population.” **Answer:** hold outcomes fixed and reshuffle labels preserving group sizes/design; rate 0.012 supports an effect under the null/design assumptions; not proof, not necessarily practically important, not necessarily every individual, and volunteers do not automatically support population generalization. **Scoring:** 3 randomization mechanism, 2 calculation, 2 evidence interpretation, 3 critique. **Objectives:** U17-L07-C01-O1–O2; U17-L07-C02-O1–O2; U17-L08-C01-O1–O2.
6. (a) Choose one of three people fairly with two coin flips. (b) In a batch, a test creates 30 true positives, 20 false positives, and 5 false negatives. Calculate the proportion of positives that are true and a modeled cost when each missed case costs 10 units and each unnecessary inspection costs 2. **Answer:** (a) HH/HT/TH assigned separately, reroll TT, each conditional chance $1/3$; (b) $30/50=60\%$; cost $5(10)+20(2)=90$. **Scoring:** 3 fair rule, 2 probability proof, 2 conditional probability, 3 cost calculation and stated assumptions. **Objectives:** U17-L09-C01-O1–O2; U17-L09-C02-O1–O2.

**Teaching boundary and interpretation check.** The simulated margins above are intentionally model-based approximations. Teach their dependence on the sampling design, representativeness, sample size, and estimated model. A bootstrap-style estimate at an all-success/all-failure sample can collapse spuriously; such cases call for a different method in a later statistics course, not a claim of certainty. The core standard is met by constructing and interpreting simulation-based uncertainty, not by memorizing an unqualified analytic formula.
