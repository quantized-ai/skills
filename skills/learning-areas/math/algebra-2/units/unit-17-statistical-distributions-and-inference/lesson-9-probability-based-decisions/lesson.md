# Lesson 17.9: Probability-based decisions

Probability supports fair allocations and decisions involving imperfect information. Equal-chance rules, conditional denominators, base rates, and explicit error costs determine whether a proposed decision procedure serves its stated objective.

## Learning objectives

- Design, verify, and repair random allocation rules using a stated fairness criterion.
- Analyze two-way test outcomes using conditional probabilities, base rates, and both error types.
- Compare probability-based decisions using explicit objectives, error costs, and assumptions.

## Learning outcome

The student can justify a random-allocation rule or probability-based decision using transparent probabilities and assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- State the allocation objective and calculate participant probabilities, including rejection and repetition rules.
- Construct a consistent two-way outcome table and use the conditional group’s total as denominator.
- Explain how base rates and false-positive and false-negative rates affect test interpretation.
- Calculate and compare probability-weighted consequences under stated costs and explain how changed assumptions can alter the choice.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fair random allocation** | When fairness means equal selection chances, every eligible participant must have the same resulting probability. Randomness alone does not ensure equality: probabilities depend on how generator outcomes map to participants. Rejection methods discard designated outcomes and repeat the same random rule; final participant probabilities account for that repetition. The eligible set, mapping, and rejection policy are specified before outcomes are observed and must yield eventual selection with probability one. Unequal weights represent a different allocation objective and require an explicit justification. | Design a complete random selection or allocation mechanism meeting a stated fairness criterion, including any rejection and repetition rule. Calculate resulting participant probabilities, verify equality when required, and diagnose and repair unfair mappings. | State the eligible set, fairness criterion, generator mapping, and repetition policy. Calculate eventual selection probabilities including rejected outcomes and verify that the procedure selects with probability one. Identify unequal mappings and justify a revision achieving the stated allocation goal. |
| **Conditional probabilities and decision tradeoffs** | A two-way table crosses true condition present or absent with test outcome positive or negative. False positives are positive outcomes when the condition is absent; false negatives are negative outcomes when it is present. Conditional probabilities use a nonempty conditioning group’s total as denominator. The probability of a positive result given the condition and the probability of the condition given a positive result condition on different events and need not be equal. Base rates affect these interpretations. Decisions compare probability-weighted consequences under stated error costs and objectives; no universal decision threshold follows from accuracy alone. | Construct a two-way outcome table, calculate the relevant conditional probabilities, and explain the effects of base rates and both error types on a screening or quality-control interpretation. Calculate and compare probability-weighted decision consequences under explicit error costs and assumptions and explain how changing them can alter the preferred choice. | Organize true conditions and test outcomes with consistent error counts and group totals. Use the correct conditional denominator, distinguish reversed conditional probabilities, and explain the role of base rates. Compare expected consequences using stated probabilities and costs and identify how another objective or cost assignment could change the decision. |
