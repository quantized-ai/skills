# Official sources and course scope

[Curriculum](curriculum.md) · [Standards coverage](standards-coverage.md)

## Standards sources

| Document | Role in this curriculum |
|---|---|
| Common Core Appendix A | Defines the traditional Algebra II pathway: polynomial, rational, and radical relationships; trigonometric functions; function modeling; and statistical inference. |
| Texas standards | Defines Algebra II content and mathematical processes, including absolute value, three-variable systems, focus–directrix parabolas, inverse relationships, and regression. This curriculum uses the Algebra II standards adopted in 2012. |
| Common Core mathematics standards | Provides the wording, hierarchy, and additional-mathematics markers for the individual CCSS standards mapped to objectives. |

These documents define the course’s combined scope. A standard’s appearance in the Common Core mathematics standards alone does not place it in the Common Core Appendix A Algebra II pathway. The curriculum represents these editions and does not assert that every jurisdiction uses the same course boundaries.

## Required content and extensions

Trigonometric functions and statistical inference are required by the Common Core Appendix A pathway. Finite geometric series, the binomial theorem, complex polynomial identities, the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, rational arithmetic, and probability-based decisions remain required where that pathway includes them, including standards marked `+`.

Texas standards add or sharpen requirements for three-variable systems solved by substitution, Gaussian elimination, and matrix technology; absolute value equations and inequalities; cubic and root transformations; rational graphs and equations; composition and inverse verification; and technology-based regression. Focus–directrix parabolas include horizontal relations, which need not be functions of the horizontal coordinate.

Function language, real exponent rules, linear systems, and arithmetic/geometric sequences include prerequisite review. These remain assessed unless prior proficiency supports acceleration. Rational inequalities, slant asymptotes, infinite geometric series, and selected complex-number topics are labeled as optional where they appear. A `+` marker is separate from the required/optional decision.

The required course excludes calculus, general matrix algebra, a full conics course, polar complex arithmetic, extensive trigonometric identities and inverse-trigonometric equations, formal normal-theory hypothesis tests, and linear-programming algorithms. Statistical inference uses simulation to interpret uncertainty.

## Mapping conventions

- **CCSS** uses expanded high-school identifiers. A lower-case suffix identifies a substandard; parent wording still applies to its children.
- **TEKS** uses `2A.7(B)` for Algebra II §111.40(c)(7)(B). `A.12(C)` and `A.12(D)` are Algebra I prerequisites. Strand `2A.1` contains mathematical process expectations.
- **Direct** means an objective teaches an explicit component of the cited standard. A multipart standard can require several objectives and assessments.
- **Supporting knowledge** enables a standard without independently satisfying its required action.
- **Prerequisite review** identifies content revisited from earlier study.
- **Extension** identifies work beyond the cited standard’s explicit bounds. This mapping label alone does not determine whether the combined course requires the work.

Each objective retains its individual mapping. The [standards index](standards-coverage.md) links back to that evidence. Purely symbolic work does not substitute for contextual interpretation where the standard requires it. Mathematical processes are documented in the [practice evidence guide](mathematical-practices.md).
