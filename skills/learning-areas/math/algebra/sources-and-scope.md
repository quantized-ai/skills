# Sources and scope

[Unit collection](curriculum.md) · [Standards coverage](standards-coverage.md) · [Reference program](<program/1. common_core_appendix.md>)

## What the collection contains

“Algebra” names this repository’s master collection of secondary mathematics units. Its scope includes foundational number and algebra work, Algebra I and II, Euclidean and coordinate geometry, triangle and function trigonometry, precalculus, probability and statistics, discrete mathematics, financial and applied modeling, quantitative reasoning, and mathematical investigation.

The collection includes the Common Core high-school content standards, including the additional-mathematics standards marked (+), and the mathematical content specified in the supplied Texas high-school snapshot. Foundational topics needed to describe these subjects have explicit lessons; they are not silently removed because an earlier course might teach them. This is not a complete kindergarten-through-eighth-grade curriculum.

Calculus is outside the collection, following the requested boundary. The Appendix names calculus as a possible later course but does not supply its content standards. Elementary limits used to describe function behavior, difference-quotient algebra, and convergence of geometric series remain precalculus content here; derivatives and integrals are excluded.

The Texas Independent Study course specifies mathematical processes and gives illustrative areas of study. Unit 54 defines investigation and communication capabilities; it does not turn every illustrative topic, such as history of mathematics or number theory, into an implied complete survey course. Similarly, “such as” examples guide applications without requiring a separate course on every example. Explicitly required lists retain their stated breadth.

## Units and programs

The authoritative curriculum is the concept table in each `lesson.md`. Its companion `standards.md` maps each objective. A unit groups related lessons and defines the full scope selected when a program references that unit. All listed concepts are required for that selected unit unless explicitly labeled optional in the concept and parent summaries.

A program selects and orders whole units, gives courses their boundaries, and explains its departures from the source pathway. A unit can appear in several courses or programs without duplicating its content. Unit numbers are stable catalog references, not a universal teaching sequence; this adapts the specification’s course-order convention to the requested unordered master collection. Program order carries the sequencing meaning.

A (+) marker means additional mathematics in Common Core, not automatically optional proficiency. An “extension” mapping describes the relationship to a particular standard, not whether the concept is optional in this collection. Some explicit precalculus additions, such as dot products or difference quotients, have no exact content-standard counterpart and are marked accordingly in their companion tables.

## Mapping conventions

- Expanded CCSS identifiers preserve domain, cluster, standard, and substandard, such as HSF-IF.C.7d; (+) is retained where present in the official text. Earlier-grade identifiers remain distinguishable from high-school identifiers.
- Full TEKS section references identify the course and expectation. Retained shorthand `2A` denotes §111.40(c), and `A` denotes §111.39(c); thus `2A.7(B)` means §111.40(c)(7)(B).
- **Direct** addresses an explicit action or component; **supporting knowledge** enables it; **prerequisite review** describes earlier learning when that relationship remains relevant; **extension** exceeds the cited requirement. Partial mappings identify their bounds.
- Multiple objectives can contribute to one standard. An index entry identifies evidence to inspect; an identifier appearing once does not establish that every component has been met or that a learner has attained proficiency.
- The Modeling conceptual category has no invented numbered content code. Mathematical practice and Texas process mappings refer to explicit reasoning, modeling, tool selection, or communication capabilities.

## Relationship to the existing Algebra 2 curriculum

The existing Algebra 2 directory remains intact. Its lesson content provides the starting material for Units 1–17 in this collection. The new copy omits assessment files, scoring instructions, and diagnostic or assessment sections to follow the current specification.

Real quadratic methods have a separate home in Unit 55, focus–directrix parabolas in Unit 56, and two-variable nonlinear systems in Unit 57. Foundational two-variable linear systems and inequalities live in Unit 24; Unit 13 extends equivalent-system reasoning to three variables. Rational inequalities and quotient asymptotes have required precalculus coverage in Unit 42, while complex division and rectangular representations are required in Unit 4. These changes make course selection depend on unit references rather than hidden lesson subsets.
