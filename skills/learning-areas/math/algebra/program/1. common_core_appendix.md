# Common Core Appendix reference program

This program groups the [Algebra master units](../curriculum.md) into clearly delimited courses. It is a locally chosen arrangement based on *Common Core Appendix A*, using the supplied Texas high-school standards for additional course options. It is not a verbatim reproduction of an official syllabus, a pacing plan, or a claim of current state course approval. Calculus is excluded.

## Selection rules and pathway

The traditional reference pathway is **Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II**, followed by one or more of the additional courses below. Trigonometry and Precalculus are available as distinct course selections; the probability, statistics, discrete, modeling, and quantitative courses provide other choices. The collection’s standards coverage includes advanced (+) content that can appear in these later courses.

Each row lists whole units in the chosen course order. Every lesson and concept in a selected unit belongs to that course; a row does not silently narrow a unit to selected lessons. A unit may occur in multiple courses, but its definition exists once in the master collection. A learner or another program may recognize previously completed units without duplicating content. Selecting an elective course makes its listed units part of that course; it does not make every course in this menu compulsory.

The boundaries are deliberately broader than the Appendix’s minimum allocations: Algebra I includes explicit foundational units and some higher-degree factoring, radical equations, finite sums, and nonlinear-intersection work because these are coherent whole units. Algebra II then extends those relationships. Triangle trigonometry and probability remain in Geometry, and unit-circle trigonometry remains in Algebra II; the separate Trigonometry course reuses them and adds advanced trigonometry. Repeated use denotes shared curriculum, not additional copies or conflicting objectives.

Course descriptions and blocks below organize existing unit capabilities only. Detailed content, methods, conditions, and mastery criteria remain in the linked lessons.

This local allocation also defers three Appendix Algebra I components: combinations of standard functions (F-BF.1b) and linear inverse-function formulas (the Algebra I portion of F-BF.4a) are in Unit 12, while empirical exponential fitting (the exponential portion of S-ID.6a) is in Unit 16; both units are selected in Algebra II below. These are declared course-boundary choices, so this Algebra I selection does not claim an exact Appendix allocation or complete Texas Algebra I compliance. Another program can add or move those whole units without changing their definitions.

The topics follow the Appendix’s critical areas, while this local whole-unit order places constructions and three-dimensional measurement after circle geometry. Unit 32 develops circle equations before Unit 33 develops circle theorems; Unit 29 then uses those theorems to justify circle and tangent constructions, and Unit 34 uses sector relationships in composite measurement. Unit 56 supplies the focus–directrix parabola content that the Appendix also places in Geometry. Geometric modeling is a separate reusable unit even though the Appendix distributes its applications across other geometry units. Unit 26 adds the Texas comparison with spherical geometry, and Unit 34 includes the advanced Cavalieri proof of sphere volume that the Appendix reserves for fourth-course content.

## Coverage and reuse

Every master unit appears in at least one course in this reference program. Course membership is defined by the tables above; the master collection remains independent of them. The integrated Mathematics I–II–III pathway described by the Appendix is an alternative program that can be assembled from the same units, not three additional subject-content requirements to duplicate in this one.

Use the collection’s [standards index](../standards-coverage.md) to inspect lesson-level evidence and [source and scope notes](../sources-and-scope.md) to distinguish official content, local extensions, and the preserved snapshot editions. A further program should state its course boundaries, reference the selected units, and describe its own allocation decisions without copying or rewriting the concept objectives.
