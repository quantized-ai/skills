# Lesson 29.4: Triangle circles and exterior tangents

Equidistance centers and right-angle loci support circle and tangent constructions.

## Learning objectives

- Construct the inscribed and circumscribed circles of a triangle and justify their defining contact or incidence conditions.
- Construct and justify tangent lines from a point outside a circle.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the correct center and radius for each triangle circle and locate the perpendicular contact points.
- Justify tangent perpendicularity and distinguish exterior, on-circle, and interior point cases.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Incircle and circumcircle** | The circumcenter is located by two perpendicular bisectors and is equidistant from all vertices; the circumradius reaches a vertex. The incenter is located by two internal angle bisectors, and its perpendicular distance to a side gives the inradius. The third incidence or tangency follows from the concurrence and equidistance theorems. | Construct the inscribed and circumscribed circles of a nondegenerate triangle and justify each construction. | Use perpendicular distance for the inradius, verify tangency to all three sides and passage through all three vertices respectively, and allow an exterior circumcenter. |
| **Tangents from an exterior point** | For center \(O\) and exterior point \(P\), the circle with diameter \(OP\) intersects the given circle at the two tangent points. The angle subtending that diameter is right, so each radius is perpendicular to its tangent. An on-circle point has one tangent; an interior point has none. | Construct the two tangent lines from an exterior point to a circle and justify the construction using the right-angle condition. | Construct the midpoint of the center-to-point segment, identify both intersections, and explain why the resulting lines touch rather than cross the circle. |
