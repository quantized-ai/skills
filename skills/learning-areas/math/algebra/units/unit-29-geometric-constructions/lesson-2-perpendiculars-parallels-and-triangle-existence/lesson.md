# Lesson 29.2: Perpendiculars, parallels, and triangle existence

Basic constructions combine to produce specified lines and test feasible triangle data.

## Learning objectives

- Construct perpendicular and parallel lines through specified points and justify the constructions.
- Use circle intersections to verify triangle inequalities and distinguish zero, one degenerate, or two reflected construction positions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Handle a point on a line and a point outside it and ensure the constructed parallel passes through the required point.
- Interpret circle-intersection outcomes geometrically rather than treating a failed drawing as proof.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Perpendicular and parallel lines** | Perpendicular construction through a point on or off a line uses equal-distance points and a perpendicular bisector. A parallel through an external point can be produced by equal corresponding angles or two perpendicular constructions. Straightedge-and-compass constructions are justified by congruence, perpendicular-bisector properties, and parallel-line criteria. | Construct perpendicular lines through points on and off a given line and a parallel through an external point using compass and straightedge and another exact method. | Make incidence conditions explicit, identify the established theorem guaranteeing perpendicularity or parallelism, and verify the line contains the specified point. |
| **Triangle inequality through construction** | Fix a side of length \(c>0\); circles of radii \(a,b>0\) about its endpoints have two intersections precisely when \(\lvert a-b\rvert<c<a+b\). Their two positions are reflected triangles. Tangency gives a degenerate collinear case, and disjoint or contained nonintersecting circles give no triangle. | Verify the triangle inequality using constructions and use the construction conditions to decide whether given side lengths form a triangle. | Relate circle separation or containment to the strict inequalities and distinguish two vertex positions from two noncongruent triangles. |
