## Concept teaching plans

### Perpendicular and parallel lines

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** To draw a perpendicular through an off-line P, what must first be found?

**Private diagnostic key:** two distinct points A,B on the line equally distant from P.

**Teach in this order:** Choose radius that crosses twice; construct locus rather than eyeball right angle; justify passage through P; compare corresponding-angle parallel construction.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** Draw circle centered P crossing line ℓ atA,B. Construct the perpendicular bisector ofAB; since PA=PB, it passes through P and is perpendicular ℓ. For a parallel through P, construct m⊥ℓ through P, then n⊥m through P; in Euclidean geometry n∥ℓ.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If tangent intersection supplies only one point, ask whether a chord midpoint can be defined; next enlarge the radius.

**Practice progression:** Perpendicular on/off line → parallel → dynamic/folding counterpart and dependency check. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Nondegenerate intersections; point lies on result; double-perpendicular/corresponding-angle theorem; actual execution; second method. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Make incidence conditions explicit, identify the established theorem guaranteeing perpendicularity or parallelism, and verify the line contains the specified point.

### Triangle inequality through construction

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Circles of radii 2 and 5 with center distance 3: two triangle vertices?

**Private diagnostic key:** no; internal tangency gives one collinear point.

**Teach in this order:** Treat circle intersections as simultaneous distance constraints; compare center distance with sum and absolute difference; distinguish two/one/zero intersections.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** Fix AB=6 and seek AC=4,BC=5. Since|5−4|<6<5+4, circles intersect twice, giving mirror-image congruent triangles. With AB=10 the circles are disjoint; no triangle can be built.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If tangency is counted as a triangle, ask whether three points are noncollinear; next compare area with the tangent configuration.

**Practice progression:** Valid construction → tangent cases → disjoint/contained cases and strict inequality proof. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Positive radii; fixed base; full strict interval; both degeneracies; reflection relationship; actual or constraint-based construction evidence. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Relate circle separation or containment to the strict inequalities and distinguish two vertex positions from two noncongruent triangles.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** Construct a perpendicular through a point P on a line and through a point Q off it, then a parallel through Q. Test triangle sides 3,4,8 and 3,4,5 by intersecting circles.

**Private worked key:** On-line P: mark equal distances A,B on either side and construct their perpendicular bisector. Off-line Q: draw a circle centered at Q intersecting the line at A,B, then construct AB's perpendicular bisector through Q. A perpendicular to that perpendicular through Q is parallel to the original line. With base length 8 and radii 3,4, circles are externally separated, so no triangle. With base 5 and radii 3,4, two intersections give reflected congruent triangles, since $1<5<7$.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.

## Additional worked coverage

For the second exact method, a dynamic construction should create chord endpoints as intersections of a circle centered at the required point with the given line, then use the perpendicular-bisector constraint. Move the point and line to check incidence remains valid; keep the circle radius large enough for two intersections. For sides 3,4,c, c=7 gives external tangency and c=1 internal tangency, both degenerate; c<1 gives one circle inside the other without intersection, and c>7 gives external separation.

Use this reasoning as instruction or private calibration. Generate a fresh independent counterpart after exposure; this is not a fixed reassessment task.

## Decision rehearsal and fading

**Make the perpendicular pass through the given point.** Let P lie off line $\ell$. Draw a circle centered at P large enough to cut $\ell$ at distinct A,B. Because $PA=PB$, P lies on AB's perpendicular bisector; constructing that bisector therefore gives the required perpendicular through P. If the circle is tangent, enlarge it: a single contact point does not supply a chord with distinct endpoints.

If the learner draws a perpendicular elsewhere, cue “Which equal-distance fact forces your line through P?” Next identify A,B and the two equal radii; then construct one pair of equal-radius arc intersections and let the learner finish. Fade to a parallel through P by taking a second perpendicular through P, citing the Euclidean double-perpendicular criterion. Inspect the construction dependencies and the separate second exact realization required by the lesson; visual right-angle appearance alone proves neither incidence nor perpendicularity.

## Additional teaching and execution activities

### On-line perpendicular and second-method realization

If P lies on ℓ, draw a circle centered P meeting ℓ at A,B; then PA=PB and P is midpoint AB. Equal-radius circles centered A,B with radius greater than PA meet above/below ℓ. Their common chord line is the perpendicular bisector AB and passes through P. This handles the on-line case separately from the off-line construction in the worked model.

For a dynamic second method, construct a line m through the specified P with a perpendicular-to-ℓ constraint. Verify the dependency contains both incidence P∈m and right angle, not only a free line that currently measures90°. Through an external P construct n perpendicular to m at P; double perpendicularity proves n∥ℓ. Drag P and ℓ and inspect the retained constraints. Alternatively fold the plane to map ℓ onto itself with a crease through P: the crease perpendicular to ℓ is selected; when P lies on ℓ, distinguish it from the trivial crease ℓ itself. A second perpendicular through external P yields the parallel. Explain exact reflection/angle constraints rather than treating a numerical angle display as proof.
