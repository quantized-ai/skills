# Lesson 29.1: Copying and bisecting

Distance and angle constraints justify basic exact constructions.

## Learning objectives

- Construct congruent segments and angles, segment bisectors, and angle bisectors with compass and straightedge and another exact method.
- Explain the geometric justification of each construction and use constructions to formulate conjectures.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify every circle center, radius, intersection, and constructed line needed to determine the intended object.
- Distinguish exact construction from a measurement sketch and justify equality or bisection geometrically.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Copying segments and angles** | An unmarked straightedge draws the line through two points; a compass transfers a radius and draws circles or arcs. Segment copying transfers distance to a specified ray. Angle copying transfers radii and a chord so that corresponding triangles are congruent. A dynamic construction must preserve constraints when its inputs move; a protractor measurement alone is not a formal construction. | Construct congruent segments and angles with compass and straightedge and with dynamic geometry or paper folding, and justify the copied measures. | Specify the source and target data, retain the transferred lengths, and use congruence or the tool constraints to justify equality rather than approximate visual matching. |
| **Segment and angle bisectors** | Equal-radius arcs centered at a segment’s endpoints determine its perpendicular bisector and midpoint. Equal distances along an angle’s rays followed by equal intersecting arcs determine an internal angle bisector through triangle congruence. Folding or dynamic constraints give a second geometric realization. | Construct segment and internal angle bisectors with compass and straightedge and another exact method, justify them, and formulate conjectures from the constructions. | Choose intersecting arcs, identify the intended internal ray, and explain the equal-distance and congruence relationships that force bisection. |
