## Concept teaching plans

### Copying segments and angles

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Is measuring 60° with a protractor the same as compass-and-straightedge copying?

**Private diagnostic key:** no; it measures rather than constructs the exact relation.

**Teach in this order:** Name allowed instruments; copy a radius then chord; specify intersection/side choices; justify SSS and reproduce with dynamic equal-radius constraints.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** To copy ∠AOB onto ray PX, draw an arc centered O meeting the rays atA,B; use its radius atP to meet PX atC. Transfer chord AB with center C to meet the new arc atD on the chosen side. Triangle OAB and PCD are SSS, so ∠CPD equals the source.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If the chord is redrawn by eye, ask which equality ensures the copied angle; next transfer the actual compass width AB.

**Practice progression:** Copy segment → copy angle → constrained dynamic/folding realization and drag check. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Actual steps/artifact; fixed radii/chord; chosen ray/side; theorem justification; exact constraints versus visual approximation. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Specify the source and target data, retain the transferred lengths, and use congruence or the tool constraints to justify equality rather than approximate visual matching.

### Segment and angle bisectors

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** What radius is needed for equal arcs from endpoints A,B to intersect twice?

**Private diagnostic key:** greater than AB/2.

**Teach in this order:** Build equalities through compass constraints; select two intersections; infer bisector from congruence/locus; repeat via a fold bringing A onto B or constrained dynamic construction.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** For segment AB, draw equal-radius circles centered A,B meeting U,V. Since UA=UB and VA=VB, U,V lie on the perpendicular-bisector locus, so UV is that line and its crossing with AB is midpoint. An angle bisector uses equal-distance ray points and equal arcs; SSS proves the two subangles equal.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If any line through midpoint is called perpendicular bisector, ask what establishes 90°; next use equidistant off-segment points.

**Practice progression:** Segment bisector → angle bisector → second realization and justification. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Radius existence; two intersections; midpoint/perpendicular conclusions; internal angle choice; actual second method/evidence. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Choose intersecting arcs, identify the intended internal ray, and explain the equal-distance and congruence relationships that force bisection.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** Describe exact constructions copying a segment and an angle, then construct the perpendicular bisector of AB and the internal bisector of angle XOY. Give reasons rather than measured approximations.

**Private worked key:** Transfer a segment's compass radius to the target ray. For an angle, draw equal-radius arcs about source and target vertices, transfer the chord between source-ray intersections, and draw the target ray; SSS justifies the copy. For AB draw equal-radius circles with radius greater than AB/2; join their two intersections, whose equal endpoint distances force the perpendicular bisector. For the angle take equal-distance points on its rays, intersect equal-radius arcs inside the angle, and join the vertex to that interior intersection; SSS makes the two angles equal.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.

## Additional worked coverage

For the required second method, a fold placing endpoint A onto endpoint B has a crease containing exactly the points equidistant from them, hence the perpendicular bisector. Folding one angle ray onto the other along the internal crease bisects the angle, with the vertex on the crease; identify the internal rather than external bisector. In dynamic geometry, use circle intersections and constrained lines with recorded centers/radii; dragging the inputs should preserve the equal-distance relationships. A visually placed midpoint is not a constrained midpoint.

Use this reasoning as instruction or private calibration. Generate a fresh independent counterpart after exposure; this is not a fixed reassessment task.

## Decision rehearsal and fading

**Arc intersection is a condition, not a drawing convenience.** To bisect segment AB of length 6, choose equal compass radii 4 from A and B. Since $4>6/2$, the circles meet in two points U,V. Both points are equidistant from A and B, so their line is the perpendicular bisector. Radius 3 produces only one intersection and cannot supply the two-point line; radius 2 produces none.

If the student's arcs fail to meet, ask how their radius compares with half AB. Next have them preserve equal radii while enlarging both; only then suggest radius 4 and leave the construction. Fade by giving a new segment with no radius choice. Inspect the actual compass/straightedge artifact and retained arcs. For a second exact method, fold A onto B and retain the crease, explaining its equal-distance property; a written folding plan does not establish execution. Keep approximate measured equality separate from construction constraints.

## Additional teaching and execution activities

### A second exact realization with inspectable dependencies

For a **dynamic copied segment**, take source endpoints A,B, target origin P and target ray r. Create a circle centered P with radius constrained to length AB; choose its intersection Q with r. Dependency chain AB→radius→circle∩ray→Q guarantees PQ=AB while A,B,P,r move. Do not type a rounded displayed length as an unrelated radius.

For a **dynamic copied angle**, use the same equal-radius and chord constraints described in the worked model, with each new circle's radius linked to the source segment rather than a fixed screen measurement. Specify the chosen side of the target ray. Drag the source angle and show that the copied angle updates; a screenshot alone without constraints may hide a freehand approximation.

For a **folded segment bisector**, fold so A coincides with B. Points on the crease remain fixed under the fold's reflection and are equidistant from A,B, so the crease is their perpendicular bisector. For a **folded internal angle bisector**, align one boundary ray with the other, retaining the crease through their common vertex inside the angle. Reflection preserves angle measure, so the two subangles agree. Other folds can produce the external bisector; identify the internal ray explicitly.

After each construction, ask for source/target data, steps, constraint evidence, a justification and one conjecture obtained by varying an irrelevant feature. Credit the second method only when its own execution is observed; describing both procedures is conceptual evidence, not two completed constructions.
