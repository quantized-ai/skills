# Lesson 3.3: Factoring quadratic trinomials

Factor integer-coefficient quadratic trinomials by matching coefficient sums and products. Extend the monic pattern to nonmonic trinomials with a middle-term split and grouping.

## Learning objectives

- Factor monic integer-coefficient trinomials using sum-product conditions and sign reasoning.
- Extract common factors and factor nonmonic trinomials with a valid middle-term split.
- Verify every coefficient and distinguish exhaustive rational-factor searches from questions about real roots.

## Learning outcome

Select and verify trinomial factors using all coefficient conditions and explain what a complete integer search establishes about rational factorization.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the leading, middle, and constant coefficients to constrain factors and signs.
- Extract a GCF, preserve the middle coefficient when splitting, and group to a complete product.
- Expand proposed factors to check every coefficient.
- Explain why an exhaustive integer-factor search can rule out rational linear factors without ruling out irrational real roots.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monic quadratic trinomials** | For integers $b,c$, the identity $(x+m)(x+n)=x^2+(m+n)x+mn$ requires $m+n=b$ and $mn=c$. Positive $c$ requires equal signs and negative $c$ opposite signs; when $c=0$, extract $x$. Exhausting the integer factor pairs without a match rules out rational linear factors of a monic integer polynomial, but does not rule out irrational real roots. | Factor a monic integer-coefficient quadratic by matching both the sum and product of the constant parts of its linear factors, and verify the product. Use signs and an exhaustive integer factor-pair search to justify whether rational linear factors exist, without inferring that real roots cannot exist. | Match both required coefficient conditions and justify the signs. Handle a zero constant by extracting the common variable factor. Check every coefficient by expansion and distinguish a failed exhaustive rational-factor search from absence of real roots. |
| **Nonmonic quadratic trinomials** | A nonmonic quadratic has a nonzero leading coefficient other than $1$. For integer coefficients, extract a numerical GCF first; write the remaining quadratic as $ax^2+bx+c$. Split its middle term using integers $m,n$ with $m+n=b$ and $mn=ac$, then group. The split preserves the polynomial. A full integer search applies after common factors are removed and can establish the absence of rational linear factors. Trial factors are valid only if every coefficient agrees. | Extract a common factor and factor a nonmonic integer-coefficient quadratic by splitting its middle term and grouping. Verify all coefficients of the selected factors and explain what an exhaustive middle-term-split search establishes about rational factorization. | Retain the extracted GCF. Use split coefficients with the required sum and product, preserve the expression, and group to factors. Check the leading coefficient, combined cross terms, and constant; distinguish an exhaustive failure from an incomplete search. |
