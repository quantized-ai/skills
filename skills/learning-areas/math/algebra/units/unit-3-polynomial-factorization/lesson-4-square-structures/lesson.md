# Lesson 3.4: Square structures

Recognize differences of squares and perfect-square trinomials as reversed product identities. Verify the required structure and continue factoring within the stated coefficient set.

## Learning objectives

- Factor differences of squares, including repeated square structure, and verify the product.
- Recognize and factor perfect-square trinomials using all three terms.
- Justify the pattern and the stopping point over rational coefficients by default or another specified set.

## Learning outcome

Distinguish square-based factoring patterns, verify their products, and justify completeness using the allowed coefficients.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify complete square components and distinguish subtraction from addition.
- Check the doubled cross term before claiming a perfect-square trinomial.
- Inspect every resulting factor for further factoring over the stated coefficient set.
- Verify the product and justify any irreducibility claim rather than stopping solely because a familiar pattern is absent.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Factoring differences of squares** | The identity $A^2-B^2=(A-B)(A+B)$ requires subtraction of two complete squares in the allowed coefficient set. Reinspect both factors and repeat when applicable. Rational coefficients are the default; factors irreducible over the rationals may split over the reals. The square-difference rule does not apply to $A^2+B^2$; in particular, $x^2+k$ with real $k>0$ has no real linear factors. | Recognize, factor, and verify differences of squares, including quartic expressions, without applying the identity to a sum. Continue factoring resulting factors and justify completeness over the stated coefficient set. | Identify the complete square roots and the subtraction operation. Preserve both conjugate factors and verify their product. Reinspect resulting factors and justify the stopping point with the allowed coefficients rather than appearance alone. |
| **Perfect-square trinomials** | A perfect-square trinomial has the exact form $A^2+2AB+B^2=(A+B)^2$ or $A^2-2AB+B^2=(A-B)^2$. Square endpoints alone are insufficient: the signed middle term must equal the required doubled product. A squared binomial is a factored form, but its base may itself need further factoring over the allowed coefficients. | Recognize and factor perfect-square trinomials, verify the product, and inspect the binomial base for further factoring. Distinguish a perfect square from a trinomial with square endpoints by checking the signed doubled cross term. | Identify square endpoints and calculate their required doubled cross term. Choose the binomial sign to match the middle term. Identify the exact failed coefficient in a false square claim and justify whether the squared base admits further factoring. |
