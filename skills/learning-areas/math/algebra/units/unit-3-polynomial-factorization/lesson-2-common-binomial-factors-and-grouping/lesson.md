# Lesson 3.2: Common binomial factors and grouping

Factor repeated polynomial expressions as single objects, then expose repeated factors by grouping. Preserve signs when rewriting opposite binomials or extracting negative common factors.

## Learning objectives

- Factor and verify expressions with repeated polynomial factors.
- Rewrite opposite binomials while preserving the negative multiplier.
- Select suitable groupings for cubic and quartic expressions and complete the resulting factorization.

## Learning outcome

Expose a shared polynomial factor through sign changes or grouping, verify the product, and justify whether further factoring is needed over rational coefficients unless another set is specified.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify an identical repeated factor at the correct structural level.
- Preserve the negative multiplier when reversing a binomial.
- Explain both uses of distribution in a valid grouping and why one failed grouping does not prove irreducibility.
- Check the full product and factor remaining reducible factors over the allowed coefficients.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Factoring a repeated polynomial expression** | The distributive identity $AU+BU=(A+B)U$ applies when $U$ is an entire polynomial. Only the outside multipliers combine. Opposite binomials satisfy $B-A=-(A-B)$; the extracted negative sign multiplies the whole outside coefficient or expression. Factoring preserves an identity and is checked by expansion. | Factor an expression containing a repeated polynomial factor and verify the complete product. Rewrite opposite binomials using a negative multiplier before factoring. | Identify the repeated polynomial as one object without changing its internal terms. Preserve the negative multiplier when matching opposite factors. Factor the common object and confirm all signs and coefficients by expansion. |
| **Factoring by grouping** | Grouping reverses distribution twice: extract a common factor from each group, then extract the identical polynomial factor shared by the groups. Reordering terms or taking a negative group factor can expose a match. One unsuccessful grouping does not establish irreducibility. Reinspect the resulting factors; rational coefficients are the default unless another set is specified. | Factor suitable cubic and quartic expressions by grouping, complete any further factorization, and verify the product. Choose groupings and signs that produce an identical common factor and explain why a failed grouping does not prove irreducibility. | Choose or rearrange groups that yield identical polynomial factors. Preserve all signs through both uses of distribution. Continue factoring reducible results over the allowed coefficients, verify the product, and avoid inferring irreducibility from one failed grouping. |
