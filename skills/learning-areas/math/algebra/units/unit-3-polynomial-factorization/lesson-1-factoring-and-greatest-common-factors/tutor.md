## Concept guidance

### Factoring as reversing distribution

Curriculum reference: **Factoring as reversing distribution** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Factor $x^2-9$ over the rationals; is this an equation to solve?

**Agent key:** $(x-3)(x+3)$ is an equivalent expression. No solution set was requested.

**Worked example:** Is $x^2-2$ irreducible over both the rationals and reals?

**Worked reasoning:** It has no rational linear factors, but over the reals it is $(x-\sqrt2)(x+\sqrt2)$. Completeness depends on the coefficient system.


#### Teaching sequence

Display an expanded expression and a product as two representations of the same quantity. Factoring asks for the product, not values of x unless an equation is supplied. Expand to verify every coefficient. Set the allowed coefficient system before declaring completion: x²−2 stops over rationals but splits over reals; a nonzero constant multiplier is retained.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which coefficient system is allowed?

If factors are reported as solutions, ask where an equals-zero condition was given. If any two-factor product is called complete, inspect each factor within the allowed system. If no rational pair is found, do not infer no real roots.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Begin by checking proposed factorizations, then construct products and compare stopping points over integers, rationals and reals where stated.

#### Assessment evidence

Require expression-versus-equation distinction, full expansion verification and a justified stopping system. A guessed factorization must recover its constant and middle coefficients as well as its leading term.

### Extracting a greatest common monomial factor

Curriculum reference: **Extracting a greatest common monomial factor** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Extract the positive monomial GCF of $12x^3y-18x^2y^2$.

**Agent key:** The GCF is $6x^2y$, leaving $2x-3y$; redistribute to verify.

**Worked example:** Extract the negative GCF from $-8x^3+12x^2$ and finish factoring.

**Worked reasoning:** $-4x^2(2x-3)$; both quotient signs follow from division by $-4x^2$.


#### Teaching sequence

Find the GCD of absolute numerical coefficients and the minimum exponent shared by all terms for each variable. Divide every term by the extracted monomial. When choosing a negative GCF, explicitly track the quotient signs. Redistribute, then inspect the remaining factor instead of assuming GCF extraction finishes the problem.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** What minimum exponent occurs in every nonzero term?

If the greatest exponent is chosen, test divisibility of the term with the smaller exponent. If a term equal to the GCF disappears, write its quotient as 1. If a negative GCF changes only one sign, divide term by term.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Move from two terms to three, missing variables, constant terms and negative leading coefficients. Include a residual difference of squares requiring another step.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess numerical and variable GCFs, all quotient terms, verification and continued factoring when applicable. Preserve the extracted coefficient throughout subsequent methods.
