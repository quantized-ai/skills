# Lesson 3.1: Factoring and greatest common factors

Treat factoring as the reverse of distribution and judge completeness in a stated coefficient set. Begin by extracting common numerical and variable factors.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish factoring an expression from solving an equation and verify factorizations by multiplication.
- Find and extract a greatest common monomial factor, preserving every quotient term and sign.
- Explain completeness relative to the allowed coefficients and inspect remaining factors.

## Learning outcome

Produce and verify a common-factor decomposition and explain whether further factoring is needed in the stated coefficient set.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish an equivalent product from an equation solution and verify every coefficient by expansion.
- Determine the numerical greatest common factor and minimum shared variable exponents.
- Preserve every quotient term and sign when extracting a positive or negative common factor.
- State the coefficient set and assess whether the remaining factors are reducible.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Factoring as reversing distribution** | Factoring rewrites a polynomial as an equal product; it does not solve an equation. Equality is an identity in the variables and is verified by multiplying and comparing every coefficient. A factorization is complete when every nonconstant factor, or base of a factor power, is irreducible over the allowed coefficients. Use rational coefficients by default; irreducibility over the rationals does not imply irreducibility over the reals. | Recognize and verify a factorization as a product equal to the original polynomial, distinguishing expression rewriting from equation solving. Judge whether a factorization is complete in the stated coefficient set and distinguish rational from real irreducibility. | Identify an equivalent product without treating factoring as equation solving. Expand and compare all coefficients, including the constant term. State the allowed coefficients and justify the claimed stopping point. |
| **Extracting a greatest common monomial factor** | For nonzero integer-coefficient terms, the positive monomial GCF combines the greatest common divisor of the coefficients with the smallest exponent of each shared variable. A missing variable has exponent zero. Divide every term by the chosen factor; extracting the negative of the GCF reverses every quotient sign. A GCF decomposition may require further factoring. | Determine the greatest common monomial factor of several integer-coefficient terms. Extract a positive or negative common factor, verify every quotient term by distribution, and inspect the remainder for further factoring. | Use the numerical greatest common divisor and minimum shared exponents. Divide every term correctly, including constant quotients and negative signs. Redistribute to check equality and identify any remaining applicable factoring method. |
