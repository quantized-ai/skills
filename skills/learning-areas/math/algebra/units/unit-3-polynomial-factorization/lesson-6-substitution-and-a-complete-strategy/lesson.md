# Lesson 3.6: Substitution and a complete strategy

Expose quadratic structure through substitution and combine common-factor, grouping, and special-product methods. Restore every substituted expression and justify completeness in the stated coefficient set.

## Learning objectives

- Identify and factor expressions quadratic in a repeated power or subexpression.
- Select and sequence factoring methods from the polynomial structure.
- Restore substitutions, verify the complete product, and justify any irreducibility claim over the allowed coefficients.

## Learning outcome

Organize a multistep factorization, reconstruct the original polynomial, and support the stated completeness of the result.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose a replacement variable that represents every occurrence of the repeated structure consistently.
- Restore the original expression everywhere and inspect the resulting factors.
- Select successive methods from structure, treating term count only as a clue.
- Verify the full product and justify completeness; failure to find a familiar pattern alone is insufficient.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quadratic structure in higher powers** | An expression $aU^2+bU+c$ is quadratic in a repeated power or polynomial subexpression $U$ when $a\ne0$ and its coefficients are constant with respect to $U$. A replacement variable organizes factoring without changing the expression. Substitute back everywhere, then inspect restored factors for further factoring. Use rational coefficients unless another set is specified; successful factoring in the replacement variable need not finish factoring in the original variable. | Identify a repeated power or subexpression that makes an expression quadratic and factor it consistently in a replacement variable. Restore the original expression in every factor, finish factoring over the allowed coefficients, and verify the complete product. | Choose a substitution that preserves all terms and coefficients. Factor in the replacement variable without altering the expression. Restore every occurrence, factor reducible results over the specified coefficients, and verify the original polynomial by multiplication. |
| **Selecting a factoring strategy** | Check a GCF first, then inspect square and cube identities, trinomial structure, grouping, or quadratic substitution. Term count suggests possibilities but guarantees none. Reinspect all resulting factors and verify the entire product. Completeness means every nonconstant factor, or base of a factor power, is irreducible over the stated coefficients. An exhaustive factor-pair search justifies rational irreducibility for integer-coefficient quadratics; a failed pattern search alone does not prove irreducibility of higher-degree factors. | Select and sequence suitable factoring methods for a mixed expression, rechecking each resulting factor and verifying the final product. Justify completeness over the requested coefficient set and distinguish an irreducibility argument from failure to find a familiar pattern. | Check common factors and choose methods justified by the full structure. Reinspect every factor and expand to verify the result. Support completeness with valid evidence for the remaining factors; do not treat term count or an unsuccessful pattern search as proof. |
