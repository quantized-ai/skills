## Teaching boundaries

## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** A learner factors x⁴−5x²+4 as (U−1)(U−4), U=x², and stops. Finish the task over the rationals.

**Private reasoning key:** Restore U to obtain (x²−1)(x²−4), then split both squares: (x−1)(x+1)(x−2)(x+2). No replacement variable may remain in the requested x-factorization.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Quadratic structure in higher powers

Curriculum reference: **Quadratic structure in higher powers** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Factor $x^4-5x^2+4$ completely over the rationals.

**Agent key:** Set $U=x^2$: $(U-1)(U-4)$ restores to $(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x+2)$.

**Worked example:** Factor $x^6+3x^3+2$.

**Worked reasoning:** With $U=x^3$, obtain $(U+1)(U+2)$, then $(x+1)(x^2-x+1)(x^3+2)$. The last cubic has no rational root.


#### Teaching sequence

Name the repeated power U only when every term can be represented without losing a coefficient or term. Factor the resulting quadratic, then replace every U. In x⁴−5x²+4, U=x² gives (U−1)(U−4), hence (x−1)(x+1)(x−2)(x+2). Use multiplication to verify both substitution and restored factors.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** After substitution back, are any factors still reducible?

If U is substituted for x instead of x², annotate each original power beside its replacement. If a U remains in the answer, restore it before assessing completion. If x²−4 is left over, apply the square-structure lesson.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Move from biquadratics to other repeated polynomial components and higher even powers where in scope. Include a proposed substitution that fails to represent an intervening term.

#### Assessment evidence

Require faithful substitution, complete restoration, further factorization in the stated coefficient system and recovery of the original expression. Solving in U alone is not factoring in x.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Selecting a factoring strategy

Curriculum reference: **Selecting a factoring strategy** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Factor $3x^3-12x$.

**Agent key:** GCF first: $3x(x^2-4)=3x(x-2)(x+2)$.

**Worked example:** A student stops at $2(x^4-1)$. Complete the factorization over the rationals.

**Worked reasoning:** $2(x^2-1)(x^2+1)=2(x-1)(x+1)(x^2+1)$; the remaining quadratic has no rational roots.


#### Teaching sequence

Use a decision sequence based on evidence: common factor, recognized square/cube pattern, quadratic structure, or grouping. After each step, inspect the resulting factors anew. Record why each selected method fits, then expand the final product. Establish the stopping point from allowed coefficients and residual structure; term count alone chooses no conclusive method.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which method applies after the common factor is removed?

If the student tries many patterns without inspecting a GCF, compare all terms for a shared factor first. If one unsuccessful attempt becomes an irreducibility claim, ask what cases were ruled out. If a valid different method works, preserve it.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Mix factorable expressions so the method is not announced, then include justified nonfactorable residuals and multiple-stage examples. Ask why an attractive but inapplicable identity fails.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess strategic selection, retained common factors, repeated inspection, verification and supported completeness. A correct product with no explanation does not demonstrate the strategy objective by itself.
