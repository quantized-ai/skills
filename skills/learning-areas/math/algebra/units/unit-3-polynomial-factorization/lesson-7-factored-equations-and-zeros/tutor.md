## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** A student divides x(x−5)=0 by x and finds only 5. Is the reasoning reversible on the original domain?

**Private reasoning key:** Division assumed x≠0, losing the valid root 0. Factor equations give x=0 or x=5, and both satisfy the original.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### The zero-product property

Curriculum reference: **The zero-product property** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Solve $x(x-4)=0$ without dividing by $x$.

**Agent key:** The union of factor solutions is $\{0,4\}$; division by $x$ would lose 0.

**Worked example:** Can you solve $(x-2)(x+1)=6$ by setting either factor to zero?

**Worked reasoning:** No. Rearranging gives $x^2-x-8=0$, not a zero-product equation with the original factors.


#### Teaching sequence

State the implication precisely: if a whole product is zero, at least one factor is zero. Solve each factor equation and combine by union. Repeated factors contribute multiplicity but not duplicate set members. Compare this with a product equal to a nonzero number, where setting factors to zero would contradict the original.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Is the complete product equal to zero?

If x is canceled from x(x−3)=0, ask whether x=0 was allowed and check it separately. If factor solutions are intersected, substitute one root into the whole product. If the right side is not zero, first form an equivalent zero equation.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Begin with two linear factors, then repeated factors, variable common factors and nonzero right sides needing rearrangement. Contrast solve versus merely factor requests.

#### Assessment evidence

Require legitimate zero form, every relevant factor equation, union without duplicates, and preservation of cases lost by division. Substitute candidates in the original equation.

### Connecting zeros, factors, and polynomial equations

Curriculum reference: **Connecting zeros, factors, and polynomial equations** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Solve $x^2=3x+4$ by factoring and relate the roots to a graph.

**Agent key:** $x^2-3x-4=(x-4)(x+1)=0$, so $x=4,-1$; these are x-intercept inputs of the difference polynomial.

**Worked example:** Does a graph in a small window establish that a cubic has no other real zeros?

**Worked reasoning:** No. Factorization or other completeness reasoning is required; finite plotting windows can omit zeros.


#### Teaching sequence

Move all terms to one side by equal operations before factoring. Link a factor x−r to the value zero at x=r through substitution, then distinguish the zero r from the graph point (r,0). A proposed factor can be checked without assuming the entire factorization is known. Keep real graph intercepts separate from nonreal algebraic solutions.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which difference polynomial must equal zero?

If zeros are read before the equation is in zero form, show the unchanged other side. If a root sign is reversed, solve x−r=0 explicitly. If an intercept is given as a bare number, ask for both coordinates.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Translate between equations, factored expressions and real intercept descriptions. Include an equation whose rearrangement introduces a GCF and one where a candidate fails an original-condition check.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess equivalent zero form, factor-to-zero reasoning, complete solutions, original substitution and correct intercept notation restricted to real inputs.
