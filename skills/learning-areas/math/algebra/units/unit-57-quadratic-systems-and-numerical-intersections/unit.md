# Unit 57: Quadratic systems and numerical intersections

Simultaneous nonlinear constraints connect algebraic solution sets with graphical intersections and carefully qualified numerical approximations.

## Main learning objectives

- Formulate simultaneous linear and quadratic constraints with meaningful units and domains. Solve simple linear–quadratic systems algebraically and graphically.
- Relate equal outputs, graph intersections, and zeros of a difference on the common domain. Use graphs, tables, and successive approximation to refine and verify numerical intersections.

## Concepts and topics

Formulating simultaneous linear and quadratic constraints; Algebraic solutions and intersection counts; Equations as intersections and successive approximations; Quadratic–quadratic systems.

## Lessons

- [57.1: Linear–quadratic systems](lesson-1-linear-quadratic-systems/lesson.md) — Linear–quadratic systems combine a line with a quadratic relation. Their real intersections must be found completely, verified in both equations, and interpreted within any contextual restrictions.
- [57.2: Nonlinear systems and numerical intersections](lesson-2-nonlinear-systems-and-numerical-intersections/lesson.md) — Function intersections can be found by solving an equality of outputs or locating zeros of their difference. Algebraic and numerical methods require common domains, justified precision, and attention to intersections a graph or sign-change search can miss.
