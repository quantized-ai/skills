# Lesson 42.7: Power functions and scaling models

Power functions connect exponent structure to domains, symmetry, monotonicity, transformations, and scale relationships. Their real domains depend on the exponent and cannot be inferred from positive-input samples alone.

## Learning objectives

- Analyze and graph rational-power functions on their real domains and real-power functions on positive inputs.
- Transform power functions and build positive-input scaling models from quantitative relationships.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reduce rational exponents before deciding negative-input and zero behavior.
- Retain transformed domains, distinguish exponent parameters from exponential bases, and interpret fitted exponents and units.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rational-power domains and graphs** | For $p=m/n$ in lowest terms with integers $m\ne0$ and $n>0$, $x^p=(\sqrt[n]{x})^m$ is real for all real $x$ when $n$ is odd and only $x\ge0$ when $n$ is even; a negative $m$ additionally excludes zero. On $x>0$, the graph increases when $p>0$ and decreases when $p<0$. For odd $n$, even $m$ gives even symmetry and odd $m$ gives odd symmetry. Endpoint and infinity behavior follow the exponent sign and the allowed branch; zeros occur only at the allowed origin. | Classify and graph rational-power functions using exponent reduction, real-domain conditions, symmetry, monotonicity, zeros, and endpoint or infinity behavior. | Reduce the exponent, apply root and negative-exponent restrictions, determine each real branch rather than extrapolating from positive inputs, and justify the stated intercepts, symmetry, and end behavior. |
| **Real powers and transformed graphs** | For arbitrary real $p$, the positive-input convention $x^p=e^{p\ln x}$ defines a power function on $x>0$; for $p>0$, a continuous extension can include $x=0$ with value zero. The case $p=0$ is constant one on positive inputs; extending it to zero must be stated separately. Transformations $y=A[B(x-h)]^p+D$ with $A,B\ne0$ map the selected parent domain and its graph by $x=h+u/B$, $y=Av+D$. A power $x^p$ has a variable base and fixed exponent, unlike an exponential with fixed base and variable exponent. | Graph real-power functions on stated domains and their input-output transformations, distinguishing them from exponential functions. | State the positive-input or explicitly extended domain, transform domains and features with the coordinate rule, handle constant powers separately, and identify whether the variable occurs in the base or exponent. |
| **Power-law scaling models** | A positive-input model $y=kx^p$ with $k>0$ gives $y_2/y_1=(x_2/x_1)^p$. Two positive observations with distinct inputs determine $p=\ln(y_2/y_1)/\ln(x_2/x_1)$ and then $k=y_1/x_1^p$, when this family is assumed. The coefficient carries output units divided by input units to power $p$. The chosen scaling relationship, observed domain, and data variation determine how far the model can be used. | Construct and use power-law models from scaling relationships or positive data and interpret their exponents, coefficients, domains, and predictions. | Use distinct positive inputs for logarithmic parameter recovery, distinguish an assumed model from a proved physical law, preserve units and feasible domains, and evaluate the reasonableness of predictions. |
