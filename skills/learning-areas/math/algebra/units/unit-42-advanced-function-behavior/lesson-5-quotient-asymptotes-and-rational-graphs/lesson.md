# Lesson 42.5: Quotient asymptotes and rational graphs

Polynomial division separates a rational function into its end-behavior model and a vanishing remainder, while local factors determine holes and vertical behavior.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and interpret oblique or polynomial quotient asymptotes using division.
- Synthesize rational graphs from domain restrictions, local behavior, and end behavior.

## Proficiency criteria

- Show that the function-minus-quotient difference tends to zero at each unbounded end.
- Distinguish holes, vertical asymptotes, quotient graphs, and allowed crossings.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Division and quotient asymptotes** | For real-coefficient polynomials $P,Q$ with $Q$ nonzero, division gives $P/Q=S+R/Q$ with $R=0$ or $\deg R<\deg Q$. On the original domain, $R/Q\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$. A linear quotient gives an oblique asymptote; higher-degree quotients give polynomial end-behavior models. The graph may cross $y=S(x)$ where $R(x)=0$ and $Q(x)\ne0$. Exact cancellation or zero remainder does not remove original exclusions. | Use polynomial division to determine oblique and higher-degree quotient asymptotes and justify their end-behavior and crossing conditions. | Produce a correct quotient and proper remainder, verify the vanishing difference, retain all original denominator exclusions, and distinguish an allowed crossing from an excluded input. |
| **Complete rational graph analysis** | Factored original and reduced forms reveal exclusions, holes, intercepts, and sign intervals. A remaining denominator zero gives local unbounded behavior whose signs depend on factor signs and multiplicities. Horizontal or oblique asymptotes follow from degree or quotient analysis. A synthesis reconciles local branches with their behavior at both unbounded ends, using technology to check rather than replace the restrictions. | Analyze and graph rational functions by coordinating holes, intercepts, signs, one-sided vertical behavior, and horizontal or oblique asymptotes. | Keep original exclusions, distinguish removable factors from poles, determine signs on both sides of each pole, and make every branch consistent with intercepts and end behavior. |
