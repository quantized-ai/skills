# Lesson 42.3: One-sided and end behavior

Elementary limit language distinguishes a function’s nearby behavior from its value at a point and describes finite or unbounded behavior at each end of its domain.

## Learning objectives

- Describe finite and infinite one-sided behavior using graphs, tables, algebra, and limit notation.
- Determine when the two-sided behavior agrees and analyze behavior as the input tends to positive or negative infinity.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish nearby values from the value at the target input and interpret infinity as unbounded behavior.
- Analyze both sides or ends separately and support numerical impressions with structural reasoning.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Finite one-sided limits** | $\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)=L$ and $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=L$ describe outputs approaching the finite real value $L$ from the allowed inputs to the indicated side. The value $f(a)$ can differ from $L$ or be undefined. A two-sided finite limit requires both one-sided limits to exist and agree. Only a side with domain inputs arbitrarily near $a$ supplies the corresponding one-sided behavior. If a nearby equal expression is a polynomial or a quotient with nonzero denominator at $a$, its value at $a$ determines the finite limit; a cancellation valid for $x\ne a$ can establish that nearby equality without assigning a value to the original function at $a$. | Determine and interpret finite one-sided and two-sided limits from algebraic structure, tables, and graphs while distinguishing them from point values. | State the approach direction, compare the two sides, read filled and missing point values separately, and justify algebraic cancellations on nearby allowed inputs. |
| **Unbounded and nonconvergent behavior** | $\lim_{x\to a^\pm}f(x)=+\infty$ or $-\infty$ describes unbounded growth or decrease and does not assign a real-number limit. A finite two-sided limit can fail because sides disagree, because of unbounded behavior, or because values continue to oscillate without approaching a single value. A finite sample of inputs cannot establish behavior for all sufficiently near inputs. | Distinguish finite limits, signed unbounded behavior, unequal one-sided limits, and nonconvergent oscillation near a specified input. | Analyze signs on each side, avoid treating infinity as a function value, and explain the reason a single finite limit fails rather than only reporting nonexistence. |
| **Behavior at infinity** | $x\to\infty$ and $x\to-\infty$ describe the two ends of an unbounded domain. Function values may approach a finite number, grow without bound, decrease without bound, or fail to settle. Leading terms determine polynomial and rational end behavior; exponential, logarithmic, and real power families require their own domain and parameter conditions. A horizontal asymptote is an end-behavior conclusion and can be crossed at finite inputs. | Analyze and communicate end behavior of polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, and real power functions using infinity notation and domain conditions. | Consider only ends present in the domain, distinguish positive and negative infinity, justify behavior from the function structure, and separate end limits from finite graph intersections. |
