# Lesson 42.6: Rational inequalities

A rational inequality is solved through sign intervals on its original domain. Endpoint rules and identically zero cases determine the complete solution set.

## Learning objectives

- Solve rational inequalities using equivalent comparisons with zero and sign analysis.
- Interpret interval solutions in context and justify every endpoint inclusion or exclusion.

## Proficiency criteria

- Retain every original denominator restriction and analyze signs on all relevant intervals.
- Do not multiply by an unknown-sign denominator without separating sign cases.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sign analysis for rational inequalities** | For rational expressions with real polynomial coefficients, rewrite the inequality as $R(x)\mathrel{\diamond}0$ with $\diamond$ one of $<,\le,>,\ge$, retaining original exclusions before combining or cancelling. For a nonzero rational expression, real numerator zeros and original excluded inputs partition the number line into intervals of constant sign. Factor signs, multiplicities, or test inputs determine the sign on each interval. Multiplication by a variable denominator requires sign cases. | Solve rational inequalities by combining terms, factoring, and analyzing every sign interval while retaining the original domain. | Form an equivalent comparison with zero, record all exclusions, justify interval signs, and avoid unqualified multiplication by a denominator of unknown sign. |
| **Endpoints, identities, and contextual solutions** | A strict inequality excludes all numerator zeros; a non-strict one includes allowed zeros. Every original denominator zero remains excluded, even after cancellation. If the combined expression is identically zero, a non-strict comparison with zero holds on the original domain and a strict one has no solution. Contextual restrictions intersect the algebraic solution, which may include intervals and isolated points. | Express complete rational-inequality solutions in interval or set notation and interpret endpoint, identically zero, and contextual cases. | Include only endpoints permitted by both the inequality and domain, preserve isolated solutions where appropriate, handle zero expressions directly, and state the contextual intersection with correct units. |
