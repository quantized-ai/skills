# Lesson 42.2: Difference quotients and average change

The difference quotient expresses average output change per input change over a variable interval. Its algebra preserves the nonzero interval and every function-domain restriction.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and simplify difference quotients for polynomial, rational, and radical functions.
- Interpret difference quotients as secant slopes and average rates with correct units and intervals.

## Proficiency criteria

- Substitute the full shifted input and retain both endpoint domains and the nonzero increment.
- Distinguish a quotient of output differences from a difference of function formulas divided separately.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Symbolic difference quotients** | For real $h\ne0$ and inputs $x,x+h$ in the domain of $f$, the difference quotient is $[f(x+h)-f(x)]/h$. Expansion, factoring, common denominators, and conjugates can simplify it. Cancelling a factor of $h$ does not retroactively permit $h=0$ in the original quotient; shifted inputs can add restrictions for rational or radical functions. | Construct and simplify symbolic difference quotients of polynomial, rational, and radical functions while preserving all original restrictions. | Substitute $x+h$ everywhere the input occurs, subtract the entire original output, use justified algebra, and retain $h\ne0$ and both function-domain conditions. |
| **Secant slopes and interval rates** | The quotient $[f(b)-f(a)]/(b-a)$ for distinct domain inputs is the slope of the secant through $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$, with output units per input unit. Setting $b=a+h$ gives the difference-quotient form. Reversing both endpoint differences preserves the slope; shrinking intervals may yield changing average rates without assigning an instantaneous rate in this lesson. | Interpret and compare difference quotients as secant slopes and average rates on specified intervals using formulas, tables, and graphs. | Use matching numerator and denominator order, compute rates on valid nonzero intervals, explain units and sign, and distinguish an interval average from an output value. |
