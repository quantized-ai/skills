# Unit 42: Advanced function behavior

This unit develops power functions, function decomposition, symbolic average rates, and precise descriptions of behavior near excluded inputs and at infinity. Quotient graphs and rational inequalities integrate algebraic structure with interval reasoning; elementary limit language supports these topics without introducing differentiation.

## Main learning objectives

- Decompose functions and coordinate the domains and meanings of their component operations.
- Construct difference quotients and interpret them as average rates on nonzero intervals.
- Describe one-sided and end behavior, classify discontinuities, and evaluate limitations of numerical displays.
- Analyze rational-function asymptotes and solve rational inequalities with complete domain and endpoint conditions.
- Analyze and transform power functions on explicit real domains and use them in scaling models.

## Concepts and topics

Composite-function decomposition; domain tests; difference quotients; secant slopes; finite and infinite one-sided behavior; elementary limit notation; continuity and discontinuities; graphing limitations; oblique and polynomial quotient asymptotes; rational graph synthesis and inequalities. Rational and real power functions; domain and parity restrictions; power transformations and scaling models.

## Lessons

- [42.1: Function decomposition](lesson-1-function-decomposition/lesson.md) — Decomposition expresses a function as an ordered combination of simpler functions and makes its domain and the meanings of intermediate quantities explicit.
- [42.2: Difference quotients and average change](lesson-2-difference-quotients-and-average-change/lesson.md) — The difference quotient expresses average output change per input change over a variable interval. Its algebra preserves the nonzero interval and every function-domain restriction.
- [42.3: One-sided and end behavior](lesson-3-one-sided-and-end-behavior/lesson.md) — Elementary limit language distinguishes a function’s nearby behavior from its value at a point and describes finite or unbounded behavior at each end of its domain.
- [42.4: Continuity, discontinuities, and graphing limits](lesson-4-continuity-discontinuities-and-graphing-limits/lesson.md) — Continuity compares nearby behavior with a function’s assigned value. Classifying discontinuities requires attention to both sides, domain endpoints, and limitations of sampled displays.
- [42.5: Quotient asymptotes and rational graphs](lesson-5-quotient-asymptotes-and-rational-graphs/lesson.md) — Polynomial division separates a rational function into its end-behavior model and a vanishing remainder, while local factors determine holes and vertical behavior.
- [42.6: Rational inequalities](lesson-6-rational-inequalities/lesson.md) — A rational inequality is solved through sign intervals on its original domain. Endpoint rules and identically zero cases determine the complete solution set.
- [42.7: Power functions and scaling models](lesson-7-power-functions-and-scaling-models/lesson.md) — Power functions connect exponent structure to domains, symmetry, monotonicity, transformations, and scale relationships. Their real domains depend on the exponent and cannot be inferred from positive-input samples alone.
