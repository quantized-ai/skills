# Lesson 42.1: Function decomposition

Decomposition expresses a function as an ordered combination of simpler functions and makes its domain and the meanings of intermediate quantities explicit.

## Learning objectives

- Represent a function as a composition of two or more functions.
- Compare original and reversed component orders, solve multistage contextual problems, and determine which intermediate values are admissible.

## Proficiency criteria

- Recompose the proposed functions to recover both the formula and its domain.
- Retain contextual meanings, units, and restrictions at every stage and demonstrate how reversing component order can change a formula, domain, or meaning.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Decomposing function formulas** | A decomposition $f=g\circ h$ means $f(x)=g(h(x))$ on inputs where $x\in\operatorname{dom}h$ and $h(x)\in\operatorname{dom}g$. More than two component functions can expose successive operations. Decompositions are not unique; a simplified formula alone does not guarantee equality of functions unless the domains agree. | Represent a given function as a composition of two or more functions and verify the formula and domain of the composition. | Identify the inner-to-outer operations, reconstruct the original expression, test each intermediate domain condition, and state any restriction needed for equality of functions. |
| **Order and modeled intermediate quantities** | In a composite model, one stage produces the input quantity for the next; units and feasible values determine allowable order and domains. In general $g\circ h$ and $h\circ g$ differ in formula, domain, or meaning. Associativity permits grouping several valid stages without reversing their order. | Decompose multistage quantitative relationships, compare the effects of reversing component order, and use the valid composition to solve contextual problems. | Explain each intermediate quantity and unit, justify the allowed domain and stage order, demonstrate why reversing order can change the result, and interpret the final output. |
