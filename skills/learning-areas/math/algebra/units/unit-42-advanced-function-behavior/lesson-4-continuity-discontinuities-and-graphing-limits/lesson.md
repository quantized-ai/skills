# Lesson 42.4: Continuity, discontinuities, and graphing limits

Continuity compares nearby behavior with a function’s assigned value. Classifying discontinuities requires attention to both sides, domain endpoints, and limitations of sampled displays.

## Learning objectives

- Classify removable, jump, infinite, and other nonconvergent discontinuities and describe one-sided behavior.
- Determine whether a missing or mismatched value can be repaired and evaluate graphing-tool evidence.

## Proficiency criteria

- Check whether the input is in the domain and compare its value with the appropriate limit.
- Use algebraic or domain evidence to confirm features hidden or suggested by a display.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Continuity and removable discontinuities** | At an interior domain input $a$, continuity means $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$ with both quantities defined; domain endpoints use the available side. A removable discontinuity at a missing or mismatched point has a finite common nearby limit $L$ and can be repaired by assigning that value. A formula simplified by cancellation may represent the continuous extension while the original function retains a hole. | Determine continuity from function values and nearby behavior and identify or repair removable discontinuities without changing the original domain silently. | Check existence of the point value and matching nearby limit, calculate the needed repair value, and distinguish an extended function from its originally restricted version. |
| **Jump, infinite, and oscillatory discontinuities** | A jump has unequal finite one-sided limits. Infinite discontinuity includes at least one unbounded one-sided behavior at a finite input. Continuing oscillation can prevent a one-sided limit even when outputs stay bounded. These behaviors cannot be repaired by changing only the point value. Piecewise formulas require evaluation of the expression valid on each side. | Classify nonremovable discontinuities from one-sided behavior, including piecewise jumps, vertical-asymptote behavior, and persistent oscillation. | Use the formula or graph branch belonging to each side, state the finite limit or failure mode separately, and explain why assigning a single point value cannot repair the mismatch. |
| **Limitations of numerical graphs** | Finite resolution can hide holes, narrow branches, rapid oscillation, or jumps and can draw apparent connecting segments across undefined inputs. A chosen window can conceal asymptotes or end behavior. Checking exact domains and factors, changing the window and step size, and examining one-sided values can expose display artifacts; numerical evidence alone is not a proof of continuity or a limit. | Evaluate graphing-calculator or software displays near discontinuities and corroborate their apparent features with domain, algebraic, and one-sided analysis. | Identify features a display can miss or falsely connect, choose informative windows or sample inputs, and support the final classification with the defining relation rather than the screen alone. |
