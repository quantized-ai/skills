# Lesson 11.1: Definition and basic evaluation

A logarithm names the exponent that produces a given positive argument. Definition-based reasoning supports exact values, calculator approximations, and the distinction between an invalid argument and a negative logarithm.

## Learning objectives

- Convert between logarithmic and exponential statements, justifying valid bases and argument restrictions.
- Evaluate logarithms in bases 2, 10, and $e$ and check exact or approximate values against known powers.

## Learning outcome

The student can interpret and evaluate a real logarithm while justifying its domain, sign, and approximate size.

## Proficiency criteria

- Match base, exponent, and argument when converting in either direction.
- Require a positive base different from one and a positive complete argument; distinguish invalid arguments from negative or zero outputs.
- Interpret common, natural, and explicit-base notation correctly.
- Use known powers to evaluate exact logarithms and check the sign and magnitude of approximations.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Logarithms as exponents** | For $b>0$ and $b\ne1$, $\log_b a=c$ means $b^c=a$: the logarithm is the unique real exponent producing $a>0$. Its output may be any real number. Base 1 lacks a unique inverse; nonpositive bases do not define exponential functions on all real inputs. For a variable argument $A(x)$, require $A(x)>0$. | Convert in both directions between equivalent logarithmic and exponential statements. Justify valid real logarithm bases and determine positive-argument restrictions. | Preserve the roles of all three quantities in either representation. Explain the base and argument conditions through existence and uniqueness of the exponent. Determine restrictions on the complete argument; distinguish nonpositive arguments from permitted nonpositive outputs. |
| **Base 2, common logarithms, and natural logarithms** | This course uses $\log x=\log_{10}x$ and $\ln x=\log_e x$; other bases are explicit. For any valid base, $\log_b1=0$ and $\log_b b=1$. Recognizable powers give exact values. For bases 2, 10, and $e$, arguments between zero and one give negative outputs; arguments above one give positive outputs. Retain exact logarithmic expressions before rounding and use nearby powers to check approximations. | Interpret common, natural, and explicit-base notation and evaluate recognizable logarithms in bases 2, 10, and $e$. Use technology to approximate logarithms and check their signs and magnitudes against nearby powers. | Identify the intended base from notation. Evaluate recognizable powers using the inverse definition. Retain exact forms until final rounding and bracket approximate outputs using corresponding exponential values. |
