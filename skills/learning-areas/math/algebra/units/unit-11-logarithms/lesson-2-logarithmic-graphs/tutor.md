## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** Is the graph of ln(4−x) to the right of its vertical asymptote x=4?

**Private reasoning key:** No. Its argument is positive only for x<4. As x approaches 4 from the left, the logarithm tends to negative infinity.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Parent logarithms and reflection

Curriculum reference: **Parent logarithms and reflection** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Reflect $(2,9)$ on $y=3^x$ to the inverse graph.

**Agent key:** The reflected point is $(9,2)$ on $y=\log_3x$. Domain and range exchange.

**Worked example:** Describe the direction and vertical asymptote of $\log_{1/2}x$.

**Worked reasoning:** It decreases on x>0 and has vertical asymptote x=0; outputs grow toward positive infinity as x approaches zero from the right.


#### Teaching sequence

Reverse points on y=b^x to obtain y=log_b(x), exchanging domain and range. The exponential's horizontal asymptote becomes x=0 and its point (0,1) becomes (1,0); no y-intercept exists. For b>1 the log increases, while 0<b<1 reverses direction; describe approach to zero from the allowed positive side and behavior as x grows.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Is the base above or below 1?

If the reflection is in an axis rather than y=x, swap coordinates explicitly. If x=0 is plotted, check argument positivity. If all logarithms are called increasing, compare log_(1/2)(1) and log_(1/2)(2).

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Match exponential/logarithmic pairs, reverse exact points and compare both valid base intervals, including tails and intercepts.

#### Assessment evidence

Require domain-range exchange, correct reflection, asymptote, intercepts, monotonicity and both end directions. A logarithmic output can be any real number.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Transformations of logarithmic graphs

Curriculum reference: **Transformations of logarithmic graphs** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Find domain, asymptote, and an exact point of $\log_2(3-x)+1$.

**Agent key:** Domain x<3, asymptote x=3; x=2 gives y=1. The inside reflection changes the permitted side.

**Worked example:** Find domain and x-intercept of $-2\log_3(x+1)+4$.

**Worked reasoning:** Domain x>-1; setting output zero gives log=2, hence x+1=9 and x=8.


#### Teaching sequence

Solve the entire argument inequality before mapping points. Find the argument's zero to locate a vertical asymptote and its positive side to determine where the graph exists. Use the parent input u by solving the inside expression=u, then transform its output. Find intercepts only inside that domain and use base/outside sign to determine approach direction.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which side makes the whole logarithm argument positive?

If log(3−x) is given domain x>3, solve 3−x>0 explicitly. If the outside multiplier is placed inside the argument without justification, follow the two transformation stages. If a boundary becomes included, evaluate the original logarithm there.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Vary reflected arguments, shifts/scales and positive/negative outside coefficients. Recover features from formulas and verify proposed points in the original rule.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess exact domain, point mapping, full range for nondegenerate transforms, asymptote side/direction and all existing intercepts. Handle zero scales separately without repairing undefined arguments.
