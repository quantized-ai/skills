# Lesson 11.5: Exponential and logarithmic equations

Logarithms make general exponential equations solvable, and exponential conversion solves logarithmic equations. Quantitative relationships can be formulated as logarithmic equations with defined variables and units. The original positive-argument conditions remain decisive when algebra produces multiple or invalid candidates.

## Learning objectives

- Solve exponential equations with linear exponents using logarithms and formulate and solve logarithmic equations using exponential conversion or equality of positive arguments.
- Determine existence and preserve original restrictions while validating exact and approximate solutions, contextual units, and rejected candidates.

## Learning outcome

The student can choose an inverse operation, derive a solution, and explain why it is valid in the original equation.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define quantities and formulate logarithmic target equations with meaningful units and dimensionless logarithm arguments.
- Isolate the exponential or logarithmic quantity before using an inverse operation.
- Check exponential target positivity and handle constant parameter cases separately.
- Intersect all original positive-argument conditions and retain them when combining logarithms or solving resulting equations.
- Keep exact expressions until final rounding and verify candidates in the original equation with appropriate contextual units.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Solving exponential equations with logarithms** | For $ab^{ct+q}=d$, with $a,c\ne0$, $b>0$, and $b\ne1$, a real solution exists exactly when $d/a>0$; it is $t=(\ln(d/a)/\ln b-q)/c$. Isolate the exponential before taking logarithms and account for the entire linear exponent. If $a=0$ or $c=0$, the equation is constant and has either every permitted input or no solution. Preserve exact form until rounding; check the original equation, contextual domain, and units. | Solve exponential equations with linear exponents by expressing the unknown in logarithmic form. Determine existence, including constant parameter cases, and verify numerical accuracy, contextual domain, and units of exponential-equation solutions. | Isolate the exponential and test target positivity when the exponential varies. Account for every scale and shift in the linear exponent. Analyze zero scale or exponent-rate cases as constant equations. Report an exact solution and verify any approximation in the original equation with valid contextual domain and units. |
| **Solving logarithmic equations and rejecting invalid roots** | For $b>0$, $b\ne1$, $\log_b A=c$ is equivalent to $A=b^c$, with $A>0$. Equality $\log_b A=\log_b B$ implies $A=B$ only where both arguments are positive. For multiple logarithms, determine the intersection of all original argument conditions before condensation. Solve the resulting algebraic equation and check each candidate in the original equation; a positive condensed product or quotient does not establish separate-argument validity. A described logarithmic relationship and a specified target determine an equation whose variables, dimensionless logarithm arguments, and contextual restrictions must be defined. | Generate logarithmic equations from quantitative relationships and solve them by exponential conversion, valid logarithm properties, or equality of positive arguments in a common base. Determine all original argument restrictions and verify algebraic candidates, rejecting those outside the original domain or failing the equation. | Define quantities and translate a logarithmic relationship and target into an equation with dimensionless logarithm arguments. Intersect the original positive-argument conditions. Use a valid inverse conversion or common-base one-to-one property to obtain candidates. Preserve restrictions through condensation and check every candidate in the original separate logarithms. |
