# Lesson 36.6: Trigonometric equations and models

Trigonometric equation solving combines principal inverse values, unit-circle symmetries, periodicity, algebraic identities, and contextual domain restrictions.

## Learning objectives

- Solve basic trigonometric equations with affine arguments and use factoring, substitution, and identities to solve reducible equations, giving complete periodic families or interval solution sets.
- Formulate and solve trigonometric equations from models, using technology where needed and interpreting all admissible solutions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve original exclusions, check transformed candidates, and enumerate every allowed solution.
- Report numerical solutions with angle units, a justified precision, and contextual meaning.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic periodic solution families** | For $\sin x=a$ with $\lvert a\rvert\le1$, use $x=\arcsin a+2k\pi$ or $x=\pi-\arcsin a+2k\pi$. For $\cos x=a$, use $x=\pm\arccos a+2k\pi$. For $\tan x=a$, use $x=\arctan a+k\pi$. Here $k\in\mathbb Z$; coinciding branches are not distinct solutions. Affine arguments require solving each angle family for the original variable. | Solve sine, cosine, and tangent equations with affine arguments as complete periodic families and as solution sets on specified intervals. | Check allowable target values, scale and shift the angle families correctly, include permitted interval endpoints, and remove duplicated solutions at extrema. |
| **Algebraic and identity-based equation methods** | Factoring, substitution in a single trigonometric quantity, and identities can reduce equations to basic families. Dividing by a trigonometric factor can lose its zeros; squaring can introduce candidates; reciprocal functions retain exclusions. Identity equations and inconsistent equations have, respectively, all allowed inputs or no inputs as their solution sets. | Solve trigonometric equations by factoring, substitution, and identities while identifying lost solutions, extraneous candidates, and special all-or-none cases. | Retain zero-factor branches before division, verify squared candidates in the original equation, enforce original domains, and present every surviving periodic family or interval solution. |
| **Equations from periodic models** | A periodic model translates a target height, displacement, or other response into a trigonometric equation. Inverse functions supply principal values and periodic families; the real time or angle domain determines admissible events. Graphs, numerical tables, or numerical root methods estimate non-exact solutions, with checks against the original model and measurement precision. | Generate and solve trigonometric equations from periodic models, evaluate solutions with technology, and interpret all admissible events in context. | Formulate the target equation with units, find every solution within the modeled interval, verify numerical candidates, and explain rounding, repeated events, and physical restrictions. |
