# Lesson 36.2: Inverse trigonometric functions

Inverse sine, cosine, and tangent reverse specified one-to-one branches, so their principal values differ from the complete angle solutions of a trigonometric equation.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and graph the principal inverse sine, cosine, and tangent functions.
- Evaluate inverse compositions using principal ranges and distinguish inverse notation from reciprocals.

## Proficiency criteria

- State both the original restricted interval and the inverse domain and range.
- Resolve inverse compositions by principal-range membership rather than automatic cancellation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Principal inverse branches** | Sine restricted to $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, cosine to $[0,\pi]$, and tangent to $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ are one-to-one. Their inverses are $\arcsin:[-1,1]\to[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, $\arccos:[-1,1]\to[0,\pi]$, and $\arctan:\mathbb R\to(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. The notation $\sin^{-1}$ denotes an inverse function, not $1/\sin$. | Construct the principal inverse sine, cosine, and tangent functions by restricting monotone branches and state their domains and ranges. | Justify one-to-one behavior on each selected interval, exchange domain and range, and distinguish each inverse from the reciprocal function. |
| **Inverse trigonometric graphs** | Graphs reflect the restricted parent branches across $y=x$. Inverse sine increases on $[-1,1]$; inverse cosine decreases there. Inverse tangent increases on $\mathbb R$ with horizontal asymptotes $y=\pm\pi/2$, neither attained. Endpoint behavior, intercepts, and symmetry follow from the restricted branches. | Graph inverse sine, inverse cosine, and inverse tangent and determine their monotonicity, intercepts, endpoint behavior, symmetry, and asymptotes. | Include inverse sine and cosine endpoints, leave inverse tangent asymptotes unattained, and make reflected points agree with the principal ranges. |
| **Principal values and inverse compositions** | $\sin(\arcsin u)=u$ and $\cos(\arccos u)=u$ require $u\in[-1,1]$; $\tan(\arctan u)=u$ holds for real $u$. Reversed compositions return the principal representative, equaling the original angle only on the selected branch. Exact values use unit-circle reasoning; technology gives approximations with a stated angle unit. Mixed compositions use a principal angle and its sign constraints. | Evaluate exact and approximate inverse trigonometric values and same-function or mixed-function compositions while enforcing principal ranges and domains. | Check that inputs are allowed, select the correct principal angle and quadrant, preserve angle units, and distinguish exact expressions from numerical approximations. |
