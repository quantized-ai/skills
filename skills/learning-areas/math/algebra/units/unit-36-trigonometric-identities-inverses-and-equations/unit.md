# Unit 36: Trigonometric identities, inverses, and equations

This unit completes the six trigonometric functions and develops identities, restricted inverses, and equation methods. Exact identities support algebraic reasoning; principal inverse values and periodicity together determine complete solution sets and contextual solutions.

## Main learning objectives

- Analyze reciprocal trigonometric functions and principal inverse functions with their domains, ranges, and graph features.
- Prove addition and subtraction identities, derive related identities, and preserve domains when rewriting expressions.
- Solve trigonometric equations exactly or numerically, describing periodic families and interpreting solutions in context.

## Concepts and topics

Reciprocal graphs; inverse sine, cosine, and tangent; principal branches; addition and subtraction formulas; double-angle and half-angle formulas; identity proofs; periodic equation families; numerical and contextual solutions.

## Lessons

- [36.1: Reciprocal trigonometric functions](lesson-1-reciprocal-trigonometric-functions/lesson.md) — Secant, cosecant, and cotangent complete the trigonometric function family through reciprocal and quotient relationships, with exclusions and graph behavior inherited from their denominators.
- [36.2: Inverse trigonometric functions](lesson-2-inverse-trigonometric-functions/lesson.md) — Inverse sine, cosine, and tangent reverse specified one-to-one branches, so their principal values differ from the complete angle solutions of a trigonometric equation.
- [36.3: Angle addition and subtraction](lesson-3-angle-addition-and-subtraction/lesson.md) — Addition and subtraction identities connect sums of angles to products of function values. Their proofs distinguish universal sine and cosine identities from tangent identities with domain restrictions.
- [36.4: Double-angle and half-angle identities](lesson-4-double-and-half-angle-identities/lesson.md) — Specializations of the addition formulas produce double-angle, power-reduction, and half-angle identities. Their use requires attention to signs and the domains of quotient expressions.
- [36.5: Proving trigonometric identities](lesson-5-proving-trigonometric-identities/lesson.md) — Identity reasoning uses valid transformations on a stated common domain. Reciprocal, quotient, Pythagorean, parity, cofunction, and angle formulas supply a connected set of tools.
- [36.6: Trigonometric equations and models](lesson-6-trigonometric-equations-and-models/lesson.md) — Trigonometric equation solving combines principal inverse values, unit-circle symmetries, periodicity, algebraic identities, and contextual domain restrictions.
