# Fresh-question generation: Unit 36 — Trigonometric identities, inverses, and equations

Every practice set, quiz and reassessment uses new questions chosen for the requested concept and current evidence. Fixed tutor examples and [calibration references](assessment.md) are not a student quiz. Read [agent-guide.md](agent-guide.md) and both files for the selected lesson.

## Generate, verify, then present

1. Select curriculum concepts and the exact still-missing proficiency components. Label a short quiz as a sample. Include procedural reasoning and a changed representation, interpretation, proof or model as appropriate; do not replace proof with arithmetic.
2. Choose a family from the table. Vary more than surface wording: change the unknown, data arrangement, representation or reasoning demand. Keep difficulty comparable on retries; do not introduce untaught requirements.
3. Construct consistent givens, explicit domains/units and enough information for a determinate answer, or explicitly ask the student to identify insufficient or impossible data.
4. Solve privately, with a complete key, accepted equivalents, required reasoning and approximation tolerance. Check with an independent route when available: substitution, exact arithmetic, inverse operation, geometric constraints, exhaustive finite enumeration or verified numerical tools. Test domain boundaries and exceptional cases. Reject and regenerate an uncertain item before showing it.
5. Compare with available history, then present one question without the key or suggestive answer choices. Feedback follows the student's response. Do not invent an external generator, randomness or persistent memory.

Declare angle units and principal inverse ranges. Check natural domains before using reciprocal, tangent-addition or half-angle quotient forms. For equations retain zero-factor branches, reject extraneous squared candidates, enumerate every permitted periodic solution and deduplicate endpoints. An identity proof must work on the stated common domain, not just match samples.

## Constructive families and verification

- **Domains before simplification:** select parent trig expressions and record all denominator and inverse-input restrictions before rewriting. Test a boundary input where an alternative representation fails, such as cot x versus 1/tan x. Never accept a canceled expression as a wider original domain.
- **Inverse/identity families:** choose an angle inside or outside a stated principal branch, or a signed ratio with sufficient quadrant information. Compute its principal representative independently with unit-circle reasoning. For half angles halve the interval; for addition quotients verify both individual cosines and the final denominator.
- **Equation construction:** choose a desired target ratio and affine argument, derive complete periodic families, then intersect them with a declared interval and deduplicate endpoints. Include impossible target magnitudes and coincident branches at extrema. For factoring/squaring variants explicitly test lost and extraneous candidates in the original.
- **Proof and graph transfer:** require a general addition/Pythagorean derivation or a common-domain identity chain, not sample equality. For transformed reciprocal graphs construct parent landmarks/asymptotes first and solve their input equations. For periodic models choose an actual time window and verify every event, using real numerical tools when needed.
