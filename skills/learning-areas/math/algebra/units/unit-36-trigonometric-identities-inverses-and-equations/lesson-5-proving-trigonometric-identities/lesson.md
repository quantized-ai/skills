# Lesson 36.5: Proving trigonometric identities

Identity reasoning uses valid transformations on a stated common domain. Reciprocal, quotient, Pythagorean, parity, cofunction, and angle formulas supply a connected set of tools.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the reciprocal Pythagorean identities and combine identities to rewrite expressions.
- Prove identities by justified transformations without assuming the conclusion.

## Proficiency criteria

- Restrict division and cancellation to their valid domains.
- Distinguish an identity proof from examples or the solution of an equation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reciprocal Pythagorean identities** | Dividing $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ by $\cos^2x$ gives $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$ when $\cos x\ne0$; division by $\sin^2x$ gives $1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x$ when $\sin x\ne0$. Rewriting in sine and cosine can expose common factors and denominators. | Derive and use the reciprocal Pythagorean identities to rewrite expressions on their valid domains. | Justify the nonzero divisor, retain its exclusions after cancellation, and coordinate reciprocal, quotient, and Pythagorean identities correctly. |
| **Identity proof and common domains** | An identity asserts equality for every input in a specified common domain. A proof transforms one side to the other or both to an established common expression using valid algebra and known identities. Cancellation, squaring, and multiplication by possibly zero expressions need domain or reversibility justification; isolated numerical matches are evidence only. | Prove trigonometric identities using justified algebraic transformations and explicitly state the common domain. | Give a logically complete chain without assuming the equality, justify every division or cancellation, and distinguish verified identities from equations true only at selected inputs. |
