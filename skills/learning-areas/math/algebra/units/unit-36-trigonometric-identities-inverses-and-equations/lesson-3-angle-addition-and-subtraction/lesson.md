# Lesson 36.3: Angle addition and subtraction

Addition and subtraction identities connect sums of angles to products of function values. Their proofs distinguish universal sine and cosine identities from tangent identities with domain restrictions.

## Learning objectives

- Prove sine, cosine, and tangent addition and subtraction formulas.
- Use the formulas to calculate exact values and rewrite trigonometric expressions with valid domains.

## Proficiency criteria

- Give a valid general derivation rather than numerical verification.
- Apply the correct signs and retain every tangent denominator condition.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sine and cosine addition formulas** | $\cos(u+v)=\cos u\cos v-\sin u\sin v$ and $\sin(u+v)=\sin u\cos v+\cos u\sin v$ hold for all real $u,v$. Unit-circle coordinate rotations or a geometric distance argument establish the identities for general angles; agreement at selected values does not prove an identity. | Prove the sine and cosine addition formulas and apply them to exact-value computation and expression rewriting. | Present a general geometric or algebraic derivation with justified identities, preserve both product terms, and give exact simplified values when component values are exact. |
| **Subtraction and cofunction identities** | Parity gives $\cos(u-v)=\cos u\cos v+\sin u\sin v$ and $\sin(u-v)=\sin u\cos v-\cos u\sin v$. Setting one angle to $\pi/2$ yields cofunction identities such as $\sin(\pi/2-u)=\cos u$. Reciprocal or quotient versions hold only where both sides are defined. | Derive sine and cosine subtraction and cofunction identities and use them to evaluate or simplify expressions. | Derive signs from parity or substitution, preserve the order in sine subtraction, and state restrictions for quotient or reciprocal cofunction identities. |
| **Tangent addition and subtraction** | $\tan(u\pm v)=(\tan u\pm\tan v)/(1\mp\tan u\tan v)$ follows by dividing the corresponding sine and cosine formulas. This quotient form requires $\cos u\ne0$, $\cos v\ne0$, and $\cos(u\pm v)\ne0$. The sine-and-cosine formulas can remain usable when this tangent form is undefined. | Prove and apply tangent addition and subtraction formulas with their complete domain restrictions. | Derive the quotient from sine and cosine, match the paired signs, and reject use of the tangent formula whenever an individual tangent or final denominator is undefined. |
