# Lesson 36.4: Double-angle and half-angle identities

Specializations of the addition formulas produce double-angle, power-reduction, and half-angle identities. Their use requires attention to signs and the domains of quotient expressions.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and use double-angle and power-reduction identities.
- Determine half-angle values with signs fixed by the half-angle location.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose an equivalent identity suited to the available information and retain denominator restrictions.
- Determine the half-angle quadrant before selecting a square-root sign.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Double-angle identities** | $\sin 2u=2\sin u\cos u$, $\cos 2u=\cos^2u-\sin^2u=1-2\sin^2u=2\cos^2u-1$, and $\tan 2u=2\tan u/(1-\tan^2u)$ where the quotient is defined. The tangent form additionally requires $\cos u\ne0$ and $\cos 2u\ne0$. | Derive the double-angle identities from addition formulas and use equivalent forms to compute exact values or simplify expressions. | Derive all three cosine forms, select forms consistent with known values, and state the restrictions on tangent calculations. |
| **Power reduction** | $\sin^2u=(1-\cos 2u)/2$ and $\cos^2u=(1+\cos 2u)/2$ follow by rearranging the cosine double-angle identities. Together with $\sin u\cos u=\sin 2u/2$, they rewrite specified quadratic trigonometric expressions without changing their real domains. | Derive power-reduction identities and rewrite squared sine, squared cosine, and sine-cosine products using double-angle functions. | Justify rearrangement, preserve the factor of one-half and correct signs, and verify that the rewritten expression retains the original domain. |
| **Half-angle values** | $\sin(u/2)=\pm\sqrt{(1-\cos u)/2}$ and $\cos(u/2)=\pm\sqrt{(1+\cos u)/2}$ require signs from the location of $u/2$. Where the relevant denominators are nonzero, $\tan(u/2)=\sin u/(1+\cos u)=(1-\cos u)/\sin u$; these alternative quotients do not share identical natural domains. | Derive half-angle identities and use angle-location information to select exact values and valid quotient forms. | Select each sign using the half-angle quadrant, distinguish boundary zeros from positive or negative values, and check the domain of the particular tangent quotient used. |
