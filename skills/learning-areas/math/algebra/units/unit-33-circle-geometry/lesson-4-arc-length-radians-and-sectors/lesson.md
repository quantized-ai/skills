# Lesson 33.4: Arc length, radians, and sectors

Similarity determines how fixed-angle arcs and sectors scale with radius.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the proportional relationship between arc length and radius and define radians.
- Derive and apply arc-length and sector-area formulas with consistent angle units.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the intended arc and central angle and distinguish angle measure, arc length, and area.
- Use proportionality or similarity to justify formulas and retain exact multiples of pi when applicable.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arcs and radian measure** | Similar circles show that the arc length \(s\) subtending a fixed central angle scales in the same ratio as radius \(r>0\). The invariant \(\theta=s/r\) defines the nonnegative radian measure of the chosen arc; one full turn is \(2\pi\) radians or \(360^\circ\). Thus \(s=r\theta\) in radians or \(s=(\alpha/360^\circ)2\pi r\) in degrees. Minor, major, and full arcs require the corresponding angular sweep. | Derive arc-length proportionality by similarity, define radians, and determine arc lengths and angle measures in degrees and radians. | Explain why the ratio is independent of radius, convert units correctly, and distinguish the length of an arc from its degree or radian measure. |
| **Sector and segment areas** | A sector occupies the same fraction of circle area as its central angle occupies of a full turn: \(K=(\alpha/360^\circ)\pi r^2=\tfrac12r^2\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\). A circular segment bounded by a chord and minor arc has area equal to sector area minus its central triangle; the corresponding major segment is the remaining circle area. | Derive the sector-area formula and use it to determine sector and circular-segment areas. | Justify the angular fraction, specify minor or major region, use radians in the half-radius-squared form, and subtract only regions contained in the selected sector. |
