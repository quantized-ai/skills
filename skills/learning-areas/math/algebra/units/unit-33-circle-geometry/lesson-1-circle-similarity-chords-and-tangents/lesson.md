# Lesson 33.1: Circle similarity, chords, and tangents

A circle’s constant-radius definition connects similarity with equal-distance and perpendicularity relationships.

## Learning objectives

- Prove all circles similar and relate equal chords, central angles, arcs, and distances from the center.
- Justify and apply radius-tangent perpendicularity and equal tangent lengths.

## Proficiency criteria

- Restrict chord comparisons to one circle or congruent circles and distinguish chord length from arc measure.
- Use the point of tangency in perpendicularity arguments and the same exterior point in equal-tangent arguments.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Circle similarity and chord relationships** | Translation aligns the centers of two circles and a positive dilation by their radius ratio maps one onto the other, proving all circles similar. A chord joins two points of a circle; a diameter passes through its center. In the same circle or congruent circles, congruent chords correspond to congruent minor arcs and central angles and equal perpendicular distances from the center. A perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects it, by right-triangle congruence. | Prove that all circles are similar and justify and apply relationships among chords, central angles, arcs, and distances from the center. | Specify the translation and scale factor, distinguish minor and major arcs, and state when circle radii must be equal for chord comparisons. |
| **Tangents and radii** | A tangent line meets a circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius there; the perpendicular from the center is the shortest distance to the line, which establishes tangency and its converse. The two tangent segments drawn from an exterior point to the same circle are congruent, by hypotenuse-leg congruence of the associated right triangles. | Prove and apply radius-tangent perpendicularity and the equality of tangent segments from a common exterior point. | Identify the point of tangency and the shared exterior endpoint and justify each theorem with distance or congruence reasoning. |
