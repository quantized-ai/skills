# Lesson 33.3: Circle segment products

Triangle similarity gives multiplicative length relationships for intersecting chords, secants, and tangents.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and apply intersecting-chord, secant-secant, and tangent-secant product theorems.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish whole secants from exterior segments and reject negative or geometrically inconsistent length solutions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intersecting chord products** | When chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at an interior point \(P\), similar triangles established by vertical and inscribed angles give \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\). Each factor is one subsegment measured from the shared interior point. | Prove and apply the intersecting-chord product theorem using triangle similarity. | Establish the correct triangle correspondence and pair the two portions of each chord; verify positivity and full-length relationships in recovered values. |
| **Secant and tangent products** | For an exterior point \(P\) and secants meeting the circle in near/far pairs \(A,B\) and \(C,D\), similarity yields \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\), using exterior times whole length. If \(PT\) is a tangent, \(PT^2=PA\cdot PB\); a tangent-chord angle and inscribed angle establish the needed similar triangles. | Prove and apply secant-secant and tangent-secant length relationships. | Use the full distance to the farther circle point rather than only the interior segment, justify the similarity proof, and check every algebraic solution against the exterior-point configuration. |
