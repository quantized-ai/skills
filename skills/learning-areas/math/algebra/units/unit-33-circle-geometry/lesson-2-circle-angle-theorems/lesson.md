# Lesson 33.2: Circle angle theorems

Intercepted arcs determine angles on, inside, and outside a circle.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and apply central, inscribed, tangent-chord, and cyclic-quadrilateral angle relationships.
- Determine angles formed by intersecting chords, secants, and tangents from their intercepted arcs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the correct intercepted arcs and distinguish half-sums from half-differences.
- Justify inscribed and cyclic-quadrilateral claims, including diameter and supplementary-angle consequences.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Central and inscribed angles** | A central angle has its vertex at the center and measures its intercepted arc in degrees. An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and sides along chords; its measure is half the intercepted arc not containing its vertex. A proof uses radii, isosceles triangles, and the angle sum, splitting into configurations as needed. Angles intercepting the same arc are congruent, and an angle subtending a diameter is right. | Prove the inscribed-angle theorem and use central and inscribed angles to determine arc and angle measures. | Account for the center lying on a side, inside, or outside the angle in the proof, and choose the arc opposite the angle’s vertex. |
| **Cyclic quadrilaterals** | A quadrilateral inscribed in a circle has opposite interior angles supplementary because their intercepted arcs together form the full circle. Conversely, a nondegenerate convex quadrilateral with a supplementary pair of opposite angles is cyclic. An exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the opposite interior angle. | Prove and apply angle properties of cyclic quadrilaterals and use the supplementary-opposite-angle converse. | Identify opposite rather than adjacent angles, explain the intercepted-arc proof, and retain convexity for the stated converse. |
| **Tangent, chord, and secant angles** | A tangent-chord angle measures half its intercepted arc. An angle formed by two chords intersecting inside a circle measures half the sum of the arcs intercepted by it and its vertical angle. An exterior angle formed by two secants, two tangents, or a tangent and secant measures half the positive difference of the far and near intercepted arcs. In particular, the angle between two tangents is supplementary to the corresponding minor central angle. | Justify and apply tangent-chord, interior-chord, and exterior-secant or tangent angle relationships. | Identify the angle location before choosing a formula, label both arcs, and derive the sum or difference relationship from inscribed angles and triangle angle relationships. |
