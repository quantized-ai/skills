# Lesson 9.7: Rational-power equations and root formulas

Solve rational-power equations without losing sign branches, and construct and verify square-root models from specified endpoint and table data.

## Learning objectives

- Solve rational-power equations while preserving all valid sign branches and original-domain restrictions.
- Construct and verify a square-root model in a specified family, explain the limits of a finite-data fit, and solve for allowed inputs.

## Learning outcome

Find complete real solution sets for rational-power equations and verify a root model against its data, chosen family, and domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Interpret a reduced rational exponent and its domain before choosing a root substitution or inverse operation.
- Retain every sign branch allowed by the original root and any intermediate even-power equation.
- Determine square-root model parameters from a specified endpoint and a distinct permitted point.
- Use technology to check remaining table values, explain that finite data do not establish a unique function family, and verify target solutions against the original model and contextual domain.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equations with rational powers** | Interpret rational exponents in lowest terms. Even denominators require nonnegative bases; negative exponents also exclude zero. Root substitution converts a rational-power equation into a power equation while exposing admissible signs. A positive even integer power can have two signed solutions when its input permits both, one when its target is zero, or no real solution for a negative target. Applying a principal reciprocal power alone can discard valid branches. Verify candidates in the original expression. | Solve real rational-power equations, including negative exponents, with their original domain restrictions. Choose root substitutions or sign cases that preserve all allowed solutions and verify them in the original equation. | Choose a substitution or sign analysis that matches the reduced exponent. Retain all branches permitted by the root and resulting power equation. Enforce even-index and negative-exponent restrictions, and verify each retained candidate in the original expression. |
| **Formulating square-root equations from tables** | In the specified family $y=a\sqrt{x-h}+k$ with $a\ne0$, the endpoint fixes $(h,k)$ and a point $(x_1,y_1)$ with $x_1>h$ and $y_1\ne k$ fixes $a=(y_1-k)/\sqrt{x_1-h}$. Remaining table values must agree with the model; technology tables or graphs check that agreement. A finite matching table does not determine the function family uniquely. Target-output equations require the radicand domain $x\ge h$, the range implied by the sign of $a$, and any contextual input bounds. | Construct a square-root function in the specified family from an endpoint and a distinct compatible point, verify the remaining data with technology, and explain the limits of a finite-data fit. Solve target-output equations and verify solutions against the original model, its range and domain, and contextual input restrictions. | Use the endpoint to fix shifts and a permitted non-endpoint point to determine the nonzero scale. Record a technology table or graph that checks the remaining data. Distinguish a fit within the chosen family from proof of a unique family. Check target attainability and retain only inputs that satisfy the original model and all domain restrictions. |
