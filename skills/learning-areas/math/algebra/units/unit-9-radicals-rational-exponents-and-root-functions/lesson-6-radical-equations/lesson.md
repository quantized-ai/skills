# Lesson 9.6: Radical equations

Solve real radical equations using isolation, valid power operations, and checks that distinguish solutions from extraneous candidates.

## Learning objectives

- Solve square-root equations, including equations with two radicals, while applying domain and sign restrictions.
- Solve cube-root equations and explain why squaring and cubing have different effects on equivalence.

## Learning outcome

Find and verify complete real solution sets of radical equations and explain any rejected candidates.

## Proficiency criteria

- Determine real-domain and principal-root sign restrictions before removing radicals.
- Isolate a radical before squaring or cubing and retain every cross term in an expanded square.
- Check every candidate from squaring in the original equation, including after repeated squaring.
- Explain why cubing real quantities is reversible while squaring can merge opposite values.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Square-root equations and extraneous candidates** | In an isolated equation $\sqrt{A(x)}=B(x)$, the real radicand requires $A(x)\ge0$ and the principal-root sign requires $B(x)\ge0$. Squaring gives $A(x)=B(x)^2$; this is equivalent to the original only with the required domain and sign conditions. Without them, squaring can introduce candidates by merging opposite signs. Substitute candidates into the original equation and distinguish undefined radicals from defined but unequal sides. | Solve square-root equations by isolation and squaring with the required real-domain and sign conditions. Verify candidates in the original equation and explain why any extraneous candidates are rejected. | Isolate the principal root and impose the appropriate radicand and opposite-side sign conditions. Solve the squared equation without assuming unrestricted equivalence. Check original values and identify whether rejection follows from an undefined expression or unequal signed values. |
| **Two radicals and cube-root equations** | Equations with two square roots may require successive isolation and squaring; each binomial square includes its cross term, and all original radicand restrictions persist. Repeated squaring produces candidates requiring original-equation checks. Cubing is one-to-one over the reals: $u^3=v^3$ exactly when $u=v$. Isolating and cubing a cube root therefore introduces no extraneous real candidates by that operation itself; restrictions and other nonreversible steps still apply. | Solve simple equations containing two square roots using staged isolation, complete expansion, and original-equation checks. Solve cube-root equations and explain the equivalence preserved by cubing compared with squaring. | Isolate and square in stages while retaining the full binomial square and domain restrictions. Verify all candidates in the original two-radical equation. Isolate and cube to solve cube-root equations, explaining why cubing itself is reversible for real quantities. |
