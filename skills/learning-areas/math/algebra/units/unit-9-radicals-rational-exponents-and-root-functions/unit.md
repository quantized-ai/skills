# Unit 9: Radicals, rational exponents, and root functions

Real roots and exponent rules connect radical arithmetic, root graphs, and equations while preserving domains and checking candidates.

## Main learning objectives

- Evaluate real nth roots and distinguish radical values from complete power-equation solution sets. Simplify roots of powers and justify required absolute values and sign conditions.
- Translate and evaluate rational powers using reduced fractional exponents and the stated real-number convention. Simplify with valid exponent laws while preserving the original domain and sign.
- Extract perfect powers from numerical and variable radicals while preserving signs and domains. Add, subtract, and multiply radical expressions using like terms, distribution, and valid root identities.
- Simplify radical quotients and rationalize one-term or binomial denominators. Explain denominator restrictions and account for inputs lost by a rationalizing multiplier.
- Graph transformed square-root and cube-root functions using exact parent-point mappings. Determine domains, ranges, endpoints or centers, and increasing or decreasing behavior from the transformation parameters.
- Solve square-root equations, including equations with two radicals, while applying domain and sign restrictions. Solve cube-root equations and explain why squaring and cubing have different effects on equivalence.
- Solve rational-power equations while preserving all valid sign branches and original-domain restrictions. Construct and verify a square-root model in a specified family, explain the limits of a finite-data fit, and solve for allowed inputs.

## Concepts and topics

Even and odd nth roots; Absolute value when extracting even powers; Rational exponents as roots; Exponent laws with stated hypotheses; Extracting perfect powers; Adding, subtracting, and multiplying radicals; Radical quotients and monomial denominators; Conjugate denominators; Square-root graphs and transformations; Cube-root graphs and transformations; Square-root equations and extraneous candidates; Two radicals and cube-root equations; Equations with rational powers; Formulating square-root equations from tables.
