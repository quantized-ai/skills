## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** A learner simplifies (1+1/x)/(1−1/x) to (x+1)/(x−1) and allows x=0. Explain why this is invalid.

**Private reasoning key:** The original inner fractions require x≠0, and its outer denominator also excludes 1. Multiplying top and bottom by x is valid only on that original nonzero domain.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Complex fractions by division

Curriculum reference: **Complex fractions by division** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Simplify $(1+1/x)/(1-1/x)$.

**Agent key:** Inner expressions require $x\ne0$ and the divisor requires $x\ne1$; reduction gives $(x+1)/(x-1)$ on that domain.

**Worked example:** Simplify $(2/x)/(4/x^2)$.

**Worked reasoning:** Multiply by the reciprocal: $(2/x)(x^2/4)=x/2$ with $x\ne0$.


#### Teaching sequence

Identify the complete numerator and denominator of the outer fraction before doing algebra. Simplify their internal fractions if useful, then divide by the entire lower expression using its reciprocal. List restrictions from every inner denominator plus the condition that the complete outer denominator is nonzero. Keep them after cancellation.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** What are the two complete operands of the outer division?

If the visual fraction bar is read as several independent divisions, rewrite the whole expression as (numerator)÷(denominator). If only inner denominators are checked, solve where the whole lower expression vanishes.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Begin with monomial inner fractions, then sums in one or both outer parts and a lower expression that vanishes at a new input. Compare an unsimplified and reduced evaluation at an allowed input.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess structural parsing, both levels of restrictions, complete reciprocal and verified simplification. The final domain must describe the original complex fraction.

### Complex fractions by clearing inner denominators

Curriculum reference: **Complex fractions by clearing inner denominators** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Clear inner denominators in $(1/x+1/y)/(1/x-1/y)$.

**Agent key:** Multiplying both parts by xy gives $(x+y)/(y-x)$; require $x\ne0,y\ne0,y\ne x$.

**Worked example:** Explain why multiplying only the top of a complex fraction by an LCD changes its value.

**Worked reasoning:** The whole quotient must be multiplied by a factor of 1: the same nonzero LCD in numerator and denominator. Both complete parts must be distributed.


#### Teaching sequence

Choose an LCD for the inner fractions and multiply both complete outer parts by it. Distribute across every term; this is multiplication by LCD/LCD, valid where that LCD is nonzero. In the two-variable example, the lower part becomes y−x, so x,y≠0 and y≠x remain necessary. Compare with the division method once understood.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Has the LCD multiplied every term on both sides of the outer bar?

If only the outer numerator is multiplied, ask what happened to the quotient's value. If one inner term is missed, expand the distributed multiplication. If x=y is allowed after clearing, evaluate the original outer denominator there.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Use one-variable sums first, then repeated factors and two-variable cases. Ask the learner to choose between clearing and division and verify agreement.

#### Assessment evidence

Require equivalent whole-part multiplication, complete distribution, original restrictions including outer zeros, and a justified simplified expression. A cleared formula may have a larger natural domain than the original.
