# Lesson 7.5: Complex rational expressions

Complex rational expressions contain fractions within a numerator or denominator. Simplifying the two parts before division and clearing inner denominators are equivalent methods when all original restrictions are retained.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify complex rational expressions by both reciprocal division and multiplication by an inner least common denominator.
- Justify equivalence while retaining exclusions from inner denominators and the entire original divisor.

## Learning outcome

The student can use both complex-fraction methods and explain why each preserves values on the original domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the complete numerator and denominator of the outer quotient.
- Demonstrate both methods: simplify the two parts before division, and distribute an inner LCD across every term in both parts.
- Require all inner denominators and the entire original divisor to be nonzero.
- Justify equivalence and explain why simplification cannot restore excluded inputs.

## Concepts

| **Complex fractions by division** | A complex rational expression is a quotient whose numerator or denominator contains rational expressions. The outer fraction bar divides the complete numerator by the complete denominator. Simplify each part, then multiply by the reciprocal of the complete denominator. All inner expressions must be defined, and the entire denominator must be nonzero. | Simplify a complex rational expression by identifying the outer quotient, simplifying its two parts, and using reciprocal multiplication. Determine and retain restrictions from all inner denominators and the entire original divisor. | Identify both operands of the outer division; simplify each before reciprocal multiplication; find all inner-denominator exclusions and, among the remaining inputs, every zero of the entire divisor; retain every exclusion. |
| **Complex fractions by clearing inner denominators** | Multiplying the entire numerator and denominator by a common inner LCD clears their fractions. The same multiplier must distribute to every term in both parts. This preserves the quotient wherever the multiplier is defined and nonzero; choose an LCD nonzero on the original domain and retain both inner-denominator and whole-divisor restrictions. | Simplify a complex rational expression by choosing an inner LCD and distributing it to the entire numerator and denominator. Justify the nonzero-multiplier condition and explain why simplification retains the entire original domain. | Choose an LCD that clears every inner denominator and is nonzero on the original domain; distribute across both complete parts; reduce and justify equivalence; retain all original restrictions. |
