# Lesson 7.6: Structure and closure

Polynomial division expresses a rational expression as a polynomial quotient plus a remainder fraction. Symbolic fraction identities establish closure, while original domains govern where each operation can be evaluated.

## Learning objectives

- Rewrite rational expressions in quotient-plus-remainder form and verify the polynomial division identity.
- Justify closure under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by a nonzero rational expression; distinguish algebraic closure from input restrictions.

## Learning outcome

The student can verify quotient-plus-remainder representations and explain both algebraic and input conditions for rational operations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Find a quotient and a remainder that is zero or has degree below the divisor degree.
- Verify the division identity by multiplication and retain original denominator restrictions.
- Use general polynomial numerators and denominators to justify closure under each of the four operations.
- Distinguish an identically zero divisor from a nonzero rational expression with some zero values, and state the resulting input restrictions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quotient-plus-remainder forms** | For polynomials $p,q$ with $q$ not identically zero, polynomial division gives $p=qQ+R$, where $R=0$ or $\deg R<\deg q$. Thus $p/q=Q+R/q$ where $q(x)\ne0$; a nonzero remainder fraction is proper. Original denominator exclusions remain even when $R=0$. | Rewrite a rational expression as a polynomial quotient plus a remainder fraction whose remainder is zero or has degree below the divisor degree. Verify the polynomial division identity by multiplication and retain the original denominator restrictions. | Produce quotient and remainder satisfying the degree condition; recombine to recover the original numerator; state the original exclusions, including when the remainder is zero. |
| **Closure and rational-number analogies** | For polynomials $p,q,r,s$ with $q,s$ not identically zero, $p/q\pm r/s=(ps\pm rq)/(qs)$ and $(p/q)(r/s)=pr/(qs)$ are rational. If $r$ is not identically zero, $(p/q)\div(r/s)=ps/(qr)$ is also rational. These identities establish algebraic closure; evaluation requires $q,s\ne0$ and, for division, $r\ne0$. A nonzero rational expression can have zero values at individual inputs. | Use general polynomial numerators and denominators to justify closure of rational expressions under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by a nonzero rational expression. Distinguish an identically zero divisor from a nonzero rational expression that may take zero values, and state the input restrictions for each operation. | Express each operation as a quotient of polynomials with denominator not identically zero; require a nonzero rational-expression divisor; separately exclude undefined operands and zero divisor values when evaluating. |
