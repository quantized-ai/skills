# Lesson 7.3: Products and quotients

Multiplication and division extend fraction operations to polynomial quotients. Products require both factors to be defined; division also requires the entire divisor to be nonzero.

## Learning objectives

- Multiply and divide rational expressions in factored form, justifying cancellation and reciprocal multiplication.
- Determine and retain restrictions from all original denominators and any zero values of a divisor.

## Learning outcome

The student can simplify rational products and quotients and explain the distinct domain conditions for each operation.

## Proficiency criteria

- Organize products so canceled quantities are visible as common factors.
- Replace division by multiplication by the reciprocal of the entire divisor, preserving operand order.
- Exclude every input where an original operand is undefined and, for division, where the divisor is zero.
- State a simplified expression with the domain of the original operation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Multiplication of rational expressions** | For polynomial numerators $p,r$ and denominators $q,s$, $(p/q)(r/s)=pr/(qs)$ where $q,s\ne0$. Cross-cancellation is cancellation of common factors in this combined fraction. The product domain is the intersection of the original factor domains, even when reduction removes denominator factors. | Multiply rational expressions, justify cross-cancellation, and reduce the resulting factors. Determine and retain the domain inherited from both original factors. | Combine numerator and denominator factors; justify cross-cancellation in the combined fraction; give a reduced result restricted to inputs where both original factors are defined. |
| **Division and nonzero divisors** | Division by a rational expression means multiplication by its reciprocal: $(p/q)\div(r/s)=ps/(qr)$. Evaluation requires $q\ne0$, $s\ne0$, and $r\ne0$. The divisor must be both defined and nonzero; original exclusions persist even if absent from the reduced denominator. | Divide rational expressions by taking the reciprocal of the entire divisor and simplifying the product. Identify and retain exclusions from undefined original operands and zero divisor values, including restrictions absent from the final formula. | Preserve operand order and reciprocate the entire divisor; simplify by valid factor cancellation; separately identify undefined operands and zero divisor values; retain all resulting exclusions. |
