## Teaching boundaries

Cancel entire factors; do not cancel matching pieces of sums. The concept-level evidence checklists below implement the existing curriculum; they do not create extra objectives.

## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** Test (x+4)/(x+6)=2/3 by canceling x and reducing 4/6.

**Private reasoning key:** At x=2 the original is 6/8=3/4, not 2/3. The x terms are not common factors of the whole numerator and denominator.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Canceling factors, not terms

Curriculum reference: **Canceling factors, not terms** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Simplify $(x^2+3x)/(x^2-9)$.

**Agent key:** Factor to $x(x+3)/[(x-3)(x+3)]$, giving $x/(x-3)$ with $x\ne-3,3$.

**Worked example:** Is $(x+2)/(x+3)=2/3$ after canceling x?

**Worked reasoning:** No. Terms cannot be canceled; at x=1 the sides are $3/4$ and $2/3$.


#### Teaching sequence

Factor complete numerators and denominators before canceling. Mark common multiplicative factors, not matching symbols inside sums. Verify the reduced form by multiplying back or cross-multiplying on the original allowed domain. Keep all exclusions, including a factor absent from the final denominator.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Is the proposed cancellation a factor of the entire numerator and denominator?

If x is canceled from (x+2)/(x+3), substitute x=1 to refute it, then distinguish sums from products. If a numerator zero is automatically excluded, ask whether the denominator is also zero there.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Start with visible factors, then require factoring and retain canceled restrictions. Contrast valid cancellation with visually similar invalid term cancellation and reverse-build equivalent restricted formulas.

#### Assessment evidence

Require complete-factor cancellation, all original exclusions, a justified equivalence and an error diagnosis. A coincident value at one input cannot validate an invalid cancellation rule.

### Opposite factors and signs

Curriculum reference: **Opposite factors and signs** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Simplify $(2-x)/(x-2)$ with restrictions.

**Agent key:** Since $2-x=-(x-2)$, the value is $-1$ for $x\ne2$.

**Worked example:** Simplify $(x-3)^2/(3-x)^3$.

**Worked reasoning:** The denominator is $-(x-3)^3$, so the result is $-1/(x-3)$, with $x\ne3$.


#### Teaching sequence

Rewrite opposite linear factors using one explicit −1 before cancellation. When powers occur, keep the exponent on −1: odd powers reverse sign and even powers do not. Factor every remaining expression and record the original denominator roots before reducing. Check a permitted numerical input to detect a lost global sign.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** How many factors of $-1$ does the power introduce?

If 2−x is treated as x−2, evaluate at zero and derive the negative relation. If a cube is treated like a square, expand the sign factor separately. If a constant result is declared unrestricted, return to the original denominator.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Vary odd/even multiplicities, opposite-factor locations and constant reductions. Ask for the sign of a result before the complete arithmetic.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess opposite-factor identification, parity, cancellation and inherited restrictions. Numerical checking supplements a symbolic factor argument rather than replacing it.
