# Unit 7: Rational expressions

Equivalent rational expressions and their arithmetic preserve original denominator exclusions and distinguish cancellation of factors from terms.

## Main learning objectives

- Classify rational expressions and evaluate them only at allowed real inputs. Simplify by canceling common nonzero factors; compare original and reduced formulas with their domains.
- Simplify rational expressions by factoring and canceling common nonzero factors while preserving signs and restrictions. Justify valid reductions algebraically and diagnose invalid cancellation.
- Multiply and divide rational expressions in factored form, justifying cancellation and reciprocal multiplication. Determine and retain restrictions from all original denominators and any zero values of a divisor.
- Add and subtract rational expressions with common or unlike denominators, distributing subtraction over entire numerators. Construct a least common denominator, justify equivalent-fraction steps, and retain original restrictions after simplification.
- Simplify complex rational expressions by both reciprocal division and multiplication by an inner least common denominator. Justify equivalence while retaining exclusions from inner denominators and the entire original divisor.
- Rewrite rational expressions in quotient-plus-remainder form and verify the polynomial division identity. Justify closure under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by a nonzero rational expression; distinguish algebraic closure from input restrictions.

## Concepts and topics

Rational expressions and allowed inputs; Original domains and equivalent formulas; Canceling factors, not terms; Opposite factors and signs; Multiplication of rational expressions; Division and nonzero divisors; Common denominators; Least common denominators; Complex fractions by division; Complex fractions by clearing inner denominators; Quotient-plus-remainder forms; Closure and rational-number analogies.

## Lessons

- [7.1: Definitions and restrictions](lesson-1-definitions-and-restrictions/lesson.md) — Rational expressions are quotients of polynomials with explicit input restrictions. Classification, evaluation, and cancellation depend on the original domain, even when a reduced formula has a larger natural domain.
- [7.2: Simplification by factoring](lesson-2-simplification-by-factoring/lesson.md) — Factoring reveals the multiplicative structure that permits cancellation in rational expressions. Opposite factors require sign changes, and every restriction from the original denominator remains in force.
- [7.3: Products and quotients](lesson-3-products-and-quotients/lesson.md) — Multiplication and division extend fraction operations to polynomial quotients. Products require both factors to be defined; division also requires the entire divisor to be nonzero.
- [7.4: Addition and subtraction](lesson-4-addition-and-subtraction/lesson.md) — Addition and subtraction combine numerators only after a common denominator is established. Factored denominators identify the least common denominator, while original denominator restrictions survive reduction.
- [7.5: Complex rational expressions](lesson-5-complex-rational-expressions/lesson.md) — Complex rational expressions contain fractions within a numerator or denominator. Simplifying the two parts before division and clearing inner denominators are equivalent methods when all original restrictions are retained.
- [7.6: Structure and closure](lesson-6-structure-and-closure/lesson.md) — Polynomial division expresses a rational expression as a polynomial quotient plus a remainder fraction. Symbolic fraction identities establish closure, while original domains govern where each operation can be evaluated.
