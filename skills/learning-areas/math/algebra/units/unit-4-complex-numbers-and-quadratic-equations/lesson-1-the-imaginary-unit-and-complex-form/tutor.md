## Concept guidance

### The imaginary unit and negative square roots

Curriculum reference: **The imaginary unit and negative square roots** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Simplify the principal square root $\sqrt{-32}$ in complex form.

**Agent key:** $4\sqrt2\,i$. This is one principal value; the equation $z^2=-32$ has both signs.

**Worked example:** Compare $\sqrt{-4}\sqrt{-9}$ with $\sqrt{36}$.

**Worked reasoning:** The first is $(2i)(3i)=-6$; the second is 6. The real nonnegative-radicand product rule cannot be extended blindly.


#### Teaching sequence

Introduce i through i²=−1, then write a negative real radicand as a positive magnitude times −1. Define √(−a)=i√a for a>0 as the principal value; contrast it with solving z²=−a, which gives both signs. Do not extend the real product-root law to arbitrary complex radicands: √(−1)√(−1)=−1 whereas √1=1.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Are you evaluating a principal root or solving an equation?

If ± is attached to a principal root, separate an expression's chosen value from an equation's solution set. If two negative radicals are multiplied under one root, evaluate each using i before multiplying and explain the failed hypothesis.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Start with negative perfect squares, then simplify nonperfect radicands, contrast expressions and equations, and test a proposed root-product identity.

#### Assessment evidence

Require i²=−1, a correctly simplified principal negative-real root, both equation roots when requested, and an explanation of why unrestricted root-product manipulation fails.

### Real and imaginary parts of a complex number

Curriculum reference: **Real and imaginary parts of a complex number** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** State the real and imaginary parts of $-3-5i$.

**Agent key:** They are $-3$ and $-5$, respectively; the imaginary part is the real coefficient, not $-5i$.

**Worked example:** Classify $0$, $7$, and $-2i$ as real or nonreal complex numbers.

**Worked reasoning:** All are complex; 0 and 7 are real, while $-2i$ is nonreal and purely imaginary.


#### Teaching sequence

Write every value as a+bi with real a and b. Zero can fill either component: a real value has b=0 and a purely imaginary value has a=0. Explain that the imaginary part is the real coefficient b, not the term bi. Equality requires agreement of both parts, so compare components separately before solving any parameter conditions.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** In $a+bi$, which real numbers are $a$ and $b$?

If the imaginary part of 3−5i is called −5i, distinguish the coefficient from its product with i. If all complex numbers are called nonreal, place 4=4+0i within the same form.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Sort real, purely imaginary and general nonreal examples, then recover missing real coefficients from equal complex expressions. Include zero without forcing it into only one applicable category.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess a,b identification, signed imaginary coefficients, embedded real numbers and componentwise equality. Classification must follow the stated definitions rather than mutually exclusive labels invented by the tutor.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

## Decision model and graduated practice

Model $\sqrt{-18}=i\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2\,i$. Squaring this one principal value gives $-18$; solving $z^2=-18$ also allows its negative. In $-4+3\sqrt2\,i$, the imaginary part is the real coefficient $3\sqrt2$. For $(a-1)+(b+2)i=3-5i$, equality of components gives $a=4,b=-7$.

If the learner appends $\pm$ to the radical, cue “Is the question asking for a value or all solutions?”; then place $\sqrt{-18}=\square$ beside $z^2=-18$; only next supply the positive-imaginary principal value, leaving both equation roots. Fade with $\sqrt{-12}$ versus $w^2=-12$ (keys $2\sqrt3\,i$ and $\pm2\sqrt3\,i$). Do not repair a correct root simplification by reteaching component notation unless their response shows that separate confusion.
