# Lesson 4.1: The imaginary unit and complex form

Extend the real number system with the imaginary unit and standard complex form. Separate a radical’s principal value from the solutions of an equation and identify the components of complex numbers.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify principal square roots of negative real numbers using the imaginary unit and valid radical rules.
- Distinguish a principal square root from the complete solution set of a square equation.
- Write, classify, and compare complex numbers using their real and imaginary parts.

## Learning outcome

Express and classify complex numbers while explaining the conventions governing negative square roots.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use $i^2=-1$ to simplify negative real square roots without applying invalid square-root product rules.
- Distinguish the principal radical value from both solutions of a negative-square equation.
- Identify real and imaginary coefficients, classify special cases, and compare corresponding components.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The imaginary unit and negative square roots** | The imaginary unit satisfies $i^2=-1$. For real $a>0$, the principal value is $\sqrt{-a}=i\sqrt a$, while $x^2=-a$ has two solutions $x=\pm i\sqrt a$. The radical symbol denotes one value. The product identity $\sqrt{uv}=\sqrt u\sqrt v$ holds for nonnegative real $u,v$ but is not valid without restrictions for negative or complex inputs. | Use $i^2=-1$ to simplify principal square roots of negative real numbers and state the restrictions on the square-root product rule. Distinguish a principal square root from both solutions of $x^2=-a$ for real $a>0$. | Use the defining relation to simplify negative real square roots. State one principal value and both equation solutions. Identify when applying the nonnegative-real square-root product rule is invalid. |
| **Real and imaginary parts of a complex number** | Every complex number has a unique form $a+bi$ with real $a,b$. Its real part is $a$ and imaginary part is $b$, not $bi$. Real numbers have $b=0$; nonreal numbers have $b\ne0$. Here, purely imaginary means $a=0$, $b\ne0$, so zero is classified as real and complex. Two complex numbers are equal exactly when corresponding real and imaginary parts agree. | Write a complex number in standard form and identify its real and imaginary parts. Classify complex numbers, including zero, and determine equality by comparing real and imaginary components. | Write real coefficients in standard complex form. Identify the imaginary coefficient rather than the imaginary term. Apply the stated classification convention and equate corresponding components. |
