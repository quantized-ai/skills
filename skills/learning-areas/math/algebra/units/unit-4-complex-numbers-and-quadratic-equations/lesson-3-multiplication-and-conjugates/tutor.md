## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** Is (2+3i)(2−3i)=4−9? Explain the decisive sign.

**Private reasoning key:** The product is 4−9i²=13 because i²=−1. The imaginary cross terms cancel, and the resulting real value is the sum of two squares.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Multiplying complex numbers

Curriculum reference: **Multiplying complex numbers** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Multiply $(2+3i)(1-4i)$.

**Agent key:** $2-8i+3i-12i^2=14-5i$.

**Worked example:** Square $3-2i$ and explain its real part.

**Worked reasoning:** $9-12i+4i^2=5-12i$; the last term subtracts 4 because $i^2=-1$.


#### Teaching sequence

Distribute complex binomials as polynomials first, leaving i² visible. Only then replace i² by −1 and collect real and imaginary parts. In (a+bi)(c+di), the bd contribution is real and negative; the cross terms supply the imaginary coefficient ad+bc. Ask the learner to predict which terms become real before calculating.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Where does $i^2$ appear after distribution?

If i² is retained as an independent component, use the defining relation. If the bd term remains positive, isolate (bi)(di)=bd(−1). If a cross term is missing, use a two-by-two multiplication grid.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Begin with multiplication by i, then signed binomials, squares and purely real products. Compare expanded arithmetic with a proposed result by checking all components.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess complete distribution, i² substitution, signed collection and standard form. A real final answer is allowed even when both factors are nonreal.

### Conjugate products and complex factorization

Curriculum reference: **Conjugate products and complex factorization** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Compute $(4+3i)(4-3i)$.

**Agent key:** The mixed terms cancel and $16-9i^2=25$.

**Worked example:** Factor $x^2+16$ over the reals and then over the complex numbers.

**Worked reasoning:** It has no real linear factorization; over the complex numbers it is $(x-4i)(x+4i)$.


#### Teaching sequence

Define conjugation as preserving a and reversing b. Expand (a+bi)(a−bi), show imaginary cross terms cancel and i² turns −b²i² into +b². The result a²+b² is positive except when both components are zero. Use the same identity backward to factor suitable real quadratics over the complex numbers.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which conjugate product becomes a sum of real squares?

If the conjugate of a−bi is −a+bi, ask which component should stay fixed. If the product is a²−b², isolate the i² sign. If positivity is claimed at zero, substitute a=b=0.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Compare conjugation with negation, calculate conjugate products and factor a sum of squares over complex coefficients. Include a real number equal to its conjugate.

#### Assessment evidence

Require correct conjugates, cancellation reasoning, the zero exception and reconstruction of complex factors. State the coefficient system before judging completeness.
