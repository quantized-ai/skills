# Lesson 53.3: Prisoners’ dilemma and chicken

Two canonical partial-conflict structures illustrate how individual incentives and collective outcomes can diverge.

## Learning objectives

- Identify and model prisoners’ dilemma structures in social situations and analyze their equilibria and inefficiency.
- Identify and model chicken structures and compare their strategic incentives with prisoners’ dilemma.

## Proficiency criteria

- Verify both players’ payoff orderings, demonstrate unilateral incentives and the jointly better alternative, and state why the model’s assumptions matter.
- Verify payoff rankings and best responses, identify the asymmetric equilibria, and distinguish a threat or commitment from a guaranteed prediction.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prisoners’ dilemma** | A prisoners’ dilemma gives each player a strictly dominant defection strategy while mutual cooperation benefits both more than mutual defection. The ranking of unilateral temptation, mutual cooperation, mutual defection, and being exploited defines the structure, not the narrative labels. | Identify and model prisoners’ dilemma structures in social situations and analyze their equilibria and inefficiency. | Verify both players’ payoff orderings, demonstrate unilateral incentives and the jointly better alternative, and state why the model’s assumptions matter. |
| **Chicken and coordination over conflict** | In chicken, each player prefers the other to yield, but mutual escalation is worst; yielding to an escalating opponent is preferable to mutual escalation. The two asymmetric pure equilibria create a conflict over which equilibrium occurs. | Identify and model chicken structures and compare their strategic incentives with prisoners’ dilemma. | Verify payoff rankings and best responses, identify the asymmetric equilibria, and distinguish a threat or commitment from a guaranteed prediction. |
