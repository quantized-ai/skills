# Lesson 53.1: Strategic games and payoff representations

Game models make players, available choices, and consequences explicit before equilibrium is analyzed.

## Learning objectives

- Recognize strategic situations and represent zero-sum and partial-conflict games with payoff matrices.
- Identify dominated strategies, best responses, and pure-strategy Nash equilibria.

## Proficiency criteria

- Specify both players and every strategy pair, identify whose payoff is shown, distinguish ordinal from cardinal information, and state omitted real-world factors.
- Compare one player’s payoffs while fixing the opponent’s strategy, account for ties, and distinguish a unilateral incentive test from joint payoff improvement.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Games, payoffs, and strategy sets** | A finite strategic game specifies players, each player’s strategies, and payoffs for every joint choice. A two-player zero-sum payoff matrix records one player’s gain and the other’s equal loss; a bimatrix records two potentially conflicting payoffs. Ordinal rankings encode order but not meaningful payoff differences. | Recognize strategic situations and represent zero-sum and partial-conflict games with payoff matrices. | Specify both players and every strategy pair, identify whose payoff is shown, distinguish ordinal from cardinal information, and state omitted real-world factors. |
| **Best responses and equilibrium** | A best response maximizes a player’s payoff given the other’s choice; a Nash equilibrium consists of mutual best responses. Dominance compares strategies against every opponent choice. An equilibrium can be inefficient and need not be unique or exist in pure strategies. | Identify dominated strategies, best responses, and pure-strategy Nash equilibria. | Compare one player’s payoffs while fixing the opponent’s strategy, account for ties, and distinguish a unilateral incentive test from joint payoff improvement. |
