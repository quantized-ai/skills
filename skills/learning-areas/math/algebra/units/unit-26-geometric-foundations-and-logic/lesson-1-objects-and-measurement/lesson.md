# Lesson 26.1: Objects and measurement

Geometric objects are defined from basic notions of position, distance, and incidence.

## Learning objectives

- Define segments, rays, angles, circles, parallel lines, and perpendicular lines precisely.
- Use segment and angle addition, measurement notation, and geometric classifications consistently.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish an object from its numerical measure and identify the hypotheses required by each definition.
- State between-point and interior-ray conditions when adding measures and distinguish definitions from diagram appearance.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic objects and incidence** | Point, line, and plane are undefined Euclidean objects; collinearity and coplanarity describe incidence. A segment consists of endpoints and the points between them; a ray has an endpoint and extends in one direction. Distinct parallel lines are coplanar and do not intersect; skew lines are noncoplanar. Perpendicular lines intersect at a right angle. | Define and distinguish points, lines, planes, segments, rays, parallel lines, perpendicular lines, and skew lines. | Use incidence and coplanarity conditions correctly; do not call skew lines parallel or equate a segment with its length. |
| **Distance and angle measure** | Distance along a line is nonnegative and additive between ordered points. An angle is formed by two rays with a common endpoint; its nonreflex degree measure lies from \(0^\circ\) to \(180^\circ\). Segment addition requires the middle point to lie on the segment; angle addition requires an interior ray. Congruent segments or angles have equal measures. A circle is the set of planar points a fixed positive distance from its center. | Define angles and circles and use segment and angle measures with valid addition relationships. | Identify endpoints, vertices, centers, and radii; write equal measures separately from congruent-object notation; check betweenness or interior-ray conditions before adding. |
| **Angle and polygon classifications** | Acute, right, obtuse, straight, adjacent, vertical, complementary, and supplementary angles have distinct measure or incidence conditions. Triangles are classified by sides and angles; isosceles means at least two equal sides. A simple polygon has noncrossing sides; convexity, concavity, and regularity are distinguished. A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides; rectangles, rhombi, and squares form inclusive subclasses. Here trapezoid means exactly one pair of parallel sides; a kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides, with rhombi included in that class. | Classify angles, triangles, and quadrilaterals from their defining properties and explain inclusive category relationships. | Distinguish adjacent from supplementary and regular from merely equilateral; identify a square as both a rectangle and a rhombus and state the adopted trapezoid convention. |
