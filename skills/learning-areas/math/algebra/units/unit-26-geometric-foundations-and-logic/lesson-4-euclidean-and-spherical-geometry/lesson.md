# Lesson 26.4: Euclidean and spherical geometry

Comparing planes and spheres reveals which geometric conclusions depend on the underlying space.

## Learning objectives

- Compare straight paths, parallelism, and triangle angle sums on a Euclidean plane and a sphere.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use great circles as spherical lines and explain why Euclidean parallel and angle-sum conclusions do not transfer unchanged.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spherical lines and triangles** | In spherical geometry, lines are great circles, and shortest paths between non-antipodal points follow minor great-circle arcs. Any two distinct great circles intersect in antipodal points, so there are no parallel spherical lines. A nondegenerate triangle bounded by minor arcs in a hemisphere has angle sum greater than \(180^\circ\), unlike a Euclidean triangle; the sphere is not treated as a flat drawing. | Compare lines, parallelism, and triangle angle sums in Euclidean and spherical geometry. | Distinguish great circles from other circles on the sphere and link the changed angle and parallel relationships to the geometric setting. |
