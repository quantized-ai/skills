## Concept teaching plans

### Conjectures from geometric representations

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Measuring three isosceles triangles gives equal base angles. What follows?

**Private diagnostic key:** a conjecture supported by examples, not universal proof.

**Teach in this order:** Construct hypotheses, vary free features, record apparent invariants and a near-counterexample; audit whether the construction accidentally imposed the conclusion.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** For a circle, construct several chords and their midpoints; draw each center-to-midpoint segment. Drag endpoints while retaining the circle and non-diameter chord. The apparent perpendicularity motivates a conjecture; independently constructing a perpendicular would bake the conclusion into the setup.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If a dynamic drag is called proof, ask which untested cases remain; next request a theorem chain explaining the invariant.

**Practice progression:** Angle investigation → congruence/triangle segments → polygon angles, diagonals and circle investigations with preserved constraints. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Actual construction/measurement evidence; all named investigation families; legitimate variation; conjecture/proof distinction; no invented tool run. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** State a precise claim and its conditions for each family; vary irrelevant features and explain why observed agreement still requires deductive justification.

### Deductive proof structure

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Is 'diagonals bisect because it is the shape shown' a proof?

**Private diagnostic key:** no; a justified theorem or argument is needed.

**Teach in this order:** List givens and target; choose coordinate, rigid-motion or axiomatic route; write dependencies before formatting; check no step assumes target.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** For parallelogram A=(0,0),B=(a,0),C=(a+b,c),D=(b,c), a,c≠0, both diagonal midpoints equal ((a+b)/2,c/2). Thus they bisect. Paragraph: explain midpoint equality; two-column: coordinates→midpoint formula→equal points→bisection; flow: two midpoint branches merge at equality.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If a conclusion is inserted as a premise, ask what previously known result licenses it; next replace that step with a definition/theorem or leave an explicit gap.

**Practice progression:** Complete a missing reason → write proof → translate formats and compare methods. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Valid general setup; all hypotheses; theorem dependencies; paragraph/two-column/flow presentations; coordinate/transformation/axiomatic methods; no circularity. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Identify givens and the target, justify every essential step, and diagnose unstated diagram assumptions, invalid converses, and circular arguments.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** A student measures many parallelograms and conjectures their diagonals bisect each other. Give a coordinate proof for vertices $(0,0),(a,0),(a+b,c),(b,c)$ with a and c nonzero. Then explain what the measurements established.

**Private worked key:** Both diagonals have midpoint $((a+b)/2,c/2)$, establishing bisection for this general representation; the assumptions avoid degeneracy. Measurements motivated the conjecture but did not prove it for every parallelogram. A two-column or flow proof must preserve the same dependency: coordinates and midpoint definition precede equality of midpoints.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.

## Decision rehearsal and fading

**Replace a picture with a dependency.** Suppose two lines intersect, creating adjacent angles $\alpha,\beta$ and the angle $\gamma$ vertically opposite $\alpha$. Linear-pair relationships give $\alpha+\beta=180^\circ$ and $\gamma+\beta=180^\circ$; subtracting yields $\alpha=\gamma$. This proof uses only straight-angle addition, not the vertical-angle theorem it establishes. A flow proof has the two linear-pair equations feeding equality; a two-column proof assigns the same reasons to those steps.

If the learner says “vertical angles are equal” as the justification, ask which earlier fact can replace the target theorem. Next identify the shared supplementary angle; then supply one linear-pair equation and leave the other and subtraction. Fade by having them recast the proof as a paragraph. Tool measurements may motivate this conjecture; record actual investigated configurations separately, and do not claim this single proof or one drag covers all required investigation families or proof methods.

## Additional teaching and execution activities

### Concrete investigations across the required families

These are practical tasks, not claims of completed experiments. Ask for one labeled construction/measurement record at a time, vary at least three allowable configurations, and retain an extreme nonspecial case. The conclusion remains a conjecture until separately proved.

- **Angles:** construct two intersecting lines; vary their intersection angle and compare opposite-angle measures. Keep intersection incidence, not a perpendicular constraint. Conjecture vertical angles are equal; the proof below supplies a separate deductive route.
- **Triangle congruence:** fix a segment AB and two positive radii satisfying strict triangle inequalities. Construct both intersections of the circles centered A and B. Compare the resulting triangles by tracing/reflection. Conjecture SSS determines a triangle up to rigid motion; do not claim the two positions are noncongruent just because orientation differs.
- **Triangle segments:** construct a freely varying nondegenerate triangle and true midpoints of two sides; connect them. Measure parallelism and the ratio to the third side. Conjecture the midsegment is parallel and half as long. Moving an endpoint freely on a side would no longer preserve the hypothesis.
- **Polygon angles:** draw simple convex quadrilaterals and pentagons; triangulate without overlaps and compare angle sums. Test a concave example using valid internal diagonals. Conjecture(n−2)180°; do not assume every diagonal fan stays inside a concave polygon.
- **Quadrilateral diagonals:** construct a parallelogram by two pairs of parallel lines, not by preassigned diagonal midpoints. Drag a side endpoint; compare the two parts of each diagonal. Conjecture bisection; the general coordinate proof above is independent of the measured examples.
- **Circles:** choose two distinct non-antipodal points on a fixed circle and construct their chord midpoint. Draw the center-to-midpoint segment and measure its angle with the chord. Conjecture perpendicularity for a nondiameter chord; a diameter midpoint is the center and the segment degenerates, so state that exclusion.

### Three deductive methods and format translation

**Axiomatic method, vertical angles:** for intersecting lines, let adjacent angles be α,β,γ in order, with α and γ vertical. Linear pairs give α+β=180° and β+γ=180°. Subtract β from both equal totals to obtain α=γ. The premises are incidence and the straight-angle/addition axioms, not measured equality.

**Coordinate method:** use the general parallelogram midpoint calculation in the concept plan. Explain why a rotation/translation of axes can put one side horizontally, and why a,c≠0 prevent degeneration; one numerical rectangle is not a general representation.

**Transformation method, equal-distance locus forward direction:** take A,B distinct and their perpendicular bisector ℓ. Reflection across ℓ swaps A and B and fixes any P on ℓ. Given the established distance-preservation property of reflection, PA equals the image distance PB. State that property as a prior result; if it has not been introduced, teach or justify it before using the proof. This proves the forward locus claim, not automatically its converse.

For the vertical-angle proof, a two-column form has statements 'α+β=180°; β+γ=180°; α+β=β+γ; α=γ' and reasons 'linear pair; linear pair; equal to same number; subtract β'. A flow version has the two linear-pair branches merge at equality, then subtraction. A paragraph expresses those same dependencies in sentences. Ask the student to translate one format into another without dropping a reason, then identify the circular false proof 'vertical angles are equal because they are congruent'.
