# Lesson 26.3: Conjecture and deductive proof

Conjecture formation and deductive arguments play complementary roles in geometric reasoning.

## Learning objectives

- Generate conjectures with geometric tools and separate evidence from proof.
- Organize and critique deductive arguments in paragraph, two-column, and flow-chart formats.

## Proficiency criteria

- Record sufficient conditions and avoid generalizing from a special or inaccurately drawn figure.
- Give a valid justification for each inferential step and identify circular reasoning or missing assumptions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Conjectures from geometric representations** | Compass and straightedge, measurement, and dynamic geometry can reveal angle, triangle, quadrilateral, and circle patterns. Varying figures while preserving hypotheses helps distinguish invariant relationships from accidental features; constructions support conjectures but numerical agreement is not proof. | Investigate angle, triangle-congruence, triangle-segment, polygon-angle, quadrilateral-diagonal, and circle relationships with geometric tools to formulate conjectures. | State a precise claim and its conditions for each family; vary irrelevant features and explain why observed agreement still requires deductive justification. |
| **Deductive proof structure** | A proof connects stated hypotheses to a conclusion using definitions, postulates, established theorems, and valid logical inference. Paragraph, two-column, and flow-chart presentations express the same logical dependencies. Coordinate, transformation, and axiomatic methods use different representations; none may assume the result being proved. | Construct and critique geometric proofs in paragraph, two-column, and flow-chart formats using coordinate, transformational, and axiomatic reasoning. | Identify givens and the target, justify every essential step, and diagnose unstated diagram assumptions, invalid converses, and circular arguments. |
