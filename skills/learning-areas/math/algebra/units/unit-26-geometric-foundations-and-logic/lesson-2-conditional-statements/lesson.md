# Lesson 26.2: Conditional statements and counterexamples

Logical forms specify what a geometric claim assumes and what it establishes.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish definitions, postulates, conjectures, and theorems.
- Analyze conditional, converse, inverse, contrapositive, and biconditional statements and use counterexamples.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify hypothesis and conclusion and distinguish logically equivalent forms from forms requiring separate verification.
- Use a counterexample that satisfies every hypothesis while contradicting the conclusion.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mathematical statements and authority** | Undefined terms provide starting language; definitions assign precise meaning, postulates are assumed, conjectures are unproved claims, and theorems have deductive proofs. Measurements and repeated observations can motivate conjectures but do not prove universal statements. | Distinguish undefined terms, definitions, postulates, conjectures, and theorems and explain the limits of observational evidence. | Classify statements by their role and distinguish an accepted assumption from a consequence justified by proof. |
| **Conditional and biconditional statements** | For propositions \(p,q\), the conditional \(p\Rightarrow q\) has converse \(q\Rightarrow p\), inverse \(\neg p\Rightarrow\neg q\), and contrapositive \(\neg q\Rightarrow\neg p\). A conditional is equivalent to its contrapositive; its converse and inverse are equivalent to each other. A biconditional requires both directions. A definition often supplies such a two-way relationship. | Write and determine the validity of conditional statements, their converses, inverses, and contrapositives, and associated biconditionals. | Negate hypotheses and conclusions accurately; verify both directions before asserting a biconditional; do not infer a converse from the original statement alone. |
| **Counterexamples and quantifiers** | One admissible counterexample disproves a universal conjecture. A successful counterexample satisfies all stated restrictions but violates the claimed conclusion; examples agreeing with a conjecture do not establish its universality. | Disprove false geometric conjectures by constructing and explaining counterexamples. | Identify precisely which hypothesis holds and which conclusion fails; distinguish disproving a universal statement from disproving a statement about a particular object. |
