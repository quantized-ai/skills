# Lesson 44.1: Discrete random variables and expected value

A random variable assigns numerical outcomes and its distribution supports weighted summaries.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and graph discrete random-variable distributions from theoretical, empirical, and simulated outcomes.
- Calculate and interpret expected value, variance, and standard deviation of a finite distribution.

## Proficiency criteria

- Account for all outcomes, combine repeated values, and label value and probability axes while distinguishing empirical estimates from theoretical probabilities.
- Use normalized probabilities and all values, distinguish a mean from a most likely outcome, and report mean and standard deviation in original units and variance in squared units.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Constructing probability distributions** | A discrete random variable maps each sample-space outcome to a real number. Equal values are aggregated; a probability mass function is nonnegative and sums to one. Theoretical counts, empirical frequencies, or a specified simulation can determine its probabilities. | Construct and graph discrete random-variable distributions from theoretical, empirical, and simulated outcomes. | Account for all outcomes, combine repeated values, and label value and probability axes while distinguishing empirical estimates from theoretical probabilities. |
| **Expected value and variability** | $E(X)=\sum_x xp(x)$ is the probability-weighted mean; $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sum_x(x-\mu)^2p(x)$ and standard deviation is its nonnegative square root. Expectation need not be a possible outcome and does not guarantee a single result. | Calculate and interpret expected value, variance, and standard deviation of a finite distribution. | Use normalized probabilities and all values, distinguish a mean from a most likely outcome, and report mean and standard deviation in original units and variance in squared units. |
