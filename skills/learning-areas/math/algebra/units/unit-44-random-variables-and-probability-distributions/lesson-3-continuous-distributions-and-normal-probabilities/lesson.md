# Lesson 44.3: Continuous distributions and normal probabilities

Continuous models assign probabilities to intervals through areas rather than point masses.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret continuous densities and calculate uniform interval probabilities.
- Calculate normal-model areas and quantile cutoffs and assess the model’s appropriateness.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use interval length ratios, trim events to the support, and distinguish zero point probability from impossibility.
- Configure mean, standard deviation, tails, and cumulative probability correctly; return cutoffs to original units and qualify normal estimates when data are skewed or bounded.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Density and uniform models** | A continuous density is nonnegative with total area one. Interval probabilities are areas, individual points have probability zero, and density height is not a probability. For a uniform distribution on $[a,b]$, $a<b$, density is $1/(b-a)$ on the interval. | Interpret continuous densities and calculate uniform interval probabilities. | Use interval length ratios, trim events to the support, and distinguish zero point probability from impossibility. |
| **Normal probabilities and inverse percentiles** | A normal model has parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma>0$; $z=(x-\mu)/\sigma$. Numerical normal areas and inverse-normal quantiles determine probabilities and cutoffs. Normality is a model assumption evaluated against shape and context. | Calculate normal-model areas and quantile cutoffs and assess the model’s appropriateness. | Configure mean, standard deviation, tails, and cumulative probability correctly; return cutoffs to original units and qualify normal estimates when data are skewed or bounded. |
