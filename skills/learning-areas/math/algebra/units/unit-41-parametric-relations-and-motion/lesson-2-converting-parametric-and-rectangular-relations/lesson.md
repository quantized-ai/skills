# Lesson 41.2: Converting parametric and rectangular relations

Eliminating a parameter reveals a rectangular relation, while introducing a parameter can encode a selected portion or traversal of a given curve.

## Learning objectives

- Eliminate parameters algebraically or through identities and preserve the exact point set.
- Construct parametric descriptions of rectangular relations, line segments, and circles.

## Proficiency criteria

- Recover all restrictions after elimination and identify any lost orientation or timing.
- Verify that a proposed parametrization produces every intended point and no unintended branch.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parameter elimination** | Elimination may solve one coordinate equation for $t$ and substitute, or use identities such as $\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1$. The derived rectangular equation may describe more points than the original parameter domain; branch, coordinate-range, and endpoint restrictions must accompany it. Squaring can introduce additional branches and division can remove points. | Convert parametric equations to rectangular relations by algebraic elimination and trigonometric identities while preserving the represented set. | Apply the appropriate elimination method, carry parameter-domain restrictions into coordinate conditions, and check points or branches potentially lost or introduced by algebra. |
| **Constructing parametrizations** | A function graph $y=f(x)$ can use $x=t$, $y=f(t)$ with its domain. A segment from $P$ to $Q$ uses $(x,y)=P+t(Q-P)$ for $0\le t\le1$; extending the parameter gives a ray or line. A circle centered at $(h,k)$ with radius $r>0$ uses $x=h+r\cos t$, $y=k+r\sin t$ with an interval chosen for the intended arc or full traversal. Parametrizations are generally not unique. | Convert rectangular relations to parametric equations and choose parameter intervals that trace specified graphs, segments, rays, lines, and circles. | Verify the rectangular equation, recover every intended point, use domain endpoints correctly, and explain the orientation and nonuniqueness of the parametrization. |
