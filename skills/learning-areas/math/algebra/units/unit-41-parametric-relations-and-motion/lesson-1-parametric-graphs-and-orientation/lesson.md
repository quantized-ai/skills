# Lesson 41.1: Parametric graphs and orientation

A pair of functions of the same parameter describes a planar relation whose tracing order may carry information beyond its rectangular graph.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret and graph parametric equations using paired values and technology.
- Describe orientation, endpoints, repeated tracing, and dependence on the parameter interval.

## Proficiency criteria

- Pair coordinates at the same parameter value and display the correct allowed interval.
- Distinguish the locus from how quickly or how many times it is traversed.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parametric representations** | A parametric relation is $(x,y)=(f(t),g(t))$ for real $t$ in a specified common domain. The parameter may represent time, angle, or another quantity; it need not be a coordinate. Its graph is the set of paired coordinate values, not two separate function graphs. Values at the same parameter must be paired. | Interpret parametric equations and construct their planar graphs from matched parameter values and technology. | Use the common domain, label coordinate and parameter units where relevant, pair values at the same input, and reconcile the plotted relation with calculated points. |
| **Orientation and traversal** | The increasing-parameter order supplies an orientation when the path has a direction of traversal. A point can be visited repeatedly, intervals can trace only part of a curve, and a relation may have repeated or stationary points. Two parametrizations can produce the same point set but different orientation or timing. | Analyze the effect of a parameter interval or reparametrization on endpoints, orientation, and repeated tracing of a planar relation. | Mark included or excluded endpoints, determine increasing-parameter direction from ordered values, and distinguish geometric equality of point sets from equality of traversal. |
