# Lesson 40.3: Hyperbolas from focal definitions

A hyperbola’s fixed absolute difference of focal distances determines two branches, a transverse axis, and asymptotes.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the standard hyperbola equation from its focal definition.
- Analyze and construct translated axis-aligned hyperbolas, including foci and asymptotes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve both branches and the strict focal-distance restrictions.
- Use the positive squared term to identify orientation and distinguish focal distance from transverse-axis length.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deriving the hyperbola equation** | For foci $(\pm c,0)$ and absolute distance difference $2a$ with $0<a<c$, eliminating radicals from $\lvert\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2}-\sqrt{(x+c)^2+y^2}\rvert=2a$ gives $x^2/a^2-y^2/b^2=1$ with $b^2=c^2-a^2>0$. The absolute value accounts for the two branches; squared equations require verification against the original distance condition. | Derive the hyperbola equation from an absolute difference of focal distances and justify the relationship among a, b, and c. | Preserve both branch cases, establish $c^2=a^2+b^2$, retain $0<a<c$, and check that the derived equation has the intended focal difference. |
| **Hyperbola features and asymptotes** | For $(x-h)^2/a^2-(y-k)^2/b^2=1$ with $a,b>0$, the center is $(h,k)$, vertices $(h\pm a,k)$, foci $(h\pm c,k)$ with $c^2=a^2+b^2$, and asymptotes $y-k=\pm(b/a)(x-h)$. For vertical orientation the positive term uses $y-k$ and the asymptotes are $y-k=\pm(a/b)(x-h)$. Asymptotes guide distant branch behavior and are not part of the hyperbola. | Graph translated axis-aligned hyperbolas and determine their centers, vertices, foci, orientations, and asymptotes. | Identify orientation from the positive term, compute $c$ by addition, use the correct asymptote slopes, and place both branches relative to the vertices. |
| **Constructing hyperbola equations** | Sufficient consistent constraints can specify a center, orientation, vertex distance $a$, focal distance $c$, or asymptote slope, with $c^2=a^2+b^2$. The asymptote slopes alone do not set the scale; a vertex, focus, or point condition supplies an additional independent quantity. | Construct axis-aligned hyperbola equations from sufficient center, vertex, focus, asymptote, or point information and identify insufficient data. | Select the correct positive coordinate term, determine positive parameters satisfying every condition, and distinguish an asymptote ratio from a complete scale specification. |
