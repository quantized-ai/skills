# Lesson 40.4: Conic equations and eccentricity

Completing the square identifies axis-aligned conics from expanded equations, while eccentricity relates focal geometry across conic families.

## Learning objectives

- Convert expanded axis-aligned conic equations to standard form and classify their real loci.
- Calculate and interpret eccentricity and connect ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola classifications to focus-directrix descriptions, treating the circle separately.

## Proficiency criteria

- Factor quadratic coefficients before completing the square and retain every constant adjustment.
- Distinguish nondegenerate conics, empty sets, and degenerate loci; enforce the conditions in the focus-directrix model.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Expanded conic equations** | Equations $Ax^2+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$ with at least one of $A,C$ nonzero and without an $xy$ term can be analyzed by completing squares and normalizing. The coefficient signs alone are insufficient: the resulting constants determine whether a real ellipse, hyperbola, parabola, circle, point, empty set, or degenerate line locus occurs. Rotated conics requiring an $xy$ term are outside this axis-aligned equation method. | Convert expanded axis-aligned quadratic relations to standard form and classify their real loci, including degenerate and empty cases. | Account for leading coefficients when completing squares, classify from the full normalized equation, and recover geometric features only when the real locus permits them. |
| **Eccentricity and focus-directrix form** | Eccentricity is $e=c/a$ for ellipses and hyperbolas: $0<e<1$ for a noncircular ellipse and $e>1$ for a hyperbola. Each can be expressed as distance to a focus equal to $e$ times distance to its corresponding directrix; the parabola has $e=1$. For a horizontal axis the directrices are $x=h\pm a/e$, paired with foci $(h\pm c,k)$; vertical cases exchange coordinates. A circle has $e=0$ as a limiting ellipse and no finite directrix in this model. | Calculate and interpret eccentricity and connect ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola classifications to the focus-directrix distance ratio. | Use focal distance divided by semimajor or semitransverse length, match each directrix to its focus, distinguish the three noncircular cases, and treat the circle separately. |
