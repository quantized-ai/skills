# Lesson 40.1: Conic sections and distance loci

A conic can be described as a plane section or as a set of points satisfying a distance condition. These descriptions organize circles, parabolas, ellipses, and hyperbolas into a coherent family.

## Learning objectives

- Identify conics from the orientation and position of a plane intersecting a double cone.
- Connect the familiar conic families to their defining distance relationships.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish one-nappe and two-nappe intersections and identify apex degeneracies.
- State each locus condition without confusing a sum of distances, a difference, or a focus-directrix ratio.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plane sections of a double cone** | A plane cutting one nappe in a closed curve yields an ellipse, including a circle when perpendicular to the cone axis. A non-apex plane parallel to a generating line yields a parabola; a plane cutting both nappes yields a hyperbola. Planes through the apex can yield a point, a line, or two intersecting lines as degenerate sections. | Classify conic sections from the position and orientation of a plane relative to a double-napped cone, including degenerate apex cases. | Identify the nappe intersections and parallel-generator condition, distinguish circle from a general ellipse, and explain why apex sections may be degenerate. |
| **Distance-locus definitions** | A circle has fixed distance from a center; a parabola has equal distance from a focus and directrix. For distinct foci, an ellipse has a fixed sum of focal distances greater than the focal separation, while a hyperbola has a fixed positive absolute difference smaller than that separation. The inequalities prevent degenerate loci. | Connect conic families to their distance-locus definitions and explain the parameter conditions for nondegenerate ellipses and hyperbolas. | Distinguish center and focus roles, use an absolute difference for both hyperbola branches, and retain the strict inequalities needed for the focal loci. |
