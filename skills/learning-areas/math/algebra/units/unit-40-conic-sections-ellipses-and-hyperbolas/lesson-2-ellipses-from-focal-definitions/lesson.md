# Lesson 40.2: Ellipses from focal definitions

An ellipse’s constant sum of focal distances determines its equation and links its axes, vertices, and foci.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the standard ellipse equation from its focal definition.
- Analyze and construct translated axis-aligned ellipses from equations or geometric data.

## Proficiency criteria

- Justify algebraic steps and retain the focal and parameter conditions.
- Distinguish semiaxis lengths from squared denominators and identify the correct major-axis orientation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deriving the ellipse equation** | For foci $(\pm c,0)$ and sum of distances $2a$ with $a>c\ge0$, eliminating square roots from $\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2}+\sqrt{(x+c)^2+y^2}=2a$ gives $x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2=1$ with $b^2=a^2-c^2>0$. Reversing the reasoning checks that the equation satisfies the original nonnegative distance sum; $c=0$ yields a circle. | Derive the ellipse equation from a constant sum of focal distances and justify the relationship among a, b, and c. | Eliminate radicals by valid rearrangement and squaring, state $a^2=b^2+c^2$, verify the distance-sum interpretation, and identify the circular special case. |
| **Ellipse features and translated equations** | For a horizontal major axis, $(x-h)^2/a^2+(y-k)^2/b^2=1$ with $a\ge b>0$, center $(h,k)$, vertices $(h\pm a,k)$, covertices $(h,k\pm b)$, and foci $(h\pm c,k)$ where $c^2=a^2-b^2$. A vertical major axis exchanges the roles of the coordinate denominators. Major and minor axis lengths are $2a$ and $2b$. | Graph translated axis-aligned ellipses and determine centers, axes, vertices, covertices, and foci from their equations. | Use the larger denominator to identify the major axis, take square roots for lengths, compute focal distance by subtraction, and retain translation signs consistently. |
| **Constructing ellipse equations** | An ellipse equation is determined by sufficient consistent geometric data, such as center, major-axis direction, and two independent axis or focal quantities. The relations $a^2=b^2+c^2$ and focal sum $2a$ convert between descriptions. Given data may fail the conditions for a nondegenerate ellipse. | Construct equations of axis-aligned ellipses from centers, vertices, foci, axis lengths, or equivalent sufficient geometric constraints. | Determine the orientation and independent parameters, distinguish full-axis from semiaxis lengths, and verify that the resulting ellipse matches every supplied condition. |
