# Unit 40: Conic sections, ellipses, and hyperbolas

This unit connects plane sections of a cone, distance loci, and rectangular equations. Ellipses and hyperbolas extend the circle and parabola families through focal definitions, derived equations, graph features, and translated forms.

## Main learning objectives

- Classify plane sections of a double cone and connect conic geometry to distance-based definitions.
- Derive and analyze axis-aligned ellipse and hyperbola equations from their focal conditions.
- Convert conic equations to standard form and construct equations from geometric information.

## Concepts and topics

Double-cone intersections; nondegenerate and degenerate conics; focal sum and difference loci; ellipse axes and foci; hyperbola branches and asymptotes; translated equations; completing the square; eccentricity and focus-directrix interpretation.

## Lessons

- [40.1: Conic sections and distance loci](lesson-1-conic-sections-and-distance-loci/lesson.md) — A conic can be described as a plane section or as a set of points satisfying a distance condition. These descriptions organize circles, parabolas, ellipses, and hyperbolas into a coherent family.
- [40.2: Ellipses from focal definitions](lesson-2-ellipses-from-focal-definitions/lesson.md) — An ellipse’s constant sum of focal distances determines its equation and links its axes, vertices, and foci.
- [40.3: Hyperbolas from focal definitions](lesson-3-hyperbolas-from-focal-definitions/lesson.md) — A hyperbola’s fixed absolute difference of focal distances determines two branches, a transverse axis, and asymptotes.
- [40.4: Conic equations and eccentricity](lesson-4-conic-equations-and-eccentricity/lesson.md) — Completing the square identifies axis-aligned conics from expanded equations, while eccentricity relates focal geometry across conic families.
