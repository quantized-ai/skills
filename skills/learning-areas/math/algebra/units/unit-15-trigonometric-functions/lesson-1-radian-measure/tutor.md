## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** On radius 2, a point travels clockwise π radians then counterclockwise π radians. Are net angle and traveled distance both zero?

**Private reasoning key:** Net angle is zero, but each leg has length 2π, so total distance is 4π. Reversal prevents using only the net angle's magnitude for distance.

## Concept guidance

### Directed angles and arc length

Curriculum reference: **Directed angles and arc length** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** On a radius-4 circle, rotate clockwise through $3\pi/2$ radians. Give signed angle and traveled arc length.

**Agent key:** The signed angle is $-3\pi/2$; distance traveled is $4\cdot3\pi/2=6\pi$, nonnegative.

**Worked example:** If radius and directed arc both double, does the angle change?

**Worked reasoning:** No: their ratio $s_d/r$ is unchanged. Radian measure is scale-invariant.


#### Teaching sequence

Define radians as directed arc displacement divided by radius and distinguish that signed angle from nonnegative distance traveled. Clockwise angles are negative under the convention; a path with no reversal has length r times the angle's absolute value. A reversal can add traveled distance without adding the same net angle. Scaling radius and arc proportionally leaves the radian angle unchanged.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Are you describing directed displacement or nonnegative distance?

If a negative length is reported for clockwise motion, separate direction from distance. If degrees are inserted directly into rθ, convert to radians. If net rotation is used for a reversing path, sum path lengths by segment.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Use positive/negative and multiple-turn rotations, radius changes and explicitly described paths. Require units and the direction convention.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess radian ratio, signed rotation, nonnegative arc length, scale invariance and the no-reversal condition for the simple distance formula.

### Degree conversion and coterminal angles

Curriculum reference: **Degree conversion and coterminal angles** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Convert 225 degrees to radians and find the representative of $-3\pi/4$ in [0,2π).

**Agent key:** $225\pi/180=5\pi/4$; adding $2\pi$ to $-3\pi/4$ also gives $5\pi/4$.

**Worked example:** Are $\pi/3$ and $7\pi/3$ the same total rotation?

**Worked reasoning:** They share a terminal point but differ by a full turn in total directed rotation.


#### Teaching sequence

Use 180°=π radians as an equality of angle measures, keeping units visible during conversion. Add integer multiples of a full turn to find a coterminal representative in a specified half-open interval. Explain that coterminal angles share an endpoint but may describe different total rotations; include only one representative at the interval boundary.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which full-turn multiple puts the result in the requested half-open interval?

If π is omitted or inverted, check a half-turn. If both 0 and 2π are listed in [0,2π), inspect the open endpoint. If coterminal means equal total rotation, count the extra full turns.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Convert both directions, reduce negative and multi-turn measures and compare intervals such as [0,2π) and (−π,π].

#### Assessment evidence

Require correct units, exact conversion, integer full-turn adjustment, interval membership and terminal-point versus total-rotation distinction.

## Decision model and graduated practice

On radius $4$ cm, clockwise rotation $3\pi/2$ has signed angle $-3\pi/2$ radians, directed arc displacement $-6\pi$ cm, and traveled length $6\pi$ cm if there is no reversal. The angle is arc divided by radius: doubling both lengths preserves it. Converting $225^\circ$ multiplies by $\pi/180^\circ$, giving $5\pi/4$.

Cue “Is this quantity directed or a nonnegative traveled length?”; next set up $s_d=r\theta$ and $s=r|\theta|$ for this single rotation; then substitute the signed angle only, leaving the two outputs. Fade with radius $3$ and counterclockwise angle $4\pi/3$ (length $4\pi$). When a path reverses direction, add each leg's length rather than applying absolute value only to net angle. Coterminal angles share a terminal point, not total traveled length.
