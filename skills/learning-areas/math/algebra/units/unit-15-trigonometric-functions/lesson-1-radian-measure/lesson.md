# Lesson 15.1: Radian measure

Radian measure connects directed rotation to arc length and radius. Degree conversion and coterminal angles locate rotations on the unit circle without confusing terminal position with total rotation.

## Learning objectives

- Relate radius, arc length, and signed radian measure, including invariance under circle scaling.
- Convert exactly between degree and radian units.
- Find coterminal representatives and locate their terminal quadrants or axes.

## Learning outcome

Express a directed rotation in compatible angular units and locate its terminal position.

## Proficiency criteria

- Justify radian measure using the arc-to-radius ratio and explain why scaling the circle preserves the angle.
- Distinguish signed angular displacement from nonnegative arc length.
- Convert angular units exactly when possible using the full-turn relationship.
- Find a representative in a specified full-turn interval and identify its quadrant or axis while distinguishing position from total rotation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Directed angles and arc length** | For a circle of radius $r>0$, directed arc displacement $s_d$ defines radian measure $\theta=s_d/r$. Counterclockwise rotation is positive; clockwise rotation is negative. For a rotation without reversal, arc length is $s=r\lvert\theta\rvert$. Scaling both arc and radius equally preserves the angle. On the unit circle, directed arc displacement equals the radian measure numerically; a full turn is $2\pi$ radians. | Interpret radian measure as a directed arc-to-radius ratio and explain its invariance under circle scaling. Represent signed rotations and calculate radius, radian measure, and arc length while distinguishing directed displacement from nonnegative length. | Form the arc-to-radius ratio with consistent length units. Justify invariance under scaling. Assign the sign from rotation direction and distinguish directed displacement from arc length. Relate radius, angle, and arc length, including the unit-circle case. |
| **Degree conversion and coterminal angles** | A full turn is $360^\circ=2\pi$ radians. Multiply a degree measure by $\pi/180$ for radians and a radian measure by $180/\pi$ for degrees. Coterminal angles differ by $2\pi k$ radians or $360k$ degrees, $k\in\mathbb Z$. A half-open full-turn interval selects one representative; coterminal angles share a terminal point but may describe different total rotations. | Convert exactly between degree and radian measure. Find a coterminal representative in a specified full-turn interval and locate its quadrant or axis without equating terminal position with total rotation. | Use the correct conversion factor and retain exact multiples of $\pi$ where possible. Add or subtract whole turns to obtain the requested representative. Identify its quadrant or axis and distinguish shared terminal position from equal rotation. |
