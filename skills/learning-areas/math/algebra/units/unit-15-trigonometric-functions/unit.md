# Unit 15: Trigonometric functions

Radian measure and unit-circle definitions support exact values, identities, periodic graphs, and sinusoidal models.

## Main learning objectives

- Relate radius, arc length, and signed radian measure, including invariance under circle scaling. Convert exactly between degree and radian units.
- Read sine and cosine from coordinates and connect them to acute-angle triangle ratios. Compute tangent and identify its complete set of excluded inputs.
- Derive special-angle values from triangle geometry and unit-radius normalization. Use reference angles and coordinate transformations to find exact ratios.
- Prove the Pythagorean identity and use its squared-value notation correctly. Simplify and rearrange expressions using the identity; distinguish identities from conditional equations.
- Construct sine, cosine, and tangent graphs from exact unit-circle values. Identify and compare domains, ranges, zeros, extrema, and fundamental periods.
- Determine sinusoidal features and distinguish angular frequency from cycle frequency with units. Graph combined transformations with correct phase, reflection, and orientation.
- Estimate sinusoidal parameters from recurring observations and a phase anchor. Interpret parameter units, produce predictions, and verify the fitted features.

## Concepts and topics

Directed angles and arc length; Degree conversion and coterminal angles; Sine, cosine, and tangent as coordinates; Quadrant signs, periodicity, and symmetry; Special triangles; Reference angles and reflected coordinates; Proof and algebraic use of the identity; Recovering ratios with quadrant information; Sine and cosine graphs; Tangent graph and asymptotes; Amplitude, midline, period, and frequency; Phase shift and transformed graphs; Parameter estimation from periodic data; Model checking and limitations.

## Lessons

- [15.1: Radian measure](lesson-1-radian-measure/lesson.md) — Radian measure connects directed rotation to arc length and radius. Degree conversion and coterminal angles locate rotations on the unit circle without confusing terminal position with total rotation.
- [15.2: Unit-circle definitions](lesson-2-unit-circle-definitions/lesson.md) — Unit-circle coordinates define sine, cosine, and tangent for angles beyond acute triangles. Reflections and rotations determine their signs, symmetry, periods, and tangent exclusions.
- [15.3: Exact special-angle values](lesson-3-exact-special-angle-values/lesson.md) — Special triangles determine exact trigonometric magnitudes. Reference angles, reflections, and rotations extend these values to other quadrants and to negative or multi-turn inputs.
- [15.4: Pythagorean identity](lesson-4-pythagorean-identity/lesson.md) — The unit-circle equation proves the Pythagorean identity for every real angle. Algebraic use of the identity distinguishes squared magnitudes from signed ratios and checks whether given conditions are compatible.
- [15.5: Parent trigonometric graphs](lesson-5-parent-trigonometric-graphs/lesson.md) — Parent trigonometric graphs track unit-circle values as the radian input changes. Sine and cosine have bounded repeating cycles; tangent has unbounded branches separated by excluded inputs.
- [15.6: Sinusoidal transformations](lesson-6-sinusoidal-transformations/lesson.md) — Sinusoidal transformations connect equations with amplitude, midline, period, frequency, and phase. Graph construction and equation recovery use these features together with an anchor that fixes position and orientation.
- [15.7: Periodic function models](lesson-7-periodic-function-models/lesson.md) — Periodic measurements support estimates of a sinusoidal model’s parameters and phase. Predictions, residuals, and contextual evidence test how well a fixed sinusoid describes the observations and where its use is justified.
