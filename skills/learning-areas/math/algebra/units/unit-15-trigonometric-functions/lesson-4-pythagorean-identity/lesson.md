# Lesson 15.4: Pythagorean identity

The unit-circle equation proves the Pythagorean identity for every real angle. Algebraic use of the identity distinguishes squared magnitudes from signed ratios and checks whether given conditions are compatible.

## Learning objectives

- Prove the Pythagorean identity and use its squared-value notation correctly.
- Simplify and rearrange expressions using the identity; distinguish identities from conditional equations.
- Recover signed ratios from one ratio and quadrant information, and reject incompatible conditions.

## Learning outcome

Justify the Pythagorean identity and use it to recover consistent signed trigonometric ratios.

## Proficiency criteria

- Prove the identity from an arbitrary unit-circle point.
- Apply valid algebraic transformations without confusing squared function values with squared inputs.
- Recover magnitudes and select signs from quadrant information.
- Verify the original conditions and identity; reject incompatible ratio/quadrant data or false identity claims.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Proof and algebraic use of the identity** | Substituting $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ into $x^2+y^2=1$ gives $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ for every real angle. Here $\sin^2\theta=(\sin\theta)^2$, not $\sin(\theta^2)$. An identity holds throughout the expressions’ common domain; a conditional equation holds only at its solutions. Algebraic rearrangements preserve valid domains. One allowed counterexample disproves an identity. Numerical agreement alone does not prove an identity without an argument establishing that the checked inputs are sufficient. | Prove the Pythagorean identity from the unit-circle equation and interpret squared trigonometric notation. Use the identity to simplify and rearrange expressions, distinguish identities from conditional equations, and disprove false identity claims with valid counterexamples. | Prove the identity for an arbitrary angle by coordinate substitution. Distinguish squared values from squared inputs. Simplify and rearrange using valid algebra. Distinguish a general proof from numerical checks and give a counterexample within the common domain when disproving a claim. |
| **Recovering ratios with quadrant information** | The identity determines a squared magnitude; the quadrant determines the sign of its square root. Sine and cosine must lie in $[-1,1]$, with strict signs in each open quadrant. Given finite tangent $t$, use $y=tx$ and $x^2+y^2=1$, hence $(1+t^2)x^2=1$, then choose coordinate signs. Tangent is formed only when cosine is nonzero. Recovered ratios must satisfy the identity and the original ratio/quadrant conditions. | Find missing sine, cosine, and tangent values from any one of these ratios and a specified quadrant using the Pythagorean identity or equivalent coordinate equations. Justify square-root signs, verify recovered ratios, and detect incompatible ratio/quadrant information. | Recover magnitudes from the given ratio using the identity or equivalent coordinate reasoning. Choose signs from the stated quadrant before forming a quotient. Reject impossible magnitudes or signs and verify the reconstructed ratios against both the identity and the original conditions. |
