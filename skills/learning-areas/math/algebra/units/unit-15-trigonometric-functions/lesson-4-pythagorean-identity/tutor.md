## Concept guidance

### Proof and algebraic use of the identity

Curriculum reference: **Proof and algebraic use of the identity** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Derive $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ geometrically.

**Agent key:** Unit-circle coordinates satisfy x²+y²=1; substitute x=cosθ,y=sinθ. This covers every real angle.

**Worked example:** Does agreement at θ=0 prove $\sin\theta=0$ is an identity?

**Worked reasoning:** No: at θ=π/2 sine is 1. An equation can hold at selected inputs without being an identity.


#### Teaching sequence

Start with the unit-circle equation x²+y²=1 and substitute x=cosθ,y=sinθ. Clarify that sin²θ means (sinθ)², not sin(θ²). Use the identity to rewrite expressions while tracking any later division restrictions. Distinguish proving an identity for all allowed angles from solving an equation true at selected angles.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which circle equation holds at every represented point?

If a sample such as θ=0 is used as proof, ask what covers every point on the circle. If squares are placed on the angle, write the function value in parentheses. If division by sine or cosine occurs, record their zeros as excluded.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Explain the geometric proof, perform basic rearrangements and critique a false identity using a permitted counterexample.

#### Assessment evidence

Require a general circle-based derivation, correct notation and identity-versus-equation reasoning. Samples can refute a claim but cannot establish the full identity.

### Recovering ratios with quadrant information

Curriculum reference: **Recovering ratios with quadrant information** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** If sinθ=3/5 and θ lies in quadrant II, find cosine and tangent.

**Agent key:** Cosine squared is 16/25 and its quadrant sign is negative: cosθ=-4/5, tanθ=-3/4.

**Worked example:** If tanθ=-2 and θ lies in quadrant IV, find sine and cosine.

**Worked reasoning:** $(1+4)x^2=1$ and x>0 give cosθ=1/√5, sinθ=-2/√5; signs satisfy the quadrant and quotient.


#### Teaching sequence

Use the identity to find the missing coordinate's squared magnitude, then choose its sign from the stated quadrant. For a tangent ratio, combine y=tx with x²+y²=1 and use coordinate signs to recover both values. Check magnitude bounds and consistency of all supplied information before calculating.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which sign is compatible with the stated quadrant?

If the principal square root is chosen automatically in a negative-coordinate quadrant, attach the quadrant sign after magnitude calculation. If |sinθ|>1 is accepted, compare with the circle. If tangent sign conflicts with the quadrant, report inconsistent data.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Recover ratios from sine, cosine or tangent, vary quadrants and include impossible or insufficient sign information. Verify the recovered values in both the identity and supplied ratio.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess magnitudes, justified signs, consistency checks and all recovered ratios. If the quadrant is absent, retain valid alternatives rather than inventing a unique answer.
