# Lesson 32.2: Parallel and perpendicular lines

Slope criteria emerge from geometric direction and right-angle relationships.

## Learning objectives

- Derive slope and prove parallel and perpendicular line criteria with their exceptional cases.
- Find equations of parallel and perpendicular lines through specified points.

## Proficiency criteria

- Treat vertical slopes as undefined and distinguish coincident from distinct parallel lines.
- Verify both point incidence and direction in each constructed equation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Slope and parallelism** | For a nonvertical line, \(m=(y_2-y_1)/(x_2-x_1)\) is independent of the selected point pair by similar right triangles. Distinct nonvertical lines are parallel exactly when their slopes agree; this follows from equal corresponding direction angles or proportional direction triangles. Distinct vertical lines are parallel, while equal slopes and a shared point give coincident lines. | Derive the slope formula and prove and apply the slope criteria for parallel lines. | Justify independence of the point pair, state the nonvertical restriction, and distinguish parallelism from coincidence when intercepts or points agree. |
| **Perpendicularity and line equations** | A quarter-turn carries a direction \((u,v)\) to \((-v,u)\); for finite nonzero slopes this gives \(m_1m_2=-1\) as the perpendicular criterion, and the converse follows from proportional rotated directions. Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular and are handled without slope division. Through \((x_0,y_0)\), use \(y-y_0=m(x-x_0)\) for finite slope or \(x=x_0\) for a vertical line. | Prove the perpendicular slope criterion and determine equations of parallel and perpendicular lines through given points. | Explain the rotation or equivalent distance argument, retain horizontal-vertical exceptions, and check the given point in the final line equation. |
