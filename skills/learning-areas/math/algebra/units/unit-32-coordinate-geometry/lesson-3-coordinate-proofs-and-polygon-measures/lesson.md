# Lesson 32.3: Coordinate proofs and polygon measures

Algebraic invariants can establish geometric claims and measure figures whose sides are not axis aligned.

## Learning objectives

- Use coordinates to prove or disprove triangle, quadrilateral, and circle relationships.
- Compute polygon perimeters and triangle and rectangle areas from coordinates.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose coordinates that preserve all given freedom and justify the geometric meaning of algebraic equalities.
- Use perpendicular heights or a justified area method and distinguish length from squared length.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **General coordinate proofs** | Distance, slope, midpoint, and line equations establish congruence of segments, parallelism, perpendicularity, and bisection. Coordinates with parameters permit proofs for whole classes of figures; translations and rotations can simplify placement only when they preserve all relevant assumptions. Substitution checks whether a point lies on a circle or line. | Prove or disprove geometric claims about triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles using coordinate methods. | Translate every algebraic result into a sufficient geometric condition, handle undefined slopes, and avoid proving a general statement only for a specially symmetric numerical case. |
| **Perimeters and coordinate areas** | Polygon perimeter is the sum of distances between consecutive vertices, including the closing side. Triangle area is \(\tfrac12 bh\) with perpendicular height; it can be found by dissection or by \(\tfrac12\lvert(x_2-x_1)(y_3-y_1)-(x_3-x_1)(y_2-y_1)\rvert\), justified from rectangle areas. A rectangle’s area is the product of adjacent perpendicular side lengths, even when rotated. | Use coordinates to determine polygon perimeters and areas of triangles and rectangles. | Respect vertex order, justify any decomposition or determinant formula, verify perpendicular dimensions for rectangles, and express area in square units. |
