# Lesson 32.4: Circle equations and intersections

A circle’s distance definition determines its equation and supports algebraic intersection analysis.

## Learning objectives

- Derive a circle equation from center and radius and recover geometric information by completing the square.
- Use circle equations to test incidence and determine line-circle and circle-circle intersections.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify when an equation represents a real positive-radius circle, a single point, or no real points.
- Check all intersection candidates in the original equations and relate solution counts to geometric position.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard and expanded circle equations** | For center \((h,k)\) and radius \(r>0\), the distance definition and Pythagorean theorem give \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\). An expanded equation \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\) is converted by completing squares. The recovered squared radius determines a circle if positive, a point if zero, or no real locus if negative; unequal squared coefficients or an \(xy\) term do not have this circle form. | Derive circle equations, graph circles from their parameters, and complete the square to recover center and radius or identify a degenerate or empty locus. | Justify the equation from distance, adjust constants correctly in completing the square, and distinguish radius from radius squared and the center from its sign in the equation. |
| **Circle incidence and intersections** | Substitution determines whether a point satisfies a circle’s distance equation. Substituting a line into a circle yields a quadratic with zero, one, or two real intersections. Subtracting two expanded circle equations eliminates squared terms and gives a line for any common points; identical equations give the same circle, while concentric unequal circles have no intersections. Solutions must satisfy both original equations. | Verify circle incidence and determine and interpret line-circle and circle-circle intersections algebraically. | Interpret tangency as one common point, handle vertical lines and identical or concentric circles separately, and reject candidates failing either original equation. |
