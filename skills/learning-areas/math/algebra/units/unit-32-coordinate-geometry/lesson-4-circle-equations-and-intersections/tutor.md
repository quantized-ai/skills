## Reasoning and error-analysis activity

**When to use:** In learn or practice mode after the first relevant method is intelligible. Ask for a justification or counterexample before revealing the key.

**Prompt:** A simplified difference of two circle equations is 0=0. A student says this proves every point in the plane is an intersection. Evaluate the claim.

**Agent key and discussion:** It means the subtraction supplies no new restriction, often because the circle equations are identical. Common points must still satisfy the original circle; equal loci give infinitely many points on that circle, not the plane.

**Respond to the attempt:** Identify which asserted step the student can justify and preserve that evidence. If they only state the final correction, ask them to test the original claim or identify its missing hypothesis. After feedback, let them revise; use a fresh configuration for independent evidence.

## Concept guidance

### Standard and expanded circle equations

Curriculum reference: **Standard and expanded circle equations** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). Its content and proficiency remain authoritative; the activities below collect evidence for that concept.

#### Calibration examples — agent keys

- **Diagnostic prompt:** What real locus is (x−1)²+(y+2)²=−4?
- **Diagnostic key:** Empty: a sum of real squares cannot be negative.
- **Worked-example prompt:** Classify x²+y²-6x+4y+9=0 and recover its geometry.
- **Worked model and reasoning:** Completing squares gives $(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=4$: center $(3,-2)$, radius 2. The distance formula explains the standard equation. A squared radius of zero gives a point and a negative one gives an empty real locus.
- **First hint:** Add the same square-completion constants to both sides.

#### Learn

- Derive the circle equation from fixed distance to (h,k).
- Complete x and y squares separately, keeping a balance of added constants.
- Read the center from the completed factors and classify radius squared as positive, zero or negative before taking a root.

#### Practice progression

Move from center-radius construction to expanded forms, graph cardinal points, then include point/empty loci and reject relations outside the circle coefficient pattern.

**Further variation and generation checks:** Generate real circles, point loci and empty loci; never take a negative squared radius as a real radius.

#### Misconceptions and responsive feedback

If signs of the center are copied from the factors, substitute that proposed center. If r² is called the radius, check a point one actual radius away.

#### Assess

Generate fresh questions without showing the method or key. Use this explicit coverage checklist; a single worked example does not cover every case:

- Justify the equation from distance, adjust constants correctly in completing the square, and distinguish radius from radius squared and the center from its sign in the equation.

**Task range to sample:** Generate real circles, point loci and empty loci; never take a negative squared radius as a real radius.

### Circle incidence and intersections

Curriculum reference: **Circle incidence and intersections** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). Its content and proficiency remain authoritative; the activities below collect evidence for that concept.

#### Calibration examples — agent keys

- **Diagnostic prompt:** Does a double root after line substitution mean two circle intersection points?
- **Diagnostic key:** No: one repeated input gives one tangent point.
- **Worked-example prompt:** Intersect x²+y²=25 with y=3 and verify the points.
- **Worked model and reasoning:** $x^2=16$, so $(-4,3),(4,3)$; both satisfy both equations. The line y=5 is tangent at (0,5), while y=6 misses. For two circles, subtract their equations to get the radical axis unless centers and radii make them coincident or concentric.
- **First hint:** Substitute the line into the circle before interpreting a discriminant.

#### Learn

- Substitute a nonvertical line or fixed x into the circle and solve the actual resulting equation.
- Interpret zero/one/two real solutions geometrically and verify both coordinates.
- For two circles subtract their equations, checking identical or concentric cases before treating the difference as a line.

#### Practice progression

Practice point incidence, secant/tangent/missing lines including verticals, then two-circle intersections and identical/concentric exceptions.

**Further variation and generation checks:** Cover line-circle and circle-circle zero/one/two intersections, plus coincident circles; verify every candidate in both originals.

#### Misconceptions and responsive feedback

If a canceled equation produces 0=0, do not invent a radical axis; compare the originals. A candidate satisfying only the difference relation may fail both circles.

#### Assess

Generate fresh questions without showing the method or key. Use this explicit coverage checklist; a single worked example does not cover every case:

- Interpret tangency as one common point, handle vertical lines and identical or concentric circles separately, and reject candidates failing either original equation.

**Task range to sample:** Cover line-circle and circle-circle zero/one/two intersections, plus coincident circles; verify every candidate in both originals.
