# Lesson 32.1: Distance, midpoints, and partitions

Coordinate differences encode lengths and proportional positions on directed segments.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and use distance and midpoint formulas and find internal division points in one and two dimensions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use positive distances and preserve exact radicals where appropriate.
- Distinguish the direction of a segment and the order of its ratio, verifying the result lies between its endpoints.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Distance and midpoint formulas** | For \(A=(x_1,y_1)\), \(B=(x_2,y_2)\), the Pythagorean theorem gives \(AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\). The midpoint is \(((x_1+x_2)/2,(y_1+y_2)/2)\), derived from equal coordinate displacements or a half-scale dilation. On a number line distance is \(\lvert x_2-x_1\rvert\). | Derive and apply distance and midpoint formulas to verify segment lengths, congruence, and bisection. | Explain the right-triangle derivation, distinguish displacement from distance, and verify that a midpoint is equidistant from and between the endpoints. |
| **Directed internal division** | For distinct endpoints \(A,B\), a point fraction \(t\) of the way from \(A\) to \(B\) is \(P=A+t(B-A)\), with \(0<t<1\) for internal division. If \(AP:PB=m:n\) with \(m,n>0\), then \(t=m/(m+n)\) and \(P=(nA+mB)/(m+n)\). The midpoint is the case \(m=n\). These formulas apply in one or two dimensions. | Find a point dividing a directed segment internally in a given fraction or ratio in one and two dimensions. | Use the correct endpoint weights, distinguish a fraction of the whole from a ratio of parts, and verify both the ratio and internal position. |
