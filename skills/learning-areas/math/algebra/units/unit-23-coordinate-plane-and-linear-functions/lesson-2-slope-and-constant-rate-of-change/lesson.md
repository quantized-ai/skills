# Lesson 23.2: Slope and constant rate of change

Slope measures a constant ratio of changes and connects the geometry of a line with the rate in a linear relationship.

## Learning objectives

- Compute and interpret slope from points, tables, graphs, and equations.
- Explain why slope is constant on a nonvertical line and handle horizontal or vertical cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use consistent coordinate subtraction and express contextual rates with units.
- Separate zero slope from undefined slope and check whether tabular rates are actually constant.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Slope as a ratio of changes** | For distinct points with \(x_2\ne x_1\), slope is \(m=(y_2-y_1)/(x_2-x_1)\). Similar right triangles formed with a nonvertical line have equal rise-to-run ratios, explaining constancy. Positive, negative, and zero slope describe increasing, decreasing, and constant linear relationships. A vertical line has undefined slope because its horizontal change is zero. | Calculate slope from points and graphs and justify its constancy using similar slope triangles. | Subtract coordinates in the same order, include signs and units, and distinguish a horizontal line with slope zero from a vertical line with no defined slope. |
| **Rates across linear representations** | For a linear function, every nonzero input interval gives the same quotient \(\Delta y/\Delta x\). Equal first differences indicate a constant rate only when input intervals are equal. In \(y=mx+b\), the coefficient \(m\) is this rate; rates from other forms can be found by rearranging or selecting points. | Determine and compare linear rates of change from tables, formulas, graphs, and contextual descriptions. | Divide by the actual input interval, interpret output units per input unit, and identify nonconstant rates instead of forcing a line through incompatible data. |
