# Lesson 23.5: Parallel and perpendicular lines

Slope relationships determine the direction of a new line through a specified point, with axis-parallel lines requiring separate treatment.

## Learning objectives

- Use slope criteria to construct parallel and perpendicular lines.
- Handle coincident, horizontal, and vertical lines explicitly.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish equal slopes from distinct parallel lines and use the negative reciprocal only for nonzero finite slopes.
- Verify the requested incidence and direction conditions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parallel lines** | Distinct nonvertical lines are parallel exactly when they have the same slope and different intercepts; coincident lines share all points. All distinct vertical lines are parallel. A line parallel to a given line through a prescribed point inherits the given direction and is determined by that point. | Determine whether lines are parallel or coincident and construct a parallel line through a specified point. | Check both slope and intercept or standard-form equivalence, handle vertical lines, and state when the prescribed point forces the same line rather than a distinct parallel. |
| **Perpendicular lines** | For two nonvertical lines with nonzero slopes, perpendicularity is equivalent to \(m_1m_2=-1\). Horizontal and vertical lines form a perpendicular pair and do not fit a two-finite-slope calculation. A required perpendicular through a point can be written from its determined slope or constant coordinate. | Determine perpendicularity and construct a perpendicular line through a specified point, including horizontal and vertical cases. | Use a negative reciprocal rather than a sign change alone, handle zero and undefined slopes separately, and verify both point membership and perpendicular direction. |
