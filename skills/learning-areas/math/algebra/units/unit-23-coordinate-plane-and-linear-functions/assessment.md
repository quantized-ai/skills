## Lesson 23.1

[Curriculum](lesson-1-coordinates-and-graphs-of-equations/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-1-coordinates-and-graphs-of-equations/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Plot $A=(-2,3)$ and $B=(4,0)$ and decide whether $(2,5)$ and $(2,4)$ lie on $y=2x+1$. Describe the graph of $x=-2$.

**Checked reasoning:** A is in quadrant II; B lies on the positive horizontal axis, not in a quadrant. $(2,5)$ satisfies the equation; $(2,4)$ does not. $x=-2$ is a vertical line containing every ordered pair with first coordinate -2. Plot with labeled axes and equal or explicitly declared scales.

**Coverage limit:** Include axis points, negative coordinates, relation membership, vertical/horizontal equations and conversion among tables, graph points and equations; inspect actual plotted points when graphing is required.

## Lesson 23.2

[Curriculum](lesson-2-slope-and-constant-rate-of-change/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-2-slope-and-constant-rate-of-change/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Find the slope through $(1,5)$ and $(4,-1)$, and compare with $y=7$ and $x=7$.

**Checked reasoning:** Slope $(-1-5)/(4-1)=-6/3=-2$. Horizontal $y=7$ has slope zero; vertical $x=7$ has undefined slope because its horizontal change is zero. Reversing both point orders leaves the ratio unchanged.

**Coverage limit:** Vary graphical scales, units, rising/falling lines, zero and undefined slopes, tables with unequal x gaps and contextual rates.

## Lesson 23.3

[Curriculum](lesson-3-slope-intercept-form-and-graph-features/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-3-slope-intercept-form-and-graph-features/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** For $y=-2x+6$, identify intercepts, zero and sign intervals, then graph it.

**Checked reasoning:** Vertical intercept $(0,6)$; horizontal intercept $(3,0)$ and zero x=3. $y>0$ for x<3 and $y<0$ for x>3 over the real domain. Two distinct points determine the line, and the slope is -2. An intercept point and its coordinate are different types of answer.

**Coverage limit:** Include positive/negative/zero slopes and restricted domains; derive the slope-intercept relationship from a fixed rate and an initial value before relying on a mnemonic.

## Lesson 23.4

[Curriculum](lesson-4-constructing-and-converting-equations-of-lines/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-4-constructing-and-converting-equations-of-lines/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Find the line through $(2,1)$ and $(5,7)$ in point-slope, slope-intercept and standard form. Also handle $(2,1)$ and $(2,7)$.

**Checked reasoning:** First slope 2, so $y-1=2(x-2)$, $y=2x-3$, and $2x-y=3$ are equivalent. Substitution verifies both points. Second is $x=2$, not a slope-intercept function y of x.

**Coverage limit:** Use two points, a point and slope, table or graph; include horizontal/vertical lines and repeated identical points that do not determine a unique line.

## Lesson 23.5

[Curriculum](lesson-5-parallel-and-perpendicular-lines/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-5-parallel-and-perpendicular-lines/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Through $(3,-1)$, find a line parallel and a line perpendicular to $y=2x+5$. Then handle a line perpendicular to $x=4$.

**Checked reasoning:** Parallel line $y+1=2(x-3)$, hence $y=2x-7$; perpendicular line $y+1=-\tfrac12(x-3)$, hence $y=-x/2+1/2$. A line through $(3,-1)$ perpendicular to vertical $x=4$ is horizontal $y=-1$.

**Coverage limit:** Include distinct parallel versus coincident lines, horizontal/vertical exceptions, point constraints and geometric justification for negative reciprocal slopes.

## Lesson 23.6

[Curriculum](lesson-6-linear-models-domains-and-transformations/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-6-linear-models-domains-and-transformations/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** A tank starts at 24 liters and drains at 3 liters/minute until empty. Build a model and domain. For $f(t)=24-3t$ on $[0,8]$, find $g(t)=f(t-2)+5$ and its inherited domain.

**Checked reasoning:** $V=24-3t$ for $0\le t\le8$; zero at 8 minutes. $g=35-3t$ with $t-2\in[0,8]$, so domain $[2,10]$. It is the shifted mathematical function; it is not automatically the original tank's physical process.

**Coverage limit:** Generate cost/rate models with feasible domains and transformations of explicitly restricted functions; distinguish horizontal shift from vertical change and mathematical equivalence from contextual interpretation.
