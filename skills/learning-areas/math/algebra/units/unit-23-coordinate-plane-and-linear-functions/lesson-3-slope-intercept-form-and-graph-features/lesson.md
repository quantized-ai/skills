# Lesson 23.3: Slope-intercept form and graph features

Slope-intercept form makes a linear function’s constant rate and initial output explicit.

## Learning objectives

- Relate slope-intercept equations to graphs, intercepts, zeros, and signs.
- Derive linear equations from constant rate and an initial value.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish y-intercepts, x-intercepts, and undefined or nonunique intercept cases.
- Interpret graph features over the stated domain rather than automatically over all real numbers.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deriving and graphing slope-intercept form** | A line through \((0,b)\) with finite slope \(m\) satisfies \((y-b)/(x-0)=m\) when \(x\ne0\), giving \(y=mx+b\), with the intercept also satisfying this equation. The graph can be constructed from the y-intercept and a slope displacement. The full algebraic domain is real unless restricted. | Derive, graph, and interpret slope-intercept equations, including constant functions. | Locate the y-intercept, use rise and run with their signs and scales, and verify that the full line or restricted graph matches the given equation. |
| **Intercepts, zeros, and signs** | For \(y=mx+b\), the y-intercept is \((0,b)\). When \(m\ne0\), the zero is \(-b/m\) and gives an x-intercept if it lies in the domain. A nonzero constant function has no zero; the zero function is zero at every domain value. Restricted-domain extrema, positivity, and negativity follow from slope and allowed endpoints. | Determine and interpret intercepts, zeros, increasing or decreasing behavior, and sign intervals of linear functions on stated domains. | Distinguish an input zero from an intercept point, handle constant and restricted-domain cases, and justify any endpoint maximum or minimum using domain inclusion. |
