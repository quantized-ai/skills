# Lesson 23.4: Constructing and converting equations of lines

Different line forms reveal different information while representing the same set of points.

## Learning objectives

- Construct line equations from points, slopes, tables, graphs, and verbal relationships.
- Convert between point-slope, slope-intercept, and standard forms with their exceptional cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Verify that each supplied point satisfies the equation and each stated rate matches its slope.
- Preserve equivalent solution sets and handle vertical lines separately.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Point-slope and two-point construction** | A nonvertical line of slope \(m\) through \((x_1,y_1)\) has form \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\). Two distinct points determine a line; if their x-coordinates differ, the slope formula supplies \(m\). Equal x-coordinates give a vertical line \(x=x_1\); coincident points alone do not determine a unique line. | Construct an equation of a line from one point and slope or from two points, including vertical and insufficient-data cases. | Use both coordinate differences consistently, distinguish distinct from coincident points, and verify all supplied geometric information. |
| **Equivalent line forms and multiple representations** | Standard form \(Ax+By=C\) requires \(A\) and \(B\) not both zero for a line. When \(B\ne0\), its slope is \(-A/B\); when \(B=0\), it is vertical. A table, graph, or verbal relationship can supply a slope and one point. Equivalent forms differ through reversible operations or nonzero common scaling. | Write and convert linear equations among standard, slope-intercept, and point-slope forms from tables, graphs, and verbal descriptions. | Respect coefficients that preclude a chosen form, retain the original solution set, and verify the constructed equation against every supplied point or condition. |
