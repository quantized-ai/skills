# Lesson 23.1: Coordinates and graphs of equations

Ordered pairs specify locations, and equation graphs collect every pair satisfying a relation.

## Learning objectives

- Plot and interpret coordinates in all four quadrants.
- Connect two-variable equation solutions to graphs and intercepts.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the stated coordinate order, scales, units, and axis conventions.
- Verify graph membership by substitution and distinguish plotted samples from the whole solution set.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cartesian coordinates** | An ordered pair \((x,y)\) gives horizontal and vertical coordinates relative to an origin. Quadrants classify signs; points on axes lie in no quadrant. Each axis has a stated scale and unit. Changing the sign of one coordinate reflects a point across the other axis; changing both reflects across both axes. | Plot, read, and interpret real coordinate pairs using labeled axes and appropriate scales. | Preserve coordinate order, place negative and zero coordinates correctly, and relate changes of signs to locations and axis reflections. |
| **Equation graphs as solution sets** | The graph of an equation in two variables is the set of all ordered pairs that make it true and defined. A table gives selected solutions. A line is determined by two distinct points, but a general curve is not determined by a few plotted points. Axis intercepts have respectively \(y=0\) or \(x=0\). | Generate and verify ordered-pair solutions of linear equations and graph the complete solution line with its intercepts. | Use at least two distinct valid points or equivalent line information, verify candidates in the original equation, and distinguish coordinates of intercepts from scalar intercept values. |
