# Lesson 37.1: Polar coordinate representations

Polar coordinates describe position by directed distance and angle, with several coordinate pairs representing the same point.

## Learning objectives

- Plot polar points and convert between polar and rectangular coordinates.
- Describe equivalent representations, including negative radii and the pole.

## Proficiency criteria

- Locate the correct ray or opposite ray and use a quadrant-correct angle when converting.
- Distinguish the unique rectangular point from its many polar coordinate pairs.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Polar coordinates and equivalent pairs** | A polar point $(r,\theta)$ lies a directed distance $r$ from the pole along the ray at angle $\theta$. For any integer $k$, $(r,\theta+2k\pi)$ and $(-r,\theta+(2k+1)\pi)$ represent the same point. The pole has $r=0$ with arbitrary angle. A chosen interval of angles and the requirement $r\ge0$ are conventions that limit representations. | Plot polar points and determine equivalent coordinate pairs under signed-radius and angle-interval conventions. | Handle negative radii by reversing direction, distinguish coterminal angles from different points, and state the nonuniqueness of the pole angle. |
| **Rectangular-polar conversion** | $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, and $r^2=x^2+y^2$. With $r\ge0$, $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and the angle is chosen by both coordinates; $\tan\theta=y/x$ alone does not determine the quadrant and is unusable at $x=0$. Nonzero axis points require their appropriate axis angles. | Convert between rectangular and polar coordinates exactly or approximately with an explicit radius and angle convention. | Reconstruct both original coordinates, select the correct quadrant or axis, distinguish exact from approximate angle values, and treat the origin separately. |
