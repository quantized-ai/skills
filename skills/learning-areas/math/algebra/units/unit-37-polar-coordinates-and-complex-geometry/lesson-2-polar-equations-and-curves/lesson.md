# Lesson 37.2: Polar equations and curves

Polar equations describe curves through radius-angle relationships; plotting and conversion must account for repeated tracing, signed radii, and exceptional points.

## Learning objectives

- Graph polar equations using points, symmetry, and technology.
- Convert simple polar and rectangular relations and verify equivalence of the represented points.

## Proficiency criteria

- Track angle intervals, pole crossings, negative radii, and possible repeated tracing.
- Check points lost or introduced when multiplying, dividing, or squaring.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Polar graph construction** | A relation $r=f(\theta)$ generates points $(f(\theta)\cos\theta,f(\theta)\sin\theta)$ over a specified angle domain. Zeros locate pole passages; signs and extreme radii guide graph shape. Circles, rays, spirals, roses, and limacons illustrate distinct radius-angle behavior. Technology must use an adequate angle window and resolution; one apparent circuit need not imply every point is traced once. | Graph polar equations by plotting informative points and using technology, describing pole passages, radius behavior, and tracing over the specified angle interval. | Plot negative radii correctly, use enough angles to resolve loops and petals, identify repeated tracing where it occurs, and reconcile the display with calculated points. |
| **Polar symmetry and rectangular relations** | The substitutions $(r,-\theta)$, $(r,\pi-\theta)$, or $(r,\theta+\pi)$ can establish polar-axis, vertical-axis, or pole symmetry when they preserve the represented set. Failure of one algebraic test does not disprove symmetry because representations are nonunique. Conversion uses $r^2=x^2+y^2$, $r\cos\theta=x$, and $r\sin\theta=y$; division by $r$ or squaring requires a separate equivalence check. | Use verified symmetries to support polar graphs and convert simple polar and rectangular relations while preserving their point sets. | Justify symmetry through represented points, identify any sign or angle restriction in a conversion, and check the pole and any points potentially lost or added by algebra. |
