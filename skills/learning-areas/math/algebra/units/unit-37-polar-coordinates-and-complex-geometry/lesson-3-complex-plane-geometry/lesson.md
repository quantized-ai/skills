# Lesson 37.3: Complex-plane geometry

Rectangular complex numbers become points and directed displacements in the plane, linking arithmetic to geometric constructions and measurements.

## Learning objectives

- Represent addition, subtraction, conjugation, and multiplication by the imaginary unit geometrically and use them in computations.
- Calculate distances and midpoints of complex points and interpret fixed-distance loci.
- Convert between rectangular and polar complex forms and explain their equivalence, including axis and zero cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve real and imaginary coordinates, subtraction direction, and lengths under conjugation and unit rotations.
- Compute distance from a difference and midpoint from an average, and recognize the radius-zero locus.
- Distinguish the modulus from the complex point, select a quadrant-correct argument, and treat zero without a unique argument.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Geometric arithmetic and conjugation** | $z=a+bi$ corresponds to $(a,b)$. Addition combines directed displacements; $z-w$ is the displacement from $w$ to $z$. Conjugation $\overline z=a-bi$ reflects across the real axis, preserving modulus. Multiplication by $i$ is a counterclockwise quarter-turn and negation a half-turn. | Represent addition, subtraction, conjugation, and multiplication by the imaginary unit geometrically and use those interpretations to compute complex numbers. | Place displacement endpoints and subtraction direction correctly, preserve lengths under conjugation and unit rotations, and reconcile the geometric result with rectangular arithmetic. |
| **Distance and midpoint in the complex plane** | The distance between $z$ and $w$ is $\lvert z-w\rvert=\sqrt{(\operatorname{Re}z-\operatorname{Re}w)^2+(\operatorname{Im}z-\operatorname{Im}w)^2}$. Their midpoint is $(z+w)/2$. Distance is a nonnegative real number, while the midpoint is a complex point; the same modulus describes loci $\lvert z-z_0\rvert=R$ for $R\ge0$. | Calculate distances and midpoints of complex points and interpret fixed-distance loci geometrically. | Compute distance from a difference and midpoint from an average, distinguish real lengths from complex positions, and recognize the radius-zero single-point case. |
| **Polar form and argument** | For nonzero $z=a+bi$, $z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$ with $r=\lvert z\rvert>0$ and $a=r\cos\theta$, $b=r\sin\theta$. Arguments differ by integer multiples of $2\pi$; a principal argument is chosen in a stated interval, here $(-\pi,\pi]$. Zero has modulus zero and no uniquely defined argument. Purely real and purely imaginary points require axis cases. | Convert complex numbers between rectangular and polar forms and explain their equivalence, including real, imaginary, and zero cases. | Choose a quadrant-correct argument, reconstruct both rectangular components, distinguish a principal argument from all arguments, and avoid assigning zero a unique argument. |
