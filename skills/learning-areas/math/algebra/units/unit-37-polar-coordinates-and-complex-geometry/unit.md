# Unit 37: Polar coordinates and complex geometry

This unit relates rectangular and polar descriptions of points, curves, and complex numbers. Geometric operations on complex numbers lead to rotation-scaling interpretations, powers, and complete sets of complex roots.

## Main learning objectives

- Convert points and curves between polar and rectangular descriptions while preserving the represented set.
- Interpret complex arithmetic, distances, and midpoints geometrically.
- Use polar multiplication, powers, and roots to calculate and explain complex-number configurations.

## Concepts and topics

Polar coordinates and equivalence; polar graphs and equation conversion; rectangular and polar complex forms; geometric addition, subtraction, conjugation, multiplication, and division; distances and midpoints; De Moivre powers; roots and roots of unity.

## Lessons

- [37.1: Polar coordinate representations](lesson-1-polar-coordinate-representations/lesson.md) — Polar coordinates describe position by directed distance and angle, with several coordinate pairs representing the same point.
- [37.2: Polar equations and curves](lesson-2-polar-equations-and-curves/lesson.md) — Polar equations describe curves through radius-angle relationships; plotting and conversion must account for repeated tracing, signed radii, and exceptional points.
- [37.3: Complex-plane geometry](lesson-3-complex-plane-geometry/lesson.md) — Rectangular complex numbers become points and directed displacements in the plane, linking arithmetic to geometric constructions and measurements.
- [37.4: Polar complex multiplication and division](lesson-4-polar-complex-multiplication-and-division/lesson.md) — Polar form turns complex multiplication into scaling and rotation, and division into reciprocal scaling and subtraction of angles.
- [37.5: Complex powers and roots](lesson-5-complex-powers-and-roots/lesson.md) — Repeated scaling and rotation yield powers and all roots of a complex number, including their geometric arrangement.
