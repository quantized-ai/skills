# Lesson 37.5: Complex powers and roots

Repeated scaling and rotation yield powers and all roots of a complex number, including their geometric arrangement.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and apply De Moivre’s formula for integer powers.
- Find all complex nth roots and justify their number and spacing.

## Proficiency criteria

- Handle zero and negative powers with the proper restrictions.
- List distinct roots without omitting or duplicating angles and verify that their powers recover the original number.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **De Moivre powers** | For integer $n$, $(r\operatorname{cis}\theta)^n=r^n\operatorname{cis}(n\theta)$, with a nonzero base for $n\le0$ and positive integer exponents permitted for zero. Repeated multiplication proves the positive-integer case; reciprocals extend it to negative integers. A nonzero number to the zeroth power is one; $0^0$ is not assigned a value here. | Derive De Moivre’s formula from polar multiplication and apply it to exact positive, zero, and negative integer powers on their valid domains. | Raise the modulus and multiply the angle, apply nonzero restrictions for zero or negative exponents, and convert back to rectangular form when exact trigonometric values permit. |
| **Complete sets of complex roots** | For $z=r\operatorname{cis}\theta\ne0$ and positive integer $n$, its distinct nth roots are $r^{1/n}\operatorname{cis}((\theta+2k\pi)/n)$ for $k=0,\ldots,n-1$. They are equally spaced around the circle of radius $r^{1/n}$; for $n\ge3$ they are vertices of a regular polygon. Zero has only the distinct root zero, of multiplicity $n$ in $w^n=0$. Roots of unity have radius one. | Determine and verify every complex nth root, including roots of unity, and explain their spacing and multiplicity distinctions. | Produce exactly n distinct roots for a nonzero input, verify the common nth power, distinguish distinct roots from multiplicity at zero, and explain why additional integer angle choices repeat the list. |
