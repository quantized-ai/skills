# Unit 13: Linear systems in three variables

Equivalent-system reasoning extends substitution and elimination to three variables, matrix representations, and complete solution classification.

## Main learning objectives

- Verify simultaneous solutions and classify two-variable linear systems. Justify reversible equation replacements and solve systems by both substitution and elimination.
- Formulate three-variable systems with consistent quantities, units, and contextual restrictions. Solve systems by both substitution and Gaussian elimination and verify ordered triples.
- Encode three-variable systems and interpret reduced row-echelon output from matrix technology. Classify systems as inconsistent, uniquely solvable, or having infinitely many solutions.

## Concepts and topics

Simultaneous solutions and reversible elimination; Substitution and elimination in two variables; Formulation and substitution; Gaussian elimination and back-substitution; Augmented matrices and technology; Contradictions and free variables.

## Lessons

- [13.1: Solution sets and equivalent systems](lesson-1-solution-sets-and-equivalent-systems/lesson.md) — Linear systems require every equation to hold simultaneously. Equivalent systems preserve the complete solution set through reversible operations; substitution and elimination provide exact solutions that can be checked geometrically.
- [13.2: Three-variable linear systems](lesson-2-three-variable-linear-systems/lesson.md) — Three-variable linear systems express simultaneous constraints on ordered triples. Substitution and Gaussian elimination reduce those constraints without changing their solution set.
- [13.3: Matrix representation and solution classification](lesson-3-matrix-representation-and-solution-classification/lesson.md) — Augmented matrices record linear systems for systematic computation. Interpreting reduced rows identifies contradictions, uniquely determined variables, and free parameters in the complete solution set.
