**Prompt:** Replace x+y=5 and x−y=1 by their sum 2x=6. Is that equivalent to the original system?

## Concept guidance

### Simultaneous solutions and reversible elimination

Curriculum reference: **Simultaneous solutions and reversible elimination** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Does replacing both equations of $x+y=3$, $x-y=1$ by only their sum preserve the system?

**Agent key:** No: $2x=4$ fixes x=2 but loses the constraint on y. Keeping one original equation also gives y=1.

**Worked example:** Explain why replacing the second equation by its difference with the first is reversible when the first remains.

**Worked reasoning:** Add the retained first equation back to recover the original second. Both directions preserve the simultaneous solution set.


#### Teaching sequence

Interpret a system's solution as satisfying every original equation simultaneously. Explain a row replacement by an equation plus a multiple of another retained equation, and show how reversing that addition recovers the original system. Keeping only the sum of two independent equations loses information. Nonzero scaling and swaps preserve constraints; zero scaling does not.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which original constraint is still retained?

If a combined equation alone is called equivalent, find a point satisfying it but not an original equation. If multiplying by zero is accepted, compare the informative constraint with 0=0. If a row is changed using an already overwritten value, label the current rows.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Compare valid and invalid operations before solving, then construct a reversible elimination step and its inverse. Include redundant equations separately from deliberately dropped constraints.

#### Assessment evidence

Require simultaneous interpretation, reversibility reasoning and identification of information loss. A final correct solution by coincidence does not justify an invalid transformation.

### Substitution and elimination in two variables

Curriculum reference: **Substitution and elimination in two variables** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Solve $x+y=7$, $x-y=1$.

**Agent key:** Addition gives 2x=8, so x=4 and y=3; both original equations check.

**Worked example:** Solve $2x+y=5$, $y=x-1$ by substitution.

**Worked reasoning:** $2x+x-1=5$ gives x=2,y=1. Substitution uses an equivalent expression for y.


#### Teaching sequence

Choose substitution when a variable is already isolated and elimination when coefficients readily cancel. Write the equivalent substituted or combined equation, solve it and recover the other variable in a retained original equation. Check both original equations. Interpret intersecting, parallel and coincident lines as one, none or infinitely many solutions rather than forcing every problem into a unique ordered pair.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Is a variable already isolated, or can coefficients cancel?

If a solved value is not substituted back, ask for the complete point. If a sign changes incorrectly when replacing an expression, use parentheses. If 0=0 or 0=c appears, classify it instead of dividing by a vanished coefficient.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Move from direct substitution to scaled elimination, then dependent/inconsistent pairs and a method-choice comparison. Require exact checks beyond visual estimates.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess a justified method, equivalent steps, complete solution set, both-original verification and geometric classification. Any valid alternative route is acceptable unless a particular method is the target.
