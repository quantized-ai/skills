# Lesson 22.2: Solving and classifying linear inequalities

Ordered comparisons require sign-sensitive operations to preserve their solution sets.

## Learning objectives

- Solve linear inequalities with rational coefficients and variables on both sides.
- Explain sign reversal and classify constant or parameter-dependent cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reverse the comparison exactly when multiplying or dividing by a negative value.
- Treat a zero or unknown-sign coefficient explicitly and verify endpoints and representative values.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equivalent inequality transformations** | Adding the same quantity to both sides preserves order. Multiplication or division by a positive constant preserves the comparison; a negative constant reverses it. Distribution and collection of like terms can reduce a linear inequality to one comparison with the variable. Multiplication by zero does not preserve a general solution set. | Solve linear inequalities with rational coefficients, grouping, and variables on both sides and justify the comparison direction at each step. | Apply operations to complete sides, reverse the comparison for negative multiplication or division, and represent and verify the full resulting solution set. |
| **Constant and symbolic-coefficient cases** | A reduced constant inequality is either always true or always false over the stated domain. For \(ax<b\), positive \(a\), negative \(a\), and \(a=0\) produce different cases. When \(a=0\), the truth of \(0<b\) determines all or no solutions; weak inequalities require their own endpoint-inclusive comparisons. | Classify linear inequalities with disappearing variables or symbolic coefficients, including positive, negative, and zero coefficient cases. | Give complete parameter cases, retain strictness or inclusion, and never divide by a quantity of unknown sign without branching. |
