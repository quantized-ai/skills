# Lesson 22.3: Compound inequalities and contextual constraints

Intersections and unions combine ordered constraints, while contexts can further restrict the permissible values.

## Learning objectives

- Solve compound inequalities using intersection or union as appropriate.
- Construct and interpret linear constraints in real and integer domains.

## Proficiency criteria

- Retain both comparisons in a chained inequality and distinguish and from or.
- Check bounds and integer feasibility in the original conditions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Intersections and unions of inequalities** | An and statement requires every condition and forms an intersection; an or statement requires at least one condition and forms a union. A chain such as \(a<x\le b\) means two simultaneous comparisons. Applying a negative multiplier to a chain reverses both comparisons; overlapping intervals may be combined without changing endpoint membership. | Solve compound linear inequalities and express their complete intersection or union graphically and in interval notation. | Use the correct logical operation, maintain every endpoint condition, and recognize empty, disjoint, bounded, or unbounded results. |
| **Contextual bounds and integer choices** | At least and at most usually give inclusive comparisons; more than and fewer than give strict ones. Quantities with fixed charges or constant rates lead to linear bounds. Nonnegative or integer domains may reduce the algebraic solution set; rounding a threshold must respect both inequality direction and strictness. | Formulate and solve one-variable linear constraints and interpret viable real or integer choices in context. | Define the quantity and units, include all contextual bounds, and identify the smallest or largest admissible integer by checking the original condition. |
