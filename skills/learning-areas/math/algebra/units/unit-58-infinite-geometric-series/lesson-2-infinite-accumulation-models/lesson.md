# Lesson 58.2: Infinite accumulation models

A convergent idealization can represent repeated proportional contributions while retaining the distinction from a finite process.

## Learning objectives

- Represent repeating decimals and repeated proportional totals as convergent geometric series.
- Distinguish finite and infinite payment models and evaluate convergent discounted streams under explicit timing and rate assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Align the start and block length, count each contribution once, reduce exact rational results, and explain the physical or contextual meaning and limits of infinite accumulation.
- Specify payment dates and valuation time, construct discounted terms and their ratio, distinguish finite sums from limits, verify convergence including zero-stream cases, and evaluate the value without assigning a finite price to a divergent stream.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Repeating decimals and accumulation** | A repeating decimal splits into a finite prefix and a geometric tail with ratio $10^{-m}$ for repeating-block length $m$. Other repeated-contribution models require the first contribution, common ratio, units, and a justified infinite-process idealization. | Represent repeating decimals and repeated proportional totals as convergent geometric series. | Align the start and block length, count each contribution once, reduce exact rational results, and explain the physical or contextual meaning and limits of infinite accumulation. |
| **Finite versus infinite financial models** | With period rate $i>-1$, a payment $d_t$ at the end of period $t$ has time-zero value $d_t/(1+i)^t$; finite value sums these terms over actual payment dates. A nonzero constant stream $d$ beginning at $t=1$ has finite infinite value $d/i$ exactly when $i>0$. If $d_t=d(1+g)^{t-1}$ with $d\ne0$ and $1+g>0$, convergence requires $(1+g)/(1+i)<1$, equivalently $g<i$, and value is $d/(i-g)$. Zero streams have value zero. A payment at time zero is added separately. | Distinguish finite and infinite payment models and evaluate convergent discounted streams under explicit timing and rate assumptions. | Specify payment dates and valuation time, construct discounted terms and their ratio, distinguish finite sums from limits, verify convergence including zero-stream cases, and evaluate the value without assigning a finite price to a divergent stream. |
