# Lesson 19.2: Proportions and direct variation

Equivalent ratios produce proportional equations, while direct variation connects constant ratios to tables and origin-passing lines.

## Learning objectives

- Solve proportions and explain the conditions behind cross multiplication.
- Identify and represent direct variation across verbal, tabular, graphical, and symbolic forms.

## Proficiency criteria

- State nonzero denominators and verify the resulting proportion.
- Distinguish constant ratios from constant differences and identify the proportionality constant with units.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Proportional equations** | A proportion equates ratios: \(a/b=c/d\) for \(b,d\ne0\). Multiplication by the common nonzero denominator gives \(ad=bc\); cross multiplication is this justified equation operation, not a rule for arbitrary sums of fractions. Equivalent-ratio scaling and unit rates provide other solution methods. | Formulate and solve proportions using scaling, unit rates, and justified cross multiplication. | Match corresponding quantities and units, preserve denominator restrictions, and verify that the solution satisfies the original ratio and context. |
| **Direct variation across representations** | Direct variation has form \(y=kx\) on a stated domain, with constant \(y/x=k\) where \(x\ne0\). Its graph lies on a line through the origin, and \((1,k)\) shows the unit rate when in the domain. A nonzero initial offset prevents direct proportionality; a context can restrict the graph to discrete values or a portion of the line. | Determine whether a relationship is proportional and construct its equation, table, and graph with an interpreted constant of variation. | Use all supplied values rather than a single ratio, identify the origin condition, and distinguish the full algebraic line from its contextual domain. |
