# Lesson 19.3: Percent relationships and change

Percent expresses a ratio per hundred and supports comparisons, multiplicative changes, and reverse calculations.

## Learning objectives

- Convert percent representations and solve for a part, whole, or rate.
- Model successive percent changes, simple interest, and relative error with the correct reference quantity.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the base of every percent and distinguish percentage points from relative change.
- Apply successive multipliers in order and reverse changes by division rather than subtracting the stated percent.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parts, wholes, and percents** | \(p\%\) means \(p/100\), and part equals rate times whole. Solving for rate or whole requires a nonzero corresponding divisor. Fractions, decimals, and percents are equivalent forms of a ratio, and percentages may exceed 100. Percentage-point differences compare two percent values directly. | Convert between fractions, decimals, and percents and determine an unknown part, whole, or percent rate. | Identify the relevant whole, distinguish a rate from its percent notation, and explain whether a result above 100 percent is meaningful. |
| **Percent increase, decrease, and successive changes** | For original amount \(P\) and decimal rate \(r\), an increase gives \(P(1+r)\) and a decrease gives \(P(1-r)\). Successive changes multiply factors; reversing a nonzero factor requires division. Relative change is \((\text{new}-\text{original})/\text{original}\) for nonzero original value and is interpreted with its base. | Compute and reverse percent changes, including successive discounts, taxes, markups, and fees, and compare relative change with percentage-point change. | Use the correct base at each stage, retain the order when fixed fees also occur, and do not add successive percentage rates as if they shared one unchanged base. |
| **Simple interest and percent error** | Simple interest is \(I=Prt\), where \(r\) is the rate per time unit and \(t\) uses that unit; total amount is \(P+I\). Percent error relative to a nonzero reference value is \(100\lvert\text{estimate}-\text{reference}\rvert/\lvert\text{reference}\rvert\). These quantify proportional accumulation and relative discrepancy. | Solve simple-interest relationships and compute percent error using a stated nonzero reference value. | Match rate and time units, distinguish interest from total amount, and state the reference used for a nonnegative relative error. |
