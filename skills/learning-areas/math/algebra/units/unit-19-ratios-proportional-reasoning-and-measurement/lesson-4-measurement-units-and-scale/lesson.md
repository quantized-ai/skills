# Lesson 19.4: Measurement units and scale

Measurement conversions and dimensional relationships preserve the meaning of quantities as their numerical representations change.

## Learning objectives

- Convert measurements and derived units using equivalent quantities.
- Distinguish linear scale from area and volume scale and check dimensional consistency.

## Proficiency criteria

- Cancel conversion units correctly and account for exponentiated units.
- Use squared or cubed scale factors for corresponding area or volume measurements.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Unit conversions and derived quantities** | A conversion factor is a ratio of equal quantities and therefore represents one. Products of such factors convert units without changing the physical quantity. Derived units such as distance per time or mass per volume arise through multiplication or division. Converting area or volume requires squaring or cubing a length conversion factor. | Convert single and compound measurements and use dimensional consistency to interpret and check formulas. | Show compatible units, orient each conversion factor to cancel the intended unit, and apply exponentiated conversion factors for areas and volumes. |
| **Scale factors for measurements** | A scale drawing uses a fixed ratio between corresponding lengths. Under uniform scale factor \(k>0\), lengths scale by \(k\), areas by \(k^2\), and volumes by \(k^3\). These statements require geometrically similar figures; a common area ratio alone does not establish similarity. | Use scale ratios to determine corresponding lengths, areas, and volumes and explain the different powers of the scale factor. | Convert units before applying a scale, distinguish the direction of the ratio, and state the similarity assumption needed for area and volume comparisons. |
