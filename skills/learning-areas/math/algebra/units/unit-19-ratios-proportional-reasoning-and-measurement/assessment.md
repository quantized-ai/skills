# Private calibration: Unit 19: Ratios, proportional reasoning, and measurement

These original prompts and checked reasoning anchors calibrate mathematical accuracy. They are **not a fixed student quiz** and are not a complete assessment blueprint. They also serve as worked examples in the paired tutors, so exposure makes them unsuitable for independent reassessment. Use [fresh-question generation](question-generation.md) and the curriculum coverage in each tutor. Break composite prompts into manageable turns. Equivalent justified solutions are valid.

## Lesson 19.1

[Curriculum](lesson-1-ratios-and-unit-rates/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-1-ratios-and-unit-rates/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** A mix uses 3 cups concentrate for 5 cups water. Express concentrate-to-water and concentrate-to-total ratios. Compare 750 g for 6 credits with 1.2 kg for 9 credits.

**Checked reasoning:** Ratios are $3:5$ and $3:8$. Unit prices are 8 credits/kg and 7.5 credits/kg, so the second has lower unit price. A ratio must name the quantities in order.

**Coverage limit:** Vary units before comparing rates, scale recipes and use noninteger scale factors; require units and a nonzero denominator.

## Lesson 19.2

[Curriculum](lesson-2-proportions-and-direct-variation/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-2-proportions-and-direct-variation/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** A proportional service costs 18 credits for 3 hours. Find its rule and cost for 7 hours. Compare with $C=6h+4$.

**Checked reasoning:** Proportional rule $C=6h$ and seven-hour cost 42. Constant ratio $C/h=6$ for positive $h$ and passage through $(0,0)$ distinguish it from $6h+4$, which has a fixed fee and is not direct variation.

**Coverage limit:** Include missing values, tables, graphs, equations and nonproportional decoys; require equal nonzero ratios rather than merely equal differences.

## Lesson 19.3

[Curriculum](lesson-3-percent-relationships-and-change/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-3-percent-relationships-and-change/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** A 200-credit item rises 20% then falls 20%. Find the final price. Find one year of simple interest on 800 credits at 5%, and percent error for measured 21 against accepted 20.

**Checked reasoning:** $200(1.2)(0.8)=192$, a net 4% decrease. Simple interest $I=Prt=40$, total 840. Percent error is $|21-20|/20\times100\%=5\%$. Percent changes use their own current bases, and accepted value must be nonzero for this error definition.

**Coverage limit:** Include unknown part/whole/percent, reverse discounts, percentage-point comparisons, simple-interest time conversion and successive multipliers; keep this curriculum's simple interest separate from compounding.

## Lesson 19.4

[Curriculum](lesson-4-measurement-units-and-scale/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-4-measurement-units-and-scale/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Convert 72 km/h to m/s. A drawing at scale 1:50 has a 4 cm length; find the real length. If a solid is enlarged by factor 3, compare length, area and volume.

**Checked reasoning:** $72\cdot1000/3600=20$ m/s. Real length is 200 cm = 2 m. Length factor 3, area factor 9 and volume factor 27 for similar figures. Squared and cubed unit conversions require squared and cubed factors.

**Coverage limit:** Use multi-step conversions, derived units, affine temperature conversion when in scope, and length/area/volume scale distinctions; never apply a linear conversion factor directly to square units.
