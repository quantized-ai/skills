# Lesson 35.1: Geometric models and measurement

An explicit idealization links a physical object or spatial situation to calculable geometry.

## Learning objectives

- Select, represent, and use geometric models of physical situations.
- Evaluate model assumptions, measurement precision, and the reasonableness of predictions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define shapes, variables, dimensions, and units consistently and justify the model’s fit to the situation.
- Relate discrepancies to assumptions or measurements and state the usable limits of the result.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Idealization and representation** | Physical objects may be represented by polygons, circles, prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres, or combinations. A model specifies which geometric properties approximate the object, relevant dimensions, and neglected features. Drawings and coordinate diagrams organize these assumptions; scale converts represented lengths to physical lengths. | Construct geometric models of physical objects and use their measures and properties to determine contextual quantities. | Justify shape selection, label all necessary dimensions, connect diagram quantities to physical quantities, and avoid treating a convenient idealization as exact without evidence. |
| **Precision and model evaluation** | Measured lengths carry finite precision; areas and volumes depend on those inputs and can magnify relative changes. Exact geometric values and approximate measurements must be distinguished. Comparing predictions with known constraints or measurements can expose a poor shape assumption, an omitted feature, or inappropriate rounding; revised models must state the changed assumptions. | Evaluate geometric model predictions using units, measurement precision, reasonableness checks, and revisions of assumptions. | Retain useful intermediate precision, distinguish exact and approximate conclusions, identify a concrete source of discrepancy, and explain how a revision affects the result. |
