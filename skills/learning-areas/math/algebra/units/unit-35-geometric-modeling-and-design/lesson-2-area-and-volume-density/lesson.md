# Lesson 35.2: Area and volume density

Density converts geometric size into quantities under explicit distribution assumptions.

## Learning objectives

- Compute and interpret quantities and densities based on area and volume.
- Combine regions with differing densities and compare geometric models using compatible units.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the numerator quantity and denominator measure and state whether a uniform-density assumption is appropriate.
- Use total quantity divided by total area or volume for an overall density, rather than an unweighted average.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Density relationships** | Area density is quantity per unit area, and volume density is quantity per unit volume. For uniform density \(\rho\), quantity \(Q=\rho A\) or \(Q=\rho V\); conversely density is the quotient. Quantities may count objects or measure mass, energy, or another contextual amount. A unit conversion changes squared or cubed units by the corresponding power of the length conversion factor. | Use area and volume densities to determine and interpret quantities, densities, and required geometric sizes. | Track compound units, distinguish mass density from number density, justify any uniformity assumption, and convert area or volume units correctly. |
| **Composite density models** | If regions have different uniform densities, each region’s quantity equals its density times its own area or volume; total quantity is the sum. Overall average density is total quantity divided by total geometric measure. A single average may describe the total while hiding meaningful local variation. | Combine and compare density models for regions of different sizes and densities. | Weight each local density by the corresponding area or volume, distinguish average density from local density, and interpret the result within the chosen partition and assumptions. |
