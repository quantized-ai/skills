# Lesson 35.3: Geometric design constraints

Design models combine geometric restrictions with a quantity to improve or optimize.

## Learning objectives

- Translate design requirements into geometric equations, inequalities, and feasible dimensions.
- Compare or optimize feasible designs and explain the consequences of model choices.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify every physical constraint and the cost, capacity, area, or other objective being compared.
- Justify feasibility and the scope of any optimality claim and interpret the selected design in context.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Feasible geometric designs** | Design constraints can involve available length or area, capacity, clearance, angle bounds, aspect ratios, material thickness, or permitted dimensions. Geometric formulas and relations turn these conditions into equations and inequalities. Ratio-based grids and scale drawings connect layout dimensions; positivity and physical boundaries restrict the feasible set. | Formulate geometric design constraints and determine feasible dimensions and proportional layouts. | Translate each requirement without omitting fixed offsets or unit conversions, distinguish exact constraints from tolerances, and check the resulting design against all conditions. |
| **Design comparison and optimization** | An objective quantity such as material cost, exposed surface, storage volume, or travel length assigns a comparison value to feasible designs. Finite alternatives can be compared directly; a parameterized family can be analyzed using algebraic structure, graphs, or justified numerical methods. A numerical search provides evidence within its searched domain and resolution, not an automatic proof of a global optimum. | Compare and optimize geometric designs under stated constraints and justify a recommendation with model-based evidence. | Use the complete relevant cost or measure, include boundary candidates where admissible, distinguish a proved optimum from a best tested design, and explain how assumptions or changed constraints could alter the choice. |
