# Unit 55 fresh-question generation

Use with the [agent guide](agent-guide.md) and the selected curriculum/tutor pair. Every quiz and reassessment uses fresh verified tasks. Calibration prompts establish expected reasoning; they are not a bank to rotate through.

## Construction and verification

1. Select an exact curriculum concept and one missing assessment case. Fix difficulty by reasoning steps and representation demand, not just larger numbers.
2. Vary quantities, context, representation, direction of reasoning and boundary cases. Use available exposure history; without saved history promise variety within this session only. Do not claim literally infinite unique valid tasks within bounded parameters.
3. Supply all necessary data, domains, units, assumptions, tie/timing rules and tolerances. If uniqueness is not intended, ask for the solution set or justified alternatives.
4. Solve privately before sending. Check with a second method, original substitution, enumerated states or independent computation as appropriate. Prepare result, decisive reasoning, accepted equivalents, limitations and likely errors. Reject ambiguous or unverified drafts.
5. A figure must actually be supplied or clearly described with enough exact data; a computed table is not an inspected plot. Estimates need a stated error tolerance supported by the data or bracket.
6. Record generated features and support level. Use a different reasoning direction or representation for transfer; mere coefficient replacement can be useful routine practice but is insufficient by itself for transfer evidence.

## Unit validation

Work over the reals; identify a nonzero quadratic leading coefficient before using quadratic formulas. Preserve zero-capable factors, both square-root branches and all candidate checks. Keep exact radicals separate from graph estimates and reject contextual roots only for specified reasons.

## Concept task families

### 55.1: Quadratic equations and the zero-product property

Construct from known factors, include repeated/zero roots and nonzero right-side rearrangements; multiply back and substitute every distinct root.

Required coverage: Factoring, zero-product hypotheses, all distinct roots, multiplicity and original checks.

### 55.1: Square-root property

Use nonzero linear coefficient and positive, zero, negative isolated squares; preserve exact radicals and verify signs before extracting roots.

Required coverage: Both branches, zero/negative cases, real-domain qualification and principal-root distinction.

### 55.2: Monic square completion

Choose signed rational linear coefficients and all isolated-square signs; check expansion of the completed form.

Required coverage: Signed half coefficient, balanced equation, expression compensation and full real root classification.

### 55.2: Nonmonic square completion

Use nonzero positive/negative/rational leading coefficients, exact arithmetic and both normalization methods; expand to verify equivalence.

Required coverage: Nonmonic scaling, exact rationals, balanced compensation and original-solution checks.

### 55.3: Derivation and use of the quadratic formula

Include negative coefficients and a<0 in derivation checks, rational coefficients and D≥0; distinguish exact radicals from rounded checks.

Required coverage: Justified derivation, a≠0, signed substitution, complete denominator and exact real solutions.

### 55.3: Discriminant and solution classification

Generate rational-coefficient cases with square/nonsquare D and irrational-coefficient countercases; enforce a≠0.

Required coverage: Root count, multiplicity, rationality and coefficient assumptions.

### 55.4: Comparing exact and graphical solutions

Contrast factoring, isolated square, completion and formula cases; require explanation of method tradeoff after one method is understood and actual graph evidence where requested.

Required coverage: All listed methods, equivalent forms, graph estimates, hidden/repeated roots and justified choice.

### 55.4: Quadratic constraints in context

Generate consistent area/motion constraints with units and stated domains; include degeneracy and infeasible/no-solution contexts.

Required coverage: Formulation, actual degree, method, original checks and justified feasible interpretation.

## Independent verification recipe

**Construct:** Generate from chosen roots or completed-square forms, including repeated roots and no real roots; preserve a nonzero leading coefficient unless deliberately testing degree reduction. For contextual tasks state feasible dimensions/time/quantity constraints before solving.

**Check before release:** Expand independently, compute discriminant, and substitute every candidate into the original equation. Compare factoring, completion and formula where appropriate. Track divisions by variable expressions and any lost cases; apply contextual filtering only after obtaining the algebraic solution set. Use exact radicals before decimal rounding.

A second pass must target the likely failure above, not simply repeat the same arithmetic. Retain the checked result, decisive assumption and accepted equivalent responses before exposing the task.
