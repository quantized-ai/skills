# Lesson 55.1: Factoring and square-root solutions

The structure of a quadratic equation determines whether product or square-root reasoning gives an efficient exact solution.

## Learning objectives

- Solve real quadratic equations by factoring and verify all distinct solutions.
- Solve quadratic equations by isolating a squared expression and applying the square-root property over the real numbers.

## Proficiency criteria

- Put the equation in zero form before using the product property, preserve all factors, distinguish multiplicity from distinct roots, and substitute results into the original equation.
- Check the sign of the isolated square, include both branches when distinct, simplify exact values, and distinguish no real solutions from a claim about every number system.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quadratic equations and the zero-product property** | A real quadratic equation has form $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with $a\ne0$. If a product of real factors is zero, at least one factor is zero; dividing by a potentially zero factor can lose a solution. Repeated factors give repeated roots but one distinct solution value. | Solve real quadratic equations by factoring and verify all distinct solutions. | Put the equation in zero form before using the product property, preserve all factors, distinguish multiplicity from distinct roots, and substitute results into the original equation. |
| **Square-root property** | For real $m\ne0$, $(mx+n)^2=k$ has branches $mx+n=\pm\sqrt k$ when $k>0$, one branch when $k=0$, and no real solution when $k<0$. The principal radical denotes one nonnegative value, not both equation solutions. | Solve quadratic equations by isolating a squared expression and applying the square-root property over the real numbers. | Check the sign of the isolated square, include both branches when distinct, simplify exact values, and distinguish no real solutions from a claim about every number system. |
