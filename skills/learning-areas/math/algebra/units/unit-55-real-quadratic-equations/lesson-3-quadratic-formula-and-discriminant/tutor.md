## Teaching boundaries

Solve over the reals; complex-root calculation, calculus and higher-degree methods are not required. If the leading coefficient vanishes, solve the actual lower degree.

## Lesson reasoning activity

**Purpose:** make the student test a consequential claim before accepting a procedure. Use after its relevant concept has been introduced; it is learning/practice evidence, not an unexposed assessment.

**Prompt:** A learner substitutes a=2,b=1,c=−4 into the formula but divides only the radical by 4. Repair grouping.

**Agent-only reasoning:** The entire numerator−1±√33 is divided by 4, giving(−1±√33)/4. Check both values in 2x²+x−4.

Ask for the first unjustified step, invite a corrected explanation, and retain correct components of the original response. Then use a different representation or new case for independent evidence.

## Derivation and use of the quadratic formula

Curriculum reference: **Derivation and use of the quadratic formula** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** In ax²+bx+c=0 with a=−2, what is √(a²)?

**Agent-only key:** 2=|a|, not−2. This sign issue matters when deriving the formula.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** Derive the quadratic formula for ax²+bx+c=0 (a≠0), then solve 2x²+x-4=0.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** Divide by a and complete: (x+b/(2a))²=(b²-4ac)/(4a²). For nonnegative discriminant the set is x=(-b±√(b²-4ac))/(2a); the ± set absorbs the sign of a when taking √(a²)=|a|. Here D=33 and roots (-1±√33)/4.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Derive from division by a and square completion, showing every denominator and why a≠0 is needed.
2. When taking the square root of a², use absolute value and explain how the±solution set permits the familiar denominator 2a.
3. Substitute grouped signed coefficients, then distinguish exact radical output from a decimal check.

### Practice progression

Derive the formula with symbols; apply it to positive/negative leading coefficients and negative b; then compare equivalent exact forms and verify both roots, including D=0 and no-real-root D<0 cases.

**Construction and verification controls:** Include negative coefficients and a<0 in derivation checks, rational coefficients and D≥0; distinguish exact radicals from rounded checks.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** Keep the denominator on the entire numerator and remember √(a²)=|a|.

If only−b is divided by 2a, rewrite the entire numerator in parentheses. If −b is misread when b<0, substitute the signed value before simplifying.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

- Justify division by the leading coefficient, substitute signed coefficients with grouping.
- Apply the denominator to the entire numerator.
- Distinguish exact radicals from rounded approximations.

**Required case selection:** Justified derivation, a≠0, signed substitution, complete denominator and exact real solutions.

## Discriminant and solution classification

Curriculum reference: **Discriminant and solution classification** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** A rational-coefficient quadratic has D=5. Does positive discriminant mean rational roots?

**Agent-only key:** No; it has two distinct real irrational roots because√5 is irrational.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** Classify x²-2x+1=0, x²-2=0 and x²+2=0 by real root count and rationality.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** Discriminants are 0,8,-8. First has one distinct rational repeated root 1; second has two irrational real roots ±√2; third has no real roots. Positive D alone does not imply rational roots. The perfect-rational-square criterion requires rational coefficients.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Separate three questions: real-root count, multiplicity and rationality.
2. Use discriminant sign for the first two, then apply the square-of-a-rational test only with rational coefficients.
3. Supply an irrational-coefficient case to show why that hypothesis cannot be dropped.

### Practice progression

Classify positive square, positive nonsquare, zero and negative D; then build a quadratic from chosen rational roots; finally critique a claim applying the rational-coefficient rule to arbitrary real coefficients without checking the assumption.

**Construction and verification controls:** Generate rational-coefficient cases with square/nonsquare D and irrational-coefficient countercases; enforce a≠0.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** Separate the sign of D from whether D has a rational square root.

If “two roots” counts a repeated value twice, ask for the distinct set. If D>0 is called sufficient for rationality, compare D=4 with D=5 and name the difference.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

### Assessment case checklist

Generate fresh tasks that collectively establish each of these curriculum obligations:

- Separate distinct root count, multiplicity, and rationality.
- Do not infer rationality solely from the sign of the discriminant or apply rational-coefficient conclusions to arbitrary real coefficients.

**Required case selection:** Root count, multiplicity, rationality and coefficient assumptions.
