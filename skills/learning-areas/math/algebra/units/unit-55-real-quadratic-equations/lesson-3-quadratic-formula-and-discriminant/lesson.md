# Lesson 55.3: Quadratic formula and discriminant

Square completion yields a general formula whose discriminant determines the number and type of real roots.

## Learning objectives

- Derive the quadratic formula by completing the square and apply it to obtain exact real solutions.
- Classify real quadratic roots using the discriminant and justified coefficient assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Justify division by the leading coefficient, substitute signed coefficients with grouping, apply the denominator to the entire numerator, and distinguish exact radicals from rounded approximations.
- Separate distinct root count, multiplicity, and rationality; do not infer rationality solely from the sign of the discriminant or apply rational-coefficient conclusions to arbitrary real coefficients.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Derivation and use of the quadratic formula** | Completing the square for $ax^2+bx+c=0$, $a\ne0$, yields $x=(-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac})/(2a)$. Over the reals the formula is evaluated only for nonnegative discriminant; a negative discriminant gives no real root. | Derive the quadratic formula by completing the square and apply it to obtain exact real solutions. | Justify division by the leading coefficient, substitute signed coefficients with grouping, apply the denominator to the entire numerator, and distinguish exact radicals from rounded approximations. |
| **Discriminant and solution classification** | For real coefficients, $D=b^2-4ac$ gives two distinct real roots when positive, a repeated real root when zero, and no real roots when negative. For rational coefficients and $D\ge0$, roots are rational exactly when $D$ is a square of a rational number. | Classify real quadratic roots using the discriminant and justified coefficient assumptions. | Separate distinct root count, multiplicity, and rationality; do not infer rationality solely from the sign of the discriminant or apply rational-coefficient conclusions to arbitrary real coefficients. |
