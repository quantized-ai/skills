# Lesson 39.3: Matrix algebra, zero, and identity

Matrix operations share some real-number algebra properties while differing in commutativity and cancellation. Zero and identity matrices depend on dimensions.

## Learning objectives

- Verify associative and distributive matrix laws and demonstrate failure of commutativity.
- Use zero and identity matrices with compatible dimensions and identify invalid cancellation.

## Proficiency criteria

- Keep factor order and dimensions intact when rewriting products.
- Distinguish a zero matrix from a scalar zero and use a correctly sized identity.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Matrix algebra laws** | Matrix multiplication is associative and distributes over addition whenever all products and sums have compatible dimensions. Even square matrices need not satisfy $AB=BA$. Reordering factors is therefore unjustified without additional information; multiplying both sides of an equation by a matrix must specify the side. | Apply and verify associative and distributive laws for compatible matrices and demonstrate that square-matrix multiplication need not commute. | Give a valid noncommuting pair, preserve factor order in expansions and regroupings, and verify all intermediate dimensions. |
| **Zero and identity matrices** | The zero matrix $0_{m\times n}$ has every entry zero and is the additive identity for that size. The identity matrix $I_n$ has ones on the main diagonal and zeros elsewhere; for $A$ of size $m\times n$, $I_mA=A=AI_n$. A zero matrix product need not force a zero factor, and cancellation requires an appropriate inverse. | Use dimension-appropriate zero and identity matrices and distinguish valid matrix identities from invalid zero-product or cancellation inferences. | Select the identity size separately on each side, compute zero products correctly, and support any claim about cancellation or nonzero factors with a valid argument or counterexample. |
