# Lesson 39.1: Matrices as data representations

Matrix entries have meaning through row labels, column labels, and units; a useful representation must preserve those meanings under manipulation.

## Learning objectives

- Organize data and relationships in matrices, using technology for larger collections, and interpret their entries and dimensions.
- Scale, combine, and compare aligned data matrices in context.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define what each row, column, and entry represents and preserve compatible labels and units, including in technology-managed arrays.
- Use entrywise operations only when the modeled categories correspond.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Matrix dimensions and labeled entries** | A real $m\times n$ matrix is a rectangular array with $m$ rows and $n$ columns; $a_{ij}$ denotes row $i$, column $j$. Matrices can represent measured data, payoffs by paired choices, or network relationships. Row and column meanings determine what each entry and operation represents; rearranging categories requires corresponding rearrangement of entries. Technology can store and manipulate larger arrays efficiently while preserving the row and column labels and units. | Construct and interpret matrices for data, payoffs, or network relationships with explicit dimensions, labels, and units, using technology for larger collections. | Identify entries by row and column, distinguish row count from column count, and explain the quantitative or relational meaning of the matrix. Organize larger collections with technology and confirm that entry meanings and category order remain correct. |
| **Data manipulation by matrices** | Correspondingly labeled matrices of equal dimensions can be added or subtracted entrywise. Scalar multiplication $cA$ multiplies every entry by $c$ and can express a unit conversion, common percentage factor, or scaled payoff. An operation is meaningful only when its row-column correspondence and units match the situation. | Manipulate data matrices by entrywise combination and scalar multiplication and interpret the resulting entries. | Check matching dimensions and categories, apply the scalar to every entry, and explain any changed units or contextual meaning. |
