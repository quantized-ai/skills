# Lesson 20.4: Like terms and equivalent linear expressions

Combining like terms exposes linear structure while the definition of equivalence distinguishes a justified identity from agreement at selected inputs.

## Learning objectives

- Simplify linear expressions with rational coefficients and interpret useful equivalent forms.
- Justify expression equivalence and use counterexamples to disprove invalid identities.

## Proficiency criteria

- Combine only identical variable parts and maintain signs across grouped sums and differences.
- Use operation properties for a general justification and distinguish that justification from numerical checks.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Collecting like terms** | Like terms have exactly the same variable factors and exponents. Their coefficients add through distributivity; constants form their own like-term group. Linear expressions may include multiple variables to the first power, without products between variables. Zero coefficients remove a term without restricting the domain. | Simplify linear expressions with rational coefficients by distribution, regrouping, and collection of like terms. | Retain unlike terms separately, include subtraction in signed coefficients, and account for zero coefficients without treating absent terms as excluded inputs. |
| **Equivalence and contextual form** | Equivalent expressions have equal values for every input in their common stated domain. Operation properties establish such equivalence generally; equality at a few inputs does not. One permitted input with unequal values disproves equivalence. A useful form can expose fixed charges, repeated quantities, totals, or percent multipliers. | Justify equivalence of linear expressions, disprove invalid identities with a permitted counterexample, and interpret what a chosen form reveals. | Explain the general property-based rewrite, retain domain conditions, and connect coefficients or factors to the represented quantities. |
