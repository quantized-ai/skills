# Lesson 20.1: Variables and expression language

Algebraic notation records operations on quantities and makes the role of each component explicit.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret variables and expression structure.
- Translate verbal and quantitative relationships into unambiguous expressions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish expressions from equations and variables from labels for units.
- Preserve the order and scope of operations in translations.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Variables, constants, and structural parts** | A variable represents a number from a specified set; it may be an unknown, a varying input, or a parameter. Constants remain fixed in the relationship under discussion. Expressions are built from operations without asserting equality. Terms are additive components; factors are multiplicative components; coefficients multiply variable parts. A grouped subexpression can be treated as one entity. | Identify variables, constants, terms, factors, coefficients, and grouped subexpressions and interpret their roles in a quantity. | Use the outermost operation to distinguish terms from factors, retain signed coefficients, and distinguish an expression from an equation or inequality. |
| **Translating quantitative language** | Symbolic expressions represent ordered operations and relationships among quantities with specified units. Grouping distinguishes a multiple of a sum from a sum with one multiplied term; subtraction and division are not interchangeable under reversal. Repeated or composite quantities can be represented by one subexpression. | Write and interpret expressions for verbal and contextual relationships, including grouped sums, ordered differences, quotients, and powers. | Define the quantities and units, preserve operation order and grouping, and explain the contextual meaning of the parts of the expression. |
