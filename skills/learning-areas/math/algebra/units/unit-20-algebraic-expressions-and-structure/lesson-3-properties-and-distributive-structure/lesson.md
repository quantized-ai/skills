# Lesson 20.3: Properties and distributive structure

Real-number properties justify rearranging and regrouping expressions without changing their values.

## Learning objectives

- Apply identity, inverse, commutative, and associative properties under valid conditions.
- Use and justify the distributive property in expansion and reverse grouping.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish operations that permit reordering or regrouping from those that do not.
- Distribute a signed factor to every term and verify the reverse rewrite.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Operation properties and exceptions** | Addition and multiplication of real numbers are commutative and associative. Their identity elements are zero and one; additive inverses exist for all real numbers and multiplicative inverses only for nonzero numbers. Subtraction and division do not have general commutative or associative laws. A rewrite involving reciprocals must retain nonzero conditions. | Apply and justify real-number operation properties and identify invalid rearrangements or inverse operations. | Name the property supporting a rewrite, preserve the grouped operation, and identify why a proposed subtraction, division, or zero-reciprocal step fails. |
| **Distribution and common linear structure** | \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) and \(a(b-c)=ab-ac\) for real quantities. The same identity operates in reverse when a common numerical factor is extracted from linear terms. A leading minus sign distributes multiplication by \(-1\). Distribution preserves the entire signed coefficient of each term. | Expand grouped linear expressions and extract common numerical factors while explaining the distributive relationship. | Multiply every term by the outside factor, retain negative and fractional coefficients, and verify reverse distribution by expansion. |
