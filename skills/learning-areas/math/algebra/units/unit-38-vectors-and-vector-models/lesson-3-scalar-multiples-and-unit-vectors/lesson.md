# Lesson 38.3: Scalar multiples and unit vectors

Scalar multiplication changes vector magnitude and possibly direction; normalization separates a nonzero vector’s direction from its length.

## Learning objectives

- Compute and represent scalar multiples and justify their magnitude and direction.
- Construct unit vectors and resolve a vector into coordinate-direction components.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the absolute value of the scalar for magnitude and its sign for direction.
- Exclude normalization of the zero vector and verify reconstructed vectors.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scalar multiplication** | $c\langle v_x,v_y\rangle=\langle cv_x,cv_y\rangle$ for real $c$, and $\lVert c\mathbf v\rVert=\lvert c\rvert\lVert\mathbf v\rVert$. A nonzero product points along $\mathbf v$ when $c>0$ and opposite it when $c<0$; if $c=0$ or $\mathbf v=\mathbf0$, the result is the zero vector with no unique direction. | Perform scalar multiplication component-wise and graphically and compute its magnitude and direction, including zero cases. | Scale both components, use an absolute value for magnitude, reverse direction precisely when a negative scalar acts on a nonzero vector, and handle zero products explicitly. |
| **Unit vectors and resolution** | For $\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0$, $\widehat{\mathbf v}=\mathbf v/\lVert\mathbf v\rVert$ has magnitude one and the same direction. Coordinate unit vectors $\mathbf i=\langle1,0\rangle$ and $\mathbf j=\langle0,1\rangle$ give $\mathbf v=v_x\mathbf i+v_y\mathbf j$. A specified magnitude $m\ge0$ in a chosen unit direction is $m\widehat{\mathbf v}$. | Construct unit vectors, express vectors as coordinate-direction sums, and construct vectors with a specified magnitude and direction. | Normalize only nonzero vectors, verify unit length and orientation, and recover both original components from the coordinate-direction sum. |
