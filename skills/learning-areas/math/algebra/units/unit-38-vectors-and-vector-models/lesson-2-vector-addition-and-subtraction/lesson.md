# Lesson 38.2: Vector addition and subtraction

Vector sums and differences describe combined and relative displacements. Geometric constructions and component calculations must agree, including cancellation and direction-sensitive magnitudes.

## Learning objectives

- Add vectors tip-to-tail, by the parallelogram rule, and component-wise.
- Determine a resultant from magnitude-direction data and perform subtraction by additive inverses, tip-to-tip geometry, and components.

## Proficiency criteria

- Keep arrows and subtraction order correct in each representation.
- Distinguish the magnitude of a resultant from the sum of individual magnitudes.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Geometric and component addition** | $\langle u_x,u_y\rangle+\langle v_x,v_y\rangle=\langle u_x+v_x,u_y+v_y\rangle$. Tip-to-tail addition joins the initial point of the first arrow to the final point of the second; the parallelogram diagonal gives the same sum for vectors placed at a common origin. The magnitude of the sum usually differs from the sum of magnitudes and can be zero. | Add vectors by tip-to-tail construction, the parallelogram rule, and components, explaining why magnitudes generally do not add. | Obtain the same directed resultant with all three methods, distinguish reinforcing and cancelling directions, and identify complete cancellation. |
| **Resultants from angular descriptions** | Vectors supplied by magnitudes and directions can be converted to a common component system, added component-wise, and converted back. Direction angles must use a common reference and unit. A zero resultant has no unique direction, even when both input vectors are nonzero. | Determine the magnitude and direction of a vector sum from magnitude-direction data. | Convert each angle to the shared convention, compute both resultant components, use the correct angle quadrant, and report no unique direction for a zero sum. |
| **Subtraction and relative vectors** | $\mathbf u-\mathbf v=\mathbf u+(-\mathbf v)$ with $-\mathbf v$ having the same magnitude and opposite direction. For arrows drawn from a common origin, $\mathbf u-\mathbf v$ points from the tip of $\mathbf v$ to the tip of $\mathbf u$. Component subtraction uses the same order. | Represent and compute vector subtraction using additive inverses, tip-to-tip geometry, and components. | Reverse the subtracted vector or connect the tips in the correct order, match the component result to the geometric arrow, and distinguish subtraction from subtracting magnitudes. |
