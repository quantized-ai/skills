# Unit 38: Vectors and vector models

Vectors encode magnitude and direction through arrows, components, and angular descriptions. Their arithmetic supports displacement, velocity, and force models, while the dot product adds an angle and projection connection.

## Main learning objectives

- Represent planar vectors by directed segments, components, and magnitude-direction pairs.
- Perform and justify vector addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication geometrically and algebraically.
- Build vector models and use dot products and projections to interpret directions, angles, and work.

## Concepts and topics

Vector notation; components; norm and direction; zero vectors; tip-to-tail and parallelogram addition; subtraction; scalar multiples and unit vectors; velocity and force models; bearings; dot products; projections; work.

## Lessons

- [38.1: Vector representations](lesson-1-vector-representations/lesson.md) — Vectors represent a directed change independently of a particular starting point. Components and magnitude-direction form describe the same vector when coordinate conventions agree.
- [38.2: Vector addition and subtraction](lesson-2-vector-addition-and-subtraction/lesson.md) — Vector sums and differences describe combined and relative displacements. Geometric constructions and component calculations must agree, including cancellation and direction-sensitive magnitudes.
- [38.3: Scalar multiples and unit vectors](lesson-3-scalar-multiples-and-unit-vectors/lesson.md) — Scalar multiplication changes vector magnitude and possibly direction; normalization separates a nonzero vector’s direction from its length.
- [38.4: Vector models, dot products, and projections](lesson-4-vector-models-dot-products-and-projections/lesson.md) — Vector models coordinate physical directions and units. Dot products extend component arithmetic to angles, perpendicularity, projection, and work.
