# Lesson 38.1: Vector representations

Vectors represent a directed change independently of a particular starting point. Components and magnitude-direction form describe the same vector when coordinate conventions agree.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish vectors from scalars and represent vectors geometrically and in components.
- Convert between component and magnitude-direction forms, treating the zero vector separately.

## Proficiency criteria

- Subtract terminal minus initial coordinates and keep direction conventions explicit.
- Use a quadrant-correct angle and distinguish zero magnitude from an assigned direction.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vectors, displacements, and components** | A planar vector $\mathbf v=\langle v_x,v_y\rangle$ has magnitude and direction and may be represented by any directed segment with those components. A segment from $P=(p_x,p_y)$ to $Q=(q_x,q_y)$ represents $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\langle q_x-p_x,q_y-p_y\rangle$. Equal vectors need not have the same initial point; vector position and scalar magnitude are different objects. | Identify vector quantities, represent them by directed segments and symbols, and determine components from initial and terminal points. | Use terminal-minus-initial subtraction, preserve arrow orientation under translation, and distinguish a vector from its magnitude or the coordinates of one endpoint. |
| **Magnitude and direction conversion** | $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert=\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}$. For magnitude $m>0$ and direction $\theta$ measured counterclockwise from the positive horizontal axis, $\mathbf v=\langle m\cos\theta,m\sin\theta\rangle$. Conversely, both component signs determine the angle quadrant. The zero vector has magnitude zero and no unique direction. | Convert nonzero vectors between component and magnitude-direction forms and identify the exceptional zero-vector case. | Use both components to determine the angle, reconstruct the components from magnitude and direction, and avoid treating the zero vector as having a specified direction. |
