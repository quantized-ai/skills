# Lesson 38.4: Vector models, dot products, and projections

Vector models coordinate physical directions and units. Dot products extend component arithmetic to angles, perpendicularity, projection, and work.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate and solve planar motion, force, and directional-bearing problems using vector sums, differences, and scalar multiples.
- Compute dot products and use them to determine angles, perpendicularity, vector and scalar projections, and work.

## Proficiency criteria

- State the coordinate, time, and bearing conventions and distinguish speed from velocity.
- Respect nonzero-vector conditions in angle and projection formulas and interpret signed results.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Velocity, force, and bearing models** | Displacement and velocity are vectors; speed is the magnitude of velocity. A constant velocity over time $t$ produces displacement $t\mathbf v$. Relative or combined velocities use differences or sums in a common reference frame; force resultants use vector sums. Bearings are measured clockwise from north or specified by a quadrant convention and must be converted to the chosen coordinate axes. | Use vector addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication to model and solve displacement, velocity, force, and directional-bearing problems. | Specify axes, units, reference frames, and bearing conventions; compute the required resultant or relative vector; and interpret its magnitude and direction in the physical setting. |
| **Dot product and angles** | $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=u_xv_x+u_yv_y=\lVert\mathbf u\rVert\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\cos\theta$ for nonzero vectors and their included angle $0\le\theta\le\pi$. Nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when the dot product is zero. A zero vector has dot product zero with every vector but does not define an angle. | Compute dot products and use them to determine included angles and perpendicularity while distinguishing the zero-vector case. | Calculate the scalar product correctly, divide by nonzero magnitudes to find an angle, and justify perpendicularity only with appropriate nonzero-vector conditions. |
| **Projection and work** | For $\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0$, $\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf u=((\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)/(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v))\mathbf v$ and signed scalar component is $(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)/\lVert\mathbf v\rVert$. The residual is perpendicular to $\mathbf v$. Work by a constant force through displacement $\mathbf d$ is $W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d$; its sign reflects the force component along or against displacement. | Compute vector and scalar projections and use dot products to interpret work by a constant force. | Exclude projection onto the zero vector, distinguish a scalar component from a vector projection, check perpendicular residuals, and report work with appropriate units and sign. |
