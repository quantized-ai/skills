# Lesson 59.5: Contextual input estimates and equation solutions

Questions about an output become equations for admissible inputs, with symbolic, tabular, and graphical methods providing different kinds of evidence and precision.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate contextual equations for target outputs and estimate or determine their valid input solutions.
- Use tables, graphs, and symbolic methods for linear and quadratic models and numerical representations for additional function families.

## Proficiency criteria

- Specify the model, target, domain, and units before solving.
- Check all candidate inputs, solution counts, and approximation precision in the original relationship.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Input estimation from outputs** | A target output \(y_0\) gives \(f(x)=y_0\); on a graph, solutions are intersections with a horizontal line. Tables bracket or suggest inputs but can miss solutions between listed rows. Increasing or decreasing behavior, asymptotes, extrema, and domain restrictions guide reasonable estimates for quadratic, rational, and exponential models; some targets have no admissible input or several. | Estimate reasonable inputs for specified outputs of quadratic, rational, and exponential models using tables, graphs, and function attributes. | Use the original domain and units, justify the estimate with nearby values or graph features, and identify nonexistent or multiple input possibilities rather than assume one solution. |
| **Linear and quadratic contextual solutions** | Questions about a model can produce target equations, equal-output comparisons, or fixed constraints. Linear equations use equivalent operations; quadratic equations permit factoring, square roots, completing the square, or the quadratic formula under their conditions. Tables and graph intersections represent the same solutions but may be approximate. Context can exclude algebraically valid negative or noninteger inputs. | Formulate and solve contextual linear and quadratic equations tabularly, graphically, and symbolically and reconcile their solutions. | Define the equation from the quantitative question, choose a justified symbolic method, compare approximate evidence with exact results, and check every candidate against the original model and context. |
| **Numerical solutions for additional families** | Exponential, logarithmic, square-root, and cubic models lead to equations whose contextual solutions can be approximated with tables and graphs. Refining an input interval narrows a solution only when the function is continuous there and the bracket or crossing evidence is valid; a rational asymptote is not a root. Graph windows, calculator precision, repeated roots, and multiple branches can hide solutions. A stated tolerance governs when an approximation is adequate. | Construct contextual target equations for exponential, logarithmic, square-root, and cubic functions and approximate and verify their solutions using tables and graphs. | Apply logarithm and root domain restrictions, choose and refine a suitable window or table interval, check for multiple or tangential solutions, and report a justified tolerance and contextual units. |
