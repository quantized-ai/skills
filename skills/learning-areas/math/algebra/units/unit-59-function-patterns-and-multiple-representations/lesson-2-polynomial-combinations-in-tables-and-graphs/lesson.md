# Lesson 59.2: Polynomial combinations in tables and graphs

Pointwise operations connect numerical tables and symbolic polynomial expressions, and their graphs reveal how component relationships combine.

## Learning objectives

- Construct polynomial sums, differences, and products in tabular, graphical, and symbolic forms.
- Compare the behavior and degrees of sums and products of linear functions and interpret contextual combinations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use a common input and compatible domains for every operation.
- Account for cancellation, zero factors, units, and the limits of finite tabular or graphical evidence.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common-input polynomial operations** | Pointwise sum, difference, and product values combine \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) at the same permitted input. A common-input table can be calculated from formulas or corresponding entries; no missing value may be invented from a finite table without an additional rule. Expanding and collecting terms yields a symbolic polynomial, and plotting paired input/output values describes its graph. | Add, subtract, and multiply polynomial functions in tables and symbolic form and represent the resulting functions graphically. | Align identical input values, preserve subtraction order, use all pairwise products in symbolic multiplication, and verify agreement across the representations on their shared domain. |
| **Sums and products of linear functions** | For \(f(x)=ax+b\) and \(g(x)=cx+d\), \(f+g=(a+c)x+(b+d)\) and \(fg=acx^2+(ad+bc)x+bd\). A sum may be linear, constant, or zero; a product is genuinely quadratic only when \(ac\ne0\), and zero factors can collapse it. Tables show constant first differences for a nonconstant linear sum and constant second differences for a genuine quadratic product at equal input steps. | Compare sums and products of two linear functions tabularly, graphically, and symbolically and identify the functions from which a resulting polynomial can be built. | Identify degree changes caused by cancellation or zero coefficients, connect differences and graph shapes to the symbolic result, and verify proposed components by recombination. |
| **Contextual polynomial combinations** | Combining functions models quantities when the operation matches their meanings: additive totals require compatible units, while multiplication can produce area, volume, weighted cost, or other product units. Decomposing a polynomial model into components can expose contribution or dimension relationships. Restrictions from physical lengths or counts remain after symbolic simplification. | Model contextual polynomial combinations and decompositions using formulas, tables, and graphs with justified operations, units, and domains. | Explain the meaning of each component and the combined quantity, represent all three forms consistently, and retain nonnegativity, count, or measurement restrictions. |
