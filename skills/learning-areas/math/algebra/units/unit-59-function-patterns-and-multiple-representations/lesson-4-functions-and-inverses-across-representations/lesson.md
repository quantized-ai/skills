# Lesson 59.4: Functions and inverses across representations

An inverse reverses a function’s input–output relationship, so comparisons of tables, graphs, and formulas must preserve complete domains and ranges.

## Learning objectives

- Compare the attributes of functions and inverses using tables, graphs, and formulas.
- Verify inverse claims graphically and tabularly and interpret their contextual meanings.

## Proficiency criteria

- Exchange complete domain and range sets and justify one-to-one behavior.
- Distinguish evidence on displayed samples from a verified claim on the whole specified domain.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Comparing inverse attributes** | For a one-to-one function \(f:D\to R\), \(f^{-1}:R\to D\) reverses every pair and reflects the graph across \(y=x\). Domain and range exchange, and an intercept pair reverses when both coordinates are admitted. Extrema require renewed analysis of allowed inputs and outputs; a turning-point extremum often signals failure of one-to-one behavior on an unrestricted domain. | Compare a function and its inverse in tables, graphs, and formulas using domains, ranges, intercepts, and any attained maximum or minimum values. | Establish the domain restriction supporting an inverse, exchange complete sets including endpoints and exclusions, and analyze each claimed extremum rather than transfer its name automatically. |
| **Tabular and graphical inverse verification** | Two finite tables represent inverse functions exactly when each defines a one-to-one mapping and every allowed input–output pair appears reversed in the other. For complete graphs, reflection across \(y=x\) with exchanged domains verifies the inverse relation. Sampled or screen-displayed graphs and tables can disprove a claim but cannot by themselves prove agreement at every unshown real input. | Verify or refute inverse-function claims using reversed table pairs and reflected graphs, with explicit domain and evidence limitations. | Check both directions and all listed pairs for finite functions, compare full specified graph domains, and distinguish exact complete evidence from a finite sample of an underlying continuous function. |
| **Contextual reversals and compositions** | An inverse model changes which quantity is input, so its input units are the original output units. A contextual forward model must be one-to-one on its reasonable domain to recover an original input uniquely. Composition represents a different construction: the output of one process becomes the input of another, requiring compatible units and intermediate values in the second domain. | Represent contextual inverse and composite relationships in function notation, tables, and graphs and verify their meanings and admissible inputs. | Distinguish reversing one model from composing two processes, define the quantities and units at each stage, and verify the relevant pair reversal or ordered composition. |
