# Unit 59: Function patterns and multiple representations

This unit connects function patterns, exact and approximate tables, symbolic operations, graphs, inverses, and contextual input questions. It makes the representational reasoning of algebraic modeling explicit across function families.

## Main learning objectives

- Use finite differences and ratios to classify, reconstruct, and evaluate linear, quadratic, cubic, and exponential models with stated domains.
- Represent polynomial combinations, quotients, and factors consistently in tables, formulas, and graphs.
- Compare functions with their inverses and verify inverse relationships tabularly and graphically on their stated sets.
- Estimate and determine contextual input values using tables, graphs, and appropriate symbolic methods, with justified restrictions and precision.

## Concepts and topics

Equal-spacing requirements; first, second, and third differences; exponential ratios; finite-difference reconstruction; rate-based model checks; tabular polynomial arithmetic; combinations of linear functions; polynomial quotient and factor evidence; inverse representations; contextual equations; numerical input estimation.

## Lessons

- [59.1: Finite differences and model reconstruction](lesson-1-finite-differences-and-model-reconstruction/lesson.md) — Equally spaced function values reveal difference or ratio patterns that support candidate models, their reconstruction, and checks against observed change.
- [59.2: Polynomial combinations in tables and graphs](lesson-2-polynomial-combinations-in-tables-and-graphs/lesson.md) — Pointwise operations connect numerical tables and symbolic polynomial expressions, and their graphs reveal how component relationships combine.
- [59.3: Polynomial quotients and factors across representations](lesson-3-polynomial-quotients-and-factors-across-representations/lesson.md) — Division identities and zero-factor relationships connect polynomial arithmetic with tables and graphs while preserving denominator exclusions and limits of evidence.
- [59.4: Functions and inverses across representations](lesson-4-functions-and-inverses-across-representations/lesson.md) — An inverse reverses a function’s input–output relationship, so comparisons of tables, graphs, and formulas must preserve complete domains and ranges.
- [59.5: Contextual input estimates and equation solutions](lesson-5-contextual-input-estimates-and-equation-solutions/lesson.md) — Questions about an output become equations for admissible inputs, with symbolic, tabular, and graphical methods providing different kinds of evidence and precision.
