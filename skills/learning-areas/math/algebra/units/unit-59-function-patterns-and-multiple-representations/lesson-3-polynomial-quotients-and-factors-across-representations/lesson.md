# Lesson 59.3: Polynomial quotients and factors across representations

Division identities and zero-factor relationships connect polynomial arithmetic with tables and graphs while preserving denominator exclusions and limits of evidence.

## Learning objectives

- Determine polynomial quotients and remainders symbolically and connect them to tabular quotients.
- Find and verify linear factors of quadratic and cubic functions using symbolic, tabular, and graphical information.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish a polynomial quotient from pointwise division when a remainder is present.
- Treat graph or table zeros as candidates unless exact information or algebra verifies them.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Division identities and tabular quotients** | For degree-three or degree-four \(p\) and degree-one or degree-two nonzero divisor \(d\), division gives \(p=dq+r\), where \(r=0\) or \(\deg r<\deg d\). A table of \(p(x)/d(x)\) agrees with \(q(x)+r(x)/d(x)\) only where \(d(x)\ne0\). Pointwise quotients equal the polynomial quotient alone when \(r=0\); a canceled zero remains excluded from the original ratio. | Determine quotients and remainders for cubic or quartic polynomial functions divided by linear or quadratic functions and connect the symbolic division to tabular values. | Verify the division identity and remainder degree, calculate only defined table entries, and distinguish quotient-plus-remainder values from the polynomial quotient itself. |
| **Linear factors from zeros and structure** | For a polynomial, \(p(r)=0\) exactly when \(x-r\) is a factor. Exact tabular zeros and graph intercepts can suggest factors of quadratic or cubic functions; approximate zeros require stated precision. Division or symbolic factorization verifies the remaining factors and multiplicity. Missing intercepts in a graph window do not establish the absence of further real zeros. | Determine and verify linear factors of quadratic and cubic polynomial functions from symbolic rules, tables, and graphs where the available information permits. | Translate a zero into a factor with the correct sign, distinguish exact from approximate input values, verify the full product including its scale, and identify unresolved factors or insufficient data. |
| **Cross-representation verification and evidence** | An exact symbolic identity holds for every input; a finite table checks only listed values unless a fixed degree bound and enough exact conditions establish uniqueness. Graphs can conceal repeated roots, closely spaced intercepts, or excluded inputs. A verified factorization or division identity supplies the general relationship underlying the visual or numerical evidence. | Compare tabular and graphical evidence with symbolic verification when evaluating proposed polynomial factors or quotients. | Identify which claims are exact, approximate, or underdetermined, preserve original exclusions, and explain how reconstruction of the dividend or product verifies the general claim. |
