# Lesson 31.2: Solving right triangles

Known triangle measurements determine missing lengths and acute angles under explicit geometric and numerical conditions.

## Learning objectives

- Solve right triangles using trigonometric ratios, inverse trigonometric ratios, and the Pythagorean theorem.
- Model indirect measurements using right triangles and interpret angle and precision conventions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Check known lengths and ratio domains, recover all unknown measurements, and confirm angle sums and side relationships.
- Define the geometric diagram and measurement assumptions, use correct angle units, and report appropriately rounded quantities.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Right-triangle solutions and inverse ratios** | Two independent measurements besides the right angle, including at least one side, determine a right triangle. Missing sides use ratios and \(a^2+b^2=c^2\); acute angles use inverse sine, cosine, or tangent. Positive sine or cosine ratios must lie strictly between zero and one for nondegenerate acute angles; the inverse tangent of a positive ratio is acute. Inverse notation denotes recovering an angle, not a reciprocal. | Determine all missing sides and acute angles in right triangles using ratios, inverse ratios, and the Pythagorean theorem. | Match each operation to the known data, reject impossible or degenerate data, distinguish inverse from reciprocal notation, and verify the solved triangle. |
| **Indirect measurement models** | Heights, horizontal distances, slopes, and line-of-sight distances can be modeled by right triangles. Elevation and depression angles are measured from a horizontal line; parallel horizontals justify equal relevant angles. Models may require observer height, inaccessible offsets, multiple connected triangles, and measured rather than exact data. | Formulate and solve right-triangle measurement models and interpret their assumptions and precision. | Draw and label the necessary triangles, distinguish horizontal from slanted distances, include relevant offsets, keep calculator angle mode consistent, and judge whether the numerical result fits the geometry. |
