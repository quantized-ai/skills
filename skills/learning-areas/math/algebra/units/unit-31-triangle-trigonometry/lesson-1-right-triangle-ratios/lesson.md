# Lesson 31.1: Right-triangle ratios

Similarity makes acute-angle side ratios independent of the size of a right triangle.

## Learning objectives

- Define sine, cosine, and tangent using similar right triangles.
- Explain and use sine-cosine complementary-angle relationships and exact special-angle ratios.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify sides relative to the chosen acute angle and prove that a ratio depends on the angle alone.
- Distinguish a ratio from an angle measure and preserve exact values when justified by special triangles.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Similarity and acute-angle ratios** | For an acute angle \(0<\theta<90^\circ\), right triangles containing \(\theta\) are similar. Define \(\sin\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{hypotenuse}\), \(\cos\theta=\text{adjacent}/\text{hypotenuse}\), and \(\tan\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{adjacent}\). Positive denominators give positive ratios; \(\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta\). Special-triangle side ratios give exact values at \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\). | Justify acute-angle trigonometric definitions by similarity and determine ratios, including exact special-angle values. | Establish similarity before asserting scale independence, use the nonhypotenuse adjacent side, and attach no length units to a ratio. |
| **Complementary-angle identities** | The two acute angles of a right triangle sum to \(90^\circ\); their opposite and adjacent legs exchange roles. Consequently \(\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta)\) and \(\cos\theta=\sin(90^\circ-\theta)\) for acute \(\theta\). The corresponding radian complement is \(\pi/2-\theta\). | Explain and use the sine and cosine relationships of complementary angles. | Derive the relationship from side roles and retain consistent degree or radian units; do not treat supplementary angles as complementary. |
