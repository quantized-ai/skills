# Lesson 31.3: Laws for general triangles

Trigonometric relationships extend from right triangles to acute and obtuse triangles through auxiliary altitudes and coordinates.

## Learning objectives

- Extend sine and cosine to obtuse triangle angles and derive the trigonometric area formula.
- Prove the Laws of Sines and Cosines and apply them to right and non-right triangles.

## Proficiency criteria

- Account for auxiliary altitudes lying outside an obtuse triangle and preserve the signs of cosine.
- Match every side to its opposite angle, select a law from the available data, and verify the resulting triangle.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Obtuse angles and triangle area** | For \(0<\theta<180^\circ\), the point at angle \(\theta\) measured counterclockwise from the positive horizontal axis on the upper unit semicircle has coordinates \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\); sine is positive and cosine is negative for obtuse angles. Supplementary angles have equal sine and opposite cosine. For sides \(a,b>0\) including angle \(C\), an auxiliary altitude gives area \(K=\tfrac12 ab\sin C\), including obtuse \(C\) with an exterior altitude. | Define sine and cosine for obtuse triangle angles and derive and apply the triangle area formula using an auxiliary altitude. | Distinguish signed horizontal projection from positive altitude, handle acute and obtuse included angles, and identify the two sides adjacent to the chosen angle. |
| **Law of Sines** | For a nondegenerate triangle, positive sides \(a,b,c\) opposite \(A,B,C\) satisfy \(a/\sin A=b/\sin B=c/\sin C\). Equal area expressions or auxiliary altitudes prove the law, with supplementary-angle sine equality covering obtuse cases. Given two angles and a side, the angle sum and the law determine the remaining sides. | Prove the Law of Sines and apply it to find unknown measurements in right and non-right triangles. | Give a proof valid for obtuse as well as acute triangles, keep opposite pairs together, and check that all recovered angles are positive with sum \(180^\circ\). |
| **Law of Cosines** | For sides \(a,b,c>0\) with \(C\) opposite \(c\), \(c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\). A coordinate projection and the Pythagorean theorem prove the formula; the projection is negative for obtuse \(C\). Angles from SSS are recovered by \(C=\arccos((a^2+b^2-c^2)/(2ab))\), using the unique angle in \((0,180^\circ)\) and an argument strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\) for a nondegenerate triangle. The law determines the third side from SAS or angles from SSS and reduces to the Pythagorean theorem when \(C=90^\circ\). | Prove the Law of Cosines and apply it to find unknown measurements in right and non-right triangles. | Derive the cross term and its sign, select the included angle correctly, check triangle existence before recovering angles, and retain intermediate precision. |
