# Lesson 47.2: Influence, residual variation, and model interpretation

Extreme inputs and unusual responses have different effects on fitted models and predictions.

## Learning objectives

- Use graphical technology to investigate leverage, outliers, and the influence of observations on a fitted line.
- Interpret fitted coefficients and residual variability and explain limitations of linear correlation.

## Proficiency criteria

- Compare coefficients and predictions using controlled data changes, distinguish the three notions, and justify data handling rather than automatically removing inconvenient observations.
- Use contextual units and domain restrictions, inspect nonlinear patterns, and avoid equating zero correlation with no relationship or strong correlation with causation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Outliers and influential observations** | A response outlier has an unusual residual; a high-leverage point has an unusual input; influence concerns how much a fit changes when an observation changes or is omitted. These notions need not coincide. Dynamic graphing can compare full-data and perturbed fits. | Use graphical technology to investigate leverage, outliers, and the influence of observations on a fitted line. | Compare coefficients and predictions using controlled data changes, distinguish the three notions, and justify data handling rather than automatically removing inconvenient observations. |
| **Contextual coefficients and unexplained variation** | Slope has response-per-input units and intercept describes a zero-input prediction, which may lie outside the observed or meaningful domain. Residual patterns and spread describe unexplained variation; a small linear correlation can coexist with a strong nonlinear relationship. | Interpret fitted coefficients and residual variability and explain limitations of linear correlation. | Use contextual units and domain restrictions, inspect nonlinear patterns, and avoid equating zero correlation with no relationship or strong correlation with causation. |
