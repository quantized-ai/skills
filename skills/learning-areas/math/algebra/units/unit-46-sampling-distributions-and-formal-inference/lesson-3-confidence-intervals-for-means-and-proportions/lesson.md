# Lesson 46.3: Confidence intervals for means and proportions

Normal-based formulas provide interval estimates when their design and distribution conditions hold.

## Learning objectives

- Calculate and interpret a confidence interval for a normal-population mean with known standard deviation.
- Calculate and interpret a population-proportion confidence interval when a large-sample approximation is justified.

## Proficiency criteria

- Verify known population standard deviation and the sampling model; calculate the correct two-sided critical value, endpoints, units, and contextual interpretation.
- Count successes and trials consistently, check approximation conditions, express uncertainty in proportion or percentage-point units, and identify invalid intervals without silently truncating them as a repair.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mean with known population standard deviation** | For an independent random sample from a normal population with known $\sigma>0$, the mean interval is $\bar x\pm z^*\sigma/\sqrt n$, where $P(-z^*\le Z\le z^*)$ is the confidence level. Using an estimated $s$ in place of $\sigma$ changes the method and its justification. | Calculate and interpret a confidence interval for a normal-population mean with known standard deviation. | Verify known population standard deviation and the sampling model; calculate the correct two-sided critical value, endpoints, units, and contextual interpretation. |
| **Large-sample population proportion interval** | The normal approximation interval is $\hat p\pm z^*\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$ for a random independent binary sample with sufficiently many observed successes and failures. It can behave poorly at small counts or near 0 and 1; the normal approximation is not justified when those conditions fail. | Calculate and interpret a population-proportion confidence interval when a large-sample approximation is justified. | Count successes and trials consistently, check approximation conditions, express uncertainty in proportion or percentage-point units, and identify invalid intervals without silently truncating them as a repair. |
