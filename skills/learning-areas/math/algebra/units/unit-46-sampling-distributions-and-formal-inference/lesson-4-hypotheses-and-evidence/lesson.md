# Lesson 46.4: Hypotheses and evidence

Hypothesis tests compare an observed statistic with its distribution under a precisely stated population claim.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate null and alternative hypotheses and explain how a statistic supplies evidence about a population claim.
- Interpret a p-value and significance level and state a contextual test conclusion.

## Proficiency criteria

- Name the parameter and population, choose a justified direction, keep hypotheses distinct from observed sample values, and articulate what departures count against the null.
- Retain the null-model conditioning, use the specified tail rule, distinguish insufficient evidence from equivalence, and separate statistical from practical importance.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Null and alternative hypotheses** | Hypotheses concern a population parameter, with equality specified in the null and a directional or two-sided alternative chosen from the question before inspecting results. A test statistic measures departure relative to the null sampling distribution. | Formulate null and alternative hypotheses and explain how a statistic supplies evidence about a population claim. | Name the parameter and population, choose a justified direction, keep hypotheses distinct from observed sample values, and articulate what departures count against the null. |
| **P-values and significance decisions** | A p-value is the null-model probability of a statistic at least as incompatible as the observation under the preselected extremeness rule. It is not the probability the null is true. Comparison with a prespecified significance level yields reject or fail to reject, not proof or acceptance. | Interpret a p-value and significance level and state a contextual test conclusion. | Retain the null-model conditioning, use the specified tail rule, distinguish insufficient evidence from equivalence, and separate statistical from practical importance. |
