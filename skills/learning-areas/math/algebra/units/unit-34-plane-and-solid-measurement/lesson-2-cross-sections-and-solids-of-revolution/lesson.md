# Lesson 34.2: Cross-sections and solids of revolution

Planar slices and rotations connect two-dimensional figures with three-dimensional forms.

## Learning objectives

- Identify cross-section shapes of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- Identify solids produced by rotating plane regions about specified axes.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the slice’s position and orientation or the rotation axis explicitly.
- Distinguish a planar section from a projected silhouette and a rotated filled region from a rotated boundary.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cross-sections** | A cross-section is the intersection of a solid with a plane. Sections parallel to a prism or cylinder base reproduce its shape; analogous pyramid and cone sections are similar to the base with changing size. Oblique slices depend on orientation and the faces intersected. A nondegenerate spherical section is a circle whose radius depends on distance from the center; tangent slices can degenerate to a point. | Identify and justify cross-section shapes of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres for specified slicing planes. | Account for intersected faces and orientation, distinguish congruent from similar sections, and recognize empty and degenerate intersections when relevant. |
| **Solids of revolution** | Rotating a filled plane region about a line generates a solid determined by distances from that axis. A rectangle about one side yields a cylinder, a right triangle about a leg yields a cone, and a semicircular region about its diameter yields a sphere. An offset axis or a region with a hole can produce a hollow solid; rotating only the boundary describes surfaces rather than the filled volume. | Identify three-dimensional solids generated by rotating two-dimensional regions about specified lines. | State the rotation axis and track radii and height, distinguish internal from external axes, and identify hollow regions instead of filling them implicitly. |
