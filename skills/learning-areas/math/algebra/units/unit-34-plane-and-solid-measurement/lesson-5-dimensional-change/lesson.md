# Lesson 34.5: Dimensional change

Uniform similarity and selective changes in dimensions have different effects on geometric measures.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and apply linear, area, and volume scale laws under uniform scaling.
- Analyze changes in perimeter, area, surface area, and volume when only selected dimensions change.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use positive scale factors and distinguish a factor in length from the corresponding square or cube.
- Recompute from the relevant formula for nonuniform change instead of assuming a similarity law.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Uniform scaling laws** | A similarity with length factor \(k>0\) multiplies perimeters and other lengths by \(k\), areas and surface areas by \(k^2\), and volumes by \(k^3\). Dissection into rectangles or prisms and approximation extends these laws beyond those shapes. Recovering a length factor from an area or volume ratio requires a positive square or cube root. | Derive and apply scaling laws for lengths, areas, surface areas, and volumes of similar figures. | Verify uniform similarity, apply the correct exponent, and recover the appropriate positive length scale from a measure ratio. |
| **Nonuniform dimensional changes** | Changing selected dimensions can preserve some measures while altering others nonproportionally. Changing only a cylinder height scales its volume and lateral area but not its base areas; changing radius affects circular areas quadratically. General analysis substitutes altered dimensions into perimeter, area, surface-area, or volume formulas and compares with the original. | Determine and explain the effects of nonuniform dimensional changes on perimeter, area, surface area, and volume. | State which dimensions vary and which remain fixed, compare the complete before-and-after formulas, and avoid applying a single similarity factor to a distorted shape. |
