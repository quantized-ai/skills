# Lesson 34.3: Surface area

Nets and curved-surface relationships distinguish lateral area from total exposed area.

## Learning objectives

- Determine total and lateral surface areas of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres.
- Measure exposed surfaces of composite solids and account for joined faces.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use perpendicular height or slant height according to the formula and identify included surfaces.
- Exclude interior joins and avoid applying a right-solid formula to an oblique shape without justification.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prism and cylinder surfaces** | For a right prism with base area \(B\), base perimeter \(p\), and height \(h\), lateral area is \(ph\) and total area is \(ph+2B\). A right cylinder unwraps to a rectangle of dimensions \(2\pi r\) and \(h\), so its lateral area is \(2\pi rh\) and total area is \(2\pi rh+2\pi r^2\). For general prisms, individual lateral face areas replace the right-prism shortcut. | Derive surface areas from nets and determine lateral and total areas of prisms and cylinders. | Identify which faces are bases and which surfaces are included, use the right-solid assumptions correctly, and report square units. |
| **Pyramid, cone, and sphere surfaces** | A pyramid’s lateral area is the sum of its triangular face areas; for a regular pyramid with common face slant height \(\ell\), it is \(p\ell/2\). A right circular cone has lateral area \(\pi r\ell\) and total area \(\pi r\ell+\pi r^2\), where \(\ell=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}\). A sphere has surface area \(4\pi r^2\). Curved cone surface and planar base are distinct components. | Determine total and lateral surface areas of pyramids and cones and the surface area of spheres. | Distinguish vertical and slant heights, sum unequal pyramid-face areas separately, and retain the correct number of bases or absence of a base. |
| **Composite and exposed surfaces** | For joined or hollow solids, total exposed area is the sum of exterior faces and exposed curved surfaces, with contacting internal faces excluded. Open containers omit missing faces; cavities may add interior surfaces if those surfaces are part of the requested boundary. | Determine surface areas of composite, open, and hollow figures using their exposed boundary components. | Record included and excluded surfaces, subtract both copies of an internal joining face when starting from component totals, and distinguish material boundary area from volume. |
