# Lesson 34.4: Volume and Cavalieri’s principle

Cross-section comparisons explain why several families of solids share volume formulas.

## Learning objectives

- Use dissection, informal limits, and Cavalieri’s principle to explain prism, cylinder, pyramid, and cone volume formulas.
- Give a Cavalieri argument for sphere volume and apply volume formulas to simple and composite solids.

## Proficiency criteria

- State equal height and equal cross-sectional area at corresponding heights before using Cavalieri.
- Distinguish perpendicular height from slant height and track cubic units, holes, and nonoverlapping pieces.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prisms and cylinders** | A prism has volume \(V=Bh\) for base area \(B\) and perpendicular distance \(h\) between base planes; layering or dissection explains the formula. Cavalieri’s principle states that solids of equal height and equal cross-sectional areas at every corresponding height have equal volume. Comparing constant-area slices gives cylinder volume \(\pi r^2h\), including oblique cylinders with the same base and perpendicular height. | Explain prism and cylinder volume formulas using layering and Cavalieri’s principle and apply them to determine measurements. | State the cross-section conditions, identify perpendicular height, and distinguish volume from capacity units after conversion. |
| **Pyramids and cones** | Dissection establishes a one-third relationship for suitable pyramids and prisms; Cavalieri comparisons extend it to pyramids of equal base area and perpendicular height. Parallel slices of pyramids or cones scale in area by the square of the distance ratio from the vertex. Polygonal-base approximation and Cavalieri then give \(V=Bh/3\) for pyramids and \(V=\pi r^2h/3\) for cones. | Give informal dissection, Cavalieri, and limit arguments for pyramid and cone volumes and apply their formulas. | Explain the one-third factor and cross-sectional scaling, distinguish slant from perpendicular height, and keep the relevant base area explicit. |
| **Sphere volume by Cavalieri** | At height \(z\) from its equator, a sphere of radius \(r\) has cross-sectional area \(\pi(r^2-z^2)\). For \(-r\le z\le r\), a cylinder of radius \(r\) and height \(2r\) minus two cones with common vertex at the midpoint has the same area at each height. Cavalieri gives \(V=2\pi r^3-2(\pi r^3/3)=4\pi r^3/3\). | Explain the sphere-volume formula through Cavalieri’s principle and use it to determine sphere measurements. | Identify the comparison solid, justify equality of every corresponding cross-section and height, and distinguish radius, diameter, and cubic quantities. |
| **Composite volumes** | Composite solids can be partitioned into nonoverlapping prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, and spheres or obtained by removing cavities from larger solids. Volumes add or subtract only when overlaps and removed regions are accounted for; unknown dimensions may be determined from similarity or right-triangle geometry. | Determine volumes and missing dimensions of composite and hollow solids using appropriate formulas and geometric relationships. | State a complete decomposition, handle removed volume and overlapping pieces correctly, verify positive feasible dimensions, and use consistent cubic units. |
