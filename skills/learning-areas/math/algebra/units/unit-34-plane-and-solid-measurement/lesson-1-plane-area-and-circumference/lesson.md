# Lesson 34.1: Plane area and circumference

Decomposition and limiting arguments connect basic shapes to formulas for plane measures.

## Learning objectives

- Derive and apply area formulas for triangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, kites, and regular polygons.
- Give informal explanations of circle circumference and area and measure composite regions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish a perpendicular height or apothem from a slanted side and use appropriate linear or square units.
- Account for every region once and explain the approximation-to-limit relationship for circles.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Polygon area formulas** | Rectangle area is \(bh\); rearrangement or dissection gives parallelogram area \(bh\), triangle area \(bh/2\), and trapezoid area \((b_1+b_2)h/2\), where heights are perpendicular. A kite with perpendicular diagonals has area \(d_1d_2/2\). A regular polygon partitions into congruent central triangles, giving area \(pa/2\) for perimeter \(p\) and apothem \(a\). | Derive by dissection and apply area formulas for triangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, kites, and regular polygons. | Identify valid base-height pairs, distinguish an apothem from a circumradius, and explain why the kite diagonal or regular-polygon conditions are needed. |
| **Circle circumference and area** | Similarity makes the ratio of circumference to diameter constant, denoted \(\pi\), so \(C=2\pi r\). Inscribed and circumscribed polygon perimeters approximate circumference; increasingly fine sectors or polygon triangles give the informal limit argument \(K=\tfrac12 rC=\pi r^2\). These are arguments about limits of approximation, without a formal calculus requirement. | Give informal similarity and limit arguments for circle circumference and area and use the resulting formulas. | Explain the constant ratio and how increasingly fine partitions approach the circle, distinguish exact pi expressions from numerical approximations, and retain the radius-diameter distinction. |
| **Composite plane regions** | A composite region can be partitioned into nonoverlapping triangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, kites, regular polygons, and sectors, or represented as a larger region minus holes. Areas add or subtract; perimeter counts only the requested boundary and excludes internal partition edges. Unprovided dimensions can follow from established geometric relationships. | Determine areas and perimeters of composite plane regions using geometric decomposition and appropriate measurement units. | Identify all pieces or removed regions, avoid overlaps and double-counted boundaries, derive necessary missing dimensions, and distinguish exterior perimeter from total boundary when holes are present. |
