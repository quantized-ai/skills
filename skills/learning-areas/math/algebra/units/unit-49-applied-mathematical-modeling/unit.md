# Unit 49: Applied mathematical modeling

A modeling cycle combines algebraic, geometric, periodic, and discrete relationships with validation in scientific, artistic, and social contexts.

## Main learning objectives

- Construct, evaluate, revise, and communicate a mathematical model using a complete modeling cycle.
- Construct and evaluate proportional and inverse-variation physical models with explicit controlled quantities and domains.
- Determine and analyze a logistic model and compare it with linear and exponential growth models.
- Analyze dimensional changes, symmetry, perspective, indirect triangle measurements, and periodic patterns in architectural and artistic representations.
- Select and apply a recursive or iterative algorithm for a contextual model and communicate its results and limitations.

## Concepts and topics

The modeling cycle; Precision, accuracy, and indirect quantities; Direct and inverse physical relationships; Radioactive decay and quadratic motion; Logistic saturation; Threshold and periodic mechanisms; Scale, perspective, and spatial design; Distance and periodic structure in applications; Iterated update rules; Algorithm validity and reproducibility.
