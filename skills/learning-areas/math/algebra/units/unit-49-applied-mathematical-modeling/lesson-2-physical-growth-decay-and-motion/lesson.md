# Lesson 49.2: Physical growth, decay, and motion

Scientific models connect functional form with a mechanism and a limited regime of applicability.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and evaluate proportional and inverse-variation physical models with explicit controlled quantities and domains.
- Use technology to fit or analyze radioactive decay and quadratic motion models and interpret their parameters and solutions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Derive the constant from compatible data, retain dimensional consistency, and distinguish evidence for a proportional law from a coincidental finite-data fit.
- Identify rate and time units, distinguish model predictions from observations, select physically admissible times, and check conclusions against the stated assumptions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct and inverse physical relationships** | Direct variation $y=kx$ and inverse variation $y=k/x$, with $x\ne0$, model relationships only when other relevant variables are controlled. Constants carry units; physical laws such as elastic force, mass-acceleration, or pressure-volume relationships depend on stated regimes and constraints. | Construct and evaluate proportional and inverse-variation physical models with explicit controlled quantities and domains. | Derive the constant from compatible data, retain dimensional consistency, and distinguish evidence for a proportional law from a coincidental finite-data fit. |
| **Radioactive decay and quadratic motion** | Constant-fraction decay can be modeled as $N(t)=N_0e^{-kt}$ with $k>0$ and half-life $\ln2/k$. Constant vertical acceleration gives $s(t)=s_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$ on a physically justified time interval; drag or changing rates may invalidate either idealization. | Use technology to fit or analyze radioactive decay and quadratic motion models and interpret their parameters and solutions. | Identify rate and time units, distinguish model predictions from observations, select physically admissible times, and check conclusions against the stated assumptions. |
