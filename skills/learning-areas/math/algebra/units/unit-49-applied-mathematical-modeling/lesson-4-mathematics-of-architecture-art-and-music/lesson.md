# Lesson 49.4: Mathematics of architecture, art, and music

Geometry and periodic functions describe spatial and temporal structures in designed forms.

## Learning objectives

- Analyze uniform and nonuniform dimensional changes, symmetry, transformations, and perspective in architectural and artistic representations.
- Use right-triangle and general-triangle distance models with dynamic geometry and technology-supported periodic functions to analyze spatial and musical patterns.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish similarity from perspective distortion, explain nonproportional area and volume changes, and preserve the geometric properties actually justified by a representation.
- State angle units and geometric assumptions, select and validate the triangle method with dynamic geometry, distinguish amplitude from frequency and phase, and relate transformations or ratios to the modeled structure without overclaiming physical accuracy.

| **Scale, perspective, and spatial design** | Similar figures scale lengths by a positive factor, areas by its square, and volumes by its cube. Nonuniform dimensional changes require substituting the changed dimensions into surface-area and volume formulas; a single square or cube scale factor then generally fails. Perspective projection generally does not preserve Euclidean lengths or parallelism.
