## Lesson reasoning activity

**Purpose:** make the student test a consequential claim before accepting a procedure. Use after its relevant concept has been introduced; it is learning/practice evidence, not an unexposed assessment.

**Prompt:** A2×3×4 box changes to 4×3×4; a learner multiplies volume by 8 because one dimension doubled. Repair using dimensions.

**Agent-only reasoning:** Original volume 24, new 48, factor 2. Factor 8 requires every linear dimension doubled; recomputation is required for nonuniform changes.

Ask for the first unjustified step, invite a corrected explanation, and retain correct components of the original response. Then use a different representation or new case for independent evidence.

## Scale, perspective, and spatial design

Curriculum reference: **Scale, perspective, and spatial design** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** Doubling every length multiplies area by 2 or 4? What if only one rectangle side doubles?

**Agent-only key:** Uniform similarity multiplies area by 4; doubling one side with the other fixed multiplies its area by 2.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** A 2×3×4 box is scaled uniformly by 2; another only doubles its first dimension. Compare volumes and surface areas.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** Original volume 24 and area 52. Uniform scaling gives volume 192 and area 208 (factors 8 and 4). Changing to 4×3×4 gives volume 48 and area 80. Perspective pictures alone do not establish these length ratios.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Compare a uniform scale with a coordinatewise change on the same object.
2. Recalculate area/volume from dimensions in the nonuniform case rather than applying a memorized power.
3. Separate actual similarity, rigid symmetry and perspective projection, identifying exactly which properties survive each representation.

### Practice progression

Scale a plane figure and a solid uniformly; change only selected dimensions and recompute; then analyze an architectural motif's transformations and explain why lengths or parallelism cannot be read indiscriminately from a perspective photograph.

**Construction and verification controls:** Supply actual dimensions, positive scale, projection assumptions and motif transformations; distinguish similarity from visual appearance.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** Are all three dimensions multiplied by the same factor?

If volume gets the area factor, count changed independent dimensions. If a photo is treated as a scale drawing, ask whether its projection preserves lengths and parallel lines.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

### Assessment case checklist

Generate fresh tasks that collectively establish each of these curriculum obligations:

- Distinguish similarity from perspective distortion.
- Explain nonproportional area and volume changes.
- Preserve the geometric properties actually justified by a representation.

**Required case selection:** Uniform/nonuniform changes, area/volume, symmetry/transformations and perspective limits.

At least one independent task must transfer the reasoning to a changed representation, context, exception or reversed question. Separate numerical correctness from missing proof, assumptions, construction, reporting or technology evidence. ## Distance and periodic structure in applications

Curriculum reference: **Distance and periodic structure in applications** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** Two tones differ only by doubling amplitude, not frequency. Does the period halve?

**Agent-only key:** No; period depends on frequency, and changing amplitude alone leaves cycle timing unchanged.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** A level observer measures a 45° elevation to a tower top at horizontal distance 20 m, with eye height 1.5 m. Compare tones 220 and 440 Hz.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** Tower height=20tan45°+1.5=21.5 m under vertical/level assumptions. The frequency ratio is 2, one octave; frequency sets cycle rate, not amplitude. A=2 sin(2π·220t) has amplitude 2 and period 1/220 s.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Draw the distance triangle from the physical sight lines and state angle units before choosing a ratio/theorem.
2. Use dynamic geometry to vary given lengths/angles and test ambiguous SSA configurations.
3. For music write amplitude, frequency and phase as separate parameters and compare their graphical effects.

### Practice progression

Solve a right-triangle height with eye offset; then a general triangle using sine/cosine laws, checking possible second triangles; finally plot a periodic wave, change one parameter at a time and explain the observed effect without inferring perceived loudness from amplitude alone.

**Construction and verification controls:** Include right and general triangles, stated degrees/radians and ambiguous SSA cases; require actual dynamic geometry and periodic plots when those objectives are assessed.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** Identify the opposite side above the observer's eye before adding eye height.

If slant distance is used as the adjacent side, label each side relative to the viewing angle. If one inverse-sine value is called unique under SSA, use geometry or the supplementary angle to test a second valid triangle.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

### Assessment case checklist

Generate fresh tasks that collectively establish each of these curriculum obligations:

- State angle units and geometric assumptions.
- Select and validate the triangle method with dynamic geometry.
- Distinguish amplitude from frequency and phase.
- Relate transformations or ratios to the modeled structure without overclaiming physical accuracy.

**Required case selection:** Pythagorean/special/right-triangle and Laws of Sines/Cosines methods, geometry tool evidence, ambiguity, amplitude/frequency/phase.

Separate numerical correctness from missing proof, assumptions, construction, reporting or technology evidence.
