# Lesson 49.1: Model formulation, computation, and revision

A complete modeling cycle makes assumptions, representations, interpretation, and validation explicit.

## Learning objectives

- Construct, evaluate, revise, and communicate a mathematical model using a complete modeling cycle.
- Determine significant figures and reporting precision and estimate indirectly measured quantities using proportionality and explicit uncertainty assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Connect assumptions to each representation, retain units and contextual restrictions, compare results with evidence, and explain a justified revision or the remaining limits of the model.
- Identify significant digits and exact inputs, distinguish rounding from measurement error, propagate relevant bounds or sensitivity, and state why a proportional model is justified before reporting an estimate.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The modeling cycle** | A model identifies quantities and units, chooses essential variables and assumptions, constructs a relationship, computes consequences, interprets them in the original context, and checks against data or constraints before revision and reporting. | **Precision, accuracy, and indirect quantities** | Precision concerns resolution or repeatability; accuracy concerns proximity to an accepted value. Significant figures and interval bounds communicate measurement limits; exact counts and definitions do not restrict precision. Under conventional significant-figure reporting, sums are rounded by decimal-place precision and products by the least significant-figure count, while intermediate guard digits prevent premature rounding.
