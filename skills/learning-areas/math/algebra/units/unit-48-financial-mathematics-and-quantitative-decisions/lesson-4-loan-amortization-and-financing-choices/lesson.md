# Lesson 48.4: Loan amortization and financing choices

A loan balance recurrence separates interest from principal and makes comparisons across financing arrangements reproducible.

## Learning objectives

- Use formulas and technology to construct and validate amortization tables and fixed-payment loan models.
- Use technology-based amortization models to compare home buying with renting and vehicle buying with leasing.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reconcile each beginning balance, interest, principal, payment, and ending balance; distinguish negative amortization and rounding adjustments and compare total payments with borrowed principal.
- Use a common time horizon, account for terminal ownership or remaining debt, identify uncertain assumptions, and support conclusions with complete cash-flow comparisons.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amortization tables** | With initial balance $B_0=P>0$, periodic rate $i\ge0$, positive integer term $n$, and end-of-period payment $d\ge0$, $B_k=(1+i)B_{k-1}-d$ before fees. Interest is $iB_{k-1}$ and principal repayment is $d-iB_{k-1}$. A fully amortizing fixed payment is $Pi/[1-(1+i)^{-n}]$ when defined; $P/n$ applies when $i=0$. Rounded schedules may need a final adjusted payment. | Use formulas and technology to construct and validate amortization tables and fixed-payment loan models. | Reconcile each beginning balance, interest, principal, payment, and ending balance; distinguish negative amortization and rounding adjustments and compare total payments with borrowed principal. |
| **Housing and vehicle finance** | Buying versus renting or leasing compares cash flows over a common horizon, including deposits, down payments, interest, fees, maintenance, taxes, insurance, and terminal asset value where specified. Mortgage and vehicle models use amortization but differ in resale and contractual assumptions. | Use technology-based amortization models to compare home buying with renting and vehicle buying with leasing. | Use a common time horizon, account for terminal ownership or remaining debt, identify uncertain assumptions, and support conclusions with complete cash-flow comparisons. |
