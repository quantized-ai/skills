# Lesson 48.3: Interest and investment growth

Interest models distinguish rate conventions, compounding, and the timing of deposits and withdrawals.

## Learning objectives

- Calculate and compare savings growth under simple, periodic, and continuous compounding with consistent rates and time units.
- Model regular investment cash flows and compare investment types using stated return, fee, liquidity, and risk assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify nominal versus effective rates, match exponents to periods, retain precision, and compare like time horizons and cash flows.
- Locate every payment in time, handle the zero-rate case, distinguish guaranteed from modeled returns, and explain how withdrawal restrictions and uncertain rates affect a comparison.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple, compound, and effective rates** | For principal $P\ge0$, time $t\ge0$, and stated rate $r$, simple interest gives $A=P(1+rt)$; periodic compounding gives $A=P(1+r/m)^{mt}$ for a nonnegative integer number of periods $mt$, with positive integer $m$ compounding periods per year and $1+r/m>0$. The rate $r$ is nominal annual interest. Effective annual yield is $(1+r/m)^m-1$; continuous compounding gives $Pe^{rt}$. Taxes, fees, and inflation require explicitly supplied assumptions. | Calculate and compare savings growth under simple, periodic, and continuous compounding with consistent rates and time units. | Identify nominal versus effective rates, match exponents to periods, retain precision, and compare like time horizons and cash flows. |
| **Annuities and investment options** | For equal end-of-period deposits $d\ge0$, periodic rate $i>-1$ with $i\ne0$, and a positive integer number $n$ of periods, future value is $d[(1+i)^n-1]/i$; for $i=0$ it is $nd$. Beginning-of-period deposits multiply this accumulation by $1+i$. Stocks, bonds, annuities, certificates of deposit, and retirement plans differ in cash-flow structure, liquidity, contractual guarantees, and uncertain returns. | Model regular investment cash flows and compare investment types using stated return, fee, liquidity, and risk assumptions. | Locate every payment in time, handle the zero-rate case, distinguish guaranteed from modeled returns, and explain how withdrawal restrictions and uncertain rates affect a comparison. |
