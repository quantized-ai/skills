# Lesson 45.3: Reports and defensible conclusions

A statistical report communicates evidence, uncertainty, and limitations in language suited to its audience.

## Learning objectives

- Communicate an investigation in written and oral forms with justified methods, clear displays, and contextual conclusions.
- Evaluate statistical claims and revise unsupported conclusions using design, measurement, graphical, and inferential evidence.

## Proficiency criteria

- Make the study reproducible at the design level; match displays and summaries to variable types, describe uncertainty and scope, and adapt language without changing the evidence.
- Identify the particular missing information or faulty inference, distinguish unavailable evidence from known error, and retain only claims supported by the actual design and uncertainty.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Statistical reporting** | A report states the question, population, design, variable definitions, collection and analysis methods, displays and summaries, uncertainty, findings, and limitations. Technical and nontechnical accounts should preserve the same substantive claim. | Communicate an investigation in written and oral forms with justified methods, clear displays, and contextual conclusions. | Make the study reproducible at the design level; match displays and summaries to variable types, describe uncertainty and scope, and adapt language without changing the evidence. |
| **Critical evaluation of published findings** | Condensed reporting can omit denominators, sampling frames, unsuccessful analyses, uncertainty, confounders, or measurement definitions. Graph scales and selective summaries can exaggerate results; correlation and causation require different evidence. | Evaluate statistical claims and revise unsupported conclusions using design, measurement, graphical, and inferential evidence. | Identify the particular missing information or faulty inference, distinguish unavailable evidence from known error, and retain only claims supported by the actual design and uncertainty. |
