# Lesson 45.2: Variability and experimental controls

Statistical models combine systematic relationships with variation whose sources determine what can be controlled.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish deterministic and statistical models and construct a model that represents sources of variability around a systematic relationship.
- Design and critique comparative experiments using random assignment, replication, and appropriate controls or blocks.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify which variation the random term represents, state plausible assumptions, and distinguish individual-response variation from the variation of a statistic.
- Preserve assignment within blocks or pairs, identify the experimental unit, and distinguish causal evidence from population representativeness and from certainty about every individual.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deterministic structure and random variation** | A deterministic model predicts one output per permitted input; a statistical model such as $Y=f(x)+\varepsilon$ adds random variation with stated assumptions. Measurement, natural, induced treatment, and sampling variation have distinct origins. | Distinguish deterministic and statistical models and construct a model that represents sources of variability around a systematic relationship. | Identify which variation the random term represents, state plausible assumptions, and distinguish individual-response variation from the variation of a statistic. |
| **Random assignment, replication, and blocking** | An experiment compares assigned conditions. Random assignment addresses systematic group differences in expectation; replication measures variability; blocking or pairing handles known heterogeneity. Blinding and controlled measurement can limit response and measurement bias. | Design and critique comparative experiments using random assignment, replication, and appropriate controls or blocks. | Preserve assignment within blocks or pairs, identify the experimental unit, and distinguish causal evidence from population representativeness and from certainty about every individual. |
