# Lesson 52.2: Social choice limits and weighted voting

Fairness criteria constrain aggregation rules, while voting weights do not directly measure decision power.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Arrow’s impossibility theorem and its implications for evaluating voting schemes.
- Enumerate winning coalitions and calculate and interpret normalized Banzhaf power indices.

## Proficiency criteria

- State the relevant assumptions and incompatible properties, distinguish a ranking theorem from a claim that all elections are useless, and explain which assumption a proposed exception changes.
- Verify the weight and quota conditions, distinguish quota from majority count, count each coalition once, identify critical members and dummies, normalize by the positive total of critical occurrences, and explain why power can differ from weight share.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arrow’s impossibility theorem** | For at least three alternatives and unrestricted individual preference rankings, no social-ranking rule simultaneously always produces a complete transitive ranking and satisfies unanimity, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and nondictatorship. Changing the domain or the kind of output changes the theorem’s applicability. | Explain Arrow’s impossibility theorem and its implications for evaluating voting schemes. | State the relevant assumptions and incompatible properties, distinguish a ranking theorem from a claim that all elections are useless, and explain which assumption a proposed exception changes. |
| **Weighted coalitions and Banzhaf power** | For nonnegative voter weights with positive total and a quota greater than zero but no larger than that total, a coalition wins when its weight reaches the quota. These conditions ensure at least one winning coalition and a positive total number of critical occurrences. A voter is critical if removing that voter changes a winning coalition to losing; a dummy is never critical. Normalized Banzhaf power is each voter’s critical-count share of all critical counts. | Enumerate winning coalitions and calculate and interpret normalized Banzhaf power indices. | Verify the weight and quota conditions, distinguish quota from majority count, count each coalition once, identify critical members and dummies, normalize by the positive total of critical occurrences, and explain why power can differ from weight share. |
