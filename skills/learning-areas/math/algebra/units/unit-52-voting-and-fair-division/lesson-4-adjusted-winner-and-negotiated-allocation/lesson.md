# Lesson 52.4: Adjusted winner and negotiated allocation

Point-valued goods can be allocated and adjusted to balance two participants’ normalized satisfaction.

## Learning objectives

- Use adjusted winner to allocate property and analyze a two-party dispute.
- Determine when trimming and adjusted allocation are feasible for divisible and indivisible goods.

## Proficiency criteria

- Normalize valuations, compute transfers and the balancing fraction, handle ties and zero denominators explicitly, and verify equal satisfaction and no envy under the model.
- Identify the divisible right or compensation assumption, distinguish a mathematical fraction from a feasible transfer, and qualify fairness claims when valuations or liquidity assumptions fail.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjusted winner procedure** | Two parties distribute equal total points across goods with additive valuations and a stated tie rule. Allocate goods initially to their higher bidder; from the higher-total party’s bundle transfer goods in increasing ratios of that party’s value to the other party’s value until equal satisfaction, splitting the final transferred good as needed. Goods valued zero by both may be assigned arbitrarily; a positive-to-zero transfer ratio is treated as infinite. Equal positive valuation totals and feasible fractional transfers are required for the usual fairness guarantees. | Use adjusted winner to allocate property and analyze a two-party dispute. | Normalize valuations, compute transfers and the balancing fraction, handle ties and zero denominators explicitly, and verify equal satisfaction and no envy under the model. |
| **Divisibility and trimming limitations** | Trimming presumes a portion can be transferred without violating the valuation model. Indivisible goods require an agreed division of ownership or use, monetary compensation, sale, or another procedure; physical splitting is not always feasible. Strategic or nonadditive valuations can undermine guarantees. | Determine when trimming and adjusted allocation are feasible for divisible and indivisible goods. | Identify the divisible right or compensation assumption, distinguish a mathematical fraction from a feasible transfer, and qualify fairness claims when valuations or liquidity assumptions fail. |
