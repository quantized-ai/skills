# Lesson 52.1: Preference schedules and election rules

Different aggregation methods can choose different winners from the same individual preferences.

## Learning objectives

- Construct preference schedules and determine outcomes under ranked and approval voting rules.
- Compare election outcomes and analyze how agendas and strategic preferences can alter collective decisions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Weight each preference group correctly, specify tie and elimination rules, and distinguish plurality, majority, pairwise winners, and approval totals.
- Use a fixed original electorate for comparisons, identify the exact changed rule or report, and distinguish a method’s systematic property from one observed outcome.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ranked and approval voting** | A preference schedule groups voters with identical rankings and records their counts. Plurality, majority runoff, instant runoff, Borda scoring under a stated point convention, and pairwise comparison use rankings differently. Approval voting instead counts all approved candidates and need not encode a complete ranking. | Construct preference schedules and determine outcomes under ranked and approval voting rules. | Weight each preference group correctly, specify tie and elimination rules, and distinguish plurality, majority, pairwise winners, and approval totals. |
| **Strategic and agenda effects** | A Condorcet winner defeats every other candidate head-to-head, but majority preferences may cycle. Agenda order, strategic ranking, candidate withdrawal, and tie rules can change outcomes. Voting-method evaluation must state the desired fairness properties. | Compare election outcomes and analyze how agendas and strategic preferences can alter collective decisions. | Use a fixed original electorate for comparisons, identify the exact changed rule or report, and distinguish a method’s systematic property from one observed outcome. |
