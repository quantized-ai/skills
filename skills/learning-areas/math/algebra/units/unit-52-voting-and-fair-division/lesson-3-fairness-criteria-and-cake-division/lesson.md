# Lesson 52.3: Fairness criteria and cake division

Continuous division uses participants’ own valuations to guarantee specified notions of fairness.

## Learning objectives

- Evaluate allocations using proportionality, envy-freeness, equitability, and Pareto efficiency.
- Apply divider-chooser and three-player last-diminisher procedures and explain their fairness guarantees and strategic roles.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use each participant’s own valuation consistently, distinguish the four criteria, and justify claimed implications only under their stated conditions.
- Track valuations and residue accurately, include the no-trimmer case, justify two-player envy-freeness and three-player proportionality, distinguish the chooser’s possible advantage from the divider’s guarantee, and avoid claiming general three-player envy-freeness.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Proportionality, envy, equity, and efficiency** | With normalized additive valuations, proportionality gives each of $n$ equal claimants at least one nth of the whole by their own measure. Envy-freeness compares each person’s share with every other share using that person’s values; equitability equalizes normalized satisfaction; Pareto efficiency forbids improving someone without hurting anyone. | Evaluate allocations using proportionality, envy-freeness, equitability, and Pareto efficiency. | Use each participant’s own valuation consistently, distinguish the four criteria, and justify claimed implications only under their stated conditions. |
| **Divider-chooser and last diminisher** | For divisible resources with additive nonatomic valuations, one of two parties cuts two portions of equal value to that divider and the other chooses their preferred portion; this guarantees proportionality and envy-freeness for both. The divider secures a half-value guarantee while the chooser may receive more by their own measure. In the three-player last-diminisher procedure, a proposed one-third share may be trimmed by successive players to their one-third value; the last trimmer takes it, or the original proposer takes it if nobody trims. Trimmings return to the residue, and the remaining two divide and choose. | Apply divider-chooser and three-player last-diminisher procedures and explain their fairness guarantees and strategic roles. | Track valuations and residue accurately, include the no-trimmer case, justify two-player envy-freeness and three-player proportionality, distinguish the chooser’s possible advantage from the divider’s guarantee, and avoid claiming general three-player envy-freeness. |
