## Lesson reasoning activity

**Purpose:** make the student test a consequential claim before accepting a procedure. Use after its relevant concept has been introduced; it is learning/practice evidence, not an unexposed assessment.

**Prompt:** Initial payments 60,−20,−10 leave surplus 30. A learner gives 30 to each of three heirs. Repair and reconcile.

**Agent-only reasoning:** Divide the total surplus equally:10 each. Final payments 50,−30,−20 sum zero, with own-value benefits each fair share plus 10.

Ask for the first unjustified step, invite a corrected explanation, and retain correct components of the original response. Then use a different representation or new case for independent evidence.

## Sealed bids and fair-share accounts

Curriculum reference: **Sealed bids and fair-share accounts** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** Three participants value all goods at totals 90,60,30. Do all have the same monetary fair share?

**Agent-only key:** No; their own fair shares are 30,20,10 respectively.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** Three heirs value one item at A=90, B=60, C=30. Apply highest-bid allocation and initial fair-share accounts.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** A receives the item. Each fair share is their own total divided by three: 30,20,10. Initial signed payment into the account is value of own award minus fair share: A pays 60, B withdraws 20, C withdraws 10. Surplus is 30.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Keep a separate monetary valuation row for each participant.
2. Allocate each indivisible item once to the highest bidder under a tie rule, then calculate each person's value of their own award.
3. Establish the payment sign convention explicitly: award value minus own fair share is paid in when positive and withdrawn when negative.

### Practice progression

Set up a multi-good valuation table and allocate it; calculate fair shares and initial payments; then include a tie and a participant winning nothing, reconciling the account without substituting average bids for individual values.

**Construction and verification controls:** Use three or more participants, additive monetary bids, unique/declared tied winners and feasible compensation; verify every item assigned once.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** How much does this person value the whole estate?

If common shares are imposed, point to differing valuation totals. If a winning participant receives rather than pays excess award value, trace how that would move them farther above their fair-share account.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

## Surplus and guarantees

Curriculum reference: **Surplus and guarantees** in [lesson.md](lesson.md#concepts). The activities below implement that row; the row remains authoritative for definitions and proficiency.

### Separate diagnostic

**Prompt:** An initial settlement leaves surplus 24 for three participants. What equal bonus does each receive, and what must net payments sum to afterward?

**Agent-only key:** Bonus 8 each; net transfers sum to zero after the surplus is fully distributed.

Use the response to choose where to begin the teaching sequence. A correct short diagnostic does not establish the concept’s complete proficiency.

### Worked model

**Prompt:** Complete the settlement where A receives an item valued by A at 90, fair shares are A=30,B=20,C=10 and the initial surplus is 30.

**Agent-only worked reasoning:** Equal surplus bonus is 10 each. Net payments are A=50, B=-30, C=-20, summing to zero. Own-value net benefits are 90-50=40, 30 and 20: each fair share plus 10. A must be able to pay 50. This proportional guarantee does not imply general envy-freeness.

Reveal the explanation in the sequence below, asking the learner to justify a consequential step before moving to the next one.

### Teaching sequence

1. Explain why each highest winning bid is at least the mean of bids for that good, yielding nonnegative total surplus.
2. Distribute the surplus once and reconcile final signed transfers.
3. Verify each own-value net benefit equals own fair share plus the common bonus, then check whether required payments are feasible.

### Practice progression

Finish an initial settlement with positive surplus; compare a zero-surplus case; then inspect a participant lacking cash and an envious valuation comparison, retaining only the proportional guarantee supported by honest additive monetary valuations.

**Construction and verification controls:** Verify nonnegative surplus from highest-bid allocation, equal distribution and zero-sum settlements; include insufficient-cash and envy countercases.

### Responsive hints and misconceptions

**First conceptual cue:** After the initial payments and receipts, what remains in the common account?

If surplus is added to both the account and payouts twice, reconcile the final zero sum. If proportionality is called envy-freeness, compare what each person values another's goods-plus-cash package.

Give one relevant cue at a time and wait. If a cue does not help, use the indicated representation/setup before demonstrating the next worked step. Mark any mathematically supported attempt assisted; a later corrected response to the same example remains exposed.

### Assessment case checklist

Generate fresh tasks that collectively establish each of these curriculum obligations:

- Balance all transfers to zero after distributing the surplus.
- Verify individual fair shares.
- Identify liquidity and envy limitations.

**Required case selection:** Complete settlement, benefit guarantees, cash balance, honest additive valuation/liquidity and envy limitations.

## Adaptive teaching examples

Use the sign convention to explain settlement, not merely list signed numbers. With item values 90,60,30, A's award is worth 90 to A but A's fair share is 30, so A contributes 60 to the account; B and C initially withdraw 20 and 10. **Conceptual cue:** “Is this person's award above or below their own fair share?” **Setup:** columns are own award value, own fair share, signed contribution. **Worked step:** A's initial contribution is \(90-30=60\); leave the other rows and account total to the learner.

The surplus 30 is one account balance, so the common bonus is \(30/3=10\). Final contributions are 50, −30, −20 and sum to zero. **Faded verification:** hide the net-benefit column and ask each learner to compute own goods value minus signed contribution: 40,30,20. For non-envy, a separate comparison is needed: B values A's package as \(60-50=10\), not \(90-50\). C receives 20 in cash but values B's cash award at 30, so C envies B despite receiving more than C's own fair share. This is a concrete failure of the inference from proportionality to envy-freeness. Preserve the requirement that A can actually pay the needed 50.
