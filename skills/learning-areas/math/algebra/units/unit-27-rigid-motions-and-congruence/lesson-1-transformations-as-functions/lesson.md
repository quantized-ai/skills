# Lesson 27.1: Transformations as functions

A transformation associates every point in the plane with an image and has identifiable measurement effects.

## Learning objectives

- Represent transformations as point functions and compare distance, angle, and shape preservation.
- Define and perform translations, reflections, and rotations geometrically and with coordinates.

## Proficiency criteria

- Specify all parameters of each motion and construct images without relying on visual resemblance.
- Distinguish isometries, uniform dilations, and directional stretches using explicit invariant or changed quantities.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Point functions and invariants** | A plane transformation is a function \(T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2\). An isometry preserves every pairwise distance and consequently angles; translations, reflections, and rotations are isometries. A uniform positive dilation preserves angles but changes lengths except at scale one. A directional stretch generally changes both lengths and angles. Representations include coordinate rules, drawings, tracing, and dynamic geometry. | Represent plane transformations as functions and compare their effects on lengths, angles, and shape. | Identify input and image points, verify the relevant invariants, and use counterexamples to reject distance or angle preservation for directional stretches. |
| **Translations and reflections** | A translation moves all points by the same directed displacement: \((x,y)\mapsto(x+a,y+b)\). Reflection fixes points on its mirror line and sends each other point across the line along a perpendicular segment bisected by it. Reflection rules for coordinate axes and \(y=x\) follow from this definition; arbitrary mirror lines are handled geometrically. | Define and perform translations and reflections using geometric constructions and coordinate rules. | Specify displacement or mirror line completely, preserve distances and angles, and verify the perpendicular-bisector condition for reflected point pairs. |
| **Rotations** | A rotation fixes a center and moves each other point on a circle about that center through a specified directed angle; counterclockwise is positive. At the origin, quarter- and half-turn rules include \((x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)\) and \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)\). For other centers, coordinates are translated to and from the center or points are constructed using equal radii and angles. | Define and perform rotations about specified centers using geometric reasoning and coordinate rules. | Retain the center, distance to it, and directed angle; distinguish clockwise from counterclockwise motion and handle centers away from the origin. |
