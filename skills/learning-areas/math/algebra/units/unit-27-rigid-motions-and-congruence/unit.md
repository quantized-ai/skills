# Unit 27: Rigid motions and congruence

Transformations act on plane points and preserve or change geometric measurements. Rigid motions give a precise meaning to congruence and support triangle congruence criteria.

## Main learning objectives

- Define and represent translations, reflections, rotations, and their compositions on and off coordinate grids.
- Determine symmetry and distinguish distance-preserving motions from other transformations.
- Establish triangle congruence from rigid motions and use valid congruence criteria.

## Concepts and topics

Transformation functions, translation vectors, reflection lines, rotation centers and angles, coordinate rules, compositions, inverse motions, symmetry, corresponding parts, SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and hypotenuse-leg.

- [27.1: Transformations as functions](lesson-1-transformations-as-functions/lesson.md) — A transformation associates every point in the plane with an image and has identifiable measurement effects.
- [27.2: Compositions and symmetry](lesson-2-compositions-and-symmetry/lesson.md) — Sequences of motions explain image matching, inverse transformations, and the symmetries of figures.
