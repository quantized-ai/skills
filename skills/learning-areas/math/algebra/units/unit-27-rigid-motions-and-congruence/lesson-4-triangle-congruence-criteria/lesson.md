# Lesson 27.4: Triangle congruence criteria

Minimal sufficient data can establish triangle congruence without comparing all six corresponding parts.

## Learning objectives

- Derive SSS, SAS, and ASA from rigid motions and prove triangles congruent using those criteria, AAS, and hypotenuse-leg.
- Use corresponding parts after congruence is established and distinguish insufficient data.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the included angle or side correctly and invoke hypotenuse-leg only for right triangles.
- Explain why AAA and general SSA do not guarantee congruence and avoid circular use of the desired corresponding parts.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SSS, SAS, and ASA** | Three sides, two sides and their included angle, or two angles and their included side determine a nondegenerate triangle up to rigid motion. Aligning specified parts by translation, rotation, and if needed reflection forces the remaining vertex; side-length triples must satisfy strict triangle inequalities. | Explain SSS, SAS, and ASA from rigid motions and apply each to prove triangle congruence. | Identify sufficient corresponding data and justify why alignment fixes the third vertex; distinguish an included angle from an unrelated angle. |
| **AAS, hypotenuse-leg, and corresponding parts** | AAS follows from the triangle angle sum and ASA. Hypotenuse-leg applies only when both triangles are right triangles, with corresponding hypotenuses and one pair of legs equal. Congruent triangles have congruent corresponding parts, which can be used after the congruence proof. AAA determines only similarity; SSA can allow different triangles. | Prove triangles congruent using AAS and hypotenuse-leg and deduce corresponding-part relationships while rejecting insufficient criteria. | Establish right angles before hypotenuse-leg, show the missing angle for AAS, and give a logically ordered congruence argument before using corresponding parts. |
