# Lesson 27.2: Compositions and symmetry

Sequences of motions explain image matching, inverse transformations, and the symmetries of figures.

## Learning objectives

- Determine images, pre-images, and motion sequences on and off the coordinate plane.
- Describe all rotational and reflectional symmetries of common polygons.

## Proficiency criteria

- Respect composition order and verify that a proposed sequence maps corresponding points correctly.
- Distinguish mapping a figure onto itself from fixing every point of that figure.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Compositions and inverse motions** | Rigid-motion composition applies point functions in a specified order; order generally matters. Inverses reverse the order and invert each motion. A sequence carrying one figure to another must map all of its points, with vertices and defining edges providing a finite description for polygons. | Find images and pre-images under compositions of rigid motions and specify sequences mapping figures on and off a coordinate plane. | Apply the rightmost function first when using composition notation, reverse operations correctly, and verify correspondence rather than matching only one vertex. |
| **Reflectional and rotational symmetry** | A symmetry maps a figure onto itself. Reflectional symmetry is described by mirror lines; rotational symmetry by centers and angles modulo a full turn, including the identity. A regular \(n\)-gon has \(n\) mirror axes and rotations by multiples of \(360^\circ/n\). Rectangles, general parallelograms, and trapezoids must be distinguished from their more symmetric special cases. | Describe the rotations and reflections carrying rectangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, and regular polygons onto themselves. | List all distinct symmetries, distinguish a square from a nonsquare rectangle and an isosceles trapezoid from a general trapezoid, and justify missing symmetries by invariant features. |
