# Lesson 27.3: Congruence from rigid motions

Congruence means that one figure can be carried exactly onto another by rigid motions.

## Learning objectives

- Decide congruence using rigid-motion descriptions and identify corresponding parts.
- Establish the equivalence between triangle congruence and equality of all corresponding sides and angles.

## Proficiency criteria

- Produce a valid motion sequence or a decisive mismatch and preserve vertex order in congruence statements.
- Justify both directions of the triangle criterion using distance and angle preservation and alignment.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Congruent figures and correspondence** | Figures are congruent when a rigid motion or a composition of rigid motions carries one onto the other. Such motions preserve distances and angle measures; orientation can reverse under reflection. Equal area or equal perimeter alone does not establish congruence. | Use rigid motions to decide whether figures are congruent and identify their corresponding sides and angles. | Give a complete correspondence and a motion justification, or identify a violated invariant; do not reject a reflected figure merely because orientation changes. |
| **Triangle congruence equivalence** | For nondegenerate triangles, rigid-motion congruence is equivalent to equality of all corresponding side lengths and angle measures. The forward direction follows from invariance; the reverse aligns a vertex and side, then the remaining vertex, allowing reflection when needed. | Show using rigid motions that triangles are congruent if and only if all corresponding sides and angles are congruent. | Explain necessity and sufficiency separately and ensure the chosen correspondences are consistent across all six parts. |
