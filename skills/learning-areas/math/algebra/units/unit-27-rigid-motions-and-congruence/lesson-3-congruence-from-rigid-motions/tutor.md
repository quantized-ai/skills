## Reasoning and error-analysis activity

**Ask:** Equal area and perimeter are offered as a complete congruence proof. Ask the student to locate the first invalid inference, repair the reasoning, and explain a check or counterexample.

**Private reasoning and response:** These do not establish a full point mapping. Require corresponding lengths/angles or a rigid-motion sequence; verify every vertex rather than a single matching pair.

If the student gives only a corrected answer, ask why the original method failed. If the reason is secure, request a different example where the distinction matters. If they remain stuck, use the targeted response below for the relevant concept and mark the attempt assisted.

## Concept teaching plans

### Congruent figures and correspondence

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Two rectangles have area 12:2×6 and 3×4. Congruent?

**Private diagnostic key:** no; side lengths differ.

**Teach in this order:** Establish vertex correspondence; propose motion sequence; verify every vertex and defining edge; derive equal measures from invariance.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** Triangle A(0,0),B(2,0),C(0,1) maps by reflection in y-axis then translation (3,2) to A′(3,2),B′(1,2),C′(3,3). Corresponding sides/angles are preserved despite reversed orientation. A full rigid sequence proves congruence.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If equal area proves congruence, ask whether side lengths can differ; next use the rectangle counterexample. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Recognize correspondence → find a motion sequence → reject area/perimeter-only evidence. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Full mapping; preserved lengths/angles; orientation reversal allowed; no equal-area shortcut. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Give a complete correspondence and a motion justification, or identify a violated invariant; do not reject a reflected figure merely because orientation changes.

### Triangle congruence equivalence

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Do equal corresponding sides/angles of nondegenerate triangles imply a rigid mapping?

**Private diagnostic key:** yes; align a vertex and side, then choose the appropriate side of that line.

**Teach in this order:** Prove forward invariants and reverse alignment separately; articulate why nondegeneracy and correspondence matter.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** Translate A onto D, rotate AB onto DE with AB=DE. Equal angle at A and AC=DF locate C on the required ray at distance AC; if the triangles initially lie on opposite sides, reflect across DE first. Thus the third vertex reaches F.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If reflection is forbidden, ask whether mirror-image triangles should fail congruence; next align the shared side and flip the half-plane. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Forward implication → reverse construction → explain needed reflection. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Both directions; correct vertex order; nondegenerate triangle; side/ray uniqueness; allowed rigid motions. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Explain necessity and sufficiency separately and ensure the chosen correspondences are consistent across all six parts.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** Triangle ABC has vertices (0,0),(3,0),(0,2). Triangle DEF has vertices (5,1),(8,1),(5,-1). Prove congruence by motions and explain why equal area alone would not suffice.

**Private worked key:** Reflect ABC in the x-axis then translate by (5,1), mapping A→D,B→E,C→F. All sides and angles correspond. Conversely, equal corresponding sides and angles let one align a vertex and side, then the third vertex, with reflection if needed. Rectangles 1-by-6 and 2-by-3 have equal area but different side lengths, so are not congruent.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.
