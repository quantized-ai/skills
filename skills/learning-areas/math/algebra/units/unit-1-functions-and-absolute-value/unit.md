## Main learning objectives

- Classify relations and identify their domain and range. Evaluate function notation and interpret quantities, units, and domain conditions.
- Represent real sets and their unions and intersections in equivalent notation. Determine domain and range with correct endpoints, extrema, and contextual restrictions.
- Describe zeros, intercepts, sign, monotonicity, and local or absolute extrema in context. Compute and interpret average rate of change, including its units and limitations.
- Derive translation, reflection, and scaling effects and recover identifiable parameters from corresponding features. Map points, domains, and ranges under combined transformations and verify parameter effects with graphing technology.
- Classify functions as even, odd, both, or neither using the domain and function identities. Connect algebraic classifications to graph and table symmetry and judge the completeness of the evidence.
- Interpret distance and graph transformed absolute value functions with their key attributes. Formulate exact-distance equations and solve them with complete sign cases and verified solution counts.
- Solve absolute value linear inequalities, including zero and negative isolated bounds. Justify complete solution sets using distance, logical connectors, and correct endpoints.
- Evaluate and graph piecewise rules, verify consistency of overlapping branches, and determine domain and range. Evaluate floor functions and construct step functions from threshold conditions with correct boundary membership.

## Concepts and topics

Functions as single-output relations; Evaluating and interpreting function notation; Representing sets of real numbers; Domain and range from representations; Intercepts, intervals, and extrema; Average rate of change; Horizontal and vertical translations; Reflections and scale factors; Combined transformations and experimental checks; Even and odd functions; Absolute value as distance and a piecewise rule; Absolute value equations; Interior and exterior distance intervals; Evaluating and graphing piecewise functions; Step functions and endpoint conventions.

## Lessons

- [1.1: Relations and function notation](lesson-1-relations-and-function-notation/lesson.md) — Relations establish which inputs are allowed and whether each has a unique output. Function notation then connects these assignments to formulas and to quantities in context.
- [1.2: Sets, intervals, domain, and range](lesson-2-sets-intervals-domain-and-range/lesson.md) — Set notation makes domain and range statements precise. This lesson connects endpoint and set operations to the inputs and outputs allowed by a representation or context.
- [1.3: Features and change in functions](lesson-3-features-and-change-in-functions/lesson.md) — Function features describe where outputs lie and how they change. Average rate of change summarizes endpoint change and must be interpreted separately from behavior inside an interval.
- [1.4: Transformations of functions](lesson-4-transformations-of-functions/lesson.md) — Input and output transformations connect formulas, corresponding points, and graph features. Combined transformations are checked by mapping coordinates and comparing predictions with technology.
- [1.5: Symmetry of functions](lesson-5-symmetry-of-functions/lesson.md) — Even and odd functions express symmetry through identities. The symmetry of the domain is part of the test, and the available representation determines how strong the evidence can be.
- [1.6: Absolute value functions and equations](lesson-6-absolute-value-functions-and-equations/lesson.md) — Absolute value connects distance, a two-branch graph, and equations with separate sign cases. Transformations explain the graph’s vertex, slopes, domain, and range.
- [1.7: Absolute value inequalities](lesson-7-absolute-value-inequalities/lesson.md) — Absolute value inequalities describe points inside or outside a distance interval. Logical connectors, exceptional bounds, and endpoint inclusion determine the complete solution set.
- [1.8: Piecewise and step functions](lesson-8-piecewise-and-step-functions/lesson.md) — Piecewise definitions assign formulas to input regions; step functions assign constant outputs to such regions. Boundary membership determines both evaluation and graph markers.
