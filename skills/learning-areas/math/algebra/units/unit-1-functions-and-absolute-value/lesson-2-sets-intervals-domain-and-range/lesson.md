# Lesson 1.2: Sets, intervals, domain, and range

Set notation makes domain and range statements precise. This lesson connects endpoint and set operations to the inputs and outputs allowed by a representation or context.

## Learning objectives

- Represent real sets and their unions and intersections in equivalent notation.
- Determine domain and range with correct endpoints, extrema, and contextual restrictions.

## Learning outcome

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe allowed inputs and attained outputs without adding or excluding values through a notation error.

## Proficiency criteria

- Translate among inequalities, intervals, and set-builder notation with identical membership and endpoint inclusion.
- Form unions and intersections using their membership conditions, including empty or single-value results.
- Derive the range from the stated domain and representation, accounting for attained extrema and excluded endpoints.
- Choose discrete or continuous domains from the quantities being modeled and derive the attained outputs without assuming that the range has the same form.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Representing sets of real numbers** | Inequality, interval, and set-builder notation describe membership in real sets. Brackets include finite endpoints; parentheses exclude them. Infinity indicates unboundedness and is never included. A union contains values in either set; an intersection contains values in both. Empty sets, singletons, and finite lists are distinct from continuous intervals. | Translate real sets among inequality, interval, and set-builder notation, distinguishing discrete sets from continuous intervals. Form unions and intersections, including empty and singleton results, with correct endpoint inclusion. | Preserve exactly the same members across representations. Include finite endpoints only when allowed and leave infinity open. Apply either-set versus both-set membership correctly, including empty and singleton cases. Do not fill gaps in a discrete set. |
| **Domain and range from representations** | Domain is the set of allowed inputs; range consists of all outputs attained on that domain. A complete finite table specifies only its listed pairs; sampled data do not define unlisted inputs or outputs. Graph endpoints, extrema, and stated formula restrictions determine these sets. Removing an input removes its output from the range only if no remaining input produces that output. Context determines whether meaningful inputs are discrete or continuous and may further restrict them. | Determine domain and range from a table, described graph, or simple formula with a stated domain, accounting for extrema and excluded points. Determine the contextually meaningful domain and derive the range from those allowed inputs. | Identify the allowed inputs before collecting outputs. Include interior extrema and only attained endpoint values in the range. Check whether another input supplies an output at a removed point. State contextual restrictions and express both sets without adding values in gaps. |
