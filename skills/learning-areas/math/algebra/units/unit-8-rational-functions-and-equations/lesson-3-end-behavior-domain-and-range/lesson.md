# Lesson 8.3: End behavior, domain, and range

End behavior and attainable outputs describe different features of a rational function. Degree comparisons, equivalent forms, and input-solving arguments determine asymptotes, domain, and range.

## Learning objectives

- Determine horizontal asymptotes and explain end behavior, including the direction of approach when established by the formula.
- Determine and express domains and ranges in interval, inequality, and set notation, including the effect of holes.

## Learning outcome

Justify a rational function’s end behavior, domain, and range without treating a horizontal asymptote as an automatically excluded output.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use degrees, leading coefficients, or polynomial division to identify horizontal asymptotes or their absence.
- Explain the vanishing remainder or dominant-term argument and use signs to justify any claimed direction of approach.
- Determine which outputs have at least one allowed real input, including outputs associated with holes.
- Express domain and range consistently in all three notations.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Horizontal asymptotes and end behavior** | For a rational function with nonzero numerator, smaller numerator degree gives horizontal asymptote $y=0$; equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients; greater numerator degree gives no horizontal asymptote. Leading terms justify these degree rules. When a horizontal asymptote exists, division gives a constant plus a proper rational remainder; as $x\to\pm\infty$, the remainder tends to zero and its sign determines the side of approach. The identically zero function has end value zero. A horizontal asymptote may be crossed or attained and does not by itself determine a range exclusion. | Determine horizontal asymptotes or their absence from degrees and leading coefficients. Explain end behavior and any claimed direction of approach using an equivalent form or dominant terms, distinguishing asymptotic values from range exclusions. | Apply the appropriate degree and leading-coefficient comparison, treating an identically zero numerator separately. Justify both ends using division or dominant terms. Use signs to support a stated side of approach and distinguish an asymptote from a prohibition on attained outputs. |
| **Domain and range in three notations** | The domain excludes all zeros of the original denominator. The range consists of outputs $y$ for which $y=f(x)$ has at least one real solution in that domain. Algebraic input-solving or justified graph analysis can establish attainability. For $a/(x-h)+k$ with $a\ne0$, the domain excludes $h$ and the range excludes $k$. A hole removes an output only if no other allowed input produces it. Interval, inequality, and set notation must describe the same included values and exclusions. | Determine rational-function domains and ranges by testing allowed inputs and attainable outputs, and express both sets in interval, inequality, and set notation. Account for output values lost through holes rather than inferring range only from asymptotes. | Establish the original domain before finding the range. Solve for an allowed input or justify graph-based attainability of candidate outputs. Check whether another allowed input supplies a hole’s output. Represent both sets consistently in all three notations. |
