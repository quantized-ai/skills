# Unit 8: Rational functions and equations

Reciprocal transformations, rational graphs, domain restrictions, and equation methods connect inverse variation and quantitative rational relationships.

## Main learning objectives

- Graph the reciprocal parent and its transformations using coordinate mappings. Explain branch behavior, symmetry, asymptotes, domain, and range from reciprocal structure.
- Classify rational-function discontinuities using original exclusions and fully reduced factors. Determine intercepts and sign intervals while retaining every original domain exclusion.
- Determine horizontal asymptotes and explain end behavior, including the direction of approach when established by the formula. Determine and express domains and ranges in interval, inequality, and set notation, including the effect of holes.
- Solve rational equations that reduce to linear or quadratic equations, including cases with no solutions or an identity. Verify candidates in the original equation and relate valid solutions to equal outputs in graphs or tables.
- Identify and formulate inverse-variation relationships using a constant product. Formulate rational equations from constant-rate relationships with defined quantities and compatible units.

## Concepts and topics

The reciprocal parent function; Transformations of reciprocal graphs; Holes and vertical asymptotes; Rational-function intercepts and signs; Horizontal asymptotes and end behavior; Domain and range in three notations; Clearing denominators and checking candidates; Multiple solutions and graphical confirmation; Inverse variation; Formulating rational equations and testing reasonableness.
