## Reasoning activity

**Prompt:** Two pumps need 4 and 12 hours separately. A student adds their times to get 16 hours together. Repair the model.

**Private reasoning key:** Rates add: 1/4+1/12=1/3 tank/hour, so the joint time is 3 hours. It is shorter than either solo time under the additive constant-rate assumptions.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Inverse variation

Curriculum reference: **Inverse variation** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Assume inverse variation and a pair $(x,y)=(3,8)$. Find y at x=6.

**Agent key:** The constant product is 24, so $y=24/6=4$; doubling x halves y.

**Worked example:** Do pairs $(1,6),(2,5),(3,4)$ support inverse variation?

**Worked reasoning:** No: products 6,10,12 differ. Decreasing outputs alone do not establish inverse variation.


#### Teaching sequence

State the inverse-variation assumption y=k/x on an appropriate nonzero domain. Use a supplied pair to find the constant product k and its units, then predict another value. Test multiple pairs for equal products; decreasing behavior alone also occurs in many other families. Distinguish doubling-input/halving-output behavior from constant difference or direct proportion.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Are you checking constant products or constant ratios?

If y/x is used as the invariant, contrast direct and inverse variation. If one pair is said to prove the model, point out that many functions pass through it. If x=0 is allowed, inspect the model denominator.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Move from a stated model and one pair to tables requiring validation, missing values and comparisons with other decreasing relationships.

#### Assessment evidence

Require model assumption, constant product and units, nonzero input, verified predictions and an evidence-based acceptance or rejection of proposed data.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Formulating rational equations and testing reasonableness

Curriculum reference: **Formulating rational equations and testing reasonableness** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Two constant-rate pumps fill the same tank in 6 and 3 hours separately. Find the joint time.

**Agent key:** Combined rate $1/6+1/3=1/2$ tank/hour gives 2 hours, less than either individual time.

**Worked example:** A job takes one worker 4 hours and two together 3 hours. Find the second worker's solo time, assuming additive constant positive rates.

**Worked reasoning:** $1/4+1/t=1/3$ gives $1/t=1/12$, so 12 hours; substitution verifies the rate equation.


#### Teaching sequence

Define a common job or distance and attach units to each rate before writing an equation. For pumps, add fractions of one tank per hour, then invert the joint rate for time. For a missing worker rate, subtract rates rather than durations. After solving, test positivity, original denominators and whether the time is physically sensible under the stated constant/additive assumptions.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Have you added rates for a common job rather than completion times?

If times are added, ask what one worker completes in one hour. If a negative time survives, compare it with the model's quantity meaning. If capacities or units differ, convert to a common job before adding.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Start with joint-time calculations, then missing solo rates and compatible travel relationships. Include impossible specifications that imply a nonpositive rate.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess variable definitions, compatible units, rational formulation, complete algebraic solution and contextual rejection of invalid roots. These models assume constant rates unless explicitly changed.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

## Decision model and graduated practice

With two pumps filling one tank in $4$ and $12$ hours separately, their rates are $1/4$ and $1/12$ tanks/hour. Add contributions to obtain $1/3$ tank/hour; one tank therefore takes $1\div(1/3)=3$ hours. Units explain why times cannot be added. This model assumes constant simultaneous additive rates and is reasonable because $3$ is shorter than either solo time.

If the learner answers $16$ hours, cue “What part of the tank does each pump fill in one hour?”; next set up $1/t=1/4+1/12$; then combine the rates to $1/3$, leaving inversion and interpretation. Fade on solo times $6,3$ hours (key $2$ hours). For inverse variation, distinguish evidence from assumption: given the model and $(3,8)$, $xy=24$ forces $y=4$ at $x=6$; merely decreasing pairs do not establish that model.
