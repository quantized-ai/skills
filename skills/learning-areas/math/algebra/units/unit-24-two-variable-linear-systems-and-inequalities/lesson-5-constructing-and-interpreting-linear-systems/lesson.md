# Lesson 24.5: Constructing and interpreting linear systems

Related quantities and multiple conditions lead to simultaneous models whose solutions must remain meaningful in context.

## Learning objectives

- Construct two-variable linear systems from tables, graphs, and verbal conditions.
- Interpret exact and approximate solutions using model assumptions and contextual domains.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define both unknowns and translate independent constraints with consistent units.
- Check simultaneous validity, nonnegativity, integrality, and the modeled range.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Representing linked quantities** | Two unknown quantities can be constrained by totals, weighted totals, constant rates, or two separate linear relationships. A table or graph supplies points and rates for each equation. Each condition must be translated independently; two versions of the same condition do not generally determine a unique pair. | Write two-variable linear systems from verbal descriptions, graphs, and tables, including total, mixture, and constant-rate relationships. | Define the variables and units, match coefficients to the correct quantities, and explain whether the information supplies independent constraints. |
| **Contextual solutions and comparisons** | An intersection can represent simultaneous totals, a meeting condition, or equality of two costs. Contextual restrictions may exclude negative, fractional-count, or out-of-interval solutions. A no-solution or dependent outcome describes conflicting or insufficient constraints in the model; approximate graphical results require contextual precision. | Solve contextual linear systems and interpret unique, absent, or nonunique solutions and their feasibility. | Check every original condition and domain restriction, state the meaning of both coordinates, and distinguish an algebraic intersection from a viable contextual choice. |
