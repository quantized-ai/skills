# Lesson 24.1: Systems and graphical solutions

The solution of a system must satisfy every equation at once, making line intersections the graphical representation of simultaneous solutions.

## Learning objectives

- Represent simultaneous equations and verify ordered-pair solutions.
- Estimate and classify system solutions from line graphs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Check every original equation and distinguish an ordered pair from its separate coordinates.
- State graphical precision and identify when a viewing window cannot settle the solution count.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simultaneous solution meaning** | A system groups constraints that must all hold. A two-variable solution is an ordered pair satisfying every equation. Two line graphs may intersect at one point, be parallel and distinct, or coincide; their common point set is exactly the solution set. | Determine whether an ordered pair solves a system and explain the correspondence between simultaneous solutions and graph intersections. | Substitute the same pair into each original equation, use the word and for simultaneous conditions, and distinguish one shared point from separate points on different lines. |
| **Graphical solution and precision** | Graphing each equation with suitable scales reveals or estimates common points. A graph supplies an approximation unless exact coordinates can be established; a coarse display can hide nearby distinct lines or an intersection outside the window. Algebraic substitution can verify an apparent intersection. | Graph two-variable linear systems by hand and with technology to estimate solutions and distinguish intersecting, parallel, and coincident lines. | Use consistent axes and a justified window, report approximation precision, and confirm visually ambiguous cases through coefficients or substitution. |
