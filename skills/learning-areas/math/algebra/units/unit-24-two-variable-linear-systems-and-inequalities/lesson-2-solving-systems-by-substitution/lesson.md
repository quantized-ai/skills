# Lesson 24.2: Solving systems by substitution

Substitution replaces a quantity by an equal expression to reduce simultaneous linear equations to one variable.

## Learning objectives

- Solve two-variable linear systems by substitution.
- Explain why the replacement preserves simultaneous constraints.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve grouping and nonzero coefficient restrictions while isolating a variable.
- Recover both coordinates and verify them in both original equations.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Substitution and reduction** | If one equation gives \(y=g(x)\), replacing \(y\) by \(g(x)\) in the other equation yields a condition on \(x\). Solving that condition and recovering \(y\) gives all simultaneous solutions when each step is equivalent. Either variable may be isolated, but division requires a nonzero coefficient. | Solve a two-variable linear system by isolating a variable, substituting, solving the resulting equation, and recovering the second coordinate. | Substitute for the entire variable term with correct grouping, preserve exact fractions, and verify both original equations rather than only the reduced one. |
| **Identity or contradiction after substitution** | A reduced contradiction means no common solution. A reduced identity means the remaining original line condition describes all solutions, rather than that every ordered pair is permitted. A free coordinate may parameterize the line, with vertical or horizontal lines represented using the other coordinate when necessary. | Interpret identity and contradiction outcomes from substitution and express the complete solution set. | Distinguish no solution from a one-parameter line of solutions, retain an original equation for dependent systems, and check parameter restrictions. |
