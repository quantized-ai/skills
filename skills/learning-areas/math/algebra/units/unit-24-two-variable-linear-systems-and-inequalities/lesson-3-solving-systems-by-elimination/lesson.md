# Lesson 24.3: Solving systems by elimination

Equivalent equation combinations remove one variable while retaining exactly the original simultaneous solutions.

## Learning objectives

- Solve linear systems by scaling and elimination.
- Prove why an elementary equation replacement preserves the solution set.

## Proficiency criteria

- Multiply complete equations, retain one original-equivalent equation, and account for signs.
- Explain both directions of the equivalence and recover and verify the full ordered pair.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Elimination by equation combinations** | Nonzero scaling preserves an equation. Replacing one system equation by itself plus a multiple of another can cancel a variable while the other equation is retained. Coefficient choices determine the needed multipliers. A contradiction or identity in the reduced equation identifies inconsistent or dependent constraints. | Solve two-variable linear systems by addition or subtraction after valid coefficient scaling and interpret exceptional reduced forms. | Scale every term, eliminate the intended variable, retain enough information to recover both coordinates, and verify results in both original equations. |
| **Proof of solution-set preservation** | Write two equations as \(E_1\) and \(E_2\). A pair satisfying both satisfies \(E_1+kE_2\). Conversely, a pair satisfying \(E_2\) and \(E_1+kE_2\) satisfies \(E_1\) by subtracting \(kE_2\). Retaining \(E_2\) makes the replacement reversible; using only the sum can admit extra pairs. | Prove in both directions that replacing one equation by its sum with a multiple of the other preserves a system’s solution set. | State which equation is retained, show the forward combination and reverse subtraction, and distinguish equivalent systems from one consequence of a system. |
