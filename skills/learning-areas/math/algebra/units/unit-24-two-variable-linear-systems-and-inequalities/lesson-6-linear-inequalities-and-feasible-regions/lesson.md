# Lesson 24.6: Linear inequalities and feasible regions

Linear inequalities define half-planes, and simultaneous inequalities define their common solution set. Contextual restrictions determine which points in that real-valued region are feasible choices.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and graph linear inequalities with correct boundary inclusion.
- Find complete intersections of half-planes and interpret feasible choices.

## Proficiency criteria

- Determine a boundary from the supplied representation and use an off-boundary test point to select the half-plane.
- Apply every strict or inclusive inequality and contextual restriction, including at edges and vertices.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Boundary lines and half-planes** | For a nonconstant linear inequality in two variables, equality defines a boundary line. Strict inequalities exclude it and use a dashed line; inclusive inequalities retain it and use a solid line. An off-boundary test point selects the half-plane, including for vertical lines. A graph, table of boundary points with included-side information, or verbal constraint can define the inequality; a finite unlabeled table alone need not determine a unique half-plane. | Construct and graph a linear inequality in two variables from sufficient tabular, graphical, or verbal information and verify boundary and nonboundary points. | Identify the correct boundary and strictness, reverse the comparison for negative scaling, select shading using a test point, and identify insufficient information rather than assume an inclusion side. |
| **Intersections of half-planes** | A system requires every inequality, so its solution set is the intersection of their half-planes. It may be bounded, unbounded, empty, or reduced to a line, ray, segment, or point. Interior points as well as permitted boundary points are solutions. Each edge or vertex is included only if it satisfies every strict or inclusive condition. | Solve systems of linear inequalities graphically and describe and verify the complete feasible region, including boundary and degenerate cases. | Retain only the common intersection, distinguish region types, verify interior and boundary candidates in every original inequality, and do not list vertices as though they were all solutions. |
| **Formulating contextual feasible sets** | Constraints compare quantities with compatible units. Maximum or minimum bounds are inclusive unless explicitly strict. Nonnegativity, additional bounds, or integrality may restrict the region. When variables count indivisible items, only integer-coordinate points are admissible; feasibility requires satisfaction of all conditions and does not itself require optimization. | Formulate systems of linear inequalities with defined quantities and interpret feasible choices subject to units, bounds, and integer restrictions. | Translate every constraint, include all contextual restrictions, and check each proposed choice in the original inequalities before interpreting its coordinates. |
