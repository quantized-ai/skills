# Lesson 24.4: Classification and choice of system method

Coefficient relationships and solution structure connect the graphical and algebraic methods for linear systems.

## Learning objectives

- Classify system solution sets using coefficients, reduced equations, and graphs.
- Choose and compare graphical, substitution, and elimination methods.

## Proficiency criteria

- Handle zero coefficients and axis-parallel lines without relying on undefined ratios.
- Distinguish exact results from approximations and justify the chosen method for the given representation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Unique, inconsistent, and dependent constraints** | For two genuine lines \(a_1x+b_1y=c_1\) and \(a_2x+b_2y=c_2\), a nonzero determinant \(a_1b_2-a_2b_1\) gives one intersection. When it is zero, elimination determines whether the lines coincide or are distinct parallel lines. Equations with both variable coefficients zero are identities or contradictions rather than lines. | Classify linear systems using coefficient relationships or equivalent reduced equations, including degenerate constant equations. | Avoid division-based coefficient ratios when a coefficient is zero, distinguish a full plane of solutions from a line when both equations are identities, and state the complete solution set. |
| **Method selection and verification** | An isolated variable favors substitution; easily matched coefficients favor elimination; a graph supports visualization and approximation. All correct methods represent the same solution set. A method choice depends on the form of the input and required exactness, not a different meaning of solution. | Select, compare, and verify graphical, substitution, and elimination solutions for the same linear system. | Explain the practical choice, reconcile approximate coordinates with an exact result, and diagnose mismatches through original-equation checks. |
