# Unit 25: Data displays and descriptive relationships

This unit develops the descriptive statistics needed to organize data, compare distributions, and evaluate linear relationships before studying inference.

## Main learning objectives

- Classify variables and construct appropriate numerical and categorical data displays.
- Compute and interpret center and spread and compare distributions with attention to shape, units, and unusual values.
- Analyze two-way tables, scatter plots, linear fits, residuals, and correlation without confusing association with causation.

- Construct and compare line, circle, and stem-and-leaf displays, explaining their information and limitations.

## Concepts and topics

Statistical variability; data types; frequency displays; dot plots; histograms; mean and median; quartiles and box plots; interquartile range; mean absolute deviation; standard deviation; outliers; two-way tables; scatter plots; linear fits; residuals; correlation; causal limits.

Line and circle graphs; stem-and-leaf displays; comparisons of representations of the same data.

## Lessons

- [25.1: Statistical variables and data displays](lesson-1-statistical-variables-and-data-displays/lesson.md) — A statistical question anticipates variability, and a useful display preserves the meaning and type of the observed variable.
- [25.2: Measures of center](lesson-2-measures-of-center/lesson.md) — A measure of center summarizes a distribution’s location but may respond differently to skewness or extreme observations.
- [25.3: Quartiles, spread, and box plots](lesson-3-quartiles-spread-and-box-plots/lesson.md) — Spread describes variation around or across a distribution, and a box plot summarizes its ordered structure.
- [25.4: Standard deviation and distribution comparisons](lesson-4-standard-deviation-and-distribution-comparisons/lesson.md) — Standard deviation and robust summaries offer different views of spread, which must be interpreted alongside shape and center.
- [25.5: Two-way categorical tables](lesson-5-two-way-categorical-tables/lesson.md) — Two-way tables record two categorical variables for each observational unit and make conditional comparisons possible.
- [25.6: Scatter plots and linear fits](lesson-6-scatter-plots-and-linear-fits/lesson.md) — Paired quantitative observations can suggest a linear relationship whose equation summarizes a trend and supports limited predictions.
- [25.7: Residuals, correlation, and causal claims](lesson-7-residuals-correlation-and-causal-claims/lesson.md) — Residual analysis and correlation assess different aspects of linear fit, while study context limits the conclusions an association supports.

- [25.8: Time-series, sector, and stem-and-leaf displays](lesson-8-time-series-sector-and-stem-and-leaf-displays/lesson.md) — Compare displays that preserve different features and evaluate their conclusions.
