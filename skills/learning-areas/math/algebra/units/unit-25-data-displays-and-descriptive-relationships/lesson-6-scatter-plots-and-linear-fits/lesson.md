# Lesson 25.6: Scatter plots and linear fits

Paired quantitative observations can suggest a linear relationship whose equation summarizes a trend and supports limited predictions.

## Learning objectives

- Construct scatter plots and describe direction, form, strength, and unusual observations.
- Fit linear models with and without technology and interpret their parameters and predictions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Keep each pair of observations together and distinguish a trend from an exact functional rule.
- Use parameter units and the observed domain to judge meaningful predictions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Paired data and scatter-plot structure** | A scatter plot represents each paired observation \((x_i,y_i)\) without connecting points as a time path unless that order is relevant. Quantitative association is described by direction, form, strength, clusters, and unusual points. Explanatory and response roles depend on the question; an association alone does not determine a causal direction. | Construct and interpret scatter plots and describe the direction, form, strength, and unusual features of paired quantitative data. | Label units and scales, preserve pairs, distinguish nonlinear association from no association, and account for clusters or unusual points in the description. |
| **Constructing a linear fit** | A line of fit approximates a data trend rather than passing through every point. A hand fit balances positive and negative vertical deviations across the data; technology can determine a least-squares line by minimizing the sum of squared vertical residuals. Two points chosen on a hand-fitted line define its equation and need not be original observations. | Fit a reasonable linear function by hand and obtain a least-squares line with technology for data suggesting linear association. | Use the overall scatter rather than connect selected data points arbitrarily, report the fitted equation, and distinguish a fitted approximation from an exact linear relationship. |
| **Interpreting linear model parameters and predictions** | In \(\hat y=mx+b\), \(m\) is the predicted change in response per unit change of explanatory variable. The intercept predicts the response at input zero but may lie outside a meaningful domain. Interpolation uses inputs within the observed range; extrapolation goes beyond it and relies on additional assumptions that the trend continues. | Interpret fitted slope and intercept and use a linear model to make and evaluate contextual predictions. | Use correct compound units, distinguish predicted from observed values, identify interpolation or extrapolation, and reject interpretations outside reasonable physical or contextual bounds. |
