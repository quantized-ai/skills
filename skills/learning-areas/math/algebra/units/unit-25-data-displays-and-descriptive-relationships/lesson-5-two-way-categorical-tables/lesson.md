# Lesson 25.5: Two-way categorical tables

Two-way tables record two categorical variables for each observational unit and make conditional comparisons possible.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and interpret joint, marginal, and conditional relative frequencies.
- Assess categorical association by comparing rates with appropriate denominators.

## Proficiency criteria

- Reconcile row, column, and total counts and identify the population conditioned on.
- Distinguish joint frequency from a conditional rate and association from proof of causation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Joint and marginal frequencies** | A two-way frequency table cross-classifies observations by two categorical variables. Interior cells are joint counts; row and column totals are marginal counts. Dividing either by the overall nonzero total gives joint or marginal relative frequencies. Categories within each variable must be mutually exclusive and cover the recorded observations. | Construct two-way frequency tables and calculate and interpret joint and marginal relative frequencies. | Count each observational unit once, reconcile all totals, use the overall total for joint and marginal proportions, and label the two variables clearly. |
| **Conditional relative frequencies and association** | A conditional relative frequency restricts the denominator to the group specified by a row or column, requiring a nonzero group count. Comparing category rates across groups reveals possible association. Reversing the conditioning changes the denominator and usually changes the value. Group size differences make raw-count comparisons insufficient. | Calculate conditional relative frequencies and use comparable group rates to describe possible categorical associations. | State the condition in words, use the correct group total, distinguish a reversed conditional comparison, and avoid causal claims based solely on the table. |
