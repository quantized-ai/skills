# Lesson 25.4: Standard deviation and distribution comparisons

Standard deviation and robust summaries offer different views of spread, which must be interpreted alongside shape and center.

## Learning objectives

- Calculate standard deviation under a stated sample or population convention.
- Compare distributions using summaries suited to shape and explain effects of outliers and transformations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the correct denominator and distinguish variance units from standard-deviation units.
- Compare center and spread separately with common scales and contextual meaning.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Variance and standard deviation** | For a complete finite population, \(\mu=\sum x_i/N\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\mu)^2/N}\). The usual sample estimate is \(s=\sqrt{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2/(n-1)}\), requiring \(n>1\). Variance is the corresponding squared-deviation average; standard deviation restores the original units. Either standard deviation is nonnegative and is zero when all observations agree. | Calculate and interpret variance and standard deviation by hand for small datasets and with technology using a stated population or sample convention. | Identify the intended denominator, distinguish squared units from original units, and select the matching technology output rather than interchange sample and population values. |
| **Comparing shape, center, and spread** | Distributions can be symmetric, skewed, unimodal, or multimodal and can contain gaps or unusual values. Mean and standard deviation often suit approximately symmetric data without influential extremes; median and interquartile range often suit skewed data. A comparison combines compatible units and scales with separate statements about center, spread, shape, and unusual observations. | Compare two or more data distributions using appropriate displays and summaries and explain possible effects of outliers. | Support each comparison with the relevant numerical or graphical evidence, justify the summary choice, and avoid interpreting greater spread as automatically greater center. |
| **Shifting and scaling data** | Under \(y=cx+a\), the mean becomes \(c\bar x+a\) and standard deviation becomes \(\lvert c\rvert s\); the corresponding population rule is identical. A shift preserves spread. Scaling by zero collapses every value; negative scaling reverses the order and reflects the distribution while preserving spread up to magnitude. | Determine and explain how shifting or scaling all observations changes the mean, standard deviation, and distribution shape. | Use the absolute scale factor for spread, include negative and zero factors, and interpret unit conversions consistently with the transformed data. |
