# Lesson 10.5: Exponential equations

Solve suitable exponential equations exactly using common bases, and approximate others with graphs or tables. Valid solutions satisfy the original equation, the exponential’s positivity constraints, and the stated precision.

## Learning objectives

- Solve common-base exponential equations using one-to-one behavior, identify impossible targets, and verify solutions.
- Approximate exponential-equation solutions with justified brackets and precision, establishing uniqueness when supported.

## Learning outcome

Choose an exact or numerical method and justify the validity and precision of the resulting solution.

## Proficiency criteria

- Isolate the exponential quantity and check whether its required value is positive.
- Rewrite suitable expressions in a common positive base different from one before equating exponents.
- Verify exact solutions in the original equation.
- Refine numerical brackets to support rounding and justify uniqueness only when established.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common-base equations** | For $b>0$ and $b\ne1$, $b^u=b^v$ if and only if $u=v$ because the exponential is one-to-one. Isolate exponential quantities, rewrite suitable expressions with a common base, and solve the resulting exponent equation. A power $b^u$ with $b>0$ is strictly positive, so a nonpositive target for that power after isolation is impossible. Base $1$ does not permit inference of equal exponents. Candidate solutions must satisfy the original equation. | Solve exponential equations by rewriting both sides with the same positive base different from one and recognize targets excluded by positivity. Justify equating exponents and verify candidate solutions in the original equation. | Check positivity after isolating the exponential quantity. Rewrite powers consistently in a valid common base. Justify equality of exponents by one-to-one behavior, solve the resulting equation, and check candidates in the original equation. |
| **Graphical and numerical solutions** | For an equation $F(x)=G(x)$, intersections have inputs where $F(x)-G(x)=0$. On an interval where this difference is continuous, opposite signs at the endpoints bracket at least one solution. Graphs locate candidates; evaluated outputs and refined brackets support numerical precision. A bracket justifies rounding only when its values round to the same requested result or further analysis resolves the rounding boundary. A strictly monotone exponential compared with a constant has at most one intersection; two monotone functions need not have a unique intersection. | Approximate an exponential-equation solution using a graph or table and verify it against the original outputs. Report a valid bracket, justify numerical precision, and establish uniqueness when the functions’ behavior supports it. | Represent both sides of the original equation and locate candidate intersections. Establish a bracket using opposite signs of a continuous difference and refine it enough to support rounding. Label numerical results as approximate and justify uniqueness only with a valid monotonicity or other argument. |
