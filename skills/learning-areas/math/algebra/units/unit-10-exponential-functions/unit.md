# Unit 10: Exponential functions

Exponential structure, growth and decay, time scales, graph transformations, and equations describe multiplicative change.

## Main learning objectives

- Distinguish exponential functions from power and polynomial functions and evaluate them. Use equal-interval ratios to identify an exponential pattern, recover its per-unit factor, and state the limits of finite data.
- Construct and evaluate percent growth or decay models and interpret their parameters. Determine an exponential formula from two observations and express its integer-step values recursively.
- Sketch exponential parents with bases two, ten, and the natural base, and compare growth with decay. Map transformed exponential graphs and determine their domain, range, intercepts, asymptote, monotonicity, and end behavior.
- Rewrite and construct exponential models for different time units and specified factor periods. Interpret continuous-rate notation and convert it to an effective per-period change.
- Solve common-base exponential equations using one-to-one behavior, identify impossible targets, and verify solutions. Approximate exponential-equation solutions with justified brackets and precision, establishing uniqueness when supported.
- Calculate and interpret exponential average rates of change and distinguish them from percentage change. Compare exponential and polynomial growth across table or graph scales without overstating finite evidence.

## Concepts and topics

Exponential functions versus power functions; Equal-interval ratios; Percent rates and parameters; Constructing a model from points and recursion; Parent graphs for bases 2, 10, and e; Transformed exponential graphs; Equivalent forms and time scales; The constant e and continuous-rate notation; Common-base equations; Graphical and numerical solutions; Average rates of change for exponentials; Comparing exponential and polynomial growth.

## Lessons

- [10.1: Exponential structure](lesson-1-exponential-structure/lesson.md) — Exponential functions place the variable in an exponent. Their values change by a constant multiplicative factor over equal input intervals; the interval length determines which factor is being compared.
- [10.2: Growth, decay, and construction](lesson-2-growth-decay-and-construction/lesson.md) — Construct exponential models from percent change or two observations with positive outputs. Explicit and recursive forms describe the same change when their initial values, input scales, and multiplicative factors agree.
- [10.3: Exponential graphs](lesson-3-exponential-graphs/lesson.md) — Exponential graphs connect multiplicative structure to domain, range, intercepts, and end behavior. Shifts, vertical scales, and reflections determine the corresponding features of transformed graphs.
- [10.4: Time units and the base e](lesson-4-time-units-and-the-base-e/lesson.md) — An exponential factor depends on its input time scale. Equivalent forms connect per-period change, doubling or halving periods, and continuous-rate notation using the natural base.
- [10.5: Exponential equations](lesson-5-exponential-equations/lesson.md) — Solve suitable exponential equations exactly using common bases, and approximate others with graphs or tables. Valid solutions satisfy the original equation, the exponential’s positivity constraints, and the stated precision.
- [10.6: Rates of change and comparisons](lesson-6-rates-of-change-and-comparisons/lesson.md) — Constant exponential ratios do not imply constant additive rates. Average rates and comparisons at different scales distinguish change over a specified interval from eventual growth behavior.
