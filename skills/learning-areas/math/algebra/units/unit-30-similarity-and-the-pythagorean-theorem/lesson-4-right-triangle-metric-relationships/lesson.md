# Lesson 30.4: Right-triangle metric relationships

Similarity among the three triangles formed by a hypotenuse altitude explains geometric means and the Pythagorean theorem.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and use altitude and leg geometric-mean relationships and the Pythagorean theorem.
- Justify and apply the Pythagorean converse, recognize triples, and derive the special right-triangle side ratios.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish the hypotenuse, its projected segments, and the altitude and use positive lengths.
- Give a similarity-based proof and reject rounded equality as proof that an angle is exactly right.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypotenuse altitude and geometric means** | For a right triangle with hypotenuse \(c=p+q\), altitude \(h\) to the hypotenuse, and legs \(a,b\) adjacent to hypotenuse segments \(p,q\), similarity gives \(h^2=pq\), \(a^2=cp\), and \(b^2=cq\). The altitude creates two smaller right triangles similar to the original and to one another. | Prove and apply the hypotenuse-altitude and leg geometric-mean relationships using triangle similarity. | Establish correct vertex correspondence for all three triangles and choose positive square roots when recovering lengths. |
| **Pythagorean theorem and converse** | For a right triangle, adding \(a^2=cp\) and \(b^2=cq\) yields \(a^2+b^2=c^2\). Conversely, positive lengths forming a triangle and satisfying this equation with \(c\) the longest side determine a right triangle, proved by comparison with a constructed right triangle and SSS congruence. Pythagorean triples are positive integer solutions and retain the relationship under uniform scaling. | Prove the Pythagorean theorem by similarity, justify its converse, and use both with exact lengths and Pythagorean triples. | Identify the longest side before using the converse, separate exact from approximate equality, and justify a right-angle conclusion rather than assuming it. |
| **Special right triangles** | Bisecting a square gives a \(45^\circ\! -45^\circ\! -90^\circ\) triangle with side ratio \(1:1:\sqrt2\). Bisecting an equilateral triangle gives a \(30^\circ\! -60^\circ\! -90^\circ\) triangle with side ratio \(1:\sqrt3:2\), in order opposite those angles. Similarity scales these relationships to any positive size. | Derive and apply exact side relationships in the two special right-triangle families. | Use congruence and the Pythagorean theorem in the derivation, match each length to its opposite angle, and preserve exact radical values until an approximation is requested. |
