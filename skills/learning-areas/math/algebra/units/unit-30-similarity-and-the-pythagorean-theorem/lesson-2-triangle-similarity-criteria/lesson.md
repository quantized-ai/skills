# Lesson 30.2: Triangle similarity criteria

Angle and side conditions provide sufficient criteria for establishing triangle similarity.

## Learning objectives

- Establish AA from similarity transformations and justify SAS and SSS similarity.
- Use similarity and congruence criteria to prove geometric relationships and solve for unknown measures.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the correct corresponding order and verify all required ratios or angle conditions.
- Distinguish same-shape conclusions from equal-size conclusions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AA, SAS, and SSS similarity** | AA follows by dilating a corresponding side to equal length and using equal angles to obtain congruence. SAS similarity requires two equal corresponding side ratios and congruent included angles; SSS similarity requires all three corresponding side ratios equal. Each criterion applies to nondegenerate triangles with positive side lengths. | Establish AA using similarity transformations and justify and apply AA, SAS, and SSS triangle-similarity criteria. | Explain the dilation-to-congruence argument, pair sides opposite corresponding angles, and reject SSA or one matching ratio as sufficient evidence. |
| **Similarity and congruence in geometric figures** | Triangles embedded in larger figures can share angles or sides and arise from auxiliary segments. Similarity supplies proportional corresponding lengths; congruence is the special case with scale factor one. A proof must establish the criterion before using its resulting proportions or equalities. | Use triangle congruence and similarity to prove relationships and determine unknown measurements in geometric figures. | Identify the relevant triangles explicitly, justify the chosen criterion, and derive equations only from verified correspondence. |
