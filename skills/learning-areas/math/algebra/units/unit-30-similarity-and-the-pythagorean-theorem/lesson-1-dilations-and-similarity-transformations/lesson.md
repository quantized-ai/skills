# Lesson 30.1: Dilations and similarity transformations

A positive dilation changes distances by one scale factor and combines with rigid motions to define similarity.

## Learning objectives

- Construct dilations about any center, verify their line and length properties, and determine images and pre-images under mixed compositions.
- Use similarity transformations to identify similar figures and distinguish nonuniform stretches.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use the same positive scale factor on every displacement from the center and distinguish lines through that center from other lines.
- Match corresponding parts consistently and reverse the order of operations when recovering a pre-image.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dilation properties** | For center \(C\) and factor \(k>0\), a dilation sends \(P\) to \(P^{\prime}=C+k(P-C)\). It fixes \(C\), multiplies every length by \(k\), preserves angles, leaves each line through \(C\) unchanged as a set, and sends any other line to a parallel line. Factors below one contract; factors above one enlarge. Zero is excluded because it collapses the plane. | Construct dilations about arbitrary centers and verify experimentally their effects on lines, lengths, and angles. | Use coordinate or dynamic measurements to verify both line cases and the length ratio; distinguish points fixed individually from a line mapped onto itself. |
| **Similarity and mixed compositions** | A similarity transformation is a composition of dilations and rigid motions. Similar figures have equal corresponding angles and a common ratio of corresponding lengths; for triangles these conditions are also sufficient. Nonuniform horizontal and vertical scaling generally destroys similarity. Invertible mixed compositions have pre-images obtained by reversing their operations. | Use similarity transformations to decide similarity and determine images and pre-images under compositions of dilations, rigid motions, and nonuniform scalings. | Give a common ratio and complete correspondence, use a center away from the origin when specified, and distinguish a uniform scale factor from separate directional factors. |
