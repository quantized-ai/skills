# Lesson 30.3: Triangle proportionality

Parallel lines and angle bisectors induce proportional partitions of triangle sides.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and apply the triangle proportionality theorem and its converse.
- Derive and apply the internal angle-bisector side-ratio theorem.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use matching side segments or whole sides consistently and establish parallelism only when the converse hypotheses hold.
- Restrict the angle-bisector result to an internal bisector meeting the opposite side.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Triangle proportionality and converse** | A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally, by AA similarity. Conversely, a line meeting two sides in the same ratio is parallel to the third side, using a constructed parallel and uniqueness of the proportional division point. Segment-to-segment and segment-to-whole ratios must not be mixed. | Prove the triangle proportionality theorem and its converse and apply them to lengths and parallelism. | Identify corresponding partitions and justify both directions; distinguish the theorem’s parallel-line hypothesis from the converse’s ratio hypothesis. |
| **Internal angle-bisector theorem** | If the internal bisector of \(\angle A\) meets \(BC\) at \(D\), then \(BD/DC=AB/AC\). An auxiliary parallel line and similar triangles can derive the theorem. The ratio does not imply that \(D\) is the midpoint unless the adjacent sides are equal. | Derive the internal angle-bisector theorem and use it to determine side partitions. | Label adjacent sides and opposite partitions consistently, show the similarity argument, and retain positivity and the requirement that the bisector is internal. |
