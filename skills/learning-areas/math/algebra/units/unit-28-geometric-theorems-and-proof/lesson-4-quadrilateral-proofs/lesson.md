# Lesson 28.4: Quadrilateral proofs

Side, angle, and diagonal conditions organize quadrilateral classifications and proofs.

## Learning objectives

- Prove parallelogram theorems and converse tests.
- Prove and apply sufficient conditions for rectangles, rhombi, and squares.

## Proficiency criteria

- Use necessary and sufficient conditions with their correct hypotheses.
- Avoid treating one special diagonal property as sufficient for every quadrilateral class.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parallelogram properties and tests** | In a parallelogram, opposite sides and opposite angles are congruent, consecutive angles are supplementary, and diagonals bisect each other. Converse tests include both pairs of opposite sides congruent, both pairs of opposite angles congruent, diagonals bisecting each other, or one pair of opposite sides both parallel and congruent. Proofs use congruent triangles and parallel-line angle relationships. | Prove and apply parallelogram properties and sufficient tests involving sides, angles, and diagonals. | State the appropriate two-sided conditions and prove the converse separately from the forward theorem. |
| **Rectangles, rhombi, and squares** | A rectangle is a parallelogram with a right angle; a rhombus is a parallelogram with all sides congruent; a square has both properties. A parallelogram is a rectangle if and only if its diagonals are congruent; it is a rhombus if and only if its diagonals are perpendicular. Rhombus diagonals bisect opposite angles. Diagonal criteria require the parallelogram hypothesis or equivalent additional conditions. | Prove and apply side, angle, and diagonal criteria for rectangles, rhombi, and squares. | Show the parallelogram condition when needed and supply both defining properties for a square; do not infer a rectangle from congruent diagonals alone in an arbitrary quadrilateral. |
