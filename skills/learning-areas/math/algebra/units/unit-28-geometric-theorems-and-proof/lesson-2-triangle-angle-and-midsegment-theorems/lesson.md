# Lesson 28.2: Triangle angle and midsegment theorems

Auxiliary parallel lines and triangle congruence establish core triangle relationships.

## Learning objectives

- Prove and apply triangle angle-sum, exterior-angle, isosceles-triangle, and midsegment theorems.

## Proficiency criteria

- Supply valid auxiliary constructions and distinguish equal-side or midpoint hypotheses from conclusions.
- State exterior-angle and midsegment relationships with the correct nonadjacent angles and scale factor.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Triangle angle sum and exterior angles** | A Euclidean triangle has interior angle sum \(180^\circ\), proved using a line through a vertex parallel to the opposite side. An exterior angle formed by extending one side equals the sum of the two remote interior angles and is supplementary to the adjacent interior angle. | Prove and apply the triangle angle-sum theorem and the exterior-angle theorem. | Use parallel-line angle relationships in the proof, identify the two remote angles, and exclude degenerate triples of collinear vertices. |
| **Isosceles triangles and their converse** | Two congruent sides in a triangle imply congruent opposite angles by triangle congruence, and two congruent angles imply congruent opposite sides. Equilateral triangles therefore have three equal angles, and equiangular triangles have three equal sides. | Prove the isosceles-triangle base-angle theorem and its converse and apply their consequences. | Match each side with its opposite angle and justify both implications with established congruence or angle results. |
| **Triangle midsegments** | A segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length. Congruence with an auxiliary construction, similarity, or a coordinate argument may establish both conclusions; the midpoint conditions are necessary to this form. | Prove and apply both the parallelism and half-length conclusions of the triangle midsegment theorem. | Identify the correct third side and use both midpoint hypotheses; distinguish the midsegment from a median or an arbitrary interior segment. |
