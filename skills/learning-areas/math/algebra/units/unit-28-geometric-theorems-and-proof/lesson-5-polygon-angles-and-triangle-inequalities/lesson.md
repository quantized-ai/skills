# Lesson 28.5: Polygon angles and triangle inequalities

Angle totals and side constraints determine whether polygon and triangle measurements are geometrically possible.

## Learning objectives

- Derive interior- and exterior-angle sums and use regular-polygon relationships.
- Apply triangle inequalities and side-angle ordering with strict existence conditions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish individual angles from totals and use a consistent turning direction for exterior angles.
- Identify impossible and degenerate side data and compare sides only with their opposite angles.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Polygon angle sums** | A simple \(n\)-gon can be triangulated to give interior-angle sum \((n-2)180^\circ\). For a convex polygon, one consistently directed exterior turn at each vertex sums to \(360^\circ\). A regular polygon has equal interior and exterior angles, with each exterior angle \(360^\circ/n\); equal side lengths alone do not give this for every polygon. | Derive and apply polygon interior- and exterior-angle sums and determine the angles of regular polygons. | Justify the triangulation count, separate angle totals from individual angle values, and retain convexity for the unsigned exterior-angle formulation. |
| **Triangle side constraints** | A nondegenerate triangle with positive side lengths \(a,b,c\) exists exactly when each side is less than the sum of the other two; equivalently \(\lvert a-b\rvert<c<a+b\). Equality produces a degenerate figure. In a triangle, larger sides lie opposite larger angles, with equal sides opposite equal angles. | Apply triangle inequalities and side-angle ordering to determine admissible measurements and compare triangle parts. | Use strict inequalities and positive lengths, check all relevant constraints, and distinguish nonexistence from degeneracy. |
