# Lesson 43.1: Sample spaces and event algebra

Events organize uncertain outcomes into sets and connect experimental frequencies with probability models.

## Learning objectives

- Represent sample spaces and compound events and calculate probabilities using valid counts or assigned weights.
- Compare theoretical probabilities with empirical estimates and explain long-run stabilization and finite-sample fluctuation.

- Construct and solve geometric probability models using areas and justify the uniform-location assumption.

## Proficiency criteria

- Specify a complete trial and exhaustive outcomes; distinguish outcomes from events and justify equal likelihood before using favorable over total counts.
- Use compatible events and denominators; distinguish expected long-run behavior from guarantees about individual runs and reject the gambler’s fallacy.

- Identify the sample region and event intersection, compute both areas in compatible units, handle overlap or excluded regions correctly, and distinguish a uniform model from a claim about actual physical selection.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sample spaces and events** | A sample space contains all mutually exclusive outcomes of a defined trial. Events are subsets; intersection means both, union means at least one, and complement means not. A probability model assigns nonnegative probabilities summing to one; equally likely finite outcomes permit counting, while unequal outcomes require weights. | Represent sample spaces and compound events and calculate probabilities using valid counts or assigned weights. | Specify a complete trial and exhaustive outcomes; distinguish outcomes from events and justify equal likelihood before using favorable over total counts. |
| **Theoretical and empirical probability** | Relative frequency estimates a model probability through repeated trials. The law of large numbers concerns stabilization of relative frequencies for independent identically distributed trials; it does not force short-run balancing or monotonic improvement. | Compare theoretical probabilities with empirical estimates and explain long-run stabilization and finite-sample fluctuation. | Use compatible events and denominators; distinguish expected long-run behavior from guarantees about individual runs and reject the gambler’s fallacy. |
| **Geometric probability from area** | A point chosen uniformly from a planar region of finite positive area has event probability $P(E)=\operatorname{area}(E\cap R)/\operatorname{area}(R)$. Polygon, circle, sector, and composite-region areas supply the measures. Uniformity means equal-area subregions have equal chance; area ratios do not apply to a nonuniform location model without additional weighting. | Construct and solve geometric probability models using areas and justify the uniform-location assumption. | Identify the sample region and event intersection, compute both areas in compatible units, handle overlap or excluded regions correctly, and distinguish a uniform model from a claim about actual physical selection. |
