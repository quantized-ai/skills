# Lesson 43.5: Fair allocation and probability-based choices

Probability models support fair allocation and evaluate decisions whose consequences depend on uncertain outcomes.

## Learning objectives

- Design and justify a random allocation mechanism meeting a stated fairness criterion.
- Analyze base-rate effects and compare decision consequences using conditional probabilities and specified costs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Describe a complete procedure that terminates with probability one under its chance model and calculate the resulting probabilities, detecting biased mappings.
- Use the correct conditioning population, include all outcome cases, and state how uncertain rates or costs could change the preferred decision.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Fair random selection** | Fairness is a declared probability criterion, such as equal selection probabilities or proportional allocation. Random digits or numbers require an explicit mapping, with rejection and repetition rules when outcomes are not evenly represented. A rejection procedure must have a positive acceptance chance on independent repetitions; it then terminates with probability one, although no fixed finite number of repetitions is guaranteed. | Design and justify a random allocation mechanism meeting a stated fairness criterion. | Describe a complete procedure that terminates with probability one under its chance model and calculate the resulting probabilities, detecting biased mappings. |
| **Base rates and decision consequences** | A two-way outcome model separates event prevalence from conditional detection and false-positive rates. Conditional probabilities after an observed signal depend on both; competing decisions can be compared through their probability-weighted consequences under explicit assumptions. | Analyze base-rate effects and compare decision consequences using conditional probabilities and specified costs. | Use the correct conditioning population, include all outcome cases, and state how uncertain rates or costs could change the preferred decision. |
