# Lesson 43.3: Conditional probability and independence

Conditioning changes the population of possible outcomes and gives a precise meaning to independence.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and interpret conditional probabilities in two-way tables, trees, Venn diagrams, and area models.
- Determine and explain independence using two-way tables, products, and conditional probabilities and distinguish it from disjointness.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify the conditioning event and denominator; retain units or population descriptions and distinguish reversed conditionals and joint probabilities.
- Verify the appropriate equality, identify zero-probability exceptions to conditional expressions, and explain the meaning in everyday language.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Conditional probability in tables and diagrams** | For $P(B)>0$, $P(A\mid B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)$. Two-way tables, probability trees, Venn diagrams, and area models represent joint, marginal, and conditional probabilities. Empirical tables approximate population probabilities. | Construct and interpret conditional probabilities in two-way tables, trees, Venn diagrams, and area models. | Identify the conditioning event and denominator; retain units or population descriptions and distinguish reversed conditionals and joint probabilities. |
| **Independence and disjointness** | Events are independent exactly when $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. When conditioning probabilities are positive this is equivalent to unchanged conditional probabilities. Two-way-table joint and marginal relative frequencies can be compared using these equalities. Nonzero disjoint events are dependent; sampling without replacement usually changes successive probabilities. | Determine and explain independence using two-way tables, products, and conditional probabilities and distinguish it from disjointness. | Verify the appropriate equality, identify zero-probability exceptions to conditional expressions, and explain the meaning in everyday language. |
