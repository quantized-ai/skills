# Lesson 43.2: Counting arrangements and selections

Counting rules distinguish ordered arrangements from unordered selections and make finite probability calculations reproducible.

## Learning objectives

- Construct organized lists, trees, and products or sums to count restricted finite outcomes.
- Select and justify permutation or combination methods to calculate counts and compound-event probabilities.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify disjoint cases and stage choices, preserve restrictions, and count every permitted outcome exactly once.
- State whether order and replacement matter, handle zero-size selections, and use a denominator with the same outcome convention as the numerator.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Addition and multiplication principles** | Counts add for disjoint alternatives and multiply for a sequence with a fixed number of available choices at each stage. Restrictions and repeated categories can change stage counts; overlapping alternatives require correcting double counts. | Construct organized lists, trees, and products or sums to count restricted finite outcomes. | Identify disjoint cases and stage choices, preserve restrictions, and count every permitted outcome exactly once. |
| **Permutations and combinations** | For integers $0\le r\le n$, ordered selections of distinct objects number $n!/(n-r)!$; unordered selections number $\binom nr=n!/[r!(n-r)!]$, with $0!=1$. Repeated-symbol arrangements divide by multiplicity factorials. Probabilities depend on the sampling and replacement mechanism. | Select and justify permutation or combination methods to calculate counts and compound-event probabilities. | State whether order and replacement matter, handle zero-size selections, and use a denominator with the same outcome convention as the numerator. |
