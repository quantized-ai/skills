# Lesson 12.4: Solving for inverse formulas

An inverse formula recovers the original input from its output. Nonconstant linear and simple rational functions connect algebraic reversal with the exchange of domains and ranges.

## Learning objectives

- Derive inverses of nonconstant linear and one-to-one linear-fractional functions.
- Verify linear reversal and specify domain and range conditions for both families, distinguishing noninvertible constant cases.

## Learning outcome

Solve for an inverse formula and justify its parameter conditions and exchanged input-output sets.

## Proficiency criteria

- Solve the defining equation for the original input and rename variables consistently.
- Reverse linear operations in order and verify the resulting identity.
- For rational formulas, clear denominators on the original domain, then collect and factor the original input.
- Identify nonconstant parameter conditions and exchange excluded inputs and unattainable outputs to state inverse restrictions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Linear inverses and reversal of operations** | For $f(x)=ax+b$ with real $a\ne0$, solving $y=ax+b$ for the original input gives $f^{-1}(x)=(x-b)/a$. Subtraction precedes division because inversion reverses operation order. On the full real domain, both functions have domain and range $\mathbb R$; any explicitly restricted original domain becomes the inverse range. A constant function on more than one input is not one-to-one. | Derive the inverse of a nonconstant linear function algebraically and state the exchanged domain and range. Interpret the reversal of operations and verify algebraically that the inverse recovers the original input. | Require nonzero slope, solve for the original input, and identify the inverse formula. State the domain-range exchange. Explain operation order and prove that applying the inverse after the function returns each allowed input. |
| **Simple rational inverses** | For real coefficients, $f(x)=(ax+b)/(cx+d)$ is nonconstant and one-to-one on its natural domain when $ad-bc\ne0$. Solving gives $f^{-1}(x)=(dx-b)/(a-cx)$. If $c\ne0$, the original domain excludes $-d/c$ and range excludes $a/c$; the inverse exchanges these exclusions. If $c=0$, the determinant condition ensures a nonconstant linear function. With a nonzero denominator polynomial and $ad-bc=0$, the quotient is constant on its domain and has no inverse function there. | Derive the inverse of a nonconstant linear-fractional function and distinguish parameter choices that produce a constant quotient. Determine the original and inverse domains and ranges, exchanging excluded inputs and unattainable outputs. | Use the nonconstant condition, clear the denominator only on its domain, and collect and factor the original input. Derive the inverse with its nonzero-denominator condition. State all four sets and justify each exchanged exclusion. |
