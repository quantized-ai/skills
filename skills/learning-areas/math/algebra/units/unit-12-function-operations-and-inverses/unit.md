# Unit 12: Function operations, composition, and inverses

Function combinations and composition preserve domain conditions; inverse relations require one-to-one behavior and appropriate restrictions.

## Main learning objectives

- Construct, evaluate, and interpret pointwise sums, differences, products, and quotients of functions. Determine combination domains and retain their restrictions after simplification.
- Construct and evaluate compositions from formulas or tables, comparing order and checking contextual units. Determine composition domains from inner existence and outer admissibility, including after simplification.
- Determine and justify whether a function has an inverse function on its stated domain; distinguish inverse and reciprocal notation. Read and graph inverse values by reversing pairs and exchanging complete domain and range sets.
- Derive inverses of nonconstant linear and one-to-one linear-fractional functions. Verify linear reversal and specify domain and range conditions for both families, distinguishing noninvertible constant cases.
- Restrict quadratic domains, justify one-to-one behavior, and derive the corresponding inverse branches and sets. Verify both inverse compositions with explicit domain conditions and diagnose failed claims caused by missing restrictions.
- Derive inverses of simple transformed exponential, logarithmic, square-root, and cubic functions. Verify both compositions, preserve family-specific restrictions, and describe graph features exchanged by reflection.

## Concepts and topics

Sums, differences, and products; Quotients of functions; Composition order and evaluation; Algebraic composition with restrictions; When an inverse is a function; Inverse values from tables and graphs; Linear inverses and reversal of operations; Simple rational inverses; Restricting a quadratic to obtain an inverse; Verifying both compositions on their domains; Exponential and logarithmic inverses; Inverses of square-root and cubic functions.

## Lessons

- [12.1: Arithmetic combinations of functions](lesson-1-arithmetic-combinations-of-functions/lesson.md) — Arithmetic combinations apply function rules to the same input. Their domains retain the original restrictions, with quotients also excluding every zero of the divisor.
- [12.2: Composition and its domain](lesson-2-composition-and-its-domain/lesson.md) — Composition feeds an inner function’s output into an outer function. Its order and domain follow the complete process, including restrictions hidden by a simplified formula.
- [12.3: Inverse relations and one-to-one functions](lesson-3-inverse-relations-and-one-to-one-functions/lesson.md) — An inverse relation reverses input-output pairs. It is an inverse function exactly when the original function is one-to-one on its stated domain; its domain is the original range.
- [12.4: Solving for inverse formulas](lesson-4-solving-for-inverse-formulas/lesson.md) — An inverse formula recovers the original input from its output. Nonconstant linear and simple rational functions connect algebraic reversal with the exchange of domains and ranges.
- [12.5: Restrictions and inverse verification](lesson-5-restrictions-and-inverse-verification/lesson.md) — Restricting a quadratic to one side of its vertex makes it one-to-one. The chosen branch fixes its inverse formula, and both compositions must be verified on their respective domains.
- [12.6: Inverse function families](lesson-6-inverse-function-families/lesson.md) — Exponential and logarithmic functions, square-root and restricted quadratic functions, and cubic and cube-root functions form inverse pairs. Their formulas, domains, ranges, and graph features must agree under reversal.
