# Lesson 5.1: Three forms of a quadratic function

Compare standard, factored, and vertex forms of a quadratic function. Read each form for the attributes it exposes and justify domain, range, and extrema.

## Learning objectives

- Convert between standard and factored forms and interpret attributes across quadratic forms.
- Identify and justify intercepts, vertex, axis, and extremum.
- Express the full real domain and range precisely.

## Learning outcome

The student can choose a useful quadratic form and explain the graph attributes visible in it.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve the leading coefficient when comparing equivalent forms.
- Distinguish real zeros and intercept coordinates from complex factors.
- Use the sign of the squared-term coefficient to justify the vertex as an extremum.
- State the symmetry axis, domain, and range with consistent signs and endpoint notation.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard form and factored form** | A real quadratic has standard form $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$, $a\ne0$, and vertical intercept $(0,c)$. Real factored form $a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$ exists exactly when both roots are real, counting multiplicity; each distinct real root $r$ gives intercept $(r,0)$. Repeated roots give one distinct intercept. Nonreal roots give no real horizontal intercepts. Equivalent forms retain the same leading coefficient; zeros alone leave its nonzero scale undetermined. | Identify the vertical intercept from standard form and real zeros and horizontal intercepts from factored form. Convert between standard and factored forms, preserving the leading coefficient, and explain when real linear factors are unavailable. | Read intercept coordinates and distinguish them from zero values. Convert equivalent forms without changing the leading coefficient. Distinguish repeated real roots, distinct real roots, and nonreal roots when interpreting factors. |
| **Vertex form, axis, and range** | For $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$ with real parameters and $a\ne0$, the vertex is $(h,k)$ and axis is $x=h$. On the full real domain, $a>0$ gives minimum $k$ and range $[k,\infty)$; $a<0$ gives maximum $k$ and range $(-\infty,k]$. Nonnegativity of the square establishes these bounds. Equal input offsets from $h$ give equal outputs. | Identify and justify the vertex, axis, opening, and extremum from vertex form. Express a quadratic function's full real domain and range in interval and inequality notation. | Read the sign of $h$ correctly. Justify the extremum using the squared term and the sign of $a$. State the axis, full real domain, and range consistently in interval and inequality notation. |
