# Lesson 5.6: Quadratic inequalities

Solve quadratic inequalities through sign intervals and square structure. Include cases with two distinct roots, a repeated root, and no real roots.

## Learning objectives

- Solve quadratic inequalities using justified signs from charts, graphs, or square form.
- Handle two-root, repeated-root, and no-real-root cases.
- State complete solution sets with valid endpoints.

## Learning outcome

The student can justify every part of a quadratic-inequality solution set, including degenerate boundary cases.

## Proficiency criteria

- Establish a valid zero-comparison form and determine the actual real boundary roots.
- Justify the sign on each interval or globally through square form.
- Apply strict or nonstrict endpoint rules without multiplying by unknown-sign expressions.
- Recognize all-real, empty, singleton, and punctured sets when appropriate rather than assuming two distinct roots.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quadratic inequalities with two real zeros** | A quadratic inequality is compared with zero after combining all terms on one side, with nonzero quadratic coefficient. Two distinct real zeros split the real line into three intervals of constant sign. Factor signs, test values, or the graph determine those signs; the solution contains whole intervals of the required sign. Zeros are included for nonstrict inequalities and excluded for strict ones. Multiplication or division by an expression of unknown sign is not an unconditional equivalence. | Solve a quadratic inequality with two distinct real boundary roots using a factor sign chart or graph. Justify endpoint inclusion and express the complete solution set in interval notation. | Establish zero-comparison form and find both real boundary roots. Justify the sign on all three intervals using valid inequality operations. Select every qualifying interval and include zeros exactly when equality is allowed. |
| **Repeated-root and no-real-root inequalities** | For a real quadratic with leading coefficient $a\ne0$, a repeated root $h$ gives $a(x-h)^2$: the sign away from $h$ is the sign of $a$, and the value at $h$ is zero. With no real root, the quadratic has the sign of $a$ everywhere. Square form justifies these global signs. Depending on the comparison, solution sets are all real numbers, empty, a singleton, or all real numbers except the repeated root. | Solve quadratic inequalities when the boundary equation has a repeated root or no real root. Justify all-real, empty, singleton, or excluded-point solution sets using square form and strict or nonstrict comparisons. | Determine the global sign from square form and the leading coefficient. Distinguish the repeated-root and no-root cases. Give the complete set and justify whether the repeated root is included; do not assume two distinct boundary points. |
