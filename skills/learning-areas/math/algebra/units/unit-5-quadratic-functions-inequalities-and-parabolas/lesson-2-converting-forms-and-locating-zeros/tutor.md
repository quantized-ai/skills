## Concept guidance

### Converting standard form to vertex form

Curriculum reference: **Converting standard form to vertex form** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Convert $x^2+6x+2$ to vertex form.

**Agent key:** Add and subtract 9: $(x+3)^2-7$, so vertex $(-3,-7)$.

**Worked example:** Convert $2x^2-8x+3$ to vertex form.

**Worked reasoning:** $2[(x-2)^2-4]+3=2(x-2)^2-5$; the compensating constant is multiplied by 2.


#### Teaching sequence

Factor a from the quadratic and linear terms while leaving the outside constant visible. Add and subtract the square of half the normalized linear coefficient inside the brackets. When returning outside, multiply the compensating constant by a. Re-expand to verify every coefficient and interpret the resulting h,k geometrically.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Did you factor the leading coefficient before completing the square?

If half of b is used before dividing by a, normalize the bracket first. If the compensating constant is not multiplied by a, compare constant terms after expansion. If the vertex sign is wrong, set the completed square's inside expression to zero.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Move from monic to nonmonic and negative-leading cases, then missing-constant reverse tasks. Include fractions only after the structural compensation is secure.

#### Assessment evidence

Require equality-preserving completion, correct outside compensation, re-expansion and vertex interpretation. Merely reporting a memorized vertex formula does not establish the requested conversion process.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Zeros and the discriminant on a real graph

Curriculum reference: **Zeros and the discriminant on a real graph** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** How many real zeros has $3x^2+2x+1$?

**Agent key:** Discriminant $4-12=-8$, so none; the real graph does not cross the x-axis.

**Worked example:** Compare $x^2-4x+4$ and $x^2-4x+3$.

**Worked reasoning:** The first has one distinct zero 2 with multiplicity 2; the second has distinct zeros 1 and 3.


#### Teaching sequence

Relate the discriminant sign to the quadratic formula: positive gives two distinct real roots, zero one repeated real root, negative none on the real graph. Connect a repeated root to contact without sign crossing. Keep root count, multiplicity and intercept-point count separate, and verify that the leading coefficient is nonzero before using the classification.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** What is the sign of the discriminant?

If a repeated root is counted as two different intercepts, ask for their coordinates. If a negative discriminant is called a graphing error, compare the vertex's position with its opening. If b² keeps the sign of b, evaluate the square separately.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Compare three discriminant signs, then choose a parameter producing each case. Use a graph description and ask what it permits about the discriminant without estimating roots.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess exact discriminant, distinct-root count, multiplicity and real-graph interpretation. Complex solutions may be mentioned when requested but are not real x-intercepts.
