# Unit 5: Quadratic functions and inequalities

Equivalent quadratic forms connect graph features, construction from attributes or data, and quadratic inequality solution sets.

## Main learning objectives

- Convert between standard and factored forms and interpret attributes across quadratic forms. Identify and justify intercepts, vertex, axis, and extremum.
- Complete the square to expose vertex attributes. Find and interpret real horizontal intercepts.
- Describe and apply quadratic transformations and recognize equivalent graphs. Construct labeled graphs from coordinates and verified attributes.
- Construct a quadratic from a vertex or specified real roots and an additional point. Diagnose sufficient, redundant, contradictory, or degenerate data.
- Construct and solve coefficient equations from three points. Verify the polynomial and its degree, distinguishing exact interpolation from regression.
- Solve quadratic inequalities using justified signs from charts, graphs, or square form. Handle two-root, repeated-root, and no-real-root cases.

## Concepts and topics

Standard form and factored form; Vertex form, axis, and range; Converting standard form to vertex form; Zeros and the discriminant on a real graph; Transformations of a quadratic parent function; Sketching from verified attributes; A vertex and an additional point; Real zeros and an additional point; Constructing a quadratic through three specified points; Uniqueness and degenerate three-point data; Quadratic inequalities with two real zeros; Repeated-root and no-real-root inequalities.

## Lessons

- [5.1: Three forms of a quadratic function](lesson-1-three-forms-of-a-quadratic-function/lesson.md) — Compare standard, factored, and vertex forms of a quadratic function. Read each form for the attributes it exposes and justify domain, range, and extrema.
- [5.2: Converting forms and locating zeros](lesson-2-converting-forms-and-locating-zeros/lesson.md) — Convert standard form into vertex form and connect real zeros with graph intersections. Use square completion and the discriminant as consistent descriptions of the same quadratic.
- [5.3: Transformations and graph construction](lesson-3-transformations-and-graph-construction/lesson.md) — Transform the quadratic parent and construct sketches from verified attributes. Distinguish the meaning of input scaling, output scaling, monotonic intervals, and sign intervals.

- [5.5: Three-point construction](lesson-5-three-point-construction/lesson.md) — Determine a quadratic from three specified points and examine the conditions for uniqueness. Separate genuine quadratic interpolation from collinear, redundant, or inconsistent data.
- [5.6: Quadratic inequalities](lesson-6-quadratic-inequalities/lesson.md) — Solve quadratic inequalities through sign intervals and square structure. Include cases with two distinct roots, a repeated root, and no real roots.
