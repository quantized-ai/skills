# Lesson 50.1: Graph models and connectivity

A graph records relationships among entities without requiring the distances or positions of a coordinate plot.

## Learning objectives

- Construct and interpret graph and directed-graph models and determine vertex degrees.
- Classify network routes and determine connectivity or directed reachability.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define what vertices, edges, directions, and weights mean; count incidences correctly and distinguish network drawings from graphs of functions.
- State the route convention, account for repeated edges or vertices, and identify all components or unreachable vertices relevant to the application.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vertices, edges, degrees, and direction** | A graph consists of vertices and incident edges; a directed graph gives each edge an orientation. Degree counts incident edge ends, with a loop contributing two; directed graphs distinguish indegree and outdegree. Multiple edges, loops, and weights require stated conventions. | Construct and interpret graph and directed-graph models and determine vertex degrees. | Define what vertices, edges, directions, and weights mean; count incidences correctly and distinguish network drawings from graphs of functions. |
| **Walks, paths, circuits, and connected components** | A walk follows incident edges; a trail repeats no edge; a simple path repeats no vertex; a circuit here is a closed trail, while a general closed walk may repeat edges. Connectedness means a path joins every vertex pair in an undirected component; edge direction restricts directed reachability. | Classify network routes and determine connectivity or directed reachability. | State the route convention, account for repeated edges or vertices, and identify all components or unreachable vertices relevant to the application. |
