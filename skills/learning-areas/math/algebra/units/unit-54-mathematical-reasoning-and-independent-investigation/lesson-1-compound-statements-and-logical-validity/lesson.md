# Lesson 54.1: Compound statements and logical validity

Truth conditions distinguish valid inference from agreement with a particular example.

## Learning objectives

- Construct truth tables for compound conditional statements and determine argument validity.
- Formulate, negate, and evaluate quantified mathematical claims using proofs, witnesses, or counterexamples.

## Proficiency criteria

- Enumerate all truth assignments, respect grouping, and test whether any row makes all premises true and the conclusion false rather than relying on familiar wording.
- Retain the domain and quantifier order, verify a counterexample satisfies the hypotheses, and distinguish evidence from universal justification.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Connectives and truth tables** | Negation, conjunction, inclusive disjunction, implication, and biconditional have specified truth conditions. An implication $p\Rightarrow q$ is false only when $p$ is true and $q$ false. Converse and inverse need not be equivalent to the original; the contrapositive is equivalent. | Construct truth tables for compound conditional statements and determine argument validity. | Enumerate all truth assignments, respect grouping, and test whether any row makes all premises true and the conclusion false rather than relying on familiar wording. |
| **Quantifiers and counterexamples** | A universal statement quantifies over a declared domain; one valid counterexample refutes it. An existential statement requires a witness. Negating a quantified statement switches universal and existential quantifiers and negates the predicate. Finite patterns can support a conjecture without proving it universally. | Formulate, negate, and evaluate quantified mathematical claims using proofs, witnesses, or counterexamples. | Retain the domain and quantifier order, verify a counterexample satisfies the hypotheses, and distinguish evidence from universal justification. |
