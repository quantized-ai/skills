# Lesson 14.3: Geometric sequences

Geometric sequences describe constant multiplication per index step. Their rules encode sign and magnitude behavior, zero-term exceptions, and constant percentage change.

## Learning objectives

- Identify a common multiplier and construct geometric rules, accounting for zero terms.
- Explain sign and magnitude behavior from the initial value and ratio.
- Translate geometric forms, model percentage change, and distinguish integer sequences from real-input exponential extensions.

## Learning outcome

Construct, evaluate, and interpret geometric sequences with explicit indexing and valid ratio assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Verify a common multiplier through the recurrence and use quotient tests only where denominators are nonzero.
- Describe sign and magnitude behavior, including constant, alternating, shrinking, growing, and zero cases.
- Produce equivalent explicit and recursive rules with complete initial conditions and integer domains.
- Convert percentage change to a multiplier on the current amount and state when a real-input exponential extension is possible.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Constant ratios and explicit formulas | A geometric sequence satisfies $a_n=ra_{n-1}$ for a fixed real multiplier $r$. For $r\ne0$ and first index $m$, $a_n=a_mr^{n-m}$. For $r=0$, retain the given first term and set all later terms to zero. Quotients identify $r$ only where the preceding term is nonzero; the all-zero sequence admits every multiplier. For nonzero terms, $r<0$ alternates signs, while $\lvert r\rvert<1$, $=1$, or $>1$ determines shrinking, fixed, or growing magnitudes. | Identify geometric sequences and construct explicit rules with correct indexing and zero-term exceptions. Explain term sign and magnitude behavior from the initial value and the ratio, including constant and zero cases. | Check a fixed multiplier and avoid division by zero when using quotient tests; use the correct index offset; handle $r=0$ without evaluating $0^0$ and recognize the all-zero ratio ambiguity; describe sign and magnitude behavior separately. |
| Geometric recursion and percent change | A complete geometric recursion supplies $a_m$ and $a_n=ra_{n-1}$ for the later allowed integer indices. Growth by decimal proportion $p\ge0$ uses $r=1+p$; loss of a proportion $0\le p\le1$ uses $r=1-p$. The multiplier acts on the current amount. A real exponential extension with unrestricted real inputs requires a positive base; negative ratios remain valid for integer-index sequences. | Translate and evaluate geometric rules in recursive and explicit forms with complete indexing, and distinguish sequences from real-input exponential extensions. Construct and interpret constant-percentage-change models using a multiplier on the current amount. | Preserve the initial value, index, ratio, and integer domain across forms and calculate matching term values; distinguish the percentage lost from the proportion retained; interpret each change as a proportion of the current amount; explain why negative-ratio rules do not define the usual real-input exponential function. |
