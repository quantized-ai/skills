# Unit 14: Sequences and finite series

Recursive and explicit descriptions distinguish arithmetic and geometric change; finite-sum formulas support accumulated quantities and deposit models.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret sequence notation and specify the permitted integer indices. Evaluate explicit rules and represent terms as discrete points.
- Construct and verify arithmetic rules from consecutive or separated terms, recognizing the limits of finite evidence. Translate between explicit and recursive forms while preserving the starting index.
- Identify a common multiplier and construct geometric rules, accounting for zero terms. Explain sign and magnitude behavior from the initial value and ratio.
- Select and justify arithmetic or geometric models from contextual and numerical evidence. Account for index gaps, ambiguous model choices, and incomplete evidence.
- Construct, interpret, expand, and evaluate finite sigma notation with inclusive bounds. Distinguish individual terms from partial sums.
- Translate finite proportional-change situations into sums with explicit contributions and endpoints. Evaluate and interpret totals with appropriate units and precision.

## Concepts and topics

Terms and indices; Recursive definitions and initial values; Constant differences and explicit formulas; Arithmetic recursive and explicit representations; Constant ratios and explicit formulas; Geometric recursion and percent change; Differences, ratios, and model selection; Comparative growth; Sigma notation and arithmetic sums; Derivation of finite geometric sums; Totals from repeated proportional change; Repeated deposits and accumulation timing.

## Lessons

- [14.1: Sequence notation and domains](lesson-1-sequence-notation-and-domains/lesson.md) — Sequences are functions on integer indices. Explicit rules and recursive definitions specify term values, indexing conventions, and finite or infinite domains.
- [14.2: Arithmetic sequences](lesson-2-arithmetic-sequences/lesson.md) — Arithmetic sequences describe constant additive change per index step. Explicit and recursive forms encode the same initial value, common difference, and indexing convention.
- [14.3: Geometric sequences](lesson-3-geometric-sequences/lesson.md) — Geometric sequences describe constant multiplication per index step. Their rules encode sign and magnitude behavior, zero-term exceptions, and constant percentage change.
- [14.4: Comparison of sequence models](lesson-4-comparison-of-sequence-models/lesson.md) — Sequence families are selected from change mechanisms and numerical evidence. Comparisons use shared integer inputs and distinguish a crossover in a finite interval from eventual dominance.
- [14.5: Finite sums](lesson-5-finite-sums/lesson.md) — Finite sums combine indexed terms into totals. Arithmetic pairing and geometric cancellation justify formulas while making bounds, term counts, and exceptional ratios explicit.
- [14.6: Finite geometric models](lesson-6-finite-geometric-models/lesson.md) — Finite geometric sums describe repeated proportional contributions and deposit accumulation. The modeled total depends on the included contributions, their timing, and the common valuation time.
