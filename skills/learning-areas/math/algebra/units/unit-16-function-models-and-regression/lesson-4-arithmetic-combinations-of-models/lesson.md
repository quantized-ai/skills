# Lesson 16.4: Arithmetic combinations of models

Arithmetic combinations build total, net, revenue, and average models from simpler functions. The operations are justified by quantity meanings, units, and the domains shared by the components.

## Learning objectives

- Construct sum, difference, product, and quotient models.
- Interpret each component and the combined quantity.
- Preserve mathematical and contextual domain restrictions.

## Learning outcome

The student can combine function models using meaningful arithmetic operations and a correctly restricted domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Choose an operation whose output units and quantity meaning match the modeled goal.
- Use shared input definitions and intersect component domains before simplification.
- Exclude divisor zeros and any additional infeasible counts, prices, or physical values.
- Interpret the combined model’s initial, limiting, total, or average values through its components.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sums and differences of functions** | Pointwise sums and differences combine compatible output quantities at the same input to form total or net models. Their domain is the intersection of component domains, further restricted by context. In $h(t)=c+Ab^t$ with $0<b<1$, the initial value is $c+A$ and the limiting level as $t$ increases without bound is $c$, provided that input extension is contextually meaningful. | Build and evaluate sum or difference models from standard function types with compatible quantities and shared inputs. Interpret component contributions, common domains, and the initial and limiting values of constant-plus-decay models. | Choose addition or subtraction consistent with the quantities and units and construct the combined function. Intersect component domains and apply any further contextual restrictions. Interpret each component and distinguish the initial and limiting levels of a constant-plus-decay model. |
| **Products, quotients, and compatible quantities** | Pointwise products and quotients combine quantities through multiplication or division of their units. Product domains intersect component domains; quotient domains also exclude all divisor zeros, even after cancellation. Context can impose further count, price, or physical restrictions. Products can represent totals and quotients can represent rates or averages. Rate times duration gives accumulated quantity when the rate is constant over that duration or represents its average; a single instantaneous rate is insufficient when the rate varies. | Construct and evaluate product or quotient models whose quantity meanings justify the chosen operation. Determine and interpret combined-model units and domains, preserving original restrictions after simplification. | Justify multiplication or division from quantity meanings and units, including constant or interval-average rate conditions when calculating accumulation. Intersect original domains, exclude divisor zeros, and retain exclusions through simplification. Apply contextual restrictions and interpret calculated totals, rates, or averages in their correct units. |
