## Reasoning and error-analysis activity

**Ask:** A variable rate at the final instant is multiplied by the entire duration to claim total accumulation. Ask the student to locate the first invalid inference, repair the reasoning, and explain a check or counterexample.

**Private reasoning and response:** This works only for a constant or interval-average rate. Ask for the amounts in two intervals with different constant rates; combine those before defining the overall average.

If the student gives only a corrected answer, ask why the original method failed. If the reason is secure, request a different example where the distinction matters. If they remain stuck, use the targeted response below for the relevant concept and mark the attempt assisted.

## Concept teaching plans

### Sums and differences of functions

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** If costs $C(t)$ exist on [0,5] and revenue $R(t)$ on [2,8], where is R−C defined?

**Private diagnostic key:** [2,5].

**Teach in this order:** Align input meanings and units; intersect domains; interpret total versus net; evaluate t=0 separately from long-run behavior.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** In $H(t)=18+50(0.8)^t$, t≥0, H(0)=68 while the limiting level is 18. The decaying term approaches zero; it does not make the total approach zero. Addition is meaningful when both terms share output units.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If 18 is called the initial value, ask for $0.8^0$; next evaluate both summands at zero. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Sum compatible models → net model with different domains → constant-plus-decay initial/limiting interpretation. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Same input; compatible output units; common domain; signs of net quantities; initial versus limiting level and contextual extension. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Choose addition or subtraction consistent with the quantities and units and construct the combined function. Intersect component domains and apply any further contextual restrictions. Interpret each component and distinguish the initial and limiting levels of a constant-plus-decay model.

### Products, quotients, and compatible quantities

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Simplify $(x^2-9)/(x-3)$.

**Private diagnostic key:** x+3, still x≠3.

**Teach in this order:** Track units through multiplication/division; preserve component domains; distinguish a constant/interval-average rate from an instantaneous rate before forming accumulated totals.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** A tank's rate is 2 L/min for 3 min then 6 L/min for 2 min. Total is 2·3+6·2=18 L; average rate is 18/5 L/min. The final instantaneous rate times 5 would give 30 L and is invalid.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If cancellation restores x=3, ask for the original denominator; if rate×time is misused, split the duration where rates are constant. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Product with interpretable units → quotient and excluded inputs → changing-rate counterexample. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Domain intersection; denominator zeros after cancellation; compatible quantity meanings; product/quotient units; constant or average rate assumption. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Justify multiplication or division from quantity meanings and units, including constant or interval-average rate conditions when calculating accumulation. Intersect original domains, exclude divisor zeros, and retain exclusions through simplification. Apply contextual restrictions and interpret calculated totals, rates, or averages in their correct units.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** For $0\le t\le6$, revenue is $R(t)=20t$ credits and cost is $C(t)=30+8t$ credits. Find profit, revenue times cost, and revenue divided by cost, discussing units and domains.

**Private worked key:** $P=R-C=12t-30$ credits. $RC=600t+160t^2$ has squared-credit units and is not profit. $R/C=20t/(30+8t)$ is dimensionless and defined throughout $[0,6]$ because the denominator is positive. Arithmetic combinations use the intersection of the original domains, with denominator exclusions added for quotients.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.

## Additional worked coverage

A temperature model with ambient temperature 20 is $T(t)=20+80(1/2)^t$, t≥0. It starts at 100 and tends to 20, not zero. The temperature excess $T(t)-20=80(1/2)^t$ tends to zero. For constant rate r=3 liters/minute over 4 minutes, rt=12 liters is an accumulation. Multiplying an instantaneous changing rate r(4) by 4 does not generally give the total; a stated interval-average rate can be multiplied by that interval's duration. For $f(x)=x^2-1$ and $g(x)=x-1$ on all reals, $f/g=x+1$ only on x≠1.

Use this reasoning as instruction or private calibration. Generate a fresh independent counterpart after exposure; this is not a fixed reassessment task.
