# Lesson 16.3: Function-family selection

Function families are selected and compared using data patterns, rates, shape, and context. Restricting the input interval can also change which extrema exist and whether bounds are attained.

## Learning objectives

- Select plausible model families from tabular patterns and contextual behavior.
- Compare representations using average rates and key features.
- Determine and compare interval extrema of square-root, reciprocal, cubic, cube-root, absolute-value, exponential, and logarithmic parent functions.

## Learning outcome

The student can justify a model-family choice and compare rates, extrema, and unattained bounds on a valid domain.

## Proficiency criteria

- Analyze equal-step differences or ratios only when the input spacing permits those tests.
- Use context and shape to qualify a family choice rather than inferring a mechanism from finite data alone.
- Compare rates and output levels over compatible intervals and units.
- Check endpoint inclusion, singularities, and interior turning behavior before reporting extrema with their attaining inputs.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Differences, ratios, shape, and context** | For equally spaced inputs, linear data have constant first differences, degree-two polynomial data have constant nonzero second differences, and nonzero positive-base exponential data have constant positive output ratios. Measured patterns are approximate; unequal spacing invalidates these unadjusted tests. Finite data support candidate families without uniquely identifying a mechanism. Graph shape, contextual behavior, and domain limits can support or rule out a candidate. | Select linear, quadratic, or exponential candidates from equally spaced data using appropriate differences or ratios and recognize when those tests do not apply. Justify or reject a candidate using graph shape, contextual mechanisms, domain limits, and the limits of finite-data evidence. | Check input spacing and ratio validity before interpreting first differences, second differences, or output ratios. Identify candidates consistent with exact or approximate patterns and qualify conclusions drawn from finite data. Use contextual behavior and graph shape to justify a family and any necessary domain restriction. |
| **Comparing representations and rates** | Average rate of change on $[a,b]$, with $a<b$, is $[f(b)-f(a)]/(b-a)$, the secant slope in output units per input unit. Compare equations, tables, graphs, and verbal models on compatible intervals and units using rates, output levels, intercepts, turning behavior, and end behavior where represented. An interval average does not establish constant change; finite observations do not determine unobserved behavior. | Calculate and interpret average rates of change over specified intervals with correct units and limits of interpretation. Compare models represented in different forms using rates, output levels, and key graph features supported by each representation. | Calculate secant rates from compatible input-output values and state output-per-input units. Compare rates and supported graph features on compatible domains without confusing initial level, final level, and average change. Distinguish an interval average from constant change and distinguish observed features from unverified behavior. |
| **Extrema on specified intervals** | An absolute extremum is the greatest or least attained output on the restricted domain. The parent families are $\sqrt{x}$, $1/x$, $x^3$, $\sqrt[3]{x}$, $\lvert x\rvert$, $b^x$, and $\log_b x$, with $b>0$ and $b\ne1$. Intersect the specified interval with the function domain; note excluded endpoints and singularities. Use known monotonicity, symmetry, allowed endpoints, and interior turning points to identify extrema without calculus. A finite unattained bound is not an extremum; unbounded behavior provides no finite bound in that direction. | Determine interval maxima and minima of square-root, reciprocal, cubic, cube-root, absolute-value, exponential, and logarithmic parent functions using domain restrictions and graph behavior. Compare extrema with their attaining inputs and distinguish attained values, unattained bounds, and unbounded behavior. | Restrict the interval to allowed inputs and identify excluded endpoints and singularities. Use monotonicity, symmetry, endpoint values, and interior turning points to determine whether extrema exist. State and compare extrema with their attaining inputs; justify any unattained bound or unbounded direction. |
