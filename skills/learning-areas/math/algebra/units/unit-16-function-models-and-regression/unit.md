# Unit 16: Function models and regression

Quantities, constraints, family selection, regression, and residual analysis connect function models to data and defensible predictions.

## Main learning objectives

- Define modeled quantities, units, graph scales, and reporting precision. Rearrange formulas to isolate a specified variable.
- Create, graph, and solve equations from quantitative descriptions. Represent simultaneous constraints with equations or inequalities.
- Select plausible model families from tabular patterns and contextual behavior. Compare representations using average rates and key features.
- Construct sum, difference, product, and quotient models. Interpret each component and the combined quantity.
- Prepare and plot paired data; calculate residuals and squared errors on a stated response scale. Use technology to obtain and verify a linear least-squares fit.
- Fit quadratic and exponential models using technology. Interpret fitted features and parameters within a meaningful domain, including degenerate constant or linear fits.
- Fit the square-root family $\hat y=a+b\sqrt{x}$ through a transformed predictor while retaining the original response scale. Verify predictions and restrictions in the original input variable.
- Calculate predictions and distinguish interpolation from extrapolation. Evaluate prediction reliability and reporting precision using data coverage, residual variation, and model assumptions.

## Concepts and topics

Variable definitions, units, and scales; Rearranging literal formulas; Equations from quantitative relationships; Feasible sets and inequality constraints; Polynomial constraints in contextual models; Differences, ratios, shape, and context; Comparing representations and rates; Extrema on specified intervals; Sums and differences of functions; Products, quotients, and compatible quantities; Data entry, residuals, and least squares; Linear regression and coefficient interpretation; Quadratic fitting; Exponential fitting and fitting scale; Square-root models from tables; Residual patterns and model adequacy; Interpolation, extrapolation, and uncertainty; Model comparison and documented revision.

## Lessons

- [16.1: Quantities and formulas](lesson-1-quantities-and-formulas/lesson.md) — Quantitative modeling begins with precisely defined variables and compatible units. Rearranging formulas then isolates quantities without losing restrictions or introducing unjustified precision.
- [16.2: Equations and constraints](lesson-2-equations-and-constraints/lesson.md) — Equations describe quantitative relationships, while simultaneous inequalities describe feasible choices. Solving either requires the model’s units, integer conditions, and contextual limits to remain attached.
- [16.3: Function-family selection](lesson-3-function-family-selection/lesson.md) — Function families are selected and compared using data patterns, rates, shape, and context. Restricting the input interval can also change which extrema exist and whether bounds are attained.
- [16.4: Arithmetic combinations of models](lesson-4-arithmetic-combinations-of-models/lesson.md) — Arithmetic combinations build total, net, revenue, and average models from simpler functions. The operations are justified by quantity meanings, units, and the domains shared by the components.
- [16.5: Regression foundations and linear fitting](lesson-5-regression-foundations-and-linear-fitting/lesson.md) — Regression fits a chosen family to paired observations rather than forcing every point onto the graph. Residuals, squared errors, and coefficient checks make a linear fit reproducible and interpretable.
- [16.6: Quadratic and exponential regression](lesson-6-quadratic-and-exponential-regression/lesson.md) — Quadratic and exponential regression extend model fitting to curvature and proportional change. The fitting method and response scale must be recorded so predictions and residuals are interpreted correctly.
- [16.7: Square-root fitting and residual analysis](lesson-7-square-root-fitting-and-residual-analysis/lesson.md) — A transformed square-root predictor permits a specific radical family to be fitted by linear regression. Residual patterns provide evidence of systematic mismatch, changing uncertainty, or observations that need investigation.
- [16.8: Prediction and model revision](lesson-8-prediction-and-model-revision/lesson.md) — Predictions depend on where data were observed and whether a model’s assumptions remain credible. Competing models are compared with new evidence and revised through a documented modeling process.
