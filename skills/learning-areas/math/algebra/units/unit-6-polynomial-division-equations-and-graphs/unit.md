# Unit 6: Polynomial division, equations, and graphs

Division, zeros, multiplicities, graph behavior, inequalities, and binomial expansion extend polynomial analysis beyond quadratics.

## Main learning objectives

- Interpret polynomial quotient and remainder. Divide linear through quartic polynomials by linear divisors.
- Divide quadratic, cubic, and quartic polynomials by quadratic divisors. Use and verify synthetic division for monic linear divisors.
- Prove and apply the Remainder Theorem. Justify both directions of the Factor Theorem.
- Generate and test possible rational roots. Reduce suitable cubic and quartic equations to linear and quadratic factors through division or factorization.
- Determine root multiplicities and local crossing behavior. Apply the complex root count with multiplicity.
- Justify conjugate roots and equal multiplicities for real-coefficient polynomials. Form real quadratic factors from conjugate pairs.
- Determine both tails from degree and leading coefficient. Find positive and negative intervals from factor signs.
- Sketch polynomial graphs from verified algebraic features. Check graph consistency with zeros, multiplicities, signs, and tails.
- Generate and explain binomial coefficients. Expand nonnegative-integer binomial powers.

## Concepts and topics

Quotient, remainder, and division identity; Long division by a linear polynomial; Long division by a quadratic polynomial; Synthetic division by $x-c$; The Remainder Theorem; The Factor Theorem; The Rational Root Theorem as a search method; Solving cubic and quartic equations by degree reduction; Multiplicity of zeros; The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra; Conjugate roots of real-coefficient polynomials; Constructing a polynomial from zeros and scale; End behavior from degree and leading coefficient; Positive and negative intervals of polynomials; Sketching a polynomial graph from factors; Transformations of the cubic parent; Pascal's triangle and binomial coefficients; The Binomial Theorem and selected coefficients.

## Lessons

- [6.1: The division algorithm and linear long division](lesson-1-the-division-algorithm-and-linear-long-division/lesson.md) — State polynomial division as an identity with a controlled remainder, then carry out long division by a linear polynomial. Distinguish an everywhere-valid identity from a quotient expression with excluded inputs.
- [6.2: Quadratic divisors and synthetic division](lesson-2-quadratic-divisors-and-synthetic-division/lesson.md) — Extend long division to quadratic divisors and use synthetic division where its assumptions apply. Interpret each algorithm’s output through the same division identity.
- [6.3: Remainders and factors](lesson-3-remainders-and-factors/lesson.md) — Derive the remainder and factor theorems from the division identity. Use evaluation to test a factor or impose a condition on an unknown coefficient.
- [6.4: Finding and solving higher-degree factors](lesson-4-finding-and-solving-higher-degree-factors/lesson.md) — Search for rational zeros systematically and use given or discovered factors to reduce cubic and quartic equations to linear and quadratic factors. Find every root of the reduced factors in the requested number system.
- [6.5: Multiplicity and the Fundamental Theorem](lesson-5-multiplicity-and-the-fundamental-theorem/lesson.md) — Interpret repeated factors as root multiplicities and connect parity to local graph behavior. State the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra and demonstrate its real-coefficient quadratic case.
- [6.6: Conjugate roots and polynomial construction](lesson-6-conjugate-roots-and-polynomial-construction/lesson.md) — Use conjugation to identify required partner roots and construct real polynomial factors. Build least-degree polynomials from zeros, multiplicities, and enough information to determine scale.
- [6.7: End behavior and sign intervals](lesson-7-end-behavior-and-sign-intervals/lesson.md) — Describe a polynomial’s distant tails from its leading term and its interval signs from real zeros. Combine these ideas without confusing eventual behavior with behavior near the middle of the graph.
- [6.8: Graph synthesis and cubic transformations](lesson-8-graph-synthesis-and-cubic-transformations/lesson.md) — Synthesize polynomial graph features from factors and analyze transformed cubic functions. Use structural evidence to draw sketches without inventing exact turning-point information.
- [6.9: Binomial coefficients and expansion](lesson-9-binomial-coefficients-and-expansion/lesson.md) — Generate binomial coefficients through Pascal’s triangle and connect them to repeated multiplication. Use the Binomial Theorem for complete expansions and targeted coefficient extraction.
