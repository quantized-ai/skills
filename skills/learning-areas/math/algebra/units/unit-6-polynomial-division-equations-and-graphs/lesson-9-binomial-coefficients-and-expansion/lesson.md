# Lesson 6.9: Binomial coefficients and expansion

Generate binomial coefficients through Pascal’s triangle and connect them to repeated multiplication. Use the Binomial Theorem for complete expansions and targeted coefficient extraction.

## Learning objectives

- Generate and explain binomial coefficients.
- Expand nonnegative-integer binomial powers.
- Find selected coefficients from the general term.

## Learning outcome

The student can justify the coefficient pattern and apply it accurately to signed and scaled binomials.

## Proficiency criteria

- Build coefficient rows with correct boundaries, adjacency, and term positions.
- Explain the two multiplication contributions behind an interior coefficient.
- Match coefficients to complementary powers of the complete signed or scaled binomial terms.
- Identify and simplify all contributions to a requested power, keeping the theorem within its nonnegative-integer scope.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pascal's triangle and binomial coefficients** | Pascal’s triangle starts with row $0$ equal to $1$; row $n$ contains $n+1$ entries, boundary ones, and interior sums of adjacent entries above. The coefficient $\binom nk$ counts choices of $k$ second terms among $n$ binomial factors. This choice interpretation explains symmetry; multiplication by one more binomial explains the adjacent-sum recurrence. | Generate binomial coefficients using Pascal’s triangle and identify their positions and symmetry through the choice interpretation. Explain the adjacent-sum pattern from multiplying by a new binomial factor. | Generate complete rows with correct boundaries and positions. Explain each interior coefficient as the sum of two multiplication contributions. Relate positions and symmetry to choices of terms from the factors. |
| **The Binomial Theorem and selected coefficients** | For integers $n\ge0$, $(A+B)^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nk A^{n-k}B^k$. Powers of the complete terms are complementary and sum to $n$; signs and scale factors must be raised to those powers. For monomial components, match the resulting variable exponent to locate contributing terms for a selected coefficient. Noninteger powers and infinite binomial series are outside this finite theorem. | Apply the Binomial Theorem for nonnegative integer powers, correctly expanding signed and scaled components and simplifying the terms. Find a specified coefficient from the general term without full expansion, justifying the variable-exponent pattern and combining contributions if needed. | Use the theorem within its nonnegative-integer scope. Pair each binomial coefficient with complementary powers of the complete signed or scaled terms. Identify and simplify all terms contributing to a requested variable power without unnecessary full expansion. |
