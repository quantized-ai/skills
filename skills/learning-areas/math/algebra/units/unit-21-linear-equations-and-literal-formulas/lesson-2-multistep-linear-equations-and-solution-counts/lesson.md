# Lesson 21.2: Multistep linear equations and solution counts

Distribution and collection of like terms reduce linear equations to forms that reveal both solutions and exceptional cases.

## Learning objectives

- Solve linear equations with variables on both sides and grouped terms.
- Classify unique, inconsistent, and identity equations by their reduced form.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve all signed coefficients while simplifying and justify every equality transformation.
- Use the resulting constant statement to distinguish no solution from every value in the domain.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Variables on both sides** | A linear equation in one real variable can be reduced through distribution and like-term collection to \(ax+b=cx+d\). Moving variable and constant terms means adding their opposites to both sides. If \(a-c\ne0\), the solution is \((d-b)/(a-c)\). Rearrangement does not require variables to occur initially on only one side. | Solve multistep linear equations involving distribution and variables on both sides and explain the sequence of equivalent equations. | Distribute over every term, retain signs, combine only like terms, and verify the final solution in the unsimplified original equation. |
| **Identity and contradiction cases** | After collecting terms, \(Ax=B\) has one solution when \(A\ne0\), no solution when \(A=0\) and \(B\ne0\), and every permitted value as a solution when \(A=B=0\). A variable disappearing is not itself evidence of no solution; the remaining equality determines the case. | Classify linear equations as having one solution, no solution, or every permitted value as a solution using their reduced forms. | State the complete solution set, interpret true and false constant equations correctly, and never divide by a coefficient that has become zero. |
