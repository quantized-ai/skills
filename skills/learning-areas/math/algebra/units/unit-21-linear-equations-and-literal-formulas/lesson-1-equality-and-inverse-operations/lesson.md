# Lesson 21.1: Equality and inverse operations

An equation specifies equal values, and solving identifies all values that make the original statement true.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret and verify solutions to equations.
- Solve one-step and two-step equations using justified inverse operations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Preserve equality by applying valid operations to both sides.
- Check candidates in the original equation and distinguish an equation from its solution set.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Equations and solution sets** | An equation asserts equality of two expressions for certain inputs in a stated domain. A solution makes both sides defined and equal. Solving means determining the entire solution set, which may contain one, several, no, or infinitely many values. Substitution verifies a candidate but does not alone prove that all solutions have been found. | Determine whether a value solves a stated equation and distinguish an equation, an identity, and its solution set. | Evaluate both original sides, reject undefined substitutions, and state whether a verification concerns one candidate or the complete set. |
| **Inverse operations and equivalence** | Adding or subtracting the same defined quantity on both sides preserves equality. Multiplication or division by a known nonzero constant is reversible and preserves the solution set. These operations isolate a variable in one-step and two-step linear equations; multiplication by zero is not reversible. | Solve one-step and two-step linear equations with rational coefficients and justify each solution-preserving operation. | Apply operations to complete sides, distinguish nonzero division from forbidden division, and verify that the resulting value satisfies the original equation. |
