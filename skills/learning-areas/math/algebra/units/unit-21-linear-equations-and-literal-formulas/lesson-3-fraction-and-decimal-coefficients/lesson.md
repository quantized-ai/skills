# Lesson 21.3: Fraction and decimal coefficients

Clearing numerical denominators or decimal places changes a linear equation to a convenient equivalent equation.

## Learning objectives

- Solve linear equations with fraction, mixed-number, and decimal coefficients.
- Use common nonzero scaling to maintain exact equivalence.

## Proficiency criteria

- Multiply every term on both sides by the same nonzero scale factor.
- Distinguish exact decimal data from approximate measurements when reporting solutions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clearing numerical denominators** | For an equation with fixed nonzero numerical denominators, multiplying both sides by a common multiple of all denominators produces an equivalent equation. The multiplier applies to every term, including integer terms and grouped numerators. A fractional coefficient differs from an expression with a variable denominator. | Solve linear equations with rational coefficients using a common-denominator transformation and exact arithmetic. | Identify every denominator, apply the multiplier across complete sides, retain signs and grouping, and verify the exact result in the fractional equation. |
| **Decimal scaling and exactness** | Terminating decimal coefficients are rational numbers and can be cleared by multiplying all terms by one sufficiently large power of ten. Exact coefficient data support exact solutions; measured coefficients may require rounded interpretations even when the algebraic calculation is exact for the stated inputs. | Solve linear equations with decimal coefficients and report solutions with precision consistent with exact or measured inputs. | Scale both sides uniformly, avoid premature rounding, and check the result and its contextual precision in the original equation. |
