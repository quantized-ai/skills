# Lesson 21.5: Linear equation models

Linear modeling turns relationships among defined quantities into solvable equations and tests the resulting values against the situation.

## Learning objectives

- Formulate and solve linear equations for totals, rates, mixtures, and motion.
- Interpret solutions with units, domain restrictions, and explicit model assumptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Define the unknown and represent all stated relationships with compatible units.
- Check the algebraic result in the original context and reject infeasible values.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Additive and weighted totals** | Linear totals combine constant and variable contributions. Weighted sums include cost as price times quantity and pure material as concentration times amount; conservation requires compatible units and a stated total. A one-variable model expresses related quantities in terms of the same unknown before equating totals. | Construct and solve one-variable linear models for additive totals, cost, and constant-concentration mixtures. | Define the unknown and all dependent quantities, distinguish a concentration from an amount, and verify conservation and nonnegative contextual bounds. |
| **Constant-rate and motion relationships** | At constant rate, amount equals rate times duration; for motion, \(d=rt\) with compatible distance and time units. Shared or different starting times determine elapsed durations. A signed velocity records direction, whereas speed is a nonnegative magnitude; linear models assume rates are constant on the modeled interval. | Formulate and solve linear constant-rate and motion relationships and interpret their assumptions and solutions. | Use consistent durations and units, distinguish accumulated distance from directed position when needed, and check whether the solution lies in the modeled time interval. |
