# Private calibration: Unit 21: Linear equations and literal formulas

These original prompts and checked reasoning anchors calibrate mathematical accuracy. They are **not a fixed student quiz** and are not a complete assessment blueprint. They also serve as worked examples in the paired tutors, so exposure makes them unsuitable for independent reassessment. Use [fresh-question generation](question-generation.md) and the curriculum coverage in each tutor. Break composite prompts into manageable turns. Equivalent justified solutions are valid.

## Lesson 21.1

[Curriculum](lesson-1-equality-and-inverse-operations/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-1-equality-and-inverse-operations/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Solve $x/4-3=2$ over the reals and check it. Explain why squaring both sides of $x=-2$ does not give an equivalent equation by itself.

**Checked reasoning:** Add 3 and multiply by nonzero 4 to obtain $x=20$; substitution gives $5-3=2$. Squaring yields $x^2=4$, which also admits 2; it is a consequence but not an equivalent solution set.

**Coverage limit:** Include membership checks, balance explanations, equations with zero/no/all solutions and operations that lose or add candidates; distinguish an expression from an equation.

## Lesson 21.2

[Curriculum](lesson-2-multistep-linear-equations-and-solution-counts/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-2-multistep-linear-equations-and-solution-counts/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Solve $3(2x-1)=4x+9$, $2(x+3)=2x+6$, and $2(x+3)=2x+7$.

**Checked reasoning:** First gives $6x-3=4x+9$, hence $x=6$ and both sides 33. Second reduces to $6=6$, all real numbers. Third reduces to $6=7$, no solutions. A cancelled variable does not automatically mean no solution.

**Coverage limit:** Generate unique, identity and contradiction cases deliberately; include negative distribution and solutions that are noninteger.

## Lesson 21.3

[Curriculum](lesson-3-fraction-and-decimal-coefficients/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-3-fraction-and-decimal-coefficients/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Solve $x/3-1/2=5/6$ and $0.4x+0.15=1.35$.

**Checked reasoning:** Multiply every term of the first equation by 6: $2x-3=5$, so $x=4$. Multiply the second by 100: $40x+15=135$, so $x=3$. Original substitutions verify both. The terminating decimals are exact as stated, not rounded measurements.

**Coverage limit:** Mix signed fractions and decimals, keep exact arithmetic until final rounding, and include models whose decimal coefficients are measured so a numerical tolerance is appropriate.

## Lesson 21.4

[Curriculum](lesson-4-literal-equations-and-parameter-cases/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-4-literal-equations-and-parameter-cases/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Isolate $x$ in $p=ax+bx$ and classify solutions of $ax=b$ for all real parameters.

**Checked reasoning:** $p=(a+b)x$, so $x=p/(a+b)$ when $a+b\ne0$. If $a+b=0$, all real x when p=0 and none otherwise. For $ax=b$, one solution $b/a$ if $a\ne0$; all reals if a=b=0; none if a=0,b≠0.

**Coverage limit:** Vary which variable is isolated, include parameter coefficients on both sides, and return excluded cases to the original equation before classifying.

## Lesson 21.5

[Curriculum](lesson-5-linear-equation-models/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-5-linear-equation-models/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Mix 2 liters at 30% concentration with x liters at 10% to obtain 20%. Then compare trips of 120 km at 40 and 60 km/h.

**Checked reasoning:** Solute balance $0.30(2)+0.10x=0.20(2+x)$ gives x=2 liters; verify 0.8 liters solute in 4 liters total. Travel times are 3 and 2 hours; equal-distance average speed for the combined trip is $240/5=48$ km/h, not the arithmetic average 50.

**Coverage limit:** Include age/consecutive-number totals, weighted mixtures, shared work and motion with declared rate assumptions; reject negative times or counts even when algebra permits them.
