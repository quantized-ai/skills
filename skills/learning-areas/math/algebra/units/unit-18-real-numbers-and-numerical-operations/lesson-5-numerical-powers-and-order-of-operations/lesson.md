# Lesson 18.5: Numerical powers and order of operations

Exponential notation and grouping conventions make numerical expressions unambiguous.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret nonnegative and negative integer exponents with their restrictions.
- Evaluate numerical expressions in conventional order and justify transformations.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish a negative base from a leading negative sign and exclude zero bases where required.
- Respect grouping, exponentiation, and equal-precedence left-to-right operations.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Integer powers** | For positive integer \(n\), \(a^n\) is a product of \(n\) equal factors. For \(a\ne0\), \(a^0=1\) and \(a^{-n}=1/a^n\). Product, quotient, and power rules follow from repeated multiplication and inverse relationships, with nonzero bases required for negative exponents and division. \(0^0\) is not assigned a value here. | Evaluate integer powers and rewrite numerical products, quotients, and powers using exponent laws under valid base restrictions. | Track signs for negative bases, distinguish \((-a)^n\) from \(-a^n\), and retain all exclusions for zero bases or denominators. |
| **Grouping and conventional operation order** | Parentheses and fraction bars group expressions. Exponentiation precedes multiplication and division, which have equal precedence and are processed left to right; addition and subtraction then have equal precedence and are processed left to right. Absolute-value bars enclose a complete expression before magnitude is taken. | Write and evaluate numerical expressions containing grouping, integer exponents, absolute values, and all four arithmetic operations. | Preserve the scope of fraction bars, grouping, and signs; do not impose a false multiplication-before-division or addition-before-subtraction rule. |
