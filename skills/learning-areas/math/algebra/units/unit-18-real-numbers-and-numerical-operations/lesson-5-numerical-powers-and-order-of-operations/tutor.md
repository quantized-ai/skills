## Reasoning and error-analysis activity

**Ask:** A student writes −3²=9 and 18÷3×2=3. Ask the student to locate the first invalid inference, repair the reasoning, and explain a check or counterexample.

**Private reasoning and response:** −3²=−9 because exponent acts before unary minus; equal-precedence division/multiplication gives 12 left to right. Require one legal operation per step.

If the student gives only a corrected answer, ask why the original method failed. If the reason is secure, request a different example where the distinction matters. If they remain stuck, use the targeted response below for the relevant concept and mark the attempt assisted.

## Concept teaching plans

### Integer powers

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Evaluate −2² and (−2)².

**Private diagnostic key:** −4 and 4.

**Teach in this order:** Identify base/exponent; expand positive integer powers; extend quotient consistency to zero/negative exponents; record exclusions.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** $3^{-2}=1/3^2=1/9$ because $3^2·3^{-2}=3^0=1$. For nonzero a, a⁰=1; zero to a negative power is undefined. Parentheses determine whether the sign is part of the base.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If a negative exponent means a negative result, ask what reciprocal cancels a positive power; next multiply the candidate by the positive power. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Positive powers → signs and parentheses → zero/negative exponents with exclusions. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Base identification; integer exponent laws; a≠0 restrictions; parity of negative bases; exact reciprocals. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Track signs for negative bases, distinguish \((-a)^n\) from \(-a^n\), and retain all exclusions for zero bases or denominators.

### Grouping and conventional operation order

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Evaluate 18÷3×2.

**Private diagnostic key:** 12, working left to right at equal precedence.

**Teach in this order:** Annotate the expression tree/grouping; execute one legal operation at a time; compare ambiguous inline writing with explicit parentheses.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** $4+2(7−3)^2/8=4+2·16/8=8$. Grouping first, then powers, then multiplication/division left to right, then addition. A fraction bar groups the entire numerator and denominator.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If multiplication always precedes division, ask which operations share priority; next rewrite 18÷3×2 as(18÷3)×2. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Grouping → equal-precedence operations → nested fractions/powers and error analysis. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Conventional order; grouping bars; unary minus; left-to-right ties; readable equivalent notation. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Preserve the scope of fraction bars, grouping, and signs; do not impose a false multiplication-before-division or addition-before-subtraction rule.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** Evaluate $-3^2$, $(-3)^2$, $2^{-3}$ and $18\div3\cdot2+4(5-3)$.

**Private worked key:** Values are $-9,9,1/8,20$. Exponentiation precedes an ungrouped negative; multiplication and division have equal precedence and proceed left to right: $18/3\cdot2=12$, then add 8.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.
