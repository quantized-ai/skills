# Lesson 18.4: Decimals and rational representations

Decimal place value and rational equivalence allow exact computation and informed use of finite displays.

## Learning objectives

- Compute with decimal representations and convert among decimal and fraction forms.
- Distinguish exact terminating or repeating decimals from rounded displays.

## Proficiency criteria

- Align place values correctly and explain how decimal scaling preserves a quotient.
- Retain repeating structure in exact conversions and label approximations explicitly.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Decimal place value and arithmetic** | Decimal digits represent integer powers of ten. Addition and subtraction align equal place values; multiplication accounts for combined decimal scales; division scales dividend and nonzero divisor by the same power of ten before division. These operations apply to positive and negative terminating decimals. | Compute decimal sums, differences, products, and quotients and justify the placement of decimal points. | Use place-value reasoning or equivalent fractions to account for the scale, apply signed-number rules, and distinguish terminating from nonterminating quotients. |
| **Terminating and repeating decimals** | Long division converts an integer quotient to a terminating or eventually repeating decimal. Repetition occurs when a nonzero remainder recurs. A terminating decimal is a fraction over a power of ten; an eventually repeating decimal can be converted to a fraction by aligning repetitions with powers of ten and subtracting. An overbar specifies the full repeating block. | Convert rational numbers between fraction, terminating-decimal, and eventually repeating-decimal forms and explain why repetition occurs. | Identify the correct repeating block, distinguish nonrepeating initial digits, obtain an exact equivalent fraction, and use approximate notation for truncated displays. |
