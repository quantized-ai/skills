# Lesson 18.7: Rational and irrational arithmetic

Number-system structure explains which sums and products must remain rational and which must be irrational.

## Learning objectives

- Prove closure of rational numbers under addition and multiplication.
- Justify mixed rational–irrational sum and product claims and identify their exceptions.

## Proficiency criteria

- Represent rational inputs by integer quotients with nonzero denominators and retain those conditions.
- Use contradiction for mixed operations and distinguish a guaranteed result from cases that may vary.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rational closure** | For rational numbers \(a/b\) and \(c/d\), with integer numerators and nonzero integer denominators, their sum is \((ad+bc)/(bd)\) and product is \(ac/(bd)\). Integer closure and \(bd\ne0\) establish rationality. Differences and nonzero quotients similarly remain rational; division by zero remains undefined. | Prove that sums and products of rational numbers are rational using their integer-quotient representations. | Show integer numerators and denominators, justify why the denominator is nonzero, and give a general argument rather than isolated numerical confirmations. |
| **Mixed rational and irrational operations** | If \(r\) is rational and \(u\) irrational, then \(r+u\) is irrational: otherwise subtracting \(r\) would make \(u\) rational. If \(r\ne0\), then \(ru\) is irrational: otherwise division by \(r\) would make \(u\) rational. The nonzero condition is essential because \(0u=0\). Two irrational inputs may have a rational or irrational sum or product, so neither result is guaranteed merely by their classifications. | Justify rational–irrational sum and nonzero-product classifications and use counterexamples to delimit claims about zero factors or two irrational inputs. | State the rationality assumption being contradicted, use a valid inverse operation, preserve the nonzero-factor condition, and distinguish must-be-irrational statements from cases that vary. |
