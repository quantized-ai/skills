# Lesson 18.2: Signed addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division

Operations on signed numbers extend whole-number arithmetic while preserving inverse relationships and the properties of operations.

## Learning objectives

- Compute and interpret the four operations on signed integers and rational numbers.
- Explain sign rules and distinguish valid division from division by zero.

## Proficiency criteria

- Track direction and magnitude in sums and differences, and justify signs in products and quotients.
- Use inverse operations to verify results and identify undefined quotients.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Addition and subtraction of signed numbers** | Addition combines directed changes on the number line; \(a+(-a)=0\). Subtraction is addition of the additive inverse: \(a-b=a+(-b)\). These rules apply to integers, fractions, and terminating decimals; the sign of a sum depends on both signs and magnitudes. | Compute signed sums and differences and interpret them as directed changes and net quantities. | Distinguish a negative sign from subtraction, apply the additive inverse to the entire subtracted quantity, and reconcile the sign and magnitude with a number-line or contextual interpretation. |
| **Multiplication and division of signed numbers** | Products and quotients of two nonzero numbers are positive for matching signs and negative for unlike signs. The rules follow from distributivity and inverse operations. Zero times a real number is zero; \(0/a=0\) for \(a\ne0\), whereas division by zero is undefined. Division by a nonzero number is multiplication by its reciprocal. | Compute signed products and quotients, justify the sign rules using operation properties, and identify zero-division exclusions. | Apply the sign rule to every factor, distinguish zero dividends from zero divisors, and verify quotients by multiplication. |
