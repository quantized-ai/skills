# Lesson 18.6: Scientific notation, estimation, and precision

Powers of ten and controlled approximation make very large, very small, and measured quantities interpretable.

## Learning objectives

- Represent and compute with scientific notation and compare orders of magnitude.
- Estimate, round, and assess numerical results with precision appropriate to the information.

## Proficiency criteria

- Normalize coefficients, distinguish exponent notation in technology, and check scale and units.
- Keep exact values until approximation is needed and state the rounding place or precision.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scientific notation and powers of ten** | A nonzero real number has normalized scientific form \(a\times10^n\), where \(1\le\lvert a\rvert<10\) and \(n\) is an integer. Multiplication and division combine powers; addition and subtraction require a common power before coefficients are combined. Zero is represented separately. Calculator E notation denotes a power of ten rather than Euler’s number. | Convert, compare, and compute quantities in ordinary and scientific notation, including technology-generated forms. | Normalize the final coefficient, preserve signs, use matching powers for addition, and justify magnitude comparisons through ratios or exponent differences. |
| **Estimation, rounding, and measured precision** | Estimation uses nearby compatible values or magnitude bounds to check plausibility. Rounding to a stated place gives an approximation whose absolute rounding error is at most half a unit in that place under nearest-value rounding. Measured quantities carry limited accuracy; reporting extra calculator digits does not add information. | Estimate numerical results, round to a justified precision, and distinguish exact values from measurements and approximations. | State the rounding convention and units, retain sufficient intermediate precision, and reject results that conflict with bounds or contextual scale. |
