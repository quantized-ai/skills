# Private calibration: Unit 18: Real numbers and numerical operations

These original prompts and checked reasoning anchors calibrate mathematical accuracy. They are **not a fixed student quiz** and are not a complete assessment blueprint. They also serve as worked examples in the paired tutors, so exposure makes them unsuitable for independent reassessment. Use [fresh-question generation](question-generation.md) and the curriculum coverage in each tutor. Break composite prompts into manageable turns. Equivalent justified solutions are valid.

## Lesson 18.1

[Curriculum](lesson-1-number-sets-order-and-distance/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-1-number-sets-order-and-distance/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Classify $-3$, $0.125$, $\sqrt2$, and $\sqrt9$ in the real number system; order $-1.4,-7/5,-\sqrt2$ and find the distance from -3 to 5.

**Checked reasoning:** $-3$ is integer and rational; $0.125=1/8$ is rational; $\sqrt2$ is irrational; $\sqrt9=3$ is natural, whole, integer and rational. $-\sqrt2<-1.4=-7/5$. Distance is $|5-(-3)|=8$. State the adopted natural-number convention if zero is involved.

**Coverage limit:** Mix overlapping classifications, equivalent representations, negative order and distance; distinguish an exact irrational value from a rounded decimal.

## Lesson 18.2

[Curriculum](lesson-2-signed-addition-subtraction-multiplication-and-division/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-2-signed-addition-subtraction-multiplication-and-division/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Compute $-7-(-3)$, $(-4)(-6)$, $18/(-3)$ and $0/5$. Explain why $5/0$ is different.

**Checked reasoning:** Results are $-4,24,-6,0$. Subtracting -3 adds 3. A negative product factor twice reverses sign. $5/0$ has no real value because no real number multiplied by zero is 5. Also $0/0$ is undefined; it does not specify a unique quotient.

**Coverage limit:** Include zero, fractions, opposite signs, counterexamples to commutativity of subtraction/division, and number-line explanations of signed addition.

## Lesson 18.3

[Curriculum](lesson-3-factors-multiples-and-fraction-arithmetic/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-3-factors-multiples-and-fraction-arithmetic/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Prime-factorize 84 and 126, find their GCF and LCM, then compute $5/6-3/4$ and $(2/3)\div(4/5)$.

**Checked reasoning:** $84=2^2\cdot3\cdot7$, $126=2\cdot3^2\cdot7$; GCF 42 and LCM 252. Common denominator 12 gives $10/12-9/12=1/12$. Division multiplies by the nonzero divisor's reciprocal: $2/3\cdot5/4=5/6$.

**Coverage limit:** Include negative fractions, mixed numbers, improper forms, all four operations and divisor-zero cases; simplify without cancelling across addition.

## Lesson 18.4

[Curriculum](lesson-4-decimals-and-rational-representations/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-4-decimals-and-rational-representations/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Write $0.375$ and $0.\overline{27}$ as reduced fractions and decide whether $7/40$ and $5/12$ terminate. Calculate $1.2\div0.03$.

**Checked reasoning:** $0.375=375/1000=3/8$. If $x=0.\overline{27}$, then $100x-x=27$, so $x=27/99=3/11$. Reduced denominator 40 has only factors 2 and 5, so termination; 12 has factor 3, so repetition. $1.2/0.03=120/3=40$.

**Coverage limit:** Include place-value alignment, decimal operations, a nonrepeating prefix followed by repetition, and denominator tests only after reduction.

## Lesson 18.5

[Curriculum](lesson-5-numerical-powers-and-order-of-operations/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-5-numerical-powers-and-order-of-operations/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Evaluate $-3^2$, $(-3)^2$, $2^{-3}$ and $18\div3\cdot2+4(5-3)$.

**Checked reasoning:** Values are $-9,9,1/8,20$. Exponentiation precedes an ungrouped negative; multiplication and division have equal precedence and proceed left to right: $18/3\cdot2=12$, then add 8.

**Coverage limit:** Vary nested grouping, zero and negative exponents with nonzero bases, fraction bars, left-to-right equal-precedence operations and undefined cases such as $0^{-1}$; avoid prescribing a value for $0^0$ here.

## Lesson 18.6

[Curriculum](lesson-6-scientific-notation-estimation-and-precision/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-6-scientific-notation-estimation-and-precision/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Compute $(3\times10^5)(2\times10^{-3})$ and write $0.000072$ in scientific notation. A length is 12.47 cm to the nearest 0.01 cm; round it to the nearest 0.1 cm and state the original rounding interval.

**Checked reasoning:** Product $6\times10^2=600$; $7.2\times10^{-5}$. Rounded length is 12.5 cm. Under the usual positive half-up convention the original interval is $[12.465,12.475)$ cm; endpoint conventions must be stated if ties matter.

**Coverage limit:** Include addition requiring common powers, normalized coefficients, estimation bounds and unit-sensitive precision; distinguish rounding a display from making a measurement more accurate.

## Lesson 18.7

[Curriculum](lesson-7-rational-and-irrational-arithmetic/lesson.md) · [Tutor](lesson-7-rational-and-irrational-arithmetic/tutor.md)

**Prompt:** Decide whether each is rational: $\sqrt2+\sqrt2$, $\sqrt2-\sqrt2$, $\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2$, and $3+\sqrt2$. Explain the last result.

**Checked reasoning:** Results $2\sqrt2$, 0, 2, $3+\sqrt2$ are respectively irrational, rational, rational, irrational. If $3+\sqrt2$ were rational, subtracting rational 3 would make $\sqrt2$ rational, a contradiction. Rational numbers are closed under addition/subtraction/multiplication and division by a nonzero rational.

**Coverage limit:** Include nonzero rational times irrational, zero times irrational, sums/products of two irrationals with contrasting results, and short contradiction proofs.
