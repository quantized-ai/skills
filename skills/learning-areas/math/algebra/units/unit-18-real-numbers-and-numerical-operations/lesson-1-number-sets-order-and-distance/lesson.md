# Lesson 18.1: Number sets, order, and distance

Real-number classifications and number-line positions connect numerical notation to order, magnitude, and distance.

## Learning objectives

- Classify real numbers and compare their positions and magnitudes.
- Represent exact and approximate real-number locations and numerical distances.

## Proficiency criteria

- Distinguish rational from irrational representations and classify zero and negative values correctly.
- Use the direction of the number line for order and nonnegative absolute differences for distance.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Real-number classifications** | Natural numbers use the convention \(1,2,3,\ldots\); whole numbers also include zero. Integers include negatives. Rational numbers are \(a/b\) for integers \(a,b\) with \(b\ne0\), and have terminating or eventually repeating decimals. Irrational real numbers have nonterminating, nonrepeating decimals. The real numbers are the union of these disjoint rational and irrational sets. | Classify numbers into nested real-number sets and distinguish exact rational and irrational representations. | List every applicable number set; do not infer irrationality from a finite displayed decimal or confuse a rational approximation with the exact number. |
| **Order and number-line location** | A real number is represented by a point on the number line; increasing values lie to the right. Inequalities compare value, including negative values. Opposite numbers lie equal distances from zero. Rational bounds locate irrational numbers approximately without replacing their exact values. | Order real numbers and locate them on number lines using equivalent forms and justified rational bounds. | Compare negative values correctly, preserve inequality direction in numerical comparisons, and distinguish exact rational locations from bounded irrational locations. |
| **Absolute value and numerical distance** | \(\lvert a\rvert\) is distance from zero and is nonnegative. The distance between real numbers \(a\) and \(b\) is \(\lvert a-b\rvert\). A signed quantity records direction relative to a reference; magnitude discards direction. Large absolute value does not imply large numerical value. | Interpret signed quantities, compute their magnitudes and distances, and distinguish comparisons of magnitude from comparisons of value. | State the reference represented by zero; report nonnegative distances with correct units and preserve signs when comparing values. |
