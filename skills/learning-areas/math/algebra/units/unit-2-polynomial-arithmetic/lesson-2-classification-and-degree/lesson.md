# Lesson 2.2: Classification and degree

Classify simplified polynomials by term count and degree. Separate these classifications and identify the information carried by a leading term.

## Learning objectives

- Classify polynomials by nonzero term count after expansion and collection of like terms.
- Determine total degree and identify one-variable leading terms and coefficients.
- Apply consistent conventions to constant and zero polynomials.

## Learning outcome

Describe a simplified polynomial by term count, degree, and leading information without confusing these properties.

## Proficiency criteria

- Expand factored expressions and collect like terms before classifying by additive term count or degree.
- Distinguish term-count names from degree and explain the effect of cancellation.
- Determine total degree in several variables; identify leading terms and coefficients in one variable.
- Assign degree zero to nonzero constants and leave the zero polynomial's degree undefined.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monomials, binomials, and trinomials** | Term count refers to nonzero additive terms after expansion and collection of like terms. A monomial has one, a binomial two, and a trinomial three; larger counts are stated directly. Term count is independent of degree. The zero polynomial has no nonzero terms and is not called a monomial in this course. | Classify a simplified polynomial by its number of nonzero terms, using the stated zero-polynomial convention. Expand factored expressions and combine like terms before determining term count. | Count only nonzero terms in the collected expansion. Assign the correct term-count name without using exponent size. Explain changes caused by cancellation and classify the zero polynomial consistently. |
| **Degree, leading term, and leading coefficient** | A nonzero monomial's total degree is the sum of its variable exponents. A nonzero polynomial's degree is the largest total degree among its terms after collection. A nonzero constant has degree $0$; the zero polynomial's degree is undefined here. In one variable, the highest-degree term is the leading term and its signed coefficient is the leading coefficient. Multivariable work here uses total degree, not an unspecified leading-term order. | Determine term and polynomial degree after simplification, including total degree in several variables and the constant and zero cases. Identify the leading term and signed leading coefficient of a nonzero one-variable polynomial. | Sum exponents within each monomial and select the largest surviving term degree. Identify one-variable leading information after cancellation. Distinguish nonzero constants from the zero polynomial and avoid assigning a multivariable leading term without an ordering convention. |
