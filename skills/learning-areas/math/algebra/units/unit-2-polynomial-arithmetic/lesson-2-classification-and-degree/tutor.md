## Teaching boundaries

Separate term count from degree; no multivariable monomial order is assumed. The concept-level evidence checklists below implement the existing curriculum; they do not create extra objectives.

## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** Is x³+2x−x³ a trinomial of degree three? Repair both claims.

**Private reasoning key:** Collection gives 2x, one nonzero term and degree one. The canceled pieces determine neither final count nor final degree.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Monomials, binomials, and trinomials

Curriculum reference: **Monomials, binomials, and trinomials** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Classify $x^2+3x-x^2+4$ by term count.

**Agent key:** It simplifies to $3x+4$, a binomial; count surviving terms, not written pieces.

**Worked example:** Compare $5x^8$, $x^2+x+1$, and $x-x$.

**Worked reasoning:** They are a monomial, a trinomial, and the zero polynomial with no nonzero terms under this curriculum's convention.


#### Teaching sequence

Collect an expression before naming it. Show how x² and −x² disappear without turning the remaining 3x and 4 into like terms. Contrast 5x⁸, which has one term, with x²+x+1, which has three. Discuss zero separately using the curriculum's no-nonzero-terms convention rather than forcing it into a named count category.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Have like terms already been collected?

If a high exponent is used to call a monomial a trinomial, ask the student to point to the three alleged additive terms. If canceled terms are still counted, evaluate their sum symbolically before recounting.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Begin with collected polynomials, then include repeated powers and complete cancellation. Ask for two polynomials of the same degree but different term counts.

#### Assessment evidence

Assess classification after collection, explanation of cancellation, and consistent treatment of zero. The learner must distinguish term count from degree in a construction or comparison task.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Degree, leading term, and leading coefficient

Curriculum reference: **Degree, leading term, and leading coefficient** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Find the degree of $4x^2y^3-2xy+7$.

**Agent key:** Total degrees are 5,2,0, so the degree is 5. No multivariable leading order was supplied.

**Worked example:** Find degree and leading coefficient of $3x^4-3x^4-2x^2+9$, then compare with the zero polynomial.

**Worked reasoning:** The collected polynomial has degree 2 and leading coefficient $-2$; the zero polynomial has undefined degree here.


#### Teaching sequence

Compute each monomial's total degree by adding its variable exponents, then select the largest surviving value. For one variable only, order powers to identify the leading term and signed coefficient. Compare a nonzero constant, degree zero, with the zero polynomial, whose degree is undefined here. Do not invent an ordering for several variables.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First cue:** What does the total degree count in one monomial? **Setup if needed:** Add its variable exponents, then compare degrees of the surviving terms.

If the largest individual exponent is reported for x²y³, ask how many variable factors the monomial contains. If cancellation is ignored, collect before ranking. If zero is called degree zero, compare it with the nonzero constant 7.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Progress from one variable to multivariable total degree, then leading cancellation and zero. Reverse the task by constructing a polynomial with a stated degree and signed leading coefficient.

#### Assessment evidence

Require total degrees, one-variable leading information after collection, and a constant/zero contrast. A multivariable leading-term question must provide an ordering or be identified as underspecified.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

## Decision model and graduated practice

For $2x^3+5x-2x^3+7$, collect the cubic terms first: $(2-2)x^3+5x+7=5x+7$. It is a degree-one binomial, because degree and term count concern the surviving expression. Compare $5x^7$, a degree-seven monomial: fewer terms do not mean lower degree. In $3x^2y^4-xy+6$, total term degrees are $6,2,0$; the total degree is $6$, without choosing a multivariable leading order.

If a learner reports degree $3$, cue “Which coefficients survive collection?”; set up $(2-2)x^3+5x+7$; then work the cancellation, leaving classification. Fade with $4x^2-x-4x^2$ (key monomial $-x$, degree $1$, leading coefficient $-1$). Follow with $4x^2-4x^2$: zero has no nonzero terms and undefined degree here. Do not infer a misunderstanding of degree solely from an unexplained answer before seeing collection.
