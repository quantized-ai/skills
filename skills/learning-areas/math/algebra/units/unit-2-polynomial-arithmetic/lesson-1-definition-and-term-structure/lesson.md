# Lesson 2.1: Definition and term structure

Define polynomials, identify their structural parts, and interpret those parts as quantities in context.

## Learning objectives

- Classify polynomial formulas while retaining any restrictions from their original expressions.
- Distinguish terms, coefficients, constants, and factors.
- Interpret simple and composite expression parts, units, and contextual input restrictions.

## Learning outcome

Justify polynomial classification and explain structural parts, contextual meanings, and permitted inputs.

## Proficiency criteria

- Classify formulas in the specified variable using the finite-sum and exponent conditions; preserve original domain restrictions during simplification.
- Identify signed terms, factors, and explicit, implicit, or zero coefficients at the correct structural level.
- Interpret expression parts and composite quantities with compatible units.
- Distinguish algebraic domain restrictions from contextual input restrictions.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What is a polynomial?** | A polynomial in $x$ is a finite sum $a_nx^n+\cdots+a_1x+a_0$ with fixed real coefficients and nonnegative integer exponents. Constants, including zero, qualify; coefficients may be irrational or fixed real parameters. Classification concerns the simplified formula: negative or fractional variable powers must be eliminated by valid simplification for it to have polynomial form. Original domain exclusions survive simplification, so agreement with a polynomial formula on the original domain does not extend that domain. | Classify a formula as polynomial in the specified variable and preserve original domain restrictions when simplifying it. Justify polynomial classification using finite sums, fixed coefficients, and nonnegative integer variable exponents. | Distinguish the variable from fixed coefficients or parameters. Apply the finite-sum and exponent conditions after valid simplification. Retain excluded inputs and distinguish a reduced polynomial formula from the original function. |
| **Terms, coefficients, constants, and factors** | Terms are signed additive parts; factors are multiplicative parts. A coefficient multiplies a variable power; the constant term is independent of the specified variable. Undisplayed unit coefficients are $1$ or $-1$; missing powers have coefficient $0$. An outer factor may contain several additive terms, so structure depends on the level being described. | Identify signed terms, variables, constant terms, and explicit, implicit, or zero coefficients in a polynomial. Distinguish additive terms from multiplicative factors at different structural levels. | Identify complete signed terms without separating coefficients from variable factors. Identify unit coefficients and zero coefficients for missing powers. Distinguish outer factors from terms within those factors. |
| **Interpreting quantities from polynomial structure** | Terms, factors, coefficients, and constants represent quantities with contextual meanings and units. A composite expression can represent one quantity within a larger expression. Expanded form displays additive contributions; factored form displays multiplicative relationships. Added quantities require compatible units; products combine units. Context may restrict inputs beyond the polynomial formula's algebraic domain. | Interpret terms, factors, coefficients, and constants using contextual quantities and units, and identify input restrictions imposed by the context. Interpret a composite expression part as a single meaningful quantity and explain its relationship to the whole expression. | Assign contextual meanings and compatible units to expression parts. Interpret a composite part as one quantity and relate it to the whole. Distinguish algebraically permitted inputs from inputs permitted by the stated context. |
