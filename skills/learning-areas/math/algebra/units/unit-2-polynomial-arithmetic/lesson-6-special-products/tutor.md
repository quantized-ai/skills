## Reasoning activity

Use after the relevant basic procedure is accessible. Reveal the prompt first, let the student identify and repair the reasoning, and close with the correct explanation. In assess mode generate a new task instead of administering this exposed example.

**Prompt:** Explain why (x−4)² and (x−4)(x+4) have different middle and constant terms.

**Private reasoning key:** The square gives x²−8x+16: the cross terms add and the negative factors multiply positively. The conjugates give x²−16: cross terms cancel and the constant product is negative.

Ask what changed between the original claim and the repaired explanation. If the response is only a guessed answer, use the corresponding concept’s targeted cue below; after feedback, collect a new independent attempt.

## Concept guidance

### Squares of binomials

Curriculum reference: **Squares of binomials** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Expand $(3x-2)^2$.

**Agent key:** $(3x)^2-2(3x)(2)+2^2=9x^2-12x+4$.

**Worked example:** Explain the first error in $(x+5)^2=x^2+25$.

**Worked reasoning:** The two cross products $5x$ and $5x$ were omitted; the correct expansion is $x^2+10x+25$.


#### Teaching sequence

Write (a−b)² as (a−b)(a−b) and retain all four products before collecting. Point out that two negative cross products combine while (−b)(−b) is positive. Identify whole components such as 3x and 2 before applying the pattern. Use distribution to justify the identity rather than requiring recall as its explanation.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** What does multiplying the binomial by itself produce?

If the middle term vanishes, ask where the two cross products went. If b² is negative, isolate (−b)(−b). If 3x is squared as 3x², show that both coefficient and variable occur twice.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Start with numerical components, then linear monomials and polynomial components. Include plus/minus squares and a missing middle coefficient to determine and justify.

#### Assessment evidence

Require component identification, both cross products, squared coefficients/powers, and repair of a false expansion. The explanation must distinguish a square of a difference from a difference of squares.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.

### Products of conjugate binomials

Curriculum reference: **Products of conjugate binomials** in the [curriculum concept table](lesson.md#concepts). Read that row’s Content, Learning Objectives, and Proficiency criteria before using these activities.

#### Calibration and worked reasoning

**Diagnostic prompt:** Expand $(2x+3)(2x-3)$.

**Agent key:** Cross products cancel, leaving $4x^2-9$.

**Worked example:** Compute $48\cdot52$ using a common center.

**Worked reasoning:** $(50-2)(50+2)=50^2-2^2=2496$; the offset is 2, not 4.


#### Teaching sequence

Expand the two conjugates once and identify the equal opposite cross products. Preserve the order a²−b² when identifying the common component a and offset b. For 48·52, compute the midpoint 50 and equal distance 2 before squaring. Ask why the cross terms cancel here but add in a squared binomial.

#### Respond to student reasoning

**First hint:** Which components agree and which have opposite signs?

If the full separation 4 is used as the offset, measure each factor's distance from the midpoint. If the final order is reversed, test a larger than b with small numbers and re-expand.

Give only the cue relevant to the observed error; wait for a revision before showing a worked step. Record any mathematical help as assistance.

#### Practice progression

Vary symbolic common parts, signed offsets and numerical products around a convenient center. Reverse the task by reconstructing conjugates from a difference of squares.

#### Assessment evidence

Collect symbolic justification, complete-component squaring and a numerical center/offset application. A recalled formula without identifying its components is incomplete evidence.

Use the [fresh-question policy](../question-generation.md); the two calibration examples above are exposed learning material, not the default quiz. Collect the curriculum's full proficiency evidence, retain demonstrated components, and reassess unresolved cases with new tasks.
