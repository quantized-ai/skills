# Lesson 17.7: Randomized treatment comparisons

Randomization distributions ask how large a treatment difference chance assignment could produce under a no-effect model. Evidence from that comparison must be separated from practical importance and population reach.

## Learning objectives

- Construct an ordered treatment-difference statistic and a randomization distribution under a sharp no-effect model.
- Preserve original assignment rules and use a preselected extremeness rule to judge the observed difference.
- Distinguish statistical evidence from practical magnitude, individual effects, and the design’s causal and population scope.

## Learning outcome

The student can conduct and interpret an informal randomization comparison that respects the experimental design.

## Proficiency criteria

- Calculate the same ordered difference in means or proportions for observed and simulated assignments.
- Hold outcomes fixed and reassign labels according to the original group sizes and any blocking or pairing.
- Interpret the at-least-as-extreme tail frequency conditionally on the sharp no-effect model.
- Qualify conclusions for practical importance, variation in individual effects, and the population supported by recruitment and sampling.

## Concepts

| Concept Title | Content | Learning Objectives | Proficiency criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Randomization distributions under no effect** | A treatment statistic is an ordered difference in group means or proportions. The sharp no-treatment-effect model says every participant’s outcome would be unchanged by treatment assignment; this permits outcomes to remain fixed while treatment labels are reassigned. Reassignments follow the original randomization mechanism: preserve group sizes in a fixed-size completely randomized design and preserve any blocks or pairs. Recalculating the same statistic for each reassignment produces a randomization distribution. Equality of average effects alone does not justify this fixed-outcome sharp-null construction. | Define and calculate an ordered treatment-difference statistic and construct its randomization distribution under a sharp no-treatment-effect model. Explain why that model permits outcomes to remain fixed and reassign labels using the original group-size, blocking, or pairing rules. | Use the same group order and statistic for observed and simulated comparisons. Hold outcomes fixed and reproduce the actual treatment-assignment mechanism. Explain the individual no-effect assumption and why changing the assignment design would simulate a different process. |
| **Significance, practical size, and causal scope** | An observed difference is compared with its valid no-effect randomization distribution using a direction chosen before examining results. A two-sided comparison based on absolute differences counts values at least as large in magnitude; a directional comparison uses the specified tail. A small tail proportion indicates incompatibility with the sharp no-effect model, not certainty about an effect; a large one does not establish equivalence. Statistical unusualness is distinct from practical effect magnitude and does not show that every individual benefits. Causal interpretation depends on sound experimental implementation, and population generalization also depends on recruitment and sampling. | Use a preselected directional or two-sided extremeness rule to evaluate a treatment difference and interpret its model-conditional tail frequency. Distinguish statistical evidence, practical magnitude, and individual effects, and state the causal and population limits supported by the design. | Count randomized statistics at least as extreme as observed using the prespecified rule. Interpret the tail proportion as evidence about the sharp no-effect model without claiming proof or equivalence. Separate practical magnitude and individual variation from statistical unusualness and restrict causal and population conclusions to the design’s support. |
