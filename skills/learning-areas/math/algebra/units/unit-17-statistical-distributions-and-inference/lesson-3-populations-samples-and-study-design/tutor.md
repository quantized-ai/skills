## Reasoning and error-analysis activity

**Ask:** A large randomly sampled survey is called proof that exposure caused the outcome. Ask the student to locate the first invalid inference, repair the reasoning, and explain a check or counterexample.

**Private reasoning and response:** Random sampling supports population association, not assignment-based causality. Ask what exposure researchers assigned; propose a specific confounder and a design repair.

If the student gives only a corrected answer, ask why the original method failed. If the reason is secure, request a different example where the distinction matters. If they remain stuck, use the targeted response below for the relevant concept and mark the attempt assisted.

## Concept teaching plans

### Parameters, statistics, and random samples

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** A school samples 40 pupils from a list of all 600. Which is a parameter: all 600's mean or the 40's mean?

**Private diagnostic key:** population mean is parameter; sample mean is statistic.

**Teach in this order:** Define target population and frame separately; specify eligible units; describe selection probabilities; distinguish fixed population quantity from varying statistic.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** To choose 3 from numbered 1..10, draw distinct labels uniformly without replacement. Every three-element subset has probability 1/120. Picking the first 3 arrivals is not an SRS; missing pupils from the list causes frame undercoverage even with random draws.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If 'random' means convenient, ask how every eligible pupil could be selected; next require a reproducible number-label mechanism. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Identify parameter/statistic → implement SRS → diagnose frame and nonresponse limitations. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Population/frame/sample; equal-subset SRS meaning; statistic variation; actual selection method; coverage/nonresponse. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Identify the population, frame, parameter, and statistic separately. Explain what varies across repeated samples and what remains fixed for the defined population. Connect known random selection mechanisms to population inference and state coverage or response limitations.

### Surveys, observational studies, and experiments

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** Volunteers are randomly assigned to two lessons. Does this guarantee population representativeness?

**Private diagnostic key:** no; assignment supports causal comparison, recruitment limits generalization.

**Teach in this order:** Ask separately 'who entered?' and 'who assigned exposure?'; classify collection and assignment; map each mechanism to its permissible claim.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** A random sample is asked about sleep and test scores without assigning sleep. This supports a population association, subject to response/measurement limits, but cannot separate sleep's influence from workload. A well-run randomized sleep intervention addresses assignment confounding but needs recruitment evidence for broad generalization.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If an observational survey is called an experiment, ask what researchers assigned; next identify exposure selection by participants. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Classify study → contrast sampling/assignment combinations → write qualified scope of conclusion. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Survey as usually observational; nonrandom experiments possible; random sampling versus assignment; causality/generalization separately justified. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Identify whether data came from questions or other measurements and whether researchers assigned the exposure. Distinguish random selection of participants from random assignment of treatments. Qualify causal and population conclusions using the actual design, without assuming perfectly balanced groups.

### Bias, confounding, and design repair

**Diagnostic — ask and wait:** A website poll receives 100000 voluntary responses. Does size remove selection bias?

**Private diagnostic key:** no.

**Teach in this order:** Identify the mechanism that systematically excludes or distorts responses; name a plausible confounder; choose a repair targeting that mechanism.

**Distinct worked model — reveal in steps:** To estimate students' travel times, interviewing only bus riders misses walkers. Repair the frame by sampling all enrolled students and recording travel mode; neutral questions address wording, but cannot alone repair missing walkers. More bus-rider responses leave the same coverage defect.

**Misconception response and hint ladder:** If every problem is solved by 'more data', ask who remains absent after enlarging the sample; next contrast chance variability with systematic selection. If the first prompt is insufficient, use the next indicated representation/setup; only then reveal one calculation or inference. Ask the student to finish the remaining reasoning.

**Practice progression:** Identify bias → distinguish confounding/chance → redesign selection, wording or assignment. Change a meaningful case or representation before increasing arithmetic size. Reuse the student's error as the focus of a new task, not by repeating an exposed answer.

**Assessment case checklist:** Undercoverage/nonresponse/voluntary response/leading questions; specific repair; larger n limitations; plausible confounding path. Select independent tasks that collectively cover these cases and the curriculum row's full proficiency; a short session may leave named cases unassessed.

**Curriculum evidence contract:** Name the specific mechanism connecting a design weakness to a distorted conclusion. Propose a revision that addresses that mechanism and state a remaining limitation. Distinguish reduced sampling variation from reduced systematic bias.

## Extended private calibration

**Prompt:** A school asks volunteers in an advanced class whether extra tutoring improves scores. It compares tutored and untutored volunteers without random assignment. Identify the population, sample, statistic, possible bias and a repair.

**Private worked key:** The intended population might be all school students and must be declared; the observed sample is the participating advanced-class volunteers. A sample average or proportion is a statistic; the corresponding population value is a parameter. Selection, prior achievement and motivation can confound the comparison. A probability sample supports broader generalization; random assignment of a tutoring offer supports a causal comparison under the design. One does not replace the other.

This previously checked composite example can connect concepts after instruction. Split it into manageable turns; it does not replace the distinct diagnostic and worked model for each concept.

## Decision model and graduated practice

A school randomly samples $40$ students from a complete list of $600$ and asks about study time. The target population is the $600$, the frame is the list, and the sample mean is a statistic estimating their fixed population mean. Repeating selection changes the statistic, not that defined parameter. This survey supports population estimation subject to response and measurement limits; it assigns no tutoring exposure.

Contrast volunteers randomly assigned to two tutoring methods: assignment supports a causal comparison under sound implementation, while volunteer recruitment limits population generalization. Cue “Who was randomly selected, and what was randomly assigned?”; next make separate selection and assignment columns; then fill only the selection mechanism, leaving conclusions. Fade by changing one mechanism at a time. If a learner says “larger sample removes bias,” ask whether excluded students ever enter the frame; increasing responses from the same incomplete frame cannot fix undercoverage.
