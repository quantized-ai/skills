# Lesson 17.8: Evaluation of statistical reports

Statistical report evaluation traces a claim back to the study design, numerical summaries, and uncertainty that support it. A sound critique identifies a specific gap and rewrites the claim without discarding valid evidence.

## Learning objectives

- Trace statistical claims to the population, study design, data, and numerical summaries supporting them.
- Evaluate denominators, absolute and relative effects, displays, uncertainty, and selective reporting.
- Rewrite overclaims with defensible causal and population scope while preserving supported evidence.

## Learning outcome

The student can distinguish supported findings from overclaims and communicate a statistically defensible version of a report.

## Proficiency criteria

- Identify what was measured, who was studied, how data were collected, and which statistics support the claim.
- Check causal language, population generalization, and claims about individuals against the design and analysis.
- Diagnose misleading denominators, effect descriptions, graph scales, omitted uncertainty, or selective reporting.
- Produce a revised claim that retains supported findings and states specific uncertainties and limitations.
