# Unit 17: Statistical distributions and simulation-based inference

Data summaries, normal models, study design, and simulations support population estimates, treatment comparisons, and critical statistical interpretation.

## Main learning objectives

- Distinguish data types, describe quantitative distributions, and justify suitable summaries. Calculate and interpret mean and standard deviation using the stated sample or population convention.
- Assess the appropriateness of a normal model from shape and context. Use mean and positive standard deviation to describe central normal intervals and approximate percentages.
- Distinguish target populations, sampling frames, parameters, and sample statistics, including repeated-sample variation. Classify surveys, observational studies, and experiments and distinguish random sampling from random assignment.
- Design reproducible simulations that preserve stated probabilities, dependence, and sampling rules. Estimate event probabilities from repeated trials and explain simulation variability.
- Construct sampling distributions of means and distinguish them from distributions of individual observations. Explain how random selection and sample size affect mean variability without eliminating systematic bias.
- Calculate sample proportions and simulate their distribution under a stated population and sampling model. Explain sample-size effects on proportion variability and distinguish them from raw-count variability.
- Construct an ordered treatment-difference statistic and a randomization distribution under a sharp no-effect model. Preserve original assignment rules and use a preselected extremeness rule to judge the observed difference.
- Trace statistical claims to the population, study design, data, and numerical summaries supporting them. Evaluate denominators, absolute and relative effects, displays, uncertainty, and selective reporting.
- Design, verify, and repair random allocation rules using a stated fairness criterion. Analyze two-way test outcomes using conditional probabilities, base rates, and both error types.

## Concepts and topics

Shape, center, and appropriate summaries; Mean, standard deviation, and units; Normal shape and model appropriateness; Standard scores and normal areas; Parameters, statistics, and random samples; Surveys, observational studies, and experiments; Bias, confounding, and design repair; Random trials and empirical frequencies; Consistency of a chance model with observations; Repeated-sample mean variability; Simulation-based margin of error for a mean; Sample proportions and sampling variation; Simulation-based margin of error for a proportion; Randomization distributions under no effect; Significance, practical size, and causal scope; Claims, evidence, and uncertainty; Fair random allocation; Conditional probabilities and decision tradeoffs.

## Lessons

- [17.1: Data distributions and summaries](lesson-1-data-distributions-and-summaries/lesson.md) — Distribution shape determines which summaries are informative. The lesson reviews center and spread while distinguishing sample estimates, complete-population quantities, and the units of standard deviation.
- [17.2: Normal distribution models](lesson-2-normal-distribution-models/lesson.md) — A normal distribution is a model whose shape must be justified before areas are interpreted. Standard scores connect original measurements to cumulative areas and estimated population percentages.
- [17.3: Populations, samples, and study design](lesson-3-populations-samples-and-study-design/lesson.md) — Statistical conclusions depend on who was measured and how the data were generated. The lesson separates population inference, causal comparison, and specific threats from bias or confounding.
- [17.4: Probability simulation and model checking](lesson-4-probability-simulation-and-model-checking/lesson.md) — Simulation approximates probabilities by reproducing a specified chance mechanism. Model checking compares observed data with a preselected statistic generated repeatedly under that model.
- [17.5: Sampling distributions of means](lesson-5-sampling-distributions-of-means/lesson.md) — Repeated random samples produce a distribution of sample means. An empirical sampling model uses that variation to develop an approximate margin for a population mean while preserving the design assumptions.
- [17.6: Sampling distributions of proportions](lesson-6-sampling-distributions-of-proportions/lesson.md) — Binary outcomes lead to sample proportions whose variation depends on the sampling process and size. A plug-in simulation turns that variation into an approximate proportion margin expressed in percentage points.
- [17.7: Randomized treatment comparisons](lesson-7-randomized-treatment-comparisons/lesson.md) — Randomization distributions ask how large a treatment difference chance assignment could produce under a no-effect model. Evidence from that comparison must be separated from practical importance and population reach.
- [17.8: Evaluation of statistical reports](lesson-8-evaluation-of-statistical-reports/lesson.md) — Statistical report evaluation traces a claim back to the study design, numerical summaries, and uncertainty that support it. A sound critique identifies a specific gap and rewrites the claim without discarding valid evidence.
- [17.9: Probability-based decisions](lesson-9-probability-based-decisions/lesson.md) — Probability supports fair allocations and decisions involving imperfect information. Equal-chance rules, conditional denominators, base rates, and explicit error costs determine whether a proposed decision procedure serves its stated objective.
