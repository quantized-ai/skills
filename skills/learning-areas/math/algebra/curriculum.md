# Algebra

Algebra is a master collection of 59 reusable units and 290 focused lessons spanning foundational algebra, Algebra I and II, geometry, trigonometry, precalculus, probability and statistics, discrete mathematics, and quantitative applications. Each lesson defines content, learning objectives, and proficiency; the collection contains curriculum definitions rather than teaching or assessment materials. Calculus is outside its scope.

Units are the reusable selection boundary. Their numbers identify catalog entries, not a mandatory learning sequence. A program chooses whole units and groups and orders them into courses. The [Common Core Appendix reference program](<program/1. common_core_appendix.md>) is one such arrangement, including named course options beyond the traditional three-course pathway. See [sources and scope](sources-and-scope.md) for source roles and boundaries and [standards coverage](standards-coverage.md) for alignment evidence.

## Main learning objectives

- Calculate and justify numerical and symbolic operations, transform expressions, and solve equations, inequalities, and simultaneous constraints with complete solution sets and valid domains.
- Represent, compare, construct, and invert functions; analyze polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, root, trigonometric, and power relationships and discrete sequences.
- Prove geometric relationships, perform constructions and transformations, connect loci with coordinates, and use measurement and trigonometry in two and three dimensions.
- Analyze vectors, matrices, complex and polar representations, conics, and parametric relations using appropriate algebraic and geometric methods.
- Represent data, design and critique studies, model probability, and make qualified statistical inferences and decisions using simulation and formal methods.
- Model financial and applied quantities, compare algorithms and collective decisions, and investigate and communicate mathematical claims with explicit assumptions and evidence.

- [2. Polynomial arithmetic](units/unit-2-polynomial-arithmetic/unit.md) — Polynomial structure supports exact arithmetic, identities, and interpretation of quantities and coefficients.
- [3. Polynomial factorization](units/unit-3-polynomial-factorization/unit.md) — Factoring methods expose product structure and connect polynomial equations, zeros, and completeness over a stated coefficient system.
- [4. Complex numbers and complex quadratic equations](units/unit-4-complex-numbers-and-quadratic-equations/unit.md) — Complex arithmetic extends the real number system, gives geometric representations of arithmetic, and supplies the nonreal solutions of real-coefficient quadratic equations.
- [5. Quadratic functions and inequalities](units/unit-5-quadratic-functions-inequalities-and-parabolas/unit.md) — Equivalent quadratic forms connect graph features, construction from attributes or data, and quadratic inequality solution sets.
- [6. Polynomial division, equations, and graphs](units/unit-6-polynomial-division-equations-and-graphs/unit.md) — Division, zeros, multiplicities, graph behavior, inequalities, and binomial expansion extend polynomial analysis beyond quadratics.
- [7. Rational expressions](units/unit-7-rational-expressions/unit.md) — Equivalent rational expressions and their arithmetic preserve original denominator exclusions and distinguish cancellation of factors from terms.
- [8. Rational functions and equations](units/unit-8-rational-functions-and-equations/unit.md) — Reciprocal transformations, rational graphs, domain restrictions, and equation methods connect inverse variation and quantitative rational relationships.
- [9. Radicals, rational exponents, and root functions](units/unit-9-radicals-rational-exponents-and-root-functions/unit.md) — Real roots and exponent rules connect radical arithmetic, root graphs, and equations while preserving domains and checking candidates.
- [10. Exponential functions](units/unit-10-exponential-functions/unit.md) — Exponential structure, growth and decay, time scales, graph transformations, and equations describe multiplicative change.
- [11. Logarithms](units/unit-11-logarithms/unit.md) — Logarithmic notation, identities, graphs, and equations invert exponential relationships and quantify growth or decay durations.
- [12. Function operations, composition, and inverses](units/unit-12-function-operations-and-inverses/unit.md) — Function combinations and composition preserve domain conditions; inverse relations require one-to-one behavior and appropriate restrictions.
- [13. Linear systems in three variables](units/unit-13-systems-and-graphical-solutions/unit.md) — Equivalent-system reasoning extends substitution and elimination to three variables, matrix representations, and complete solution classification.
- [14. Sequences and finite series](units/unit-14-sequences-and-series/unit.md) — Recursive and explicit descriptions distinguish arithmetic and geometric change; finite-sum formulas support accumulated quantities and deposit models.
- [15. Trigonometric functions](units/unit-15-trigonometric-functions/unit.md) — Radian measure and unit-circle definitions support exact values, identities, periodic graphs, and sinusoidal models.
- [16. Function models and regression](units/unit-16-function-models-and-regression/unit.md) — Quantities, constraints, family selection, regression, and residual analysis connect function models to data and defensible predictions.
- [17. Statistical distributions and simulation-based inference](units/unit-17-statistical-distributions-and-inference/unit.md) — Data summaries, normal models, study design, and simulations support population estimates, treatment comparisons, and critical statistical interpretation.
- [18. Real numbers and numerical operations](units/unit-18-real-numbers-and-numerical-operations/unit.md) — This unit establishes the numerical language and arithmetic used throughout the catalog, from whole-number factors and signed fractions to powers of ten and justified approximations.
- [19. Ratios, proportional reasoning, and measurement](units/unit-19-ratios-proportional-reasoning-and-measurement/unit.md) — This unit links ratios and rates to proportional equations, percent change, and the units that give quantitative results meaning.
- [20. Algebraic expressions and structure](units/unit-20-algebraic-expressions-and-structure/unit.md) — This unit develops the language and equivalence of expressions so that substitution and symbolic rewriting have explicit numerical meaning.
- [21. Linear equations and literal formulas](units/unit-21-linear-equations-and-literal-formulas/unit.md) — This unit defines equations as conditions on possible values, develops justified linear solution methods, and applies the same reasoning to formulas and contextual relationships.
- [22. One-variable linear inequalities](units/unit-22-one-variable-linear-inequalities/unit.md) — This unit represents ordered constraints as sets of possible values and develops justified methods for individual and compound inequalities.
- [23. Coordinate plane and linear functions](units/unit-23-coordinate-plane-and-linear-functions/unit.md) — This unit connects ordered pairs, equations of lines, and constant rates of change, culminating in linear models and their transformations.
- [24. Two-variable linear systems and inequalities](units/unit-24-two-variable-linear-systems-and-inequalities/unit.md) — This unit interprets shared linear constraints as simultaneous solution sets, develops exact and graphical solution methods, and extends intersections to inequality regions.
- [25. Data displays and descriptive relationships](units/unit-25-data-displays-and-descriptive-relationships/unit.md) — This unit develops the descriptive statistics needed to organize data, compare distributions, and evaluate linear relationships before studying inference.
- [26. Geometric foundations and logic](units/unit-26-geometric-foundations-and-logic/unit.md) — Precise geometric language supports measurement, classification, conjecture, and proof. Plane geometry is distinguished from spherical geometry so that the role of assumptions remains explicit.
- [27. Rigid motions and congruence](units/unit-27-rigid-motions-and-congruence/unit.md) — Transformations act on plane points and preserve or change geometric measurements. Rigid motions give a precise meaning to congruence and support triangle congruence criteria.
- [28. Geometric theorems and proof](units/unit-28-geometric-theorems-and-proof/unit.md) — Deductive arguments establish angle, triangle, and quadrilateral relationships and their useful converses. Polygon relationships and triangle centers extend those arguments to connected families of figures.
- [29. Geometric constructions](units/unit-29-geometric-constructions/unit.md) — Exact constructions use geometric constraints to create and justify figures. Compass-and-straightedge methods are compared with another exact geometric method, and extended to polygons, triangle circles, and tangents.
- [30. Similarity and the Pythagorean theorem](units/unit-30-similarity-and-the-pythagorean-theorem/unit.md) — Dilations and rigid motions define similarity and explain proportional relationships. Triangle similarity proves proportionality theorems and establishes right-triangle metric relationships.
- [31. Triangle trigonometry](units/unit-31-triangle-trigonometry/unit.md) — Triangle similarity defines acute-angle ratios; angle extensions support laws for every nondegenerate triangle. Proofs, inverse ratios, and admissible-data analysis connect exact relationships with measurement models.
- [32. Coordinate geometry](units/unit-32-coordinate-geometry/unit.md) — Coordinates translate distance, direction, partition, and incidence into algebra. These tools support general geometric proofs, polygon measurement, and the equation of a circle.
- [33. Circle geometry](units/unit-33-circle-geometry/unit.md) — Similarity, congruent triangles, and angle relationships explain circle geometry. Theorems about chords, arcs, tangents, and secants connect to lengths, radians, and sectors.
- [34. Plane and solid measurement](units/unit-34-plane-and-solid-measurement/unit.md) — Area and volume formulas are explained through dissection, limiting arguments, and cross-sections. Measurement extends from polygons and circles to surfaces, composite solids, and dimensional changes.
- [35. Geometric modeling and design](units/unit-35-geometric-modeling-and-design/unit.md) — Geometric models turn physical descriptions into measurable shapes, density relationships, and design constraints. Model choice, computation, interpretation, and revision remain connected throughout.
- [36. Trigonometric identities, inverses, and equations](units/unit-36-trigonometric-identities-inverses-and-equations/unit.md) — This unit completes the six trigonometric functions and develops identities, restricted inverses, and equation methods. Exact identities support algebraic reasoning; principal inverse values and periodicity together determine complete solution sets and contextual solutions.
- [37. Polar coordinates and complex geometry](units/unit-37-polar-coordinates-and-complex-geometry/unit.md) — This unit relates rectangular and polar descriptions of points, curves, and complex numbers. Geometric operations on complex numbers lead to rotation-scaling interpretations, powers, and complete sets of complex roots.
- [38. Vectors and vector models](units/unit-38-vectors-and-vector-models/unit.md) — Vectors encode magnitude and direction through arrows, components, and angular descriptions. Their arithmetic supports displacement, velocity, and force models, while the dot product adds an angle and projection connection.
- [39. Matrix algebra and transformations](units/unit-39-matrix-algebra-and-transformations/unit.md) — Matrices organize quantitative relationships and define linear transformations. Dimension-aware arithmetic leads to determinants, inverses, matrix equations for systems, and geometric interpretations of planar transformations.
- [40. Conic sections, ellipses, and hyperbolas](units/unit-40-conic-sections-ellipses-and-hyperbolas/unit.md) — This unit connects plane sections of a cone, distance loci, and rectangular equations. Ellipses and hyperbolas extend the circle and parabola families through focal definitions, derived equations, graph features, and translated forms.
- [41. Parametric relations and motion](units/unit-41-parametric-relations-and-motion/unit.md) — Parametric equations describe coordinated quantities through a shared input and retain timing, orientation, and repeated traversal that a rectangular relation can conceal. Linear, circular, and projectile models connect the representation to motion.
- [42. Advanced function behavior](units/unit-42-advanced-function-behavior/unit.md) — This unit develops power functions, function decomposition, symbolic average rates, and precise descriptions of behavior near excluded inputs and at infinity. Quotient graphs and rational inequalities integrate algebraic structure with interval reasoning; elementary limit language supports these topics without introducing differentiation.
- [43. Probability and counting](units/unit-43-probability-and-counting/unit.md) — Finite and geometric sample spaces, counting, conditional probability, and random allocation provide the probability foundations used in geometry and statistics.
- [44. Random variables and probability distributions](units/unit-44-random-variables-and-probability-distributions/unit.md) — Discrete and continuous probability models connect counts, expected value, spread, and decision analysis.
- [45. Statistical investigation and communication](units/unit-45-statistical-investigation-and-communication/unit.md) — Study design, variability, and evidence-based communication define the complete statistical investigation process.
- [46. Sampling distributions and formal inference](units/unit-46-sampling-distributions-and-formal-inference/unit.md) — Probability-based sampling distributions support confidence intervals and hypothesis tests beyond the simulation-based inference already in the collection.
- [47. Regression methods and influential data](units/unit-47-regression-methods-and-influential-data/unit.md) — Alternative fitting criteria and influence analysis explain why different lines summarize the same observations differently.
- [48. Financial mathematics and quantitative decisions](units/unit-48-financial-mathematics-and-quantitative-decisions/unit.md) — Rates, cash flows, amortization, and risk models make income, spending, borrowing, saving, and insurance decisions mathematically explicit.
- [49. Applied mathematical modeling](units/unit-49-applied-mathematical-modeling/unit.md) — A modeling cycle combines algebraic, geometric, periodic, and discrete relationships with validation in scientific, artistic, and social contexts.
- [50. Graphs and network optimization](units/unit-50-graphs-and-network-optimization/unit.md) — Vertex-edge models distinguish route inspection, tours, connecting networks, and precedence relationships.
- [51. Scheduling and packing algorithms](units/unit-51-scheduling-and-packing-algorithms/unit.md) — Heuristic schedules and packings are compared against resource and precedence constraints and provable lower bounds.
- [52. Voting and fair division](units/unit-52-voting-and-fair-division/unit.md) — Collective choices and resource allocations depend on explicit rules, preference information, and definitions of fairness.
- [53. Game theory and theory of moves](units/unit-53-game-theory-and-theory-of-moves/unit.md) — Strategic models compare simultaneous choices with outcome-dependent moves and countermoves.
- [54. Mathematical reasoning and independent investigation](units/unit-54-mathematical-reasoning-and-independent-investigation/unit.md) — Logical analysis, conjecture testing, argument, and communication support independent mathematical inquiry across the collection.
- [55. Real quadratic equations](units/unit-55-real-quadratic-equations/unit.md) — Equivalent transformations produce complete real solution sets through factoring, square roots, square completion, and the quadratic formula.
- [56. Parabolas as geometric loci](units/unit-56-parabolas-as-geometric-loci/unit.md) — The focus–directrix definition yields vertical and horizontal parabola equations and connects geometric parameters with quadratic relations.
- [57. Quadratic systems and numerical intersections](units/unit-57-quadratic-systems-and-numerical-intersections/unit.md) — Simultaneous nonlinear constraints connect algebraic solution sets with graphical intersections and carefully qualified numerical approximations.
- [58. Infinite geometric series](units/unit-58-infinite-geometric-series/unit.md) — Limits of geometric partial sums define infinite accumulation and quantify the error of finite approximations.
- [59. Function patterns and multiple representations](units/unit-59-function-patterns-and-multiple-representations/unit.md) — This unit connects function patterns, exact and approximate tables, symbolic operations, graphs, inverses, and contextual input questions. It makes the representational reasoning of algebraic modeling explicit across function families.
